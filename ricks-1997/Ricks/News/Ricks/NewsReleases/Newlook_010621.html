Ricks College News Release
June 21, 2001

Ricks announces new look 

as it becomes BYU-Idaho

    
REXBURG, Idaho – A new visual identity system has been developed for Brigham Young University–Idaho that will replace existing logos and word marks used at Ricks College.

Gone will be the current logo of the Grand Teton and the words "Ricks College."

The new visual identity system will be used when Ricks officially becomes known as BYU-Idaho beginning Aug. 10.

The new designs were developed in conjunction with BYU in Provo, Utah, and BYU-Hawaii. The three BYU institutions that are affiliated with The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints will have a shared identity system using common designs.

Brian Memmott, coordinator of graphic services at Ricks, says, "A visual identity helps maintain a consistent and unified way of presenting yourself to the public. A visual identity works similar to a person’s face, but in this case it’s the face of a university. As people get to know you, it takes on your personality."

The new system relies entirely on lettering and does not use a graphic symbol such as a mountain. It includes a formal word mark that will be used on letterhead and scholarly materials, an informal word mark that will be used on clothing, signs and brochures, a round medallion, and a college seal reserved for diplomas and legal documents.

Memmott says the school decided to use the abbreviation "BYU" as part of the identity system because it’s so universally recognized. In many cases, it’s even more recognized than the school’s full name "Brigham Young University."
