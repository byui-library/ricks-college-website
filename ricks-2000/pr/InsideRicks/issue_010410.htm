FACULTY MEMBER HONORED FOR EXEMPLARY SERVICE

Richard Bird was honored April 6 as the recipient of the Exemplary Faculty of the Year Award by the Ricks College Faculty Association. Bird has taught in the Art Department at Ricks for 30 years and also served as director of Graphic Services at Ricks for 23 years. Besides teaching, he has stayed active as a designer and artist -- believing that in order to be a better teacher, one must be a practicing artist. As part of the prestigious honor, Bird addressed his peers at the Faculty Association banquet. The teacher said, "I love teaching. It is exciting for me to help someone understand a concept, a process or even in explaining individual steps toward solving a problem."

Division Honorary Faculty Awards were presented to Tony Turley in agriculture and biological science, Kent Marlor in behavioral and social sciences, Phil Packer in business, Gordon Black in engineering and technology, Kim Van Wagoner in health and education, Larry Thompson in language and letters, Ed Williams in physical and math sciences, and Kathleen Gee in religious and family living. The Honorary Non-Classroom/Extended Teaching Faculty Award was presented to the acting director of learning assistance Karl Edwards and the Honorary Adjunct-Part-Time Faculty Award went to Dawn Anderson of the English department. This year's Innovation In Teaching Awards were given to Roy Huff in the religion department and Stephen McNeil and Jack Weyland, both in the physics department. Robyn Bergstrom of the communications department was presented with the Academic Advisor of the Year Award which is chosen by the student body.

Retiring faculty members include Moana Boyle, Don Dansie, Alden Partridge, Golden Snell, Tony Turley, Peter Valora and Ed Williams.

PREMIERE OF ORATORIO WARMLY RECIEVED

The premiere of Daniel E. Gawthrop's The Passion and The Promise of Our Lord Jesus Christ received standing ovations and grand applause from audiences on campus; in Washington, D.C.; Fairfax, Virg.; and Salt Lake City, Utah. The audience at the Tabernacle on Temple Square included members of President's Club whose philanthropic gifts make such programs possible.

The Salt Lake Tribune evaluated, "This oratorio, indeed, was the apotheosis of richly clad melodies and commodious harmonies. Gawthrop's 'Passion' is a collage of succinct movements possessing moments of exhilarating inspiration and originality. . . . The large choir and orchestra, comprised of 350 Ricks College students, had mastered the piece."

The experience is uniquely rewarding for Ricks music students. One student, Chris Goodwin, commented, "Every time I have the opportunity to play and listen to the powerful music that Brother Gawthrop has written, I truly feel of the Spirit, and my testimony of the Savior burns inside of me. And I love to share that with others through our performances."

The Passion and The Promise features three soloists: tenor Michael Belnap, soprano Kristine Ciesinski and baritone Norman Bailey. The Salt Lake Tribune review stated, "Belnap had the lion's share of soloistic responsibilities. His operatic voice had no trouble filling the cavernous venue. Ciesinski displayed a dazzling instrument and when Bailey sang, it was excitement on every note."

Under the direction of Dr. Kevin Call, the oratorio will be presented in a recording session in the Barrus Concert Hall on Thursday, April 12, at 7:30 p.m. It will be presented as a six-stake fireside Easter Sunday, April 15 at 7 p.m. in the Hart. The final graduation performance will be Friday, April 27, at 8 p.m. in the Hart. Complimentary tickets are available from the Ticket Office, however no tickets are required for the fireside.

GROUND-BREAKING SERVICES PLANNED FOR MULTI-USE BUILDING

Ground-breaking services for a 54,000 square-foot, multi-use building on the Ricks College campus will be held Wednesday, April 18, at 2 p.m. in the Taylor Chapel. Elder F. Melvin Hammond of the First Quorum of the Seventy of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints will preside at the event. Students and employees of Ricks and the general public are invited to attend.

Following the services participants will meet at the building site south of the parking lot adjacent to the Kimball Student and Administrative Services Building for the formal ground-breaking ceremony.

SIFE TEAM NAMED REGIONAL CHAMPIONS

The Ricks College Students in Free Enterprise (SIFE) team was named the regional champion during a competition in Los Angeles April 2-4. The Ricks team was also named one of 15 national finalists in the VISA Responsible Use of Credit competition. Regional champions will present their projects at the SIFE National Exposition in Kansas City, Mo., May 13-15. The national champion in the four-year division will travel to London in July to compete at the inaugural SIFE World Cup. The Ricks team consists of 75 members and completed 34 projects this year.

"This was a very successful year for SIFE education outreach programs," Nygren said. "We helped raise over $1,400 for milk to help the children of Ecuador. We provided free consulting services to several local businesses, and completed numerous educational programs through Madison County. There is no question in my mind that this has been one of the best educational experiences and career opportunities for these students while at Ricks College."
