ELDER MAXWELL ADDRESS GRADUATES

Elder Neal A. Maxwell likened Ricks College graduates to the college's symbol of the Vikings who "journeyed, explored and conquered." During the April 22 commencement, he said, "Your task is to conquer yourselves, not ships, lands and castles. This battle is the one in which you especially are to 'come off conqueror.'  It is fought every day. In fact, it is a continuing process which commenced a long, long time ago." Elder Maxwell, a member of the Quorum of the Twelve, was the keynote speaker at the 111th commencement ceremonies during which 3,012 graduates received degrees. Graduates ranged in age from 17 to 57 with 1,856 women, 1,156 men and 1,085 returned missionaries. Elder Maxwell told the graduates that their first commencement was "way back of the beyond when you commenced as spirit sons and daughters of God. In fact, you have been you for a long time. Once, long, long ago, you commenced your first estate and did well there, now you have commenced your second estate."

He explained that when life's battle is taken seriously, Awe further develop the Christ-like attributes and apply them with a sense of proportion. For instance, a brief kindness to a stranger in a transit lounge is commendable, but how about regular kindnesses to a sometimes irritating little sister or an imperfect roommate? We really can do 'the things of most worth' and still not 'omit' other good but lesser things." He added, "An important segment of your second estate has been your time at Ricks College. This segment of time and experience has enhanced your storehouse of truths, facts and skills. Now you will continue to commence in the journeys of your individual souls." He added an analogy, "The mortal experience . . . is not like a college course which we can passively audit. Instead, we are taking life's course for credit and there are no summers off - not even semester breaks."

EDUCATORS VISIT RICKS COLLEGE

Members of the Idaho State Board of Education, college and university presidents, and other state education officials got a close-up view of Ricks College during a visit to campus last Wednesday. They were given a tour by President David Bednar and the President's Council and also hosted to a dinner. The visit to the Ricks campus was in conjunction with State Board meetings held in Idaho Falls. Several of the group had never been to Ricks before, said Academic Vice President Don Bird. Included in the group were members of the state board, the state superintendent of schools, student body presidents and officers, and the presidents of Boise State University, Idaho State University, College of Southern Idaho, North Idaho College, Eastern Idaho Technical College, and Lewis Clark State College. "I think it's good that they came up to see who we are and what we're all about," he said. "We are a major player in undergraduate education in the state with the largest freshman class of any institution in the state."

State Board of Education President Harold Davis of Idaho Falls stated that private institutions such as Ricks, Albertson College and Northwest Nazarene are important educational resources in the state. Davis said the public and private schools need to work more closely together. "Once we know each other, we can work together," he said of the visit. "There's more accomplished here than one can imagine."

"EXPRESSIONS OF FAITH" TOUR WILL CONCLUDE WITH RECORDING SESSIONS

The general public is invited to attend two recording sessions of sacred music that will be held Friday, May 5, at Ricks College. The "Expressions of Faith" concert will feature the Ricks College Collegiate Singers and Chamber Orchestra as they conclude a two-week tour throughout the Northwest. The concerts will be held at 6 and 8:30 p.m. in the Barrus Concert Hall of the Snow Center for the Performing Arts. Tickets are $2 for the general public and $1 for Ricks students. They may be purchased at the Ricks College Ticket Office by calling (208) 356-2230 or over the Internet at http://www.byui.edu/tickets . Several of the works will be recorded in the concert hall for the LDS Archival Recordings Project, whose mission is to produce archival recordings of compositions and musicians representative of the Latter-day Saint heritage of music.

SHOWTIME COMPANY SETS FINAL PERFORMANCE

Showtime Company from Ricks College will hold its final performance in Salt Lake City on Monday, May 22, at Salt Lake City's West High School Auditorium, 241 N. 300 W., at 7 p.m. Proceeds will go to the LifeLine Adolescent Treatment Center in North Salt Lake. Admission is $5 per person or $25 per immediate family. Tickets may be purchased and/or reserved by calling LifeLine at (801) 936-4000. This show concludes 21 years of Showtime Company and will be the last opportunity to see a production under the direction of Russell Bice and Wilson Brown. Following its month-long tour in April and May, the musical theater group will give way in September to a new contemporary ensemble that will be launched by the Division of Performing and Fine Arts. The performance will feature "Lesson of Love," a tour show now on the road for four weeks following graduation in April. The group will perform in 14 states from Wisconsin to New York during April and May. "Lesson of Love" is a musical excursion that examines facets of love --  from romantic and family love to love of country, God, and self.
