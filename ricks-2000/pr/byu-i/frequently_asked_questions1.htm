What bachelor's degree programs will be offered at BYU-Idaho?
Beginning in 2001 we anticipate offering upper-division classes leading to integrated bachelor's degrees in accounting, English, history and recreation education, along with specialized degrees in business management, elementary education, interior design and nursing. We also will expect to begin offering upper-division, secondary education programs in the areas of biology, earth/physical science, English, family and consumer education, health, history, mathematics and social studies.

While the programs and time line are subject to change, in 2002 we expect to offer upper-division classes leading to an integrated bachelor's degree in piano pedagogy. Secondary education programs in the areas of chemistry and music would also be added.

In 2003, integrated bachelor's degrees in agronomy, animal science, art, biology, communications, information systems, landscape horticulture and mathematics, along with specialized degrees in computer science, computer engineering, construction management and mechanical engineering would be added. Also added would be a secondary education program in art, physics and Spanish.

In 2004 and 2005 upper-division classes leading to integrated baccalaureate degrees will be offered in chemistry, economics, family science, geology, health science, integrated studies, physics, psychology, sociology and technology management, along with specialized degrees in early childhood education and social work.

What is the difference between integrated and specialized bachelor's degrees?
Integrated bachelor's degrees will require up to 45 hours in the principle area of study while specialized bachelor's degrees will require up to approximately 70 hours. Integrated degrees will require students to study in multiple disciplines and will a provide broad-based preparation for employment and life.

Will BYU-Idaho still offer associate degrees?
Yes. In addition to bachelor's degrees, we will offer specialized associate degrees that prepare students to be employable after completing two years of schooling. The specialized associate degrees will include agricultural management, architectural drafting technology, automotive technology, beef production, CAD technology, computer systems technology, criminal justice, culinary arts, electronics engineering technology, general studies, floral design, landscape horticulture, nursing, paramedicine, photography, piano pedagogy, pre-school education and welding.

Has BYU-Idaho been accredited and what does this mean to students?
Yes. Ricks, which has been fully accredited for its associate degree programs for many years, received notice March 21 that it is now a candidate for full-scale accreditation of its proposed 46 bachelor’s degree programs. Candidacy status was granted by the Commission on Colleges and Universities of the Northwest Association of Schools and Colleges. This organization was established by schools in the Northwest to ensure that institutions within the region meet certain standards of performance, integrity and quality.

Students should not be concerned that BYU-Idaho will not have full accreditation for its first few years of operation. Academic Vice President Don Bird explains that the recent approval of BYU-Idaho as a candidate for accreditation is good enough for other colleges, universities and employers to accept when considering BYU-Idaho transfer students and graduates. “Universities and colleges accept each other’s credits as long as you are a candidate for accreditation,” Bird explains. “However, if you’re not accredited, that’s a different story. For example, Ricks does not accept transfer credit from non-accredited schools.”

The accreditation commission also plans to undertake a full-scale evaluation of BYU-Idaho in the spring of 2004 when the first BYU-Idaho bachelor’s degrees are granted.

Ricks currently has articulation agreements for its general education classes with all state schools in both Idaho and Utah. The agreements allow Ricks general education credits to be transferred to other schools. As BYU-Idaho brings new courses on line, the school will work out articulation agreements with other schools to accept the course credits upon transferring.

What is the expected enrollment of BYU-Idaho?
We expect to experience a modest increase in the size of the student body during the next few years. We will maintain our present level of approximately 8,600 freshman and sophomore students.

The projected number of students on campus at any given time is roughly estimated at 9,200 for 2001-02; 9,800 for 2002-03; 9,900 for 2003-04; 10,600 for 2004-05; and 11,600 for 2005-06.

What will tuition be at BYU-Idaho?
Because of the added costs of offering upper-division classes along with annual inflationary costs, tuition will be raised to $1,240 per semester beginning in the Fall Semester of 2001. This reflects a $150 tuition raise from our current level of $1,090. Tuition pays about 30 percent of the actual operating costs of the school while The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints funds the remainder.

How will BYU-Idaho handle more students?
Modest additions to our academic and activity space will be necessary. However, specific details have not been announced. We are working on plans for a new ecclesiastical building that will be used for both religious and academic activities. A new building that will replace the Spori Building will help to accommodate additional students and faculty.

To accommodate the greatest number of students, we will utilize our facilities year round and offer a complete array of courses during the summer as well as the traditional fall and winter semesters.

Students will be admitted on one of four academic tracks. Besides the traditional fall-winter track, we will offer a winter-summer track, a summer-fall track and a year-round Fast Track program. These four tracks will allow us to accommodate more students throughout the span of a year.

This will result in more students being served during a full calendar year. We estimate that 9,600 students will be served annually during the 2001-02 year, 10,300 during the 2002-03 year, 10,700 during the 2003-04 year, 12,500 during the 2004-05 year and 14,500 during the 2005-06 year.

What is the reason for the change from a two-year to a four-year institution?
The change, which was made by the Church Board of Education in June, will allow more students to receive a four-year education at a Church-sponsored school. Church President Gordon B. Hinckley stated in his announcement: "This change of status is consistent with the ongoing tradition of evaluation and progress that has brought Ricks College from infant beginnings to its present position as the largest privately owned two-year institution of higher education in America." 

Will the focus of BYU-Idaho be different than the focus of Ricks College?
We will continue to be teaching oriented and student focused. We will emphasize undergraduate education and will award associate and bachelor's degrees. Graduate degree programs will not be offered. The transition will be accomplished in such a way as to preserve the school’s autonomy and identity. Faculty rank will not be part of the academic structure of the new four-year institution.

Will students still be able to transfer to BYU-Provo?
Yes. During the transition period, transfers to BYU-Provo will continue as in the past. Elder Eyring said, "One of the things that will remain is some of the distinctive features of Ricks College which have included some people coming there for a brief time to transfer to other places. That surely will continue. So there will be . . . just an addition of then the possibility of young people staying for the four years if they choose. But I would assume that much of what has gone on in the past would continue with a nice addition."

Will BYU-Idaho be governed by BYU-Provo?
Obviously we will work very closely with BYU-Provo. In making the announcement, President Hinckley stated: "For the immediate future, the president of BYU-Idaho will report directly to the commissioner of the Church Educational System."

When will the name change take effect?
For the time being, we will continue as Ricks College. We will begin using the name Brigham Young University-Idaho when the Fall Semester begins in early September. 

What was the reason for the name change?
In making the announcement, President Gordon B. Hinckley stated: "The new four-year school will be known as Brigham Young University-Idaho, with the name change designed to give the school immediate national and international recognition."

Will Thomas E. Ricks be honored in any way?
President Hinckley stated: "The memory of Thomas E. Ricks will continue to be appropriately honored and perpetuated."

What admissions changes will there be?
Our plan at the present time is to continue the admission policies we have in place.

When will intercollegiate athletics be discontinued?
Intercollegiate athletics will be phased out at the end of the 2001-2002 academic year. Current commitments to players and leagues will be honored. 

Why is intercollegiate athletics being phased out?
President Hinckley stated, "It takes too much money. It takes a great amount of time and energy. We think that in this instance we would like to change the emphasis. In the first place, Ricks is a long ways from everybody. You have to travel great distances to play. That’s a factor in it. It’s a very costly program. We would hope that with an intramural program of athletics and sports, that the need could be met and that the great emphasis would be on academics."

Will the character of the school and student body change?
We will maintain the present character of our student body. Our overall philosophy and approach of admitting students will not change. Admission will continue to be based on a combination of factors, including academic achievement, leadership qualities and ecclesiastical involvement.

We will also maintain the same character of our faculty. Unlike most other universities, we will not have academic rank for our faculty members. While new faculty will need to be hired, we intend to retain our present faculty and offer retraining as needed. While the figures will undoubtedly change, approximately 25 new faculty members may be needed for the 2001-02 school year, 20 for the 2002-03 year, 5 for the 2003-04 year, 25 for the 2004-05 year, and 25 for the 2005-06 year.

We will emphasize "the scholarship of learning and teaching" and stress the need for both our students and faculty to become more effective learners and teachers.

We will continue to foster a nurturing, spiritual environment, which will continue to be referred to as "the spirit of Ricks."
