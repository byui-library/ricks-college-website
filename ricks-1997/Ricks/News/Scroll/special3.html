On March 4, 1842, Sara M. Granger Kimball decided to organize a group of women to help get supplies to the builders of the Nauvoo temple. Little did she know that it would lead to "the largest and by all measure the greatest women's organization on earth," as President Boyd K. Packer called the Relief Society organization at the 1998 April General Conference.

Eliza R. Snow submitted the group's ideas to President Joseph Smith, who told them he would help organize them into the Church. Twenty women and three men were present on March 17, 1842, as the first meeting of the Female Relief Society of Nauvoo.

Joseph Smith specified that the group's goals were to help the sick and needy, to help strengthen family morals and virtues in the community. In that first year, approximately 1,189 women became members.

In 1844, due to some problems, the organization ceased to function until 1866 when President Brigham Young officially reorganized the Relief Society. Since then, the Relief Society has encouraged women to get involved and serve others.

The Relief Society membership has reached more than 4.2 million worldwide. President Packer said that "strong Relief Societies carry a powerful immunizing and healing influence for the mothers and the daughters, for the single parents, for the single sisters, for the aging, for the infirm."

Virginia U. Jensen, the first counciler in the General Relief Society Presidency has said that the Relief Society has "programs designed to help (us as) women to find meaning and purpose in life for ourselves and our families."

Most Ricks women do not have the obligation of raising a family, but Relief Society offers teachings and values applicable to women of their age.

"For college age, it prepares us for real adult life, teaches us responsibility," Layla Hasbuhlina, a freshman from Rostov, Russia, said.

Others like the chance to get to know other women with the same beliefs.

"Relief Society allows you to be yourself. You don't have to be fake. You're all there to lift each other up," Kristin Turner, a sophomore from Chandler, Ariz., said.
