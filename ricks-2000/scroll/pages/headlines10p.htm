PICKUP BASKETBALL THRIVES IN THE HEART OF RICKS STUDENTS

by Amanda Hansen
scroll staff


Sometimes when a guy plays basketball he is affiliated. He wears team colors, looks out for teammates and listens to a coach. Other times he stands on the sidelines like a hungry dog at its bowl,alone, pawing the ground and waiting for satisfaction.

Such is the way of pickup basketball. No refs, no scoreboard, no cheerleaders. Only nonaffiliated players in Hart T-shirts waiting to get a game.

"I play for the competition. It's part of my life," Jason Wood from Lees Summit, Mo., said

"If I'm not playing basketball, I'm thinking about it," Mike Hintze from Ogden, Utah, said.

The rules are simple. The first 10 guys on the floor play. If you're extra you wait. Like clockwork, as soon as 11 points are scored, five guys walk off,five guys walk on and play resumes.

While the majority of pickup players are male, females are not unwelcome.

"When girls come to ball its good. If they come to bring it, I don't have a problem," Hintze said.

"The girls never shoot. It's not that [the guys] don't want them to shoot, it's just that they get intimidated and they won't," Kevin Shaffer from Red Bluff, Calif., said.

"Ninety percent of the girls are just there to pick up on the guys anyway," Earnest Attumada said.

With no referees or mothers in the stands, when it comes to pickup games, every man has to fend for himself. Those who don't go home hungry.

"It gets competitive, and if they're going to call cheap fouls, then I'm going to do the same," Shaffer said.

"I'm a short, white guy with no talent so I've got to bring an attitude or I've got nothing," Matt Dunstun from West Linn, Ore., said.

"Mostly there's just some pushing and shoving. I've never really seen anyone duke it out yet," Todd Christensen from Orem, Utah, said.

"I tripped someone on purpose once. I thought he was being a jerk," Bob Hendrick, from Orem, Utah, said.

With the only formal equipment on the court being the ball, five mesh jerseys and the occasional knee brace, fouls are left to the discretion of players.

"If the guy's nice, then he calls the foul when he makes it. If not it's up to the offense to call it," Hintze said.

"Pride prevails on the court, and it gets annoying. It's just a game. If you step out, say you stepped out. If you travel, give up the ball. If you foul, call the foul," Burk said.

Surprisely the main concern of most of the players interviewed was the occasional vulgarity.

"It makes you feel uncomfortable when you hear it. This is Ricks, and we came here to get away from stuff like that," Attumada said.

"Most of the cussing happens when tall guys miss their trash shots. There was this big guy last time I played and he could have been Shaq, but no. He missed two lay-ups in a row, swore after both of them and then when he missed his third one, he ripped the velcro pad right off the wall," Spencer Burk from Perris, Calif., said.

Just because there are no rules doesn't mean anything goes. Pickup basketball has its court ettiquete.

"Don't shoot too much. Everyone thinks that they're good; and they always take the shot. If you take the shot, then they get mad at you," Shaffer said.

"It frustrates me when people don't play very hard. They stand around on defense and mess around on offense," Christensen said.

"I can't stand the NBA actor type. This is pickup ball, not some organized shoot around. There's no room for whiners," Hintze said.
