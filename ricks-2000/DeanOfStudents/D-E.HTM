Dance Office
The ASBYU-Idaho Dance Office is located in Manwaring Center 373, and coordinates and regulates all dances on the BYU-Idaho Campus including ASBYUI, church, club, and housing unit dances. Its purpose is to assist in providing wholesome music and dancing on campus. The following policies and procedures apply:

1. Calendaring and Scheduling
Each sponsoring organization must calendar and schedule its proposed dance with the Scheduling Office, Manwaring Center 250 (496-2242). After scheduling the building and room, the next step is to schedule the Music and D.J. Call the ASBYUI Dance Programs Director at ext. 2280.

2. D.J. and Music
ASBYUI approved music and D.J.’s are required for all on campus dances. The Dance Office will provide your dance with approved music and a disc jockey, for a minimal fee ($35.00 MC ballrooms, $45.00 for other buildings on campus) to be paid in advance. Scheduling of a D.J. must be completed at least one week in advance of the dance or activity.

3. Lighting
Strobe and flashing lights are not appropriate for campus dances.

4. Volume
Music volume should be low enough to allow two persons standing side by side to carry on a normal conversation. (Church Bulletin #24 - June 1982).

5. Security
ASBYUI sponsored dances provide paid security to enforce the dress and grooming standards at dances. Responsibility to maintain university dress and grooming standards at dances other than those sponsored by ASBYUI rests with the sponsors (bishops, advisors, dorm head residents etc.).

6. Sponsoring and Publicity
Stakes, wards, clubs, organizations and housing units may provide dances for their own membership and in some cases, invite guests. Under no circumstances should "invited guests" include an open invitation to the general student population. Publicity and invitations to attend a dance should be directed at a target audience, such as a particular stake, ward, or club. Announcements and publicity, like "everyone is invited" are prohibited unless the dance is sponsored by ASBYUI. Do not put advertisements for an organization’s dance in the Scroll, hang posters, or pass out flyers on campus.

Development and Alumni Relations
The BYU-Idaho Development and Alumni Relations Office, located in Kimball 220, is a division of the LDS Foundation which provides services for alumni, friends, businesses, parents, and students associated with BYU-Idaho. It encourages and facilitates private contributions to enhance the quality of a BYU-Idaho education and to bless the lives of students. This private support becomes the resources necessary to help BYU-Idaho provide the best possible teachers, facilities, and academic programs and makes available educational opportunities to deserving students through scholarships, student loan funds, and other programs. The goal is two-fold: to augment with private contributions the support the Church provides through tithing dollars and to serve those who want to give something of value back to BYU-Idaho as an expression of appreciation for the blessings they have received in their lives.

Disabled Student Services
Following admission guidelines, BYU-Idaho is committed to the full inclusion of all individuals who can benefit from educational opportunities. The Office of Services for Students with a Disability (SSD), located in Mckay Library 123, provides academic accommodations for all qualified students who have documented learning, emotional, and/or physical disabilities (as defined by Section 504 and the ADA) and have need for accommodation.

The ultimate goal is to promote individual independence within the academic atmosphere of the university. Students are assisted in this process by SSD and by the support services and programs available to all BYU-Idaho students.

When students with a disability request a specific accommodation they must submit (written) documentation of the disability and need for accommodation. The following policies would ordinarily be followed: SSD may provide services so that students with disabilities can maximize their potential at BYU-Idaho with a minimum of impact from the effects of the disability. "Reasonable Accommodations" may include (but not be limited to) such services as: accessibility, extended time on tests, volunteer note takers, preferential seating, alternate testing, and adaptation for special needs. SSD may also provide a wide range of services as outlined in the BYU-Idaho Catalog.

Students will have on file with SSD, independent professional documentation which supports the need for the accommodation. Documentation must be filed prior to accommodation provision. SSD will evaluate the documentation and approve or disapprove the requested accommodation, or take additional action to secure more information. Disruptive Behavior
BYU-Idaho is committed to providing a wholesome academic, cultural, social and spiritual environment for students. Individuals who disrupt this environment by behavioral action, intimidation, threats or violence whether in BYU-Idaho approved housing or directed towards the institution, employees, students, guests, or other persons using BYU-Idaho facilities -- in any class, activity, or special event, may be required to leave the institution or its properties when their behavior is such that it results in concern for the individual’s welfare, the welfare of others or if the behavior hinders others from being able to perform or be productive in respect to their educational goals. Such acts or behavior are incompatible with general health and safety practices, the mission of the university, the Honor Code and the principles of the restored gospel of Jesus Christ.

In cases involving an individual with a disability, including mental disabilities, this policy will operate to make a determination based upon an individual’s behavior rather than upon the individual’s status of having a disability.

When it is determined that an individual is involved in disruptive behavior (including, but not limited to: verbal abuse, theft or damage to property, interference with normal business transactions, assault, battery, or any other form of physical abuse, and any conduct that is detrimental to the health or safety of any individual) that person will receive an appropriate sanction, including, but not limited to, suspension from enrollment or employment (in accordance with university policy) and/or being banned from access to university property.

Driving Permit
Students whose association with the university requires them to operate a university-owned vehicle must obtain a driving permit from the Campus Police Department, located in Kimball 150. Students who need to operate vans and pickups are required to take a van driving course that is two hours of class time and is held in Kimball 245 on Tuesdays at 7:30 p.m. The van check drive is held on Saturdays at 8:00 a.m. in the Kimball parking lot. Both the class and check drive are required before a permit is issued.

Student who need to drive sedans only are required to take a one hour class which is held on Tuesday at 7:30 p.m. in Kimball 245. Students should be sure to inform the instructor that they are there for the sedan class.

Applications for driving permits are available at the Campus Police Office. Call ext. 2448 for more information on driving permits, or use of university vehicles.

Drug Abuse Prevention
BYU-Idaho supports student participation in programs for the prevention of the inappropriate use of drugs, controlled substances, and tobacco/alcohol abuse. The university has a Drug Prevention Office where students may get help for drug related problems. The HOPE program provides students with assistance in identifying and coping with a drug problem through education, counseling, evaluation and referral to resources outside the campus if necessary. For assistance call the Drug Prevention Office at ext. 2296.

Drugs and Alcohol Use/Possession Disclosure
The use or possession of illegal substances or alcohol is prohibited at all times and in all places. Individuals found in violation of this prohibition will be disciplined in accordance with current policy. Disclosure of a violation may be made to a students’ parent/guardian by the Dean of Students Office. This disclosure can be made with or without students’ consent if students are under 21 years of age. There may be effects on students’ financial aid if they are convicted of controlled substance use and/or abuse.

Eligibility Requirements
1. Academic Requirements
Eligibility to participate in BYU-Idaho events and activities is based upon cumulative GPA. Students will be considered making satisfactory academic progress if they maintain a cumulative GPA including transfer credit, above 2.0 and if their satisfactory progress is above 75%.

2. Departmental and Organizational Standards
Some programs, groups, clubs, or organizations may establish a higher academic standard than that which has been adopted by the university. When membership in a national organization requires a standard which must be met in order for students to participate in that organization or activity and that policy is different than the university standard, the requirement set by the national organization may be followed.

3. Academic Probation
Should students cumulative GPA fall below 2.0 or if their satisfactory progress is below 75% they will be placed on academic probation. The Dean of Students will notify them and program advisors or sponsors, in writing, of their ineligibility to participate in the program(s) in which they are involved. Students will be removed from probation when their cumulative GPA once again equals or exceeds 2.0 and their satisfactory progress is above 75%.

2. Emergency Telephones
Emergency telephones are placed at various locations around the main campus area. These telephones are mounted on the walls of campus buildings or as free standing pedestals. They are identified by a blue light directly above the phone box and the words "Emergency Phone" written in red letters appear on the door to the telephone box. All campus elevators have emergency phones mounted inside. These phones may be used to summon assistance in the event of an emergency. Emergency phones are directly connected to the campus 911 system and upon activation the University Police dispatcher is immediately advised of the location of the telephone that is activated. To activate the telephone, simply turn the handle in the direction indicated on the phone box cover, open the door and push the button. When the phone is answered you will have two way communication with the dispatcher who will send the appropriate emergency service personnel to your location. It is a criminal offense to use any emergency telephone to make a false alarm or to report false information. Violators will be subject to criminal prosecution and/or university disciplinary action.
