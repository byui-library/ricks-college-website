SAFETY AT RICKS COLLEGE

On August 10, 2001, Ricks College legally and officially became a four-year university and was renamed Brigham Young University–Idaho (BYU–Idaho). Since this report is for the calendar year 2000, the institution name has not been changed for this report.

Ricks College is committed to providing a "wholesome academic, cultural, social, and spiritual environment" for its students, faculty, staff, and administrators wherein students can learn and grow both spiritually and intellectually in a diverse community committed to high moral values. Located in Rexburg, Idaho, Ricks College and Rexburg are known for a crime rate below that of other areas in the state and country. The college administration, City of Rexburg, and campus community are committed in their efforts to maintain a safe and secure community through a sharing of responsibility. Any program providing for the safety and security of persons cannot be effective unless every individual within the community accepts responsibility for his or her part. This does not imply that the college and surrounding community are free from the various social maladies encountered throughout other parts of the nation.

This information is provided to the college community to assist individuals in helping to maintain their personal safety. It is designed to apprize the community and other interested persons of current and immediate past crime statistics. In addition, it documents Ricks College’s policies designed to make the campus a safer environment. In accordance with the requirements of the Jeanne Clery Disclosure of Campus Security Policy and Campus Crime Statistics Act, Ricks College makes every effort to:

C Inform students and employees annually of Ricks College policies and programs that promote safety and security;
C Document and publish campus and community crime data;
C Provide information to students, faculty, administration, and staff members which will enable them to live and work in a crime-free environment by being proactive in crime prevention and awareness;
C Provide victims of forcible and nonforcible sex offenses with information concerning their rights as well as pertinent College policies;
C Publicize Ricks College’s drug and alcohol policies and encourage total abstinence;
C Provide data concerning institutional graduation rates; and
C Make timely warning reports to the college community of reported crimes.

PUBLICATION AND DISTRIBUTION OF INFORMATION

Ricks College makes a concerted effort to provide every student, faculty, staff, and administrator with a complete copy of the annual Clery Disclosure of Campus Security Policies and Campus Crime Statistics either as an e-mail document or as a hard paper copy. The report is prepared for distribution annually and is available for the start of the fall semester. Employees and students are provided information by e-mail notifying them of the availability of the Campus Safety Report or the location where and how they can obtain a hard paper copy. This procedure is followed at the beginning of each semester or term for all new incoming students. Employees who do not have e-mail accounts are provided written notification of the availability of the report. New employees are provided a hard paper copy of the report during their new employee orientation held within 30 days of their being hired.

Hard paper copies are available on request through the Dean of Students Office and the Personnel Office.

POLICE SERVICES

Police Authority

The Ricks College Division of the City of Rexburg Police Department provides police, fire, parking, and safety services 24 hours a day to a growing campus community consisting of approximately 9,000 full and part-time students and approximately 1,000 full, part-time, and adjunct faculty, staff and administrative employees. The Campus Police division consists of nine full-time sworn officers, five office staff employees, and 10 to 15 part-time student employees.

Training and Patrol Methods

Ricks College Campus Police officers are sworn Rexburg City Police officers, trained and certified by the Idaho State Peace Officer Standards and Training Council. Ongoing in-service training in patrol procedures, investigative techniques, crime detection and suppression, defensive tactics, evidence gathering, traffic accident investigation, crime prevention and awareness, first aid, firearms, legal updates (to keep current with new laws) and court decisions to help officers maintain their proficiency. Funds are provided for officers to attend workshops and seminars offered by state and federal law enforcement agencies.

Departmental personnel patrol the campus in both uniformed patrol on foot, in vehicles, and on bicycles as well as plain clothes foot and motor patrol. The college law enforcement communications center is staffed 24 hours a day and has direct radio contact with all campus and on-duty emergency services personnel within the community. The campus is equipped with an enhanced 911 emergency telephone system that can immediately access the communications center from any telephone on campus.

Services

Campus Police officers are responsible for providing a full range of public safety services to the Ricks College campus community. This includes the recording of all reported crimes, follow-up investigations, traffic accident investigation, medical and/or fire emergencies, enforcement of criminal, alcohol, drug, and traffic laws and any other matters requiring police presence or assistance.

Investigations

Investigators who are responsible for bringing cases to a successful conclusion follow up on all incident and crime reports. These investigators are seasoned officers and are trained in handling crime scenes and investigations. Officers from outside agencies may be used for resources and assistance in handling matters as needed.

Reporting Crimes

Campus police handle all campus crime reports, investigations, traffic accidents, enforcement of laws regulating underage drinking, the use of controlled substances, weapons, and other incidents—such as medical and fire emergencies on the Campus. The college administration is committed to the reporting and investigation of all crimes committed on campus. Ricks College Administration encourages students, faculty, staff, and administrators to promptly report all crimes to the Campus Police Division of the Rexburg City Police Department or the law enforcement agency having jurisdiction over the area where the crime occurred. Students living off campus should report all criminal activity to the Rexburg City Police Department. Campus ecclesiastical leaders and counselors in the Ricks College Counseling Center are encouraged to work with individuals who come to them for parishioner or professional counseling. Counselors and ecclesiastical leaders are encouraged to inform the persons they are counseling of the procedure for voluntarily and confidentially reporting crimes. The administration encourages all employees to report any and all crimes as soon as practical to permit an appropriate police response to the reported incident.

Local law enforcement agencies provide the Campus Police Division statistics on crime occurring on properties which are owned, operated, or used by the college or on properties which are adjacent to college-owned properties. Crime Information: Campus Police issues crime information in a timely manner to the Scroll, R News & Notes, and campus radio stations KRIC and KWBH when reports of serious crimes committed on campus are considered by the college to pose a foreseeable threat to the safety of students and employees. In addition, news articles may solicit campus community assistance in identifying suspects of crimes as well as crime prevention and personal protection.

Operation Identification: Employees and students are encouraged to engrave identifying numbers on personal property and to maintain an accurate inventory of their belongings. Engravers can be borrowed at no cost from the Campus Police and the Housing Office.

Security Survey Program: Periodic security surveys of campus facilities are conducted by Campus Police personnel. These surveys identify areas on campus where lighting can be improved and the security of physical facilities can be strengthened.

Housing Personnel Orientation: Prior to the beginning of the fall semester, owners, managers, housing supervisors, head residents, and resident assistants are taught how to assist students who become victims of crimes and how to avoid criminal victimization.

Sexual Offenses and Self-Protection Seminars: Campus Police personnel are trained in the presentation of seminars intended to help students learn what they can do to protect themselves from sexual offenses. The seminars are offered on a continuing basis through clubs and ward organizations.

Personal Safety Week: Each semester the Campus Police and Dean of Students Office sponsor a week of activities and events focusing on personal safety.

Community Policing Program: Ricks College Campus Police personnel subscribe to the concept of community policing and look for opportunities to present crime prevention and self-protection information to individuals and informal groups as an integral part of their daily patrol responsibilities. The officers are attempting to get out of the patrol cars and mix with the students and staff to develop better relationships with the campus community.

Drug Abuse Instruction: Ricks College Campus Police provide instruction regarding the identification of various dangerous substances and the dangers of drug abuse. The instruction which is provided to students registered for health classes is intended to help students learn to recognize the signs of drug abuse and to know what resources and support groups are available to help individuals experiencing drug or alcohol-related problems. The instruction is also available to other organizations upon request.

Crime Prevention Booths: Once each semester informational booths are set up in the Ricks College Manwaring Student Center, and crime awareness and prevention literature is distributed and videos are displayed to interested persons.

Building Security: Most academic buildings are unlocked until 10:30 p.m. for evening classes, research projects, and special events. When the campus is officially closed, however, buildings are locked and are not open for use without possession of a late pass, which permits students receiving approval to remain after 10:30 p.m. but no later than 1 a.m. Custodians are required to immediately report any suspicious situations to the police.

Rape Awareness: Campus Police officers have rape prevention programs that are available to any group upon request.

Escort Service: The Ricks College Campus Police provide a walking escort service for college students and employees during the night and early morning hours. Women can contact the Campus Police to request an escort, and department personnel will walk the requesting party to or from buildings on campus and housing units in the immediate campus area.

Sexual Offense Programs: Ricks College is committed to providing educational programs across campus aimed at the prevention of forcible and nonforcible sexual offenses and, more specifically, to promote greater awareness of rape, acquaintance rape, and sexual assault. Programs presented by the college are designed to educate faculty, administration, staff, and students as to methods and strategies to avoid becoming a victim of a sex offense, to inform them of their rights and responsibilities if they become victims, and to educate potential perpetrators of sexual offenses of the possible consequences of such actions. These educational programs are regularly presented on campus and are available for wards, clubs, classes, and other interested campus community groups upon request. To learn more about sex-related offenses, ALL members of the campus community are encouraged to participate in one of the college- sponsored programs at least once during their stay at the college. Program schedules may be obtained by calling the Dean of Students Office or the Campus Police.

Numerous pamphlets on various topics of crime prevention and personal protection are available at no cost from the Ricks College Campus Police Department, the Deans Office, the Housing Office and the Counseling Center. Topics include: Campus Police Services, General Crime Prevention, Personal Protection, Rape Awareness, Bicycle Safety, and Operation Identification. Information on safety and security is provided to students and employees regularly through seminars, videotapes, bulletins, posters, brochures, radio broadcasts, and student newspapers.

CRIME DEFINITIONS

The data shown above reflect offenses and arrests reported to the Ricks College Campus Police and are compiled in accordance with the definitions used in the Uniform Crime Reporting System of the Department of Justice, the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) as modified by the Hate Crime Statistics Act. Data on disciplinary referrals is taken from statistical reports provided by the Dean of Students Office based on required reporting categories. In both instances, the data may not reflect the actual rates of crime on campus but, rather, may reflect the rate that the campus community reports crime and the efficiency of the Campus Police in following up on the reported crimes. The Uniform Crime Reporting System of the Department of Justice, the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) uses the following definitions.

Murder: The willful (non-negligent) killing of one human being by another.

Manslaughter: The killing of another person through negligence.

Forcible Sex Offense Definitions: Any sexual act directed against another person, forcibly and/or against that person’s will; or not forcibly or against the person’s will where the victim is incapable of giving consent. Forcible sex offenses include:

Forcible Rape: The carnal knowledge (sexual intercourse) of a person, forcibly and/or against that persons will; or not forcibly or against the person’s will where the victim is incapable of giving consent because of his/her temporary or permanent mental or physical incapacity or because of his/her youth.

Forcible Sodomy: Oral or anal sexual intercourse with another person, forcibly and/or against that person’s will; or not forcibly against the person’s will where the victim is incapable of giving consent because of his/her youth or because of his/her temporary or permanent mental or physical incapacity.

Sexual Assault With An Object: The use of an object or instrument to unlawfully penetrate, however slightly, the genital or anal opening of the body of another person, forcibly and/or against the person’s will; or not forcibly or against the person’s will where the victim is incapable of giving consent because of his/her youth or because of his/her temporary or permanent mental or physical capacity.

Forcible Fondling The touching of the private body parts of another person for the purpose of sexual gratification, forcibly and/or against that person's will; or, not forcibly or against the person's will

where the victim is incapable of giving consent because of his/her youth or because of his/her temporary or permanent mental incapacity

Nonforcible Sex Offense Definitions

A nonforcible sex offense is "unlawful, nonforcible sexual intercourse." Examples of nonforcible sexual offenses include incest and statutory rape. Ricks College has chosen to make other noncontact, sex-related offenses including exhibitionism, voyeurism, and the making of obscene harassing phone calls subject to college sanctions and discipline.

Incest: Nonforcible sexual intercourse between persons who are related to each other within the degrees wherein marriage is prohibited by law.

Statutory Rape: Nonforcible sexual intercourse with a person who is under the statutory age of consent.

Robbery: Taking or attempting to take anything of value from the care, custody, or control of a person or persons by force or threat of force or violence and/or by putting the victim in fear.

Aggravated Assault: An unlawful attack by one person upon another for the purpose of inflicting severe or aggravated bodily injury. This type of assault usually is accompanied by the use of a weapon or by means likely to produce death or great bodily harm. (It is not necessary that the injury result from an aggravated assault when a gun, knife, or other weapon is used which could and probably would result in serious personal injury if the crime were successfully completed.)

Burglary: The unlawful entry of a structure to commit a felony or a theft. For reporting purposes this definition includes: unlawful entry with intent to commit a larceny or felony; breaking and entering with intent to commit a larceny; housebreaking; safecracking; and all attempts to commit any of the aforementioned. Idaho law defines theft from a vehicle as burglary.

Arson: The unlawful and intentional damage, or attempt to damage, any real or personal property by fire or incendiary device.

Motor Vehicle Theft: The theft or attempted theft of a motor vehicle. (Classify as motor vehicle theft all cases where automobiles are taken by persons not having lawful access even though the vehicles are later abandoned—including joyriding.)

Larceny Theft: The unlawful taking, carrying, leading, or riding away of property from the possession or constructive possession of another. Examples are thefts of bicycles or automobile accessories, shoplifting, pocket picking, or the stealing of any property or article that is not taken by force and violence or by fraud. Attempted larcenies are included. Embezzlement, "con" games, forgery, worthless checks, etc., are excluded.

Liquor Law Violations: The violation of laws or ordinances prohibiting: the manufacturing, sale, transporting, furnishing, or possession of intoxicating liquor; maintaining unlawful drinking places; bootlegging; operating a still; furnishing liquor to a minor or intemperate person; using a vehicle for illegal transportation of liquor; drinking on a train or public conveyance; and all attempts to commit any of the aforementioned. (Drunkenness and driving under the influence are not included in this definition.)

Drug-related Violations: Violations of state and local laws relating to the unlawful possession, sale, use, growing, manufacturing, and making of narcotic drugs. The relevant substances include opium or cocaine

Weapons Possession: The violation of laws or ordinances dealing with weapon offenses, regulatory in nature, such as: manufacture, sale, or possession of deadly weapons; carrying deadly weapons, concealed or openly; furnishing deadly weapons to minors; aliens possessing deadly weapons; and all attempts to commit any of the aforementioned.

The possession or use of any firearm, wrist rocket, paintball gun, or other deadly weapon on campus is prohibited by Ricks College policy except for law enforcement officers.

Hate Crimes: The crimes listed above and any other crimes become Hate Crimes when bodily injury to any person occurs and when the victim is intentionally selected because of the actual or perceived race, gender, religion, sexual orientation, ethnicity, or disability of the victim. These are crimes reported to any campus security authority or local police agency and then reported in the Campus Crime Statistics according to the table notations at the end of the crime statistics. The absence of any such notations are indicative of the lack of Hate Crimes being reported.

It is the stated policy of Ricks College in accordance with the Campus Security Act (Clery Act) to provide direction for the release and dissemination of information to the campus community on certain crimes which represent a continuing threat to students and employees and which were reported to officials with significant responsibility for student and campus activities, campus police, or local police. A list of campus security authorities is published in the Campus Security Report and designates those college officials with significant responsibility for student and campus activities under the requirements of the Clery Act. These individuals are required to report crimes reported to them immediately to the Campus Police Department.

The Campus Police Division of the Rexburg City Police Department will be responsible for providing relevant information on a daily basis when it has been determined that facts exist that there is reason to believe that a crime has occurred which may be considered a threat to students and employees. The primary concern of the administration is to ensure that members of the campus community are made aware of these crimes so they may adjust their work patterns and habits to incorporate a higher degree of awareness of personal safety within their lives. Information provided must include the nature, date, time, and general location of occurrence of each reported crime and the disposition of the complaint if known

Any required information may not be disclosed when a law prohibits such disclosure or when the disclosure would jeopardize the confidentiality of the victim. All information provided must be open to public inspection within two business days of the initial report being made. If new information about a log entry becomes available to the Campus Police, this information should be recorded in the log not later than two business days after the information becomes available to the police or a campus security authority.

If there is clear and convincing evidence that the release of any required information would jeopardize an ongoing criminal investigation or the safety of an individual, cause a suspect to flee or evade detection, or result in the destruction of evidence, such information may be withheld until that damage is no longer likely to occur from the release of such information.

Should a serious crime occur, the Captain of the Campus Police Division of the Rexburg City Police Department would make an appropriate release of information after consultation with the chief of the Rexburg City Police Department and the vice president of Student Life. These crimes are reported to the campus community through the e-mail system, R News & Notes, the Scroll, and campus radio stations KRIC and KWBH. The information will be disseminated to the campus community in an appropriate manner including but not limited to e-mail, R News & Notes, the Scroll, campus radio stations KRIC and KWBH as well as other local broadcast media, print media, and flyers. Daily crime logs are posted on the Campus Police web site at: http://www.byui.edu/police/daily.htm.

PERSONAL SAFETY AND PROTECTION

The safety and well-being of our students, faculty, staff, and community patrons are a high priority at Ricks College. However, the full support and cooperation of the entire college community are required to allow for the pursuit of knowledge in a safe and secure environment. Often, the best defense against being the victim of a sex offense or any crime is one's own personal alertness and awareness, and taking care not to place oneself at risk. The best advice for minimizing the possibility of becoming a victim is to be selective in your activities at all times by not putting yourself at risk. In order to avoid situations that may place an individual at greater risk of becoming a victim of a rape or sexual assault, it is advisable to:

1. Be aware of your rights and obligations. Don't give mixed messages. You have both a right and an obligation to say "NO."
2. Let a friend or roommate know who you will be with, where you will be, and when you expect to return. Leave an address and phone number.
3. Be sensitive to the spirit, your intuition, and your instincts. If you feel you may be at risk, take control by leaving the situation immediately. Don't be afraid to "make waves" if you feel threatened, pressured, or coerced into an activity that is against your will.
4. Attend activities with friends and in groups. "Look out" for one another.
5. Walk with confidence and walk with another person. Stick to well-lighted, populated areas.
6. Refrain from drug and alcohol use.
7. Be extremely selective as to whom you date and where you go with the date.
8. Avoid Internet dates.

Sexual Assault

The high standards of moral purity which each student and employee commit to as a condition of their enrollment or employment at Ricks College precludes individual involvement in behaviors contrary to their Honor Code commitment to "live a chaste and virtuous life," "respect others," and to "obey the law," including refraining from immoral or abusive behavior. The right of individuals to attend an institution of higher education where they can pursue their education in a safe and supportive environment and be free from all forms of abuse, assault, harassment, and coercive conduct is an inherent right. In reality, unlawful and immoral behaviors are an unfortunate part of our society and may occur at Ricks College. In this light, all types of sexual assaults or offenses are a very serious concern to Ricks College. Ricks College is committed to educating the campus community about the nature and consequences of sexual assault as well as to assist victims or perpetrators in understanding their rights under the Honor Code. This information may be found in the Student Handbook under Honor Code Procedures, Section IV, subsection E.

Sexual assaults should be viewed as a terrifying infringement of an individual's right to exercise their own agency over their body. When this agency is taken away by an assault, the victim loses the ability to make their own sexual choice. The very nature of a sexual assault is an act of control or domination that strips one of the parties of their right to moral agency. The end result is the victim is damaged both physically and emotionally. A larger by-product of the assault produces extreme and conflicting feelings in the victim such as guilt, fear, shame, anger, and depression. These emotions must be dealt with if the victim is to return to their "normal" life.

Any violation of another individual’s right to be free from forcible or non forcible sexual offenses constitutes a serious breach of Idaho criminal law in addition to being a violation of their Honor Code commitment. A violation of this policy by students or employees may result in dismissal from school or termination from employment, in addition to possible prosecution under the laws of the State of Idaho. When any person becomes aware of a forcible or nonforcible sexual offense they should immediately report it to the Dean of Students Office and should also report the offense to the campus police or to the appropriate police agency having jurisdiction over the offense.

Sexual offense definitions are found at the end of the Crime Statistics section.

Students

A victim of a sexual offense allegedly perpetrated by a student or any other person may choose, in addition to reporting the offense to the police, to report the alleged perpetrator to the Dean of Students Office, Kimball 230, 496-1120, for the assessment of appropriate disciplinary sanctions. The Dean of Students Office will adhere to the guidelines established in this policy and those published in the Ricks College Honor Code Procedure as outlined in the Student Handbook.

The Honor Code Procedure provides a system by which violators of the Honor Code are treated fairly and disciplined appropriately with regard to the nature of the offense. College behavioral discipline is intended to protect the special interests and purposes of the college and the interests of the individual student.

The accuser will be treated with consideration and understanding and will be encouraged to seek appropriate legal redress by reporting the incident to appropriate college or local authorities. The accuser will also be notified of the existence of, and be encouraged to seek, appropriate medical, legal, ecclesiastical, educational, and professional and parental counseling and assistance to deal with the situation. Specifically, the Dean of Students Office will provide the accuser with the following information and options:

1. Inform the accuser of the option to notify college and local enforcement authorities, including Campus Police and local police, of the incident and the option to be assisted by campus authorities in notifying such authorities if the accuser so chooses;
2. Inform the accuser of existing counseling, mental health or student services for victims of a sexual offense, both on campus and in the community;
3. If the accuser is a student or is living on campus, provide information concerning options for, and available assistance in, changing academic and living arrangements if requested and if these changes are reasonably available.

In order for the college to investigate and, if justified, discipline the accused, the accuser must identify the accused and provide sufficient details of the event verbally or in writing to allow the college to make an evaluation of the facts. Another person may accompany the accuser to any resulting campus disciplinary proceeding for the purpose of providing support. The accuser has a right to be informed of the outcome of the disciplinary proceeding without the consent of the accused if the proceeding is held with respect to an alleged sexual offense. The college will act responsibly to respect the privacy, welfare, and safety of the accuser.

The alleged perpetrator will be invited to the Dean of Students Office to be informed of the nature of the allegation and to participate in a fact-finding interview. The accused will be treated with consideration and will be allowed to respond verbally or in writing to the allegation.

If the accused admits the facts as presented by the accuser, a determination will be made as to an appropriate disciplinary sanction. The range of appropriate sanctions may vary according to the circumstances of each case. Possible sanctions are outlined in Section VII of the Student Handbook and range from a verbal warning to expulsion.

If the accused denies the allegation and states in whole or in part that the allegation is untrue, further investigation will be made by the staff of the Dean of Students Office. If the Dean of Students Office determines it is more probable than not that the sex offense as reported by the accuser did take place, appropriate college sanctions will be imposed. Both the accused and the accuser will be informed of the right and process of review. If reviewed, the matter will then be heard by the Standards Committee, which will make a final determination according to the Honor Code procedures as outlined in the Student Handbook. The Standards Committee may invoke any sanction listed in Section VII, ranging from a verbal warning to expulsion. A copy of this process may be obtained at the Dean of Students Office on the second floor of the Administration Building.

If it is not feasible for the college to come to a determination with respect to the allegation and the accuser pursues legal redress through the criminal or civil courts, the college determination may be deferred pending the outcome of the legal process. Depending upon the court action, the college may impose an appropriate sanction retroactive to the date of the sexual offense.

The accused will be cautioned to have no contact with the accuser during this process. The college will act responsibly to protect the accused person’s privacy and welfare in respect to the processes at the college.

FIRES, WINTER STORMS AND EARTHQUAKES

Fires

C If you discover a fire, activate the nearest fire alarm, then call 911.

Do not attempt to rescue others unless you are certain you can do so safely. When fire fighters arrive, inform them of the location of persons still in the building.

C When a fire alarm sounds, close windows, open drapes, and exit quickly to the nearest stairwell. Close doors behind you. Never use elevators.

If the door is hot or the hallway is too smoky, stay in the room. Stuff towels or blankets around door cracks. Stay low to the floor where the air is fresher and cooler. Go to the window and attract attention by waving a sheet or coat outside.

After you have left the building, stand clear of the structure and obey the instructions of fire fighters and police. Do not reenter the building until you have permission.

Winter Storms

C If you get stranded, DO NOT LEAVE YOUR CAR. Run your engine and heater ten minutes per hour. Keep your window cracked for air on the opposite side of the car from which the wind is blowing. Do not allow the tail pipe to be covered with snow. Put out a signal such as a distress flare, colored balloon, or flag so you can be seen.

C If you are unable to avoid exposure or build a fire for warmth, cut the padding out of your car, boat, or household furniture seats. Wrap the padding around your body. Cover the padding with synthetic material (i.e., nylon or polyester) to hold the padding against you. Remember, synthetics do not burn, they melt. Stay away from fire.

Earthquakes

Remain calm and try to reassure others.

If you are indoors, don't rush outside. Get under a desk or table and hold onto it. Stay away from windows and outside walls.

If you are outdoors, move to an open area away from trees, buildings, walls, and power lines.

If you are driving, pull over to the side of the road and stop. Avoid overpasses and power lines. Stay inside your car until the tremors cease.

Prepare for aftershocks.

Check yourself first, then others for injuries. Do not move seriously injured persons unless they are in immediate danger of further injury.

Do not use the phone. All available phone lines must be reserved for emergency worker communications.

Check for fires or fire hazards. If you smell or hear the sound of natural gas escaping, turn off your gas meter.
