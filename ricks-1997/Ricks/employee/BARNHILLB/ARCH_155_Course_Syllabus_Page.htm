I. COURSE DESCRIPTION

Fundamental principles of efficient house planning with the correlation of architectural drafting techniques. Emphasis will include standard construction materials and building code regulation and design standards. Drawings include footing/foundations, floor plans, elevations, schedules, sections/details, electrical plans, plot plan, and drawing index.

II. INTRODUCTION

Residential Drafting and Design is a course dealing with current architectural drafting practices and techniques required of the architectural draftsman. As well as drafting working drawings, the course has been correlated with handout problems to assist the student in learning the basic principles and concepts of architectural drafting.

A. Credit Hours 3

B. Lecture hours/week 2 +

C. Class Lab hours/week 2 +

It is required to attend the entire class time, including the class lab time. In addition to the class lab, each student will be required to spend outside of regular class the necessary drawing time to complete the given drawing assignments. Normally, the amount of time outside of class will be different for each student, however, in the past, successful students have budgeted between six to eight hours per week outside of regular class time to do their homework assignment. Late lab passes, good for the entire semester, signatures required from the division chairman and instructor, are for students wishing to use the lab from 10:00 p.m. to 1:00 a.m., or early morning hours.

III. PURPOSES AND OBJECTIVES OF THE COURSE

Specifically, the student who successfully completes Residential Drafting and Design will be able to identify, describe, or perform the following:

1. Architectural lettering and the ability to letter neatly and legibly in a current architectural style shown in class. The architectural technique of lettering is shown in class and required of each student to develop this important skill.

2. The skill in the use and care of all common draftsman's tools.

3. The current architectural drawing techniques using lines of various thickness and weight material symbols, etc.

4. The points to be considered in the selection and location of a building on the site, referred to as "House Orientation" or "site orientation."

5. Know standard sizes of building materials for use in detailing and dimension calculations.

6. Learn and develop appreciation of design standards associated with space design of residential projects.

7. The fundamental types of house framing and construction.

8. Become acquainted with span tables and determine sizing of joist and rafters.

9. Develop skills in drawing floor plans, elevations, wall sections, plot plan, and footing/foundation plans along with other common architectural drawing problems.

10. Skill in the selection and use of handbooks, reference materials and other sources of architectural drafting resource information, to include Sweet's Catalog, Architectural Graphic Standards, etc.

11. Become acquainted with city ordinances and building codes and their application requirements for a residence.

V. EVALUATION AND GRADING

What you learn in class supersedes by about ten times the simple grade you receive. A student learns by doing and this drafting class requires A LOT OF DOING to become proficient. During lectures, students are expected to listen and take notes. NO DRAFTING DURING LECTURES IS ACCEPTABLE. Points will be deducted if this is not followed. In evaluation of each students performance and mastery of assignments and course work, the following criteria will be used for student evaluation.

A. Attendance, class participation, attitude, and individual initiative: The college attendance policy is in force. Regular attendance and punctuality will influence the final grade and the level of achievement. Studies have indicated that grading and attendance have a direct relationship:

a. Missing lectures significantly lowers comprehension of course material, thus performance is decreased.

b. Class participation and involvement is essential to the goals of the course.

c. In past years students with more than a few absences had the lowest grades.

To teach the importance of attendance, a point system is used at the end of the semester. Your attendance points will be tallied and added to your accumulated points for the final grade. The point system for attendance is as follows:

1. Each student is given 100 points at the beginning of the semester.

2. Five points will be deducted for each absence.

3. Two points will be deducted for each tardy.

4. Two points will be deducted for leaving class early.

5. One point will be deducted for setting idle and wasting valuable class time.

6. Five points will be deducted for misuse of the drafting lab.

7. One point for leaving desk name card in slot after class is over (each occurrence).

8. Two points for drafting during lecture time.

B. Oral Expression: Your initiative to build your architectural vocabulary and the communication of the terminology related to architectural is of vital importance. Review the glossary of your text often. This will be observed throughout the semester. No specific point value is placed on this aspect. Remember, "The glory of God is intelligence." (D&C 50:24)

C. Handout Drawing Assignments: It is very important to keep current with the class schedule of drawing assignments. All homework should be completed before the unit test to give you adequate experience and training to show proficiency. Late assignments will be accepted only one week after assigned. Your assignments are due at a specified date; it is your responsibility to do your own work and deliver your drawings on time. See assignment sheet and follow it closely!!

D. Drawing Project: Upon learning the architectural concepts of drafting, the student will draft working drawings to exhibit the skills mastered. This work is essentially the knowledge or essence of what you have learned in the course and drafting skills developed. Your drawing project should qualify your skills developed in the class. See your assignment sheet for schedule of the various drawings included as the project. Upon instructor's evaluation, the student can reclaim 80% of missed points by correcting the specific drawing. The evaluation of these points is done at the end of the term.

E. Quizzes, Tests, and Final: There are 10-11 quizzes, 3 major unit tests, and final exam to evaluate the student's acquired skills and knowledge of the course material. Quizzes are given as listed on the assignment sheet. THERE WILL BE NO MAKE-UP OF QUIZZES. YOU MUST BE IN CLASS TO TAKE THE QUIZ. The final exam will be a comprehensive objective test.

All testing times are posted in your Assignment Sheet, so follow it closely to be prepared when an evaluation is given.

VI. EQUIPMENT/MATERIALS AND LAB POLICY

All designers, draftspersons, and architects should begin to acquire equipment at an early stage in their education. Several points are important to consider when buying equipment: 1) don't buy too much, 2) avoid novelty items, 3) get good quality, and 4) take care of it so that it will last a lifetime. Consider your investment well spent.

The following list is the required equipment/materials list for this class:

1. Only one of the two pencil types is required: 1) Mechanical drawing pencil and F, H, 2H, 3H, and 4H or non-print leads or pencils; 2) Metric drawing pencils: .5 mm, .7 mm, and .9 mm. Leads for the metric pencils are:

a. .5 mm - 2H, 4H, or non-print }

b. .7 mm - F, H, 2H } (Lead substitutions are an individual preference)

c. .9 mm - H }

2. Drafting erasers (pink pearl or nylon or vinyl)

3. Drafting tape, erasing shield, drafting brush, and sandpaper pad.

4. Architectural scale and civil engineers scale.

5. Triangles: 30-60 degrees, 45 degrees or optional adjustable triangle.

6. Lead pointer or sharpener. Sharpener is not required with metric pencils.

7. House plan template (must be 1/4" = 1'-0" scale). Timely T-30 or equivalent is recommended. There are a number of floor plan templates that will work for the class. If you already have a floor plan template, you don't have to buy another.

8. Lettering Guide, Ames type only.

9. French curve, any size will do.

The required equipment listed above is available at most engineering supply stores. For student convenience the book store has an drawing instrument packet which includes most of the required equipment listed above. You will be required to purchase equipment not included in the packet.

In addition to your equipment you should acquire a roll or pad of sketching paper and sheets of drawing vellum without borders as required. This is real thin "see through" type paper. The sketching paper comes in white or canary. A black felt-tip pen is helpful in creating your sketches.

VII. "CONDUCT AND USE" STANDARDS FOR THE DRAFTING LABS

A. General Requirements:

1. Room organization is not to be changed without permission; drawing boards and chairs should not be moved from assigned areas without permission.

2. Scales must not be removed from drafting machines.

3. Minor adjustments on machines can be made by the student. This will be illustrated in class.

B. Personal Responsibilities:

1. Horseplay or general rowdiness is not acceptable behavior. Stay at your designated drafting station. Students are responsible for laboratory equipment, and will be charged for damages or loss incurred through thoughtless or careless activity.

2. The labs are reserved for Drafting and related subjects. THE FOLLOWING ACTIVITIES ARE NOT CONSIDERED TO BE CONSISTENT WITH LABORATORY OBJECTIVES:

a. drinking or eating in laboratory areas

b. reading newspapers

c. rowdy or unnecessary conversation

d. individual, nonapplicable study at such times when drawing surfaces are needed

3. It should never become necessary to use ballpoint pens in labs.

4. Each student is responsible for housekeeping his area -- leave it clean!! All used tape should be placed on the class tape ball or in trash cans provided. If your drawing surface is dirty, ask instructor for cleaner and clean it.

C. Lab Policy: Within the first week of class, the student is to select a drafting table that will be his/her lab station for the term. Lockers are available at the back of the classroom, simply place your personal lock on a locker of your choice. All materials must be removed from the locker at the end of the semester.

D. Blueprint Room: The blueprint room is open from 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. Monday through Friday for students to copy his/her work for assignments, etc. PLEASE plan your schedule and adhere to this policy!!
