Vikings split home games

by Riana Jevne
JEV01001@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Ricks forward Kyle Nead pulls down a rebound over Salt Community’s forward Jeff Blackett. Timothy Robison / Scroll
The Ricks men’s basketball team displayed an aggressive defensive zone their competitors couldn’t handle as the Vikings beat UVSC 82-75 Thursday night, then later experienced a set back in their conference race Saturday night by losing to Salt Lake Community College 83-65.

The Vikings put up a fight against the Utah Valley Wolverines after previously losing to them in their first conference match-up 66-85.

Fouls filled the court with Ricks at 30 personal fouls and the Wolverines at 20. Center Aaron McKee and forwards Tyler May and Jeremy Thomas fouled out of the game in attempts to stop Utah Valley.

“[The fouls] were actually in our favor because Utah Valley missed a lot of foul shots,” Coach Clyde Nelson said. “We knew we had to battle them inside with their big, solid people.”

Utah Valley shortly answered back with baskets until forward Kelly Nead stepped up to put in three three pointers to put the Vikings ahead seven minutes into the game at 16-9. Nead later added another three point shot enabling Ricks to lead 29-16 with eleven minutes remaining in the first half.

“We shot the ball really well. I still think Utah Valley has got the best group of guys in the league; they could beat anybody if they played hard,” Nelson said. “It was a great win. We shot it well and we played good, solid defense.”

Utah Valley’s turn came late in the first half as they tied with the Vikings 33-33, then at 39-39 with 12 seconds remaining. The Vikings’ offense held strong in the final 20 minutes as they held UVSC to only one other lead change. Ricks hit 13 of 19 from the free throw line and shot 57 percent from the floor.

Nead led total points with 20, Hiatt at 19, and Bowman with 18 points.

Ricks vs SLCC

Ricks suffered a disappointing loss to the Salt Lake Bruins Saturday night as they went into the game with Salt Lake four rankings below them in conference standings.

It was a battle to get to the basket and the Vikings spent the majority of the night circling the court for a decent shot position.

“I think our biggest thing is that we were still celebrating from beating Utah Valley Thursday night instead of getting ourselves ready to play for tonight. You’ve got to be ready to play every night. I don’t think we were ready to play,” Nelson said. “Anything that we talk about, they did better than us tonight. They beat us at every phase of the game.”

A lack of execution was evident as the Vikings took five minutes to obtain more than two points and made only one of their first eight shots of the first half.

Nead sparked a run with the help of Bowman to put Ricks within one point, but later fell to 12 points from the Bruins with six seconds remaining in the first half.

“I think [the loss] might give us a little bit more fire knowing that we’re not unstoppable,” Nead said. “It gives us a little more eye-opening when we loose a game to come out and play a little harder next time.”

Ricks’ outside shooting appeared non-existent in the second half at 28 percent with their three point field goals.

Nead led with scoring at 17 points, Hiatt with 15 points, and Bowman and center Kyle Stevens at 11 points each.

With a win against Salt Lake, Ricks would have jumped ahead two or three teams in their conference, but now consequently feel the pressure to continue to compete in the middle of it.

They will take on Snow College and Dixie State College, both ranked in the top three teams of the conference, on the road this week.

“We’ve been playing with a lot of confidence, and we’ve been playing good. But there’s a fine line with that and we went over that line tonight because now we’re playing with confidence, but we quit working hard. That’s when somebody sneaks up and bites you, and that’s what happened with us,” Nelson said. “It’s not going to destroy our confidence because we know we can play with people in our league now, which I think a month ago we didn’t know we could.”
