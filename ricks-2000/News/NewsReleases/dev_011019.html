October 19, 2001

Patricia Pinegar speaks   

on what matters most


REXBURG, Idaho –Patricia A. Pinegar, former general Primary president of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints, spoke to students and faculty of Brigham Young University-Idaho during Tuesday’s devotional.

Sister Pinegar opened her talk by speaking directly to the students: "I really do admire you. Why do I admire you? Because President Hinckley speaks so highly of you, and I believe him."

She continued speaking to the students about her respect for them and her concern about their welfare in a confused and mixed-up world. "As a grandparent, I pray daily for the children, that you will be able to recognize and respond to the whisperings of the Spirit," she said.

She shared an experience that she had while visiting her daughter in California. "I visited the Young Women in my daughter’s ward and asked one young woman what helped her to make righteous choices. The girl responded, ‘I think of my future children cheering for me in heaven as I get sealed in the temple. They are my cheerleaders.’ I believe that there are those beyond the veil cheering for you."

She said that to feel true peace while upon this earth, every person must be righteous. "There is nothing that this earth calls fun or enjoyable that can compare with the feeling of peace and righteousness that comes from living the gospel," she said.

"Today I would like to speak to you about what is most important," said Sister Pinegar. "What matters most to you? What are you doing to get there? Do you have any feelings of peace as you take those actions to reach your goal?"

Once it has been determined what matters most, Sister Pinegar told listeners to compare that goal with farming. "The first time a young boy helped his father plow a field, the rows were crooked. His father stopped him on the tractor and told him to focus on the post far at the end of the field. If he stayed on course to that post, then the rows would be straight," she said. "So should we set our eyes on our post and not let anything keep us from it."

Two things that Sister Pinegar urged students to do daily were to have personal prayer and scripture study. "I know that you have heard about these two things a lot," he said. "And I’m afraid that you may feel a little like Namaan, who when he was told to wash in the River Jordan didn’t understand."

Next week’s devotional speaker will be Sander Larson, former Rexburg resident who recently returned from serving as president of the Washington Seattle Mission for three years. The retired Ricks College administrator now lives in the Salt Lake City area. The devotional will be broadcast Tuesday at 2 p.m. on KBYU, 100.5 FM, and rebroadcast that night at 9 p.m.
