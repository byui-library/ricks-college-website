Recycling encouraged on campus

by Emily Toy
TOY010018@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

For students who are from communities that participate in mandatory recycling, the lack of recycling in this area could be appalling, Kevin Twitchell, Recycling Club adviser said.

The effort toward recycling on campus has been increased since Amanda Bowman, a junior from Manteca, Calif., and president of the club, started pushing the effort.

Recycling is a mandatory effort in Manteca, she said.

“The Recycling Center in Rexburg, Fibres International, is great to work with,” Twitchell said.

The center works with Madison High School, as well, and has a center in Idaho Falls.

Prior to teaching at BYU-Idaho, Twitchell was a national speaker about pollution prevention.

The most important thing we can do for the environment is reduce our consumption, Twitchell said. The second step is to reuse; rather than just tossing it away or into a recycling bin, reuse it.

For example, the computer science department uses a lot of paper, but they do as much paperless grading as possible to reduce their consumption. Then they reuse the paper that they do use by having it available for scratch paper. Finally, when both sides of the paper have been used, they send it to be recycled, Twitchell said.
