BYU-Idaho professors run for city council

by Chad Phares
pha98001@byui.edu
News Staff

Hart
City Hall may soon take on a new look.

After the Nov. 6 city council election, it may be difficult for Rexburg citizens to distinguish City Hall from a faculty lounge.

BYU-Idaho professors Kendell Nielsen and Kenneth Hart are running for two of the three available spots on city council this year.

Despite the link they share as BYU-I employees, there are few similarities between the candidates’ platforms, as well as their reasons for seeking election.

“I wasn’t going to run,” Nielsen said. “People for a few years had approached me. After Sept. 11, I didn’t feel like I’d done enough yet so I felt it was my turn.”
Hart has a different reason for running.

“I see things that are happening and I question it,” Hart said. “You can complain to your neighbor or say, ‘maybe I’ll run myself.’”

Hart, an accounting professor, has handed out more than 200 personal information sheets that state his views on council issues. He said that he thinks that the council wants to spend too much money before all is planned or approved.

“They need someone with a financial background,” Hart said.

Nielsen said he believes increased public involvement is most important in strengthening relations between citizens and council members.

Nielsen
He specifically mentioned a desire for an increased citizen awareness concerning a proposed recreation center.

“I think there will be some kind of recreation center,” Nielsen said. “And it should be planned in detail so that people who vote know exactly what they are voting for.”

Nielsen said that if elected, he would continue to listen to citizens before making a decision concerning the center.

It is doubtful that city taxpayers would be willing to support an indoor swimming and recreational facility, Hart said.

“[City council members] aren’t in touch with what others are going through,” Hart said.

Hart said that the council’s pay raise and what he describes as their “questionable decision making” illustrates his point.

The council approved the pay raise after a city-appointed citizens’ committee recommended the increase.

Nielsen, on the other hand, said that there should be two main concerns for the city council.

Nielsen said that first, the university and the city should be fairly treated, and second, the community must be kept strong.

In hopes of achieving this, Nielsen has a Web page, kendellforcouncil.com, in which he asks for citizens’ opinions concerning issues such as taxes, planning and zoning, BYU-I relations and community service ideas.
“I don’t come with a personal agenda,” Nielsen said. “I’m not pushing anything except to have Rexburg continue forward in a good way.”
