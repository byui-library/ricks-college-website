AWS: strengthening sisters at BYU-Idaho

by Elizabeth Hatchett
HAT01004@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Heidi Fiss, a sophomore from Ontario, Canada and Sherrie Grigg, a sophomore from Idaho Falls, work together to prepare baskets that will be given to needy families on St. Patrick’s Day. Sara Burt / Scroll
The theme for ASBYUI this year is Doctrine and Covenants 108:7: “Strengthen Thy Brethren. ... ”

The representatives of the Associated Women Students are busy strengthening their sisters as they prepare for Mothers’ Weekend and the close of another semester.

A total of 50 representatives are in AWS, but the association includes many more members than some women on campus may be aware of.

“AWS is for all the girls on campus,” Secretary and Social Executive Director Shalise Adams said. “A lot of girls don’t know it, but it is for all girls.”

The representatives are divided into 10 executive counsels, each with their own responsibilities, but as the year ends, they are each assigned a responsibility in regards to Mothers’ Weekend, Adams said.

Activities for Mothers’ Weekend include a mother-daughter/son look-alike contest and a flower and bridal open house. There will also be concerts, recitals and art shows.

“We want the mothers to come to see the talent and to feel the spirit of this college,” AWS Adviser Kris Fillmore said. “The number-one goal is to strengthen the relationship between the mothers and their children.”

Mothers’ Weekend may be the biggest event that these women plan and present to the students of BYU-Idaho, but there is much more going on in the background.

“The goal of AWS is to bring women to Christ,” Janet Stevens, a two-year member of AWS, said.

AWS plans activities throughout the year that are centered around strengthening the testimonies of Latter-day Saint women.

“We follow the mission of the school which is to bring people to Christ. We enhance that by developing women and by serving one another,” Kalli Kronmiller, women’s vice president, said.

Representatives of AWS also work towards bettering other women, Amelia Pearce, one-semester member of AWS, said.

“We keep the honor code and set a good example,” Pearce said. “We uplift the women and help them understand that they are daughters of God.”

The members of AWS are not just the red jacket girls; they are women of faith, integrity and virtue, Stevens said.

“You can tell that these girls generally care for others and have testimonies. They help preserve the spirit of Ricks,” she said.
