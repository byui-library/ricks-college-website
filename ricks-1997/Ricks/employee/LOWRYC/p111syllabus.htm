 Grades:

You grade in the class will be determined through four criteria:  exams, journal, class participation/assignments, and study guides.  Each of these is explained below.

 Exams:

There will be five midterm exams.  Midterm exams will be given in the testing center.  Each midterm will be worth 100 points.  There will also be a final exam that will cover the last unit and include a comprehensive section.  The final is worth 200 points.  Tentative exam dates are shown on the schedule below.  Exams determine 70% of your grade.  There are no makeup exams.  The exams are given in the testing center allowing you to have a few days to work one in.  If you miss an exam, you will get a zero for it.

Note:  The final exam is mandatory.  If you do not take it, you will automatically fail the course!  The final must be taken during the time scheduled by the college.  It is college policy that exceptions are only granted through Max Checketts, Assistant Academic Vice President.  Travel plans are not considered extenuating circumstances for adjusting your test schedule.  Please don’t bother Br. Checketts.  Simply accept the fact that you have a schedule to abide by as part of being a student.

 Journals:

The assigned reading that you do for the course is to provide you with a basic background in psychological concepts and terms.  You will also have the opportunity to read on psychological topics in which you are interested and to consider how psychology affects you.  You will keep a journal of your reading and thinking.  Your journal will be collected three times during the semester and graded.  Because of class size, the journal will not be read entirely.  I’ll randomly select an entry each time I grade the journal, so your journal grade will come from three randomly selected entries.  The journal will be worth 10% of your grade.

What to include

You may select just about anything for your reading.  It needs to deal with psychology and that is about the only limit.  What I want is for you to learn about psychological topics that interest you and stimulate your thinking.  If you start reading something and you don’t like it, after a few pages, just discard it and try something else.  Book chapters and journal articles are especially appropriate.  One of the goals of the course is to help you to develop life-long learning skills, so that you will continue learning after you leave college.  So, you’re particularly encouraged to read from sources like books and journals like Scientific American as well as magazines and newspapers.  You may also find it useful to read chapters from other intro textbooks or chapters from more advanced textbooks that deal with a topic in which you’re interested.

The journal isn’t a formal paper.  It is a place for you to write about your reading and thinking.  I want to see that you’ve had an experience with a psychological topic that interests you.  You don’t have to write a summary of what you’re reading – I don’t want a summary.  I want your comments, criticisms, insights, and questions.  I especially want to see how you tie your readings into what we’re covering in the course.  How is it relevant? and, how does it apply to your life or interests?  Even though this is not a formal paper, you must use proper English.  One of the most valuable skills you can learn anywhere and anytime is how to express your thoughts in writing.

You may also use the journal to record thoughts that you’ve had in class or discussions that you’ve had with friends, family, and roommates.  Use the journal to record your thinking about behavior, experience, and psychology in general.

How often should you write? as frequently as you like.  The more writing you do, the better you are likely to do in the class.  This sort of deep thinking about a topic makes it more likely for you to understand and remember it.  That will translate into better exam scores.  It will also help you in class participation.  Now, I know that I have to set a minimum amount of writing for some of you.  So here it is – the minimum is one entry per week.  I will check your journals to see if you have done that and you’ll lose points for not doing it.  I will also skim through them to make sure that not all of your entries are simply thoughts.  I WANT YOU TO DO READING TOO.  I expect you to read at least two outside sources per month.

 Participation/Assignments:

Learning is an active process.  You can’t just sit and soak up all the stuff that there is to learn in a course; you must engage the material.  I’ll try to help you.  It is difficult to do in a large class, but we’ll use several strategies to get everyone involved.  Sometimes I’ll have you break into groups to discuss questions.  Then I’ll randomly select people to share what they’ve discussed.  I may ask groups to come and do a 5-minute presentation on what they found.  Sometimes I’ll post problems on the board and have you use psychology to solve them.  I’ll randomly select people to give their solutions.  Sometimes we’ll have debates and panel discussions.  I’ll have you break into groups for a few minutes to prepare, but realistically the bulk of the preparation will have to be done before coming to class.  Therefore, it is extremely important to study the material before coming.  Sometimes I’ll give you a topic and have you write a paper in 5 minutes.  I’ll grade you on your participation.  I’ll ensure that each of you has several opportunities to participate throughout the semester.  With the 5 minutes papers, I’ll only be able to randomly select a few of them for grading but I’ll make sure that each of you get to be graded on one throughout the semester.  Your participation is worth 10% of your grade.  How often will this happen?  I think we’ll make a game of it.  I’ll bring a six-sided die to class and roll it at the beginning of class.  Your base chance of an activity will be 1 in 6.  However, it will accumulate as days without an activity pass.  If the first time I roll it there isn’t an activity, then the next day the odds will be 2 in 6 (1 out of 3).  The next day it will be 3 in 6 (1/2), etc.  Neither you nor I will know exactly when it will happen.  I know that occasionally you’ll have to miss class for good reasons, like sickness.  I’ll excuse you from three activities.

 In addition to the in-class assignments, there will be 11 out-of-class assignments that are designed to help you explore a few areas of psychology more deeply.  Detailed instructions for the assignments will be given in class several periods before the assignments are due.  The schedule shows the tentative dates for these assignments.  Assignments are included in the 10% participation grade.

 Study Guides:

You will need to turn in your answers for each chapter’s study guide.  Working through these assignments carefully and thoughtfully will help you prepare for the exams.  Generally the study guides are due the first day that we begin a chapter.  This will help you to benefit from the lectures.  The schedule shows the due dates for the study guides.

 Turning in Assignments:

All assignments are due at the beginning of the class period listed on the schedule (unless we change it in class).  LATE ASSIGNMENTS WILL NOT BE ACCEPTED.

 Grades:
