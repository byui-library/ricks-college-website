Effective ways to avoid injury.

Build up your level of activity gradually over the weeks.

  • Do not set your goals to high
  • For activities such as jogging, brisk walking, jumping rope and etc. limber up gently before and after exercise.
  • For other activities, build up to your target zone, and cool down slowly afterwards.

Listen to your body for early warning pains.

  • Too much exercising can cause injuries to joints, feet, and legs. Do not exercise beyond early warning pains in these areas or more serious injuries may result.

Be aware of possible signs of heart problems such as:

  • Pain or pressure in the left or midchest area, left neck, shoulder, or arm during or just after exercising. (Vigorous exercise may cause a side stitch while exercising- a pain just below your ribs- which is not the result of a heart problem.
  • Sudden dizziness, cold sweat, pallor, or fainting.

For outdoor activities, take appropriate precautions under special conditions.

On hot, humid days:

  • Exercise during the cooler parts of the day.
  • Exercise less than normal for a week until you adapt to the heat.
  • Drink lots of fluids, particularly water
  • Watch for signs of heat stroke- feeling dizzy, weak, light headed, and/or excessively tired; sweating stops; or body temperature becomes dangerously high.
  • Wear a minimum of light, loose fitting clothes.
  • Avoid rubberized or plastic suits, sweatshirts, and sweat pants.

On cold days:

  • Wear one layer less of clothing than you would if you were outside but not exercising. Wear several layers of light clothes rather than one heavy layer.
  • Use old mittens, gloves, or cotton socks to protect your hands.
  • Wear a hat, since 40% of your body's heat is lost through your neck and head.

On rainy, icy, or snowy days:

  • Be aware of reduce visibility (for yourself and for drivers) and reduced traction on pathways.

Other handy tips:

  • Avoid strenuous exercise for at least 2 hours after eating a meal. If you exercise vigorously, wait for twenty minutes until you eat.
  • Use proper eye protection equipment, and good cushioned shoes.
  • Hard or uneven surfaces are more likely to cause injuries.
  • If you run or jog land on your heals rather than on the balls of your feet. This will minimize the strain on your feet and lower legs.
  • Joggers and walkers should also watch for cars and wear light colored clothes or reflective stripes so that drivers can see you. Face oncoming traffic and do not assume that drivers will see you.
  • If you bicycle, wear a helmet and use a light and reflectors on the wheels. Ride with the traffic and try to avoid busy streets.
