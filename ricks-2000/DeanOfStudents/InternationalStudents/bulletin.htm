Welcome to BYU-Idaho

We=re excited that you=ve selected Brigham Young University-Idaho. Whether you are a newly arrived or returning student, the International Student Office is here to assist you during your BYU-I experience.

Where Is The Office?

The International Student Office is located in the Kimball Administration Building, second floor, Room 230. We are housed in the Dean of Students Office. The phone number is 496-1120. We are open Monday through Friday from 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. (closed during noon hour).

All New & Transfer Students

The International Student Office is required to have record and copy of your I-94 (white card), Form I-20 (Certificate of Eligibility), and passport (Canadian students are not required to have passports in order to enter the United States). Please come and visit the International Student Office during the first week of school and bring these documents. We will make copies of your documents and record vital dates and numbers. This will make it possible to replace these items if lost or stolen. Transfer students must report to the International Student Office within 15 days of the first day of classes. Failure to do will result in being out of status, which causes timely and costly paperwork.

Maintaining Status

It is very important that all international students maintain I-20 status. This includes:

§         Maintain a full course of study (12 credit hours per semester) Any exception must be documented by the International Student Office

§         Maintain a valid passport (check your expiration date)

§         Apply for an extension of your I-20 within 30 days of its expiration

§         Attend only the school authorized to attend.

§         Follow procedures to transfer

§         Do not work more than 20 hours per week during the school year.

§         Do not work on or off campus without authorization from the International Student Office.

§         Enroll only for appropriate classes (continuing education, language test credits, or home study courses do not count towards full-time enrolment.)

§         Report any change of address to our office.

Failure to maintain status may result in being barred from entering the United States for three to ten years, based on the amount time that a person was unlawfully present in the United States.

Financial aid is very limited to international students. Any student requiring assistance must first go visit Brother Macbeth in the International Student Office. Students are expected to use their resources for Brigham Young University-Idaho, and financial assistance is not available for those saving for future educational goals or visits home.

Employment

You must have a United States Social Security number and be enrolled full-time to be eligible to work on campus. Work authorization letters, and social security card instructions and applications are available at the International Student Office. After receiving authorization and social security number, you may seek on-campus employment through the Student Employment Office located in the Kimball Building 244.

A dependent spouse and child who are on F-2 visas may not work while they are in the U.S.

On-Campus Employment

You may only work part-time (up to 20 hours per week) during the academic year (September through April). Full-time summer employment is possible in certain areas, but students should be aware that full-time employment is limited and often difficult to obtain. During summer terms, International students must attend a minimum of two terms in order to maintain student employment, unless they have a full-time position.

Off-Campus Employment

There are some options for off-campus employment, but students must first complete a full academic year. There are three off-campus work options. Students desiring the Optional Practical Training off-campus employment, should contact the International Student Office at least four months prior graduation to be aware of the application process and minimum 90 day processing time. Curricular Practical Training (internships) take less processing time, and no fee (other than class registration). Severe Economic Hardship is an employment option available for student who have unforeseen and unplanned hardships or emergencies.

Taxes

All students are subject to state and federal income taxes unless exempt through a tax treaty. You are, however, not subject to social security taxes for the first five years of study.

Green Card Lottery

Each year the United States State Department offers non-immigrant status students an opportunity to apply for a lottery system drawing for permanent residency.

Before You Leave the U.S.

Make sure before you ever leave the U.S. that you check your I-20. This needs to have a current signature (within the past year) of the International Student Advisor. Also make sure you have your I-94, and passport (valid dates) prior to your departure. You will not be able to re-enter without these current documents.

BYU-Provo English Waiver

International Students from non-English speaking countries desiring to transfer to BYU-Provo, must meet their English standards.  You must have a TOEFL of 500 or above with all categories over 50 or 250 or above with two categories over 50.  Or you can waive the TOEFL with taking English 215, 215C, 216 or 252 with an earned grade of "B" or higher.  This must be completed prior to application.

Passport Renewal

Students can get the address and phone numbers their home country=s consulate office in the U.S. by conducting a web search for “foreign consulates” on any of the search engines on the Internet.  This is needed to apply for passports. It is suggested that you start application for passports at least six months prior to the expiration date. Most passports renewals are done by mail
