Course Overview:

This course is designed to further prepare broadcasting students for careers in  video
production and television broadcasting.  It gives them the opportunity to build upon the
foundation of video production skills acquired in Communication 259.
 
This course will allow you to create useful video projects that will help you in obtaining
your first job in video production or television broadcasting.  This course will focus primarily on single camera video production techniques.  Additionally, the instructor will meet with small production teams weekly as these teams work on their chosen video projects.
 
Each student will leave this class with a demo reel that includes samples of their work.
This demo reel will assist you in advancing your educational advancement as well as opening the door to your first job in video production or television.   

Communication 259 is a prerequisite for this course.
Communication 259 and 267R CANNOT be taken concurrently.

The general layout of the class is described below:

As we progress throughout the semester, the instructor will meet with your production
team Tuesday during class time.  During these meetings you will discuss the progress of your
current project.  The instructor will share with you information that will make your video project experiences more enjoyable.  We will reserve our Thursday class meeting for discussing advanced techniques utilizing the Video Toaster/Flyer system.  Please keep in mind that you will need to spend more time than just one hour a week on your video projects.  As with the Communication 259 course, you will have to sign up to use the video production lab.  The sign up book is in Spori 226.

If this is the first time you are taking Comm 267R, your video production team must
complete the projects listed below.  If you are repeating this class for the second time, you will
need to discuss with the instructor the types of projects you would like to work on this semester.

The instructor must approve all video projects before you can begin to work on them.
A story board of each of your video projects will be due before you start shooting.  I will go over your storyboard with you and give you guidance on how to improve your project.  Due to the length of the mini-doc, a basic script instead of the storyboard will be required.  Failure to turn in a storyboard or script will cause your grade for that particular project to be dropped one full letter grade.  There are times during a semester real-world projects are made available to this class.  In such instances, the instructor reserves the right to change video projects to allow your team to produce such real world projects.
