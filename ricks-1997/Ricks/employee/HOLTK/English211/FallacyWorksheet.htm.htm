1. ____________________ also known as "begging the question." This fallacy occurs when someone reaches a conclusion by a logical path that assumes the conclusion to be true: "We can trust the recommendations of the FDA because the FDA's recommendations are good ones," or "Students like rock music because it is the most enjoyable music around."

2. ____________________ also called "false dilemma," presents two and only two alternatives for consideration when other possibilities exist; says there are only two possible and neatly opposed answers. "Either you are in favor of recalling the mayor, or you are a supporter of her political platform." "America: Love it or leave it."

3. ______________________ assumes the popular position is almost necessarily the correct position; believe it because everyone else does or "everyone knows." "One million people can't be wrong."

4. ______________________ also known as "poisoning the well"; Latin word meaning "to the person"; attacking the person presenting the argument rather than the argument itself; substitutes irrelevant judgments of an individual for reasonable evaluations of an issue. "Because she is extremely wealthy, our mayor cannot properly represent this city."

5. _____________________ fallacy sets up an opponent whose ideas are so clearly wrongheaded that they can easily be refuted. Sometimes the straw man is created by ignoring the strong points of an opponent's position; creates and then attacks a distorted version of the opposition's argument. "The Democratic candidate wants the federal government to house everyone, feed everyone, care for everyone's children, and provide medical care for everyone. And he's going to take 50 percent of every dime you make to do it." "How long will America tolerate softheaded opponents of gun control who want only criminals to have guns?" (Advocates of gun control vary in their views, but they do not want only criminals to have guns.)

6. _____________________ draws a broad conclusion from a few unrepresentative examples. "Students in Professor Burton's eight o'clock freshman biology class are often late. Today's college students are irresponsible and unreliable."

7. ______________________ compares things that are not truly comparable, thereby arriving at questionable conclusions. "You won't catch me rock climbing. I don't want to die falling off a rock." "But you can kill yourself falling in the bathtub, too."

8. ____________________ fear, authority, pity, snobbery, etc. Fear: implicitly threatens the audience, "Will there be a job waiting when you leave college?" Snob appeal: to desire for prestige, "It's not for everyone. The best never is." Pity: "Professor Lowe, I must get an A. If you don't give me an A, I won't get into law school." Authority: "Nine out of ten doctors surveyed recommend. . ."

9._____________________ draws its name from a Latin phrase meaning "after this." Also called "false cause." It is a pattern of thinking that confuses sequence with causation. "Since I walked under that ladder yesterday, I've lost my wallet and received a speeding ticket."
