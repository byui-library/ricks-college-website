Periods ( . )
They signal the end of a complete thought, a complete sentence.  They indicate a complete stop with the voice.
 
Commas ( , )
They signal a partial stop, a pause.  A comma is used in the following ways:
   
(1)  to separate a series of adjectives -- The heavy, unpainted, broken door
 
(2)  to separate a series of adverbs -- She moved slowly, deliberately, painfully.
 
(3)  to separate an appositive -- Susan, my oldest sister, won.
 
(4)  to separate a dependent from an independent clause.  This will be true only if the DC precedes the IC.  If the DC follows the IC, no comma is used.  Example: Although I can, I won't.  I won't although I can.
 
(5)  to separate words in a list -- Jerry, Frank, Harry, and Sue are all here.
 
Colon ( : )
This signals that a series follows. Go to the store and buy the following: bread, eggs, milk, cheese, and apples.
 
Semicolon ( ; )
Use this punctuation
(1) to join two independent clauses if they are related in meaning and structure.
Example:  I came; I saw; I conquered.  He gave her the money; she gave him the horse.
When you join independent clauses with a semicolon, you do not capitalize the first word of the second clause.
   
(2)  to make a distinction in a list of words: apples, oranges, and pears; potatoes, onions, and carrots; and almonds, cashews, and filberts.
Run on Sentences.
Although not many students write run-on sentences, each semester, a few do.  Consequently, review the following points:
 
1.  Do not join two independent clauses with a comma.
 
IC, IC.      is wrong.            IC. IC.   is right.
IC; IC.      is right.               IC, and IC   is right
IC; therefore, IC. is right.
 
2.  Do not join two independent clauses without any punctuation.
 
IC IC.    is wrong.
 
Again, the above examples in number one are right.
 
3.  Remember to make a distinction between the meanings of independent and dependent clauses.  With adverbial dependent clauses, follow these guidelines:
 
IC, DC.    is wrong.             DC, IC.    is right
 
Wrong.  The dark clouds meant rain, the rain lasted for two weeks.
Wrong.  The dark clouds meant rain the rain lasted for two weeks.
 
Right.  The dark clouds meant rain; the rain lasted for two weeks.
Right.  The dark clouds meant rain, and the rain lasted for two weeks.
