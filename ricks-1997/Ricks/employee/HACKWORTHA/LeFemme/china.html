In the begining, the world consisted solely of swirling clouds of red dust, a whirlpool of chaos. In this dust storm. the giant P'an Ku took shape. When first formed, he was sound asleep; as the dust clouds nourished him, he grew to an immense size.One day, the giant's own immense snores woke him up. Upon opening his eyes and seeing only chaos, he became irritated and smashed into the clouds with one giant fist.

Pieces flew apart; the light, bright, warm fragments rose up and formed the sky, while the heaver, darker cold pieces fell and became the earth. P'an ku stood between the sky and earth, supporting the heavens on his head. The sky rose higher and higher; the earth became thicker. It is said that P'an Ku stood there with the sky on his head for 18,000 years.

Bored, P'an Ku took up a mallet and began to carve the universe into the shapes it holds today. Then he carved four huge mountains to hold up the heavens so he could lay down and sleep.

He slept forever. His breath became the clouds and wind; his snores became the thunder that booms during storms; his eyes became the sun and moon, while his blood flowed from his body and became rivers, oceans, and lakes. From P'an Ku's skin and hair came plants and trees, while his teeth and bones turned into metals, minerals, and precious stones.

Other gods eventually formed and roamed the world that P'an Ku had created. One goddes was named Nu Kua. She had the lower half of a dragon and the upper half of a beautiful woman. As she wandered through P'an Ku's paradise, she became lonely. Something was missing, Nu Kua felt; something P'an Ku had forgotten. She knelt on the ground and fell into deep thought. After a while, she began to scoop up lumps of the yellow clay and form them into human shapes, breathing life into them until the world was populated. Then she gently lifted her creations and whispered to them the secrets of love, so that they could continue to populate the world on their own.
