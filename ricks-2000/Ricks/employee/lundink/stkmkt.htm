The Stock Market Game

• How to obtain information and analysis about companies and industries

• Different approaches for making the best stock selections – and when to buy or sell them

--How to Play

Objective

To increase the value of your stock portfolio more than any other student in your class by the end of this class. See the syllabus for the beginning and ending dates

Rules

1. On the first day of the game, you will invest a total of $30,000 (play money, of
---course!) in any stocks listed on markets in the United States. No transaction fees will
---be charged for your initial purchases, but fees will apply to any sales or purchases
---made during the game (see rule #3).

2. You must initially invest equal amounts of $10,000 in three different stocks.

3. You may sell any one of your stocks at any time and buy another stock in its place.
---However, you must pay a transaction fee of $10 per buy or sell order. Here's an
---example:

After a month, you decide to sell one of your stocks that has declined in value from the original $10,000 to $8,000. After deducting $10 for the transaction fee to sell, $7,990 remains. You then purchase shares of another company valued at $7,980 ($7,990 minus $10 purchase transaction fee).

4. Maintain a file containing the following information on your stock portfolio:

• Reasons for buying a stock, both initially and during the game.
• Reasons for selling a stock during the game.
• Number of shares of each stock you own at the close of the market each week.
• Current stock price of each stock you own at the close of the market each week.
• Detailed record of any buy or sell transactions: Date of transaction, name of company for shares sold, number of shares sold, price per share sold, value of shares sold after deducting transaction fee, name of company for shares purchased, number of shares purchased, price per share purchased, value of shares purchased after deducting transaction fee.

6. For 20 points, turn in a record of your transactions with a total of the amount of money you made or lost at the bottom as follows:

Total gains   = $560
Total losses = $130
Net gain      = $430
