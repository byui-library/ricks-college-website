Ricks College News Release
Jan 22, 2000

Apostle uses student discussion to teach principles at Ricks 

LDS Church Apostle Richard G. Scott held an open discussion with students during Tuesday’s devotional in the Hart Auditorium at Ricks College.

Scott said, "This afternoon I will share with you some principles that, if understood and consistently applied, will bring enormous blessings throughout your life. They are not difficult for me to explain, nor for you to understand. However, they will require of you significant, determined effort to yield their full potential."

He said people can learn by what they see, hear and feel. "Careful observation of meaningful things can provide a rich resource of truth," he said. "Write those vital things down and follow them."

Scott said, "By far the more powerful source of direction in your life, is what the Holy Ghost prompts you to feel."

He also shared seven recommendations that he said would enhance our capacities to "recognize truths the Lord would have (us) learn through the Spirit," and he asked students in the audience to comment on the importance of these seven items.

First, Scott told the students to establish a set of guiding principles.

Scott then asked the students to discuss the importance of faith in Jesus Christ.

"Jesus Christ descended below all things to help us gain our goals, he deserves to have our trust," a student said.

"Faith is abiding trust in the Lord," Scott agreed.

He then asked the students to comment on why prayer is important.

"I couldn’t get through a day without prayer," a student said. "I need to know that the Lord is by my side."

"If we are not communicating with our Heavenly Father, how can we know what he wants us to do?" another student said.

Scott added, "Don’t fall asleep when you’re praying. You are talking to the most important personage that exists. Be alert and don’t be routine."

The discussion then moved to the importance of the scriptures and Scott emphasized the importance of the combination of prayer and scripture study.

He then asked the students to comment on the importance of temple worship.

"I’ve found that as I go to the temple more often, my school work is easier and I am stronger to resist any temptations," a student said.

"I bear witness of the importance of the temple. It is hard to be on earth without one that you are sealed to," Scott said. "It is worth qualifying no matter what it requires, to enjoy the blessings of the temple."

Scott asked the students why listening to good music is important.

"Music is important mainly because it is what inspires us," a student said.

"Can bad music hurt you?" Scott asked.

"When you are listening to stuff that was not inspired by the spirit . . . the spirit retracts from us," a student answered.

"Bad music leads to improper acts," Scott added.

The final principle Scott invited students to comment on was the importance of staying morally clean.

"I was just recently engaged. . . . There is a powerful love and bond of purity that is between me and my fiancé knowing that we both kept ourselves, for our entire lives, morally clean. And I am grateful the purity and the happiness that comes from leading such a standard," a student said.

"You will be more grateful as you kneel across the alter and you see that worthy wife, and you being worthy, being sealed there,"Scott said.

Another student explained that people who are not morally clean do not have the blessings of the spirit.

"Set a standard that outside the covenant of marriage, you will never stimulate those emotions in the private parts of another person’s body, nor allow them to do that to you. You just simply will never do it," Scott said.

"The greatest motivation for me in life comes from knowing that you have a father in heaven who loves you and wants your eternal happiness. And knowing the Savior and believing that he will help you," Scott said.
