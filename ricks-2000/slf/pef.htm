HISTORY: PERPETUAL EMIGRATION FUND

The Perpetual Emigration Fund was established in 1849 to provide a method of raising funds to assist the many immigrant saints embarking and completing the wrought-stricken journey west. Many of the early LDS converts sold all they had in order to travel from far off lands and simply make it to this country; these arrived penniless and without the means to continue their trek across the greater part of the country. Without the assistance of the Perpetual Emigration Fund, indeed, some would not have been able to even set out from their respective countries.

As with many of Brother Brigham’s inspired programs for the saints, the essence of the plan was simple—monies were collected; immigrants were helped; and once safe in the Salt Lake Valley, they were expected to pay back what they had been given in order to help those who came after them. As Dallin H. Oaks put it, "...these enterprises called forth the sacrifices that molded pioneers into Saints and prepared Saints for exaltation. Pure in Heart, p. 118-119"
