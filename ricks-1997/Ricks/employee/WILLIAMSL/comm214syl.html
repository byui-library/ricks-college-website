 Comm 214: Reporting and Research

IN COMPLIANCE WITH THE AMERICANS WITH DISABILITIES ACT, A LARGE-PRINT VERSION OF THIS SYLLABUS IS AVAILABLE TO HELP YOU.

This class is designed to help your skills as journalist and marketer and as a communicator and researcher. Successful completion of this course should give you unique skills in using spreadsheets and databases, in using the internet for research, in understanding business and in reporting. You will gain skills in interviewing and in old-fashioned reporting and in using new media to communicate.

INFORMATION BASICS:
Communication 214: Reporting and Research 18350, MWF 12:00 to 12:50 p.m. SPO 175 MW, SPO 357 F Instructor: Lane Williams ext. 2921, home (359-1457 . . . I will gladly take calls at home, of course, but ask some discretion in those calls) williamsl@ricks.edu Office 104 Spori. Office hours 10 to 10:50 daily or by appointment. I am usually available directly after class for short consultations . . .
Watch for class updates via e-mail or via our newsgroup: ether:ReportingandResearch. I also have a website that will have a copy of this syllabus and other stuff -- it should expand as time goes on. Access it though the faculty directory on the Ricks website. The address is: http://www.byui.edu/Ricks/employee/WILLIAMSL/default.htm

We have four mottoes for this class:
· Be not afraid.
· Have a documents state of mind.
· No more lazy thinking and reporting.
· Think unique stories.

Our objectives are straightforward enough:
1. Learn the basics of computer-assisted reporting.
2. Learn the basics of marketing research.
3. Learn how to do research at county buildings for reporting.
4. Make the internet a more basic part of your reporting, research and communication.
5. Learn how to analyze data -- and critically think about results.
6. Improve your interviewing and communication skill.
7. Improve your appreciation for the difficult, joyous craft that is good journalism.

Prerequisites:
NONE, except you must be a communication major or have the consent of the instructor. It would be good to have some basic knowledge of keyboarding, of the World Wide Web and of newswriting -- inverted pyramid style.

You will need Steve Weinberg's Reporter's Handbook, a ZIP disk, material for a portfolio and access to the World Wide Web and access to your campus electronic mail account.

Assignments:
ALL ASSIGNMENTS MUST BE SAVED AS RETURNED BACK TO YOU AND TURNED IN AT THE END OF THE SEMESTER IN A PORTFOLIO.

By the way, any assignments may be dropped or altered to be made less extensive during the semester by me . . . so stay alert. (This would, of course, make other assignments count more proportionally.)

We will have four major assignments: These four will represent 60 percent of your grade or essentially 15 percent each.

1. Using five or more sources, you must craft a business plan that will either: open a business in this or your home community -- or you will show how to finance a start-up business.

2. By the end of the third week, you will pick $10,000 in imaginary stocks and $10,000 in imaginary mutual funds, then craft a one-page paper describing, logically, at least four reasons why you picked the stocks and mutual funds you did and why you think they will be successful -- including at least three sources. You must then record how much money you make or lose over time-- and compare with a traditional savings account of four percent at your bank -- monthly in a spreadsheet. You will turn in your proposal/logic early in the semester -- for which the assignment will be mostly graded. If your stocks/mutual fund totals are among the top 10 percent (that is the top one or two people) in the class, then you will get extra credit to the tune of one half step up in letter grade. You must turn in your final spreadsheet with your portfolio.

3. You will write a three to four-page article in news style. This article MUST COME FROM A DATABASE YOU CREATED OR ACQUIRED and analyzed using SQL. A copy of the database query results with the accompanying SQL script will be required. This article must be spelled correctly, read well and contain at least three interviews in addition to the numbers you crunched. Above all else, it must be logically sound and show coherent thinking. In addition, you must find at least one web site source and one paper source to back up your research. This is a computer-assisted reporting project and an oral presentation of it will represent your final project.

4. You will teach a "skill" using newsgroups or a student-server webpage you built. You may do a combination of the two. By skill, I mean something that will help other students in their jobs as journalists/marketers. It could be a primer on effective photo composition. It could be a primer on three unusually interesting sources for reporters or marketers (other than those talked about in class.) It could be a presentation on the challenges facing the news media. It could be useful strategies to get a job. I will give several suggestions you may pick. Part of this assignment will be to use the news group. By the end of the third week, you must submit your planned "teaching" idea. The following week, you will receive the weeks you must post your teaching. During the semester, each student MUST ask smart questions on the newsgroup of at least two presenters and let them respond. SO AS EVIDENCE, in your portfolio, you will include the questions you asked of others as part of your "teaching" assignment -- they both may not come in the same week. No face-to-face questions allowed. PRESENTERS must submit in the portfolio not just the completed assignment, their questions to others, but also the questions they received and responses they sent. You will be graded on your thoughtful questions, your excellent responses, your clear spelling and grammar and the unique richness of presentation. Creativity in using these media are primary here. This is about a four-page paper worth of writing/designing. Don't expect too much in-class discussion on how to make these media work for you, but there will be some. You must review each person's teaching.

Next, you will have seven to 10 in-class assignments. These will make up about 20 percent of your final grade.
These will be database and spreadsheet samples you must complete within a time allotted. You may also have one PageMill assignment. We will spend one week in lab discussing a concept, then no later than the next week we will have you complete an assignment based on that concept.

Third, you will have seven to 10 out-of-class assignments. These will make up about 20 percent of your grade.
Among those out-of-class assignments will be two exams covering the text and other concepts including websites. Among the assignments may also be outlining the textbook, going to a the courthouse and retrieving as much data on a person as you can possibly find, completing a random interview, doing three or four internet searches to determine specific types of information, finding a logically weak news article and completing an article by adding new information. You will have one week warning on these assignments, so keep up. You will be expected to read the entire text for the exams. One assignment -- extra credit -- may be completing some genealogical records, but we'll have to see how that develops with other lectures. We think that would be a useful skill to help you acquire in research.

FINAL: There will be no "exam" final. Instead, you will give an oral presentation, complete with creative overheads of your computer assisted-reporting project, AND we will have a short, 10-question quiz showing you reviewed each presenter's "teaching." We will also compare notes on stocks and mutual fund successes. You will have a small party, as well. You will turn in your portfolio.

GRADING:

You should note that 94+ is an A
90-94 is anA-
87-89 is a B+
84-86 is a B
and so on in 10-point increments.

CHEATING -- and plagiarism -- IN ANY FORM MAY RESULT IN FAILING THE CLASS.

In general, other than as listed, expect no extra credit. Grading is, in part, your responsibility, so keep track of everything. If you think I've goofed on records or otherwise, let me know, I'll talk with you. I have an open-door policy. I do reserve the right to curve the class via natural groupings if I find scores dropping lower than I think is reasonable

NOTES:
During the semester, we will have one field trip to Idaho Falls to visit courthouses, etc., so plan on doing that. We'll discuss the most convenient time. One or two classes may be you visiting the library to view a video on your own time. We will have other library class lectures.

This class may well be unlike anything you've tried before. It could be hard. I will endeavor to make the content useful, interesting and fun. The skills taught in this class are things I wish I had learned in college. They, I believe, will help you. Brandt Houston, for example, has said that use of a database may become as common in a newsroom as a word processor. Web productions are in search of excellent people and ever increasing in size and power and the right business plan can make you a millionaire. I hope to wet your appetite to real skills while teaching you the power of clear thinking.

Good luck., material for a portfolio and access to the World Wide Web and access to your campus electronic mail account.
