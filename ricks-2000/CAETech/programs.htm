Degree Programs Available

The following is a brief description of each major offered within the Department of Computer Aided Engineering Technologies.  For a full catalog description of each major and required courses, please follow the appropriate link.

Degree Name *Degree Program Description
CAD/CAM Technology (325) Associate of Arts and Sciences The major in CAD/CAM Technology (325), gives graduates an introductory education into engineering technology relating to Computer-Aided Design (CAD), Computer-Aided Manufacturing (CAM), and Advanced Welding Processes Technology while completing the general education course requirements for the Associate of Arts and Sciences degree. The program is designed for students who have an interest in discovering what technology is about and how it is applied in today’s world. This major will help focus a student’s interest in the area of technology.
Computer Aided Design Technology(048) Associate of Science The major in CAD/CAM Technology, (048), involves graduates in the process of translating creative ideas and concepts into models, computer simulations, or working drawings used to produce new machines or products. Emphasis is placed on traditional design concepts coupled with strong Computer-Aided Design (CAD) skills. The curriculum includes Computer-Aided Design (CAD), Solids Modeling, Computer Rendering and Animation, CAD customization, Computer-Aided Manufacturing (CAM), and Computer Numerical Control (CNC). This program is designed for immediate employment, as well as transfer to a technology program at a four year university.
Manufacturing Engineering Technology(087) Specialized Associate Graduates are involved in selecting the methods of production, operations, machine selection, and tooling.  They deal with the actual production processes through which designer's concepts are translated into manufactured goods in a cost efficient manner.  The curriculum includes Computer Aided Design (CAD), Computer Aided Manufacturing (CAM), Computer Numerical Control (CNC), Quality Control, Tool Design, Process Planning, and Hydraulics & Pneumatics.  Program is designed for transfer to university or immediate employment.
Welding Engineering Technology(110) Specialized Associate

Graduates are provided hands-on training in the Welding processes and develop skills leading to Welder Certification.  They are taught to recognize and understand the processes involved and make intelligent decisions in terms of material usage and of the variables in processing. The curriculum includes a solid foundation in Welding Theory, Metallurgy, Welding Examination, Quality Control, and Computer Aided Design (CAD).  Program is designed for transfer to a university or immediate employment.

*Associate of Arts and Sciences vs. Specialized Associate degrees:

Associate degrees in a Specialized Discipline or Specialized Associate degrees offer students the opportunity for considerable specialization in a major field of study along with a carefully selected curriculum of General Education.   Those who plan to transfer to a four-year college or university after graduating from Ricks in one of these programs may need to complete certain General Education requirements at the transfer institution. 

Associate of Arts and Sciences degrees concentrate heavily on general education requirements during the first two years, and then focus  more heavily on major and minor requirements during the junior and senior years at a four-year college or university.

Specialized associate degrees are common in engineering and technology based programs and usually result in the student completing a bachelors degree in a shorter length of time due to tight course sequencing.  Associate of arts and science degrees allow the student to select a tentative major in their designated area of interest while exploring different areas of study until they have reached a firm decision on their major field of study.

Please feel free to contact your advisor if you need further clarification concerning the different associate degree programs.
