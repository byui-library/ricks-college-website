Wrestler B.J. Wright finds strength from mom

by Benjamin Martin
MAR00002@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Behind every hero there is a driving force pushing him/her to succeed. For B.J. Wright, the only All-American wrestler for the Vikings’ last season, it was his mother.

Ever since he was 4 years old, Wright, a sophomore from Elberta, Utah, remembers his mother supporting him as a wrestler.

“[Mom] and Dad used to wake us up every Saturday [for the wrestling meets], and we would lie on their bed while they got ready,” Wright said.

Wright and his three brothers all wrestled growing up, and all received the same support. His six sisters, however, were left out of the excitement.

“My sisters used to get jealous because wrestling always went over the top of anything else,” Wright said.

Every time he went to a tournament anywhere in the country, his mother was there, he said.

“Mom’s vacations were planned around wrestling,” Wright said. “She wouldn’t let me go to a tournament without her.”

Wright always felt the support from his mother at the meets.

“I think she loves the sport more than I do. If we were wrestling, she was happy,” he said.

She also supported her children so strongly because she enjoyed seeing them succeed, Wright said.

“I think she saw the talent and wanted to push us. All four of us have always be really good,” he said.
Wright recalled a time when his mother pushed him especially hard to stay in the Western Regional Tournament when he was 12 years old.

“I hurt my arm in a match and had to come out. I won the match and advanced when time ran out, but I told my mom I thought my arm was broken. She told me to get back out there, but the trainers told me my arm was broken in two places, and I had to forfeit the rest of the tournament,” Wright said.

Wright ended up placing sixth in the tournament after forfeiting the remaining matches.
