I want to share some ideas from Parker J. Palmer's book: Let Your Life Speak.  I hope you can glean some meanings from them.

 

"We arrive in this world with birthright gifts- then we spend the first half of our lives abandoning them or letting others disabuse us of them.  As young people, we are surrounded by expectations that may have little to do with who we really are, expectations held by people who are not trying to discern our selfhood but to fit us into slots.  In families, schools, workplaces, and religious communities, we are trained away from true self toward images of acceptability; under social pressure like racism and sexism our original shape is deformed beyond recognition; and we ourselves, driven by fear, too often betray true self to gain the approval of others.

The Invitation (inspired by Oriah Mountain Dreamer, Native American Elder, May 1994)

It doesn't interest me what you do for a living.

I want to know what you ache for, and if you dare to dream of meeting you heart's longing.

It doesn't interest me how old you are.

I want to know if you will risk looking like a fool for love, for dreams, for the adventure of being alive.

It doesn't interest me what planets are squaring your moon.

I want to know if you have touched the center of your own sorrow, if you have been opened by life's betrayals or have become shriveled and closed from fear of further pain!

I want to know if you can sit with pain, mine or your own, without moving to hide it or fade it or fix it.

I want to know if you can be with joy, mine or your own; if you can dance with wildness and let ecstasy fill you to the tips of you fingers and toes without cautioning us to be careful, be realistic, or to remember the limitations of being a human.

It doesn't interest me if the story you're telling me is true.

I want to know if you can disappoint another to be true to yourself; if you can bear accusations of betrayal and not betray your own soul.

I want to know if you can be faithful and therefore trustworthy.  I want to know if you can see beauty even if it's not pretty everyday, and if you can source you life from God's presence.

I want to know if you can live with failure, yours and mine, and still stand on the edge of a lake and shout to the silver moon, 'Yes!'

It doesn't interest me where you live or how much money you have.

I want to know if you can get up after the night of grief and despair, weary, bruise to the bone, and do what needs to be done for the children.

It doesn't interest me who you are, how you came to be here.

I want to know if you will stand in the center of the fire with me and not shrink back.

It doesn't interest me where or what or with whom you have studied.

I want to know what sustains you from the inside, when all else falls away.

I want to know if you can be alone with yourself; and if you truly like the company you keep the  empty moments.

Who are you... really??

Before the 17th and 18th centuries The doctrine of Imminence (the idea that God was present in the world and it's phenomenon:  people, plants, animals, tress, etc.) pervaded the thinking of most people until after the start of the scientific revolution which promoted the the doctrine of  Transcendence (the idea that God was in his heavens and no longer actively involved or present with the world) that allowed modern science to objectify and in essence become hostile to nature.

 

From The Power of Myth  by Joseph Campbell and Bill Moyers (page 98-99)

"The idea of the supernatural as being something over and above the natural is a killing idea.  In the Middle ages this was the idea that finally turned that world into something like a wasteland, a land where people were living inauthentic lives, never doing a thing they truly wanted to because the supernatural laws required them to live as directed by their clergy.  In a wasteland, people are fulfilling purposes that are not properly theirs but have been put upon them as inescapable laws.  This is a killer.  the twelfth-century troubadour poetry of courtly love was a protest against this supernaturally justified violation of life's joy in truth.  So too the Tristan legend and at least one of the great versions of the legend of the Grail, that of Wolfram van Eschenvbach.  The spirit is really the bouquet of life.  It is not something breathed into life, it comes out of life.  This is one of the glorious things about the mother-goddess religions, where the world is the body of the Goddess, divine in itself, and divinity isn't something ruling over and above fallen nature."

"However, our story of the Fall in the Garden sees nature as corrupt; and that myth corrupts the whole world for us. Because nature is thought of as corrupt, every spontaneous act is sinful and must not be yielded to.  You get a totally different civilization and a totally different way of living according to whether your myth presents nature as fallen or whether nature is in itself a manifestation of divinity, and the spirit is the revelation of the divinity that is inherent in nature."

                                                 ___________________________

It seems as if our culture views nature as evil and fallen and treats it accordingly.  The problem arises when we treat ourselves in a likewise demeaning and inappropriate way especially after having been blessed with and by the ordinances of the Gospel of Christ.

We are the offspring of God, created and then begotten in his own image, as male and female.  His Spirit is within us and gives us life, temporal and spiritual.  That being the case, the bliss that we follow would be the manifestation of the Spirit that is within us and gives us life.  The ordinances of the Gospel are made of no account if we do not awaken to the truths of our own theology!

Our Spiritual Stewardship:

We seem to live in a spiritually dumb society, according to Danah Zohar, in her recent book Spiritual Intelligence.  When we do not recognize our own spiritual heritage and treat ourselves with contempt and meanness we are guilty of spiritual abuse and violence. 

 Is the life you are living- the life you were sent here to live?  Are you open to the Spirit of God within you which speaks in the still, small voice that with gentle insistence is calling you to honor your inner kingdom and to nurture the grace that is found there.

Your life can be a Sacramental Life if you are true to who you are and the spiritual origins of the one who beget you.  Please read the wonderful talk given by Truman G. Madsen:  'The Sacramental Life'.

The ordinances of the Gospel of Christ 'enliven us with the Spirit of God and with that Spirit to guide, comforter and heal us, we have the most  wonderful mentor in the cosmos to guide us in our Dance of  Life.  Do you hear the music? 

The Issue of Obedience... from Parker Palmers book :To Know as We are Known

"The word "obedience" does not mean slavish, uncritical adherence; it comes from the Latin root audire, which means "to listen."  Obedience requires the discerning ear, the ear that listens for the reality of the situation, a listening that allows the hearer to respond faithfully to the personal implications of  that reality, whatever it may be."  

God does not want our outward behavioristic -mechanistic conformity.  The Pharisees should have taught us that.  God wants our hearts, our inner kingdoms.

"Truth is a fidelity rather than a conformity.  What is the difference between the two?  Conformity is a relation towards another which is owing to another by reason of the other's nature.  Fidelity is a relation towards another which one owes to oneself by reason of one's own nature.  Conformity obligates from the outside.  Fidelity obligates from within."  Leslie Dewart

Here is an interesting Letter from my daughter regarding:  obedience/conformity/control

dated: February 2001

Sorry dad, I wanted to write you yesterday but alas, the internet isn't

working anymore at our house. This is the first time I have been to a

computer.

My thought:

One night I was reading in the D&C and I came across a scripture that said

something about lying and how unacceptable it was. I don't remember the

verse, but I do remember thinking about all the times I had lied in my life,

and how it didn't seem like a big deal. Yes, I could see how the really

"big" lies would be, but not the little ones.

That reminded me of something I heard you said once.  You asked the

question of why the typical Sunday school or seminary class was so concerned

about teaching the "evils" of rated R movies, swearing, morality problems,

and drugs, instead of focusing on problems like lying, pride, and hard

heartedness (which are found verse after verse in the scriptures.)

I thought about this. Why?

Control...

Obedience is an important principle in the scriptures. We hear all the time

that it is important to be obedient, which could be defined differently for

different people, but for the sake of argument, let's assume obedience

simple means following the commandments. Well... the way I see it, people

in the church can control the first set of problems: swearing, R rated shows, immorality and drugs 

can be overcome by most with will-power, the factor of control.  But what about

pride?  How can you overcome that with will-power?  How can you control the

pride in your heart? You can't. That is something that you have to turn to

the Lord for.

I believe that most latter-day saints, including me, can't handle that.  We

say with our minds and mouths that our lives are in the Lord's hands, but in

reality, with the way we live, they are far from it.  So we focus on the

things we can control, the things we have been trained to control.

Situation:  I have an experience where I realize that pride consumes my soul

and what do I do?  I say to myself  "Oh, that is unfortunate and hope one day

that the problem will work itself out..."  but, if I had an experience where I

was sexually immoral, I would be in the bishop's office, crying and trying

with all of my might to repent properly.

But, according to C. S. Lewis, pride is the greatest of all sins.  Every other

is a mere 'flee bite' in comparison.

This doesn't seem consistent. I wish my grief about pride would be just as

great or even greater as the sexual sin, but it isn't.  Why?

I don't know, I guess pride just doesn't seem like a "real" sin.  I had no

control over the pride, I didn't ask for it to be there, so how can I be

accountable for it?  But the sexual sin, I was in complete control of and so I

feel that it is a greater sin.

Back to the 'control issue' again...

It is important to be "obedient" to the first set of problems.  As long as

the obedience is the result of the right intent, but it is also important

not to get stuck and think "this is all I  need to do,"

the second set of problems are a whole new ball game.  Perhaps a growing in

the spiritual life-- to really turn your life over to Christ in a new way.  To

understand ultimately that you are helpless and have no control in which you

cling onto for dear life... to realize your nothingness and that in itself,

is humility.

mmmm...

Just my thoughts...

I will email you later answering your other questions.

love you much father-

Camie

The Nature of Truth...

Due in large part, to our modernist mechanistic/materialistic notions of truth- we have reduced truth to reflect accurate information, facts, figures and knowledge about the world at large.  I am reminded however, of the scripture: "...ever learning but never able to come to a knowledge of the truth."

What if our paradigm of the truth were fashioned after the ideas of man?  What if there were much more to truth than just the mechanistic notions of Newtonian Physics with it's mathematical formulas and laws that are suppose to govern the known physical world?  What if our search for truth --using the scientific method is medieval and steeped in ideas and values hundreds of years old?

I am proposing that the missing elements of truth revolve around two things:                     1. consciousness  and  2.  relationships.

When Christ revealed the 93rd section of the Doctrine and Covenants to Joseph Smith He told him in verse 22-25 that: 

 "Ye were also in the beginning with the Father; that which is Spirit, even the Spirit of truth; and truth is knowledge of things as they are, and as they were and as they are to come; and whatsoever is more or less than this is the spirit of that wicked one who was a liar from the beginning."

My thoughts on the sentence:  'Ye' refers to the children (us) who partake of Christ's glory and we are manifestations of the 'spirit of truth' (or consciousness and which is another reference to our relationship to Christ).  'Things' refer to the children  who reflect their spirit integrity of truth by 'knowing' (being conscious and awake to) Christ as their light, life and law-- as they are, were and are to come (see D&C 88:11-13). This simple declaration has  to do with our eternal conscious/life nature and relationship to the Giver of all Life!  The other option being-- the spirit of the wicked one who is a liar/deceiver and desires to rob us of the light and truth of Christ.

My idea is simply, that the scripture we use to define our Mormon truth (D&C 93:24) is taken out of context, not only out of section but out of sentence!  How can we ignore the profound doctrine of the the 93rd section in relation to understanding truth ( Christ and those begotten through Him)  and then isolate a middle part of a sentence that fits in so well with the secular culture's paradigm of truth and call it good?  I can't help but be moved to believe there may be much more to truth than that.

Here is an section of an interesting letter I received from my missionary son, Brad, when he was serving in the Philippines, June of 1999:

"I have been thinking of the people that I come in contact with everyday, especially with those of a strong belief in another religion.  I'm finally beginning to see or at least I think I do, that their feelings, beliefs, and paradigms about Christ and their religion are just as strong and real as mine are.  And that, mine are no more true, in a sense, If I am not living as a loving and merciful person.  That I could actually be more 'wrong' and further from Christ than those of other sects, if I do not love.  That the claim of love has actually the greatest claim of all."

Truth is centered and grounded in Christ.  We manifest the spirit of truth in our own lives by living with the eternal integrity of our natures and by treating others with the same respect, reverence and compassion.  If we flounder on this simple truth then shame on us.

Do we have the courage to be true to our own natures and follow our heart to the life giving energy that comes from our bliss?
