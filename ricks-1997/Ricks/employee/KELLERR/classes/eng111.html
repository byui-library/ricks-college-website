Course Objectives and Procedures

College Writing helps students develop basic writing skills to incorporate the composing process and to write proficiently on a college level by requiring expository, academic, and argumentative essays totaling 5000 words of drafted and edited prose.

" Objectives of English 111C

1. Provide practice in college-level writing since only writing teaches writing. This practice results from four formal writing assignments dealing with topics you generate from the thematic chapters, individual experiences, readings, and discussions. These writings represent the four chief aims of writing: to reflect, to inform, to persuade, and to speculate.

2. Provide experience with different writing organizations or strategies. This experience comes with the writing of eight elaborate paragraphs (approximately 250 words each) exemplifying rhetorical paradigms of narrating, describing, defining, explaining a process, reporting cause and effect, classifying, comparing and contrasting, and persuading. these will become a strategy portfolio in which the student revises two paragraphs for a grade (100 points)

3. Incorporate fundamental library and research writing skills by writing a minor research paper. This assignment involves principles of assimilating, evaluating, and using information from primary and secondary sources to produce a single paper. This library paper is worth 150 points.

4. Review/Learn basic elements of good writing such as idea development, topic invention, paragraph structure, introductions, controlling ideas, support, rhetorical modes and devices, and conclusions.

5. Recognize that good writing comes from good revision. Most people are satisfied with the first draft, especially when pressed time elements prevent revision and rewriting, but good writing only comes with the time required for rewriting.

6. Review/Learn basic elements of grammar and mechanics. This is not a course for English majors where you must learn jargon of little-used exceptions found in grammar. But this course will review basic principles of grammar and mechanics to make communications easier and clearer.

7. Develop individual writing theories. All students come to this class with individual theories about writing based on year writing and learning. Teachers often insist all class members follow the one theory that the teacher uses. However, you will experiment with different approaches to pre-writing, writing, rewriting in the hopes of finding the approach that best fits with individual personalities and with different writing circumstances.

8. Evaluate writing and grammar skills. Your grade in this class will result from four formal essays (reflect, inform, persuade, speculate)--100 points each; a minor research paper (150 pts.); drafts (25 pts.); a grammar final (100 pts.); and an expository strategy portfolio (100 pts.). In addition, I give daily quizzes and/or in-class writing exercises (10 pts. each) that cannot be made up.

9. Plan your time. You know what is expected of you for each class period. Check your syllabus regularly. Get your assignments in on time. All work is due at the beginning of class. I DO NOT ACCEPT LATE WORK.

‘ Conduct of the Class

The basic pattern of the course is one of (1) theory, (2) practice, (3) critique. The course of theory is the text supplemented by materials furnished by the instructor. The theory of each assignment is thoroughly discussed in informal classroom sessions. Included in these discussions are numerous examples of the application of each principle to students= own fields and to their specific writing problem. Following such discussions, the students then write their papers and hand them in. The teacher then reads the papers and makes written or oral comments about the paper.

‘ Grading and Evaluation

The teacher will evaluate all formal writing assignments and comment on what works well in the document and offer suggestions for improvement. The teacher will assign a letter grade to the document and record a number grade in the roll book. Most often the number grade follows the breakdown below:

A = 95% C = 75%

A- = 92% C- = 72%

B+ = 88% D+ = 68%

B = 85% D = 65%

B- = 82% D- = 62%

C+ = 78% F = 59%

The teacher reserves the write to slightly alter the number points depending on the student=s performance. For example, instead of recording a 92% for an A-, the teacher may record a 90%.

‘ Teacher Conferencing

Most student questions and writing concerns are handled in the classroom through class or individual discussion. Students may schedule an appointment with the teacher for more extended conferencing. Rarely will the teacher read an entire document during a conference but rather will assist the student in identifying the writing frustration and in developing options to solve the communication problems.

Students who wish to conference with the teacher about a graded paper must meet the following two conditions (there are no rewrite options to improve a graded assignment):

1. Wait 24 hours before making an appointment with the teacher.

2. Come to the appointment with a list of suggestions to improve the paper. The teacher will discuss those options with the student.

‘ Academic Honesty

ATo be in compliance with the Ricks College Code of Honor, students are expected to be honest in all academic work and to help others fulfill the same responsibility. One serious form of academic dishonesty is plagiarism, i.e., presenting another person=s writings or ideas as if they were one=s own. Most writing required at Ricks College will consist of the student=s own composition. To avoid plagiarizing, care must be taken to accurately identify and credit information that is not original with the student.

AMinor offenses will result in an >F= on the exam or assignment. It may also be necessary to repeat the work and still receive a reduced grade for the course. Major offenses involving premeditated cheating will result in an >F= for the course and action by the Dean of Students Office such as probation or dismissal from school. Some offenses may also have legal implications.

AAccused students will always have the right to know the specific charge, hear the evidence and have an appropriate hearing, if needed.@ (Ricks College Catalog, 1999-2000, p. 20)
