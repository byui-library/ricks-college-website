MEN'S CHOIR HONORS LINCOLN

The Men's Choir was invited to the Idaho State Capitol for special recognition of Lincoln's Day. They gave spirited presentations for the House of Representatives and for the State Senate. The brief program also included an oration of the Gettysburg Address by Rexburg resident Scott Kidd dressed as Abraham Lincoln. The Speaker of the House relinquished his presiding position to "President Lincoln" for the duration of program. The Men's Choir's final strains of "God Bless America" brought the legislators to their feet in standing ovation. The choir was also invited to the office of Governor Dirk Kempthorne. Choir director Kendell Nielsen said the feelings generated through the music were "so powerful it was amazing." Governor Kempthorne remarked to the group, "This is probably the best sounds we have ever heard in this office." Members of the Men's Choir continued to enjoy their opportunity to be in the Capitol and serenaded those gathered about the rotunda in an impromptu continuation of patriotic strains of praise. Ricks College recognizes President's Day as a holiday, and no classes will be held on Monday, February 21.

FORENSICS WIN NATIONAL ROUND ROBIN TOURNAMENT

Two students from Ricks just returned from winning the first National Junior College Round Robin Tournament. Hillary Clemens and Jason Peterson won the tournament for Ricks College. They received numerous full-ride scholarship offers from interested universities, as well as the title of "The Best of the Best of the Best." This win carries the utmost prestige. The tournament invited the ten top forensics programs in the country to each send their one best debate team. Each team debated each other team in a round robin. The colleges invited to participate were Bethany Lutheran from Minnesota; Casper College and Northwest College from Wyoming; Clackamas College from Oregon; Fresno City College, Los Angeles City College, and Modesto Jr. College from California; Johnson County Community College from Kansas; The College of Eastern Utah; and Ricks College.

NEW TRACK RECORD

He may have tagged behind in his younger days, but Kish Beverly has out paced his brother Bryan to set a new Ricks College indoor record in the 400-meter dash. Kish surpassed Bryan's record set in 1993 by .20 of a second at 47.98 in the 400-meters at the Mountain States Games earlier this month. The men's and women's indoor season continues with the remaining meet scheduled as the Intermountain Championship in Pocatello on March 4. So far, six women and three men have qualified from Ricks College for the NJCAA outdoor finals in May. They include four shot putters, Suzie Clayton, Cathy Fuailemaa, Tia Iketau and David Wood; three pole vaulters, Mandy Kelly, Lisa Preston and Mark Berry; and two runners, Kristen Ogden in the 3,000-meters and Ben Wynn in the 5,000 meters.

STORM CANCELS CLASSES

High winds and rain roared through the campus just like the rest of eastern Idaho on Monday afternoon as the area was placed under tornado watch and high wind alert. No major damage was reported on campus. However, power outages caused classes to be dismissed and concern for those trying to submit on-line applications for admission prior to the February 15 deadline. Campus Police reported one person stranded in the Clarke Building elevator, but most calls to the campus dispatcher were simply questions about when the power would be restored. Campus Police were preparing to lock up buildings as a precautionary measure when the power was restored about 6 p.m. Pockets of disrupted power linger throughout the valley causing some isolated inconveniences. For Ricks College, this resulted in the Scroll, the college newspaper, being unable to meet its normal publication time line due to the longer power outage in Idaho Falls where it is printed.
