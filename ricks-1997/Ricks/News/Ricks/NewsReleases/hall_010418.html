Ricks College News Release
April 18, 2001 
New vice president named 
at Ricks College

REXBURG, Idaho — Athletic Director Garth Hall was appointed Wednesday to be the new vice president of advancement by Ricks College President David A. Bednar.

He replaces Brent Kinghorn, who will be leaving the college to serve as a mission president for The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints in Albania beginning July 1. Hall will assume the new position June 1.

Kinghorn served as vice president of community services since the position was created in 1978.

In making the announcement, President Bednar said, "Hall brings a rich combination of vision, perspective, skill, desire and experience that will bless our students and this institution during this time of transition."

The president explained "advancement" will replace the term "community services" because it more clearly defines Hall’s role as Ricks becomes Brigham Young University-Idaho.

"Advancement" is commonly used by colleges and universities as a term to describe efforts to advance their mission. The goal of advancement is to enhance an institution by building support – be it in the form of public understanding, financial, alumni loyalty or new students.

"As we become Brigham Young University–Idaho, this is a time then we must advance and articulate our mission to a variety of publics," the president said.

Hall was appointed athletic director in September 1997 and also has been serving as the new director of the Student Activities Program since December. Because Ricks is phasing out intercollegiate athletics during the coming year, a new athletic director will not be named.

He holds a bachelor’s degree in physical education with a minor in psychology from Utah State University, a master’s degree from USU in physical education with a minor in education administration, and a doctorate from Brigham Young University in athletic administration with a minor in organizational behavior.

He has taught coaching philosophy and techniques at Idaho State University and BYU. He also taught management of athletics at Tulane University.

Hall served as head football coach at ISU from 1988-92 and assistant football coach at Wake Forest, BYU, Tulane and Oregon State.

He also founded a consulting firm that studied and evaluated athletic organizations.

He and his wife, Sharon, have been married for 33 years and live in Rexburg. They have six children and 11 grandchildren. Their son Ryan played baseball and their sons Scott and Curtis played baseball for Ricks.
