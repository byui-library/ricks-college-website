The Eve of St. Agnes
by Rod Worthington
Information taken from
The New Catholic Encyclopedia
and
The Twentieth Century Encyclopedia of Catholicism

The Eve of St. Agnes is celebrated on the 21st of January.  Little is known about this Roman Saint that was martyred in the third or fourth century.  She is recorded as a young virgin at the age of thirteen years old.  The legend tells of her as a very beautiful girl with many men trying to win her hand in marriage.

When Agnes rejected all the men this privilege, she was sent to the home of prostitutes because she was a Christian.  The men visiting Agnes in the house of prostitution lost site of lust and regained a respect for her because of her prayers of faith.

Agnes was brought before a judge and condemned to die.  The execution consisted of her being strangled, beheaded, and burnt at the stake.  According to Catholic beliefs, if a young woman stays a virgin, she will see her husband in a vision.  This belief is meant to give all young virgins a hope for marriage.
