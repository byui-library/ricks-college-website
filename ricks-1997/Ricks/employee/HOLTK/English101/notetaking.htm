SCORING GUIDELINES

2 points 1. Set up your paper with a 2 1/2-3" margin, by drawing a verticle line down the left side and drawing a line 2" from the bottom of your last page of notes. The margin is for key words, text headings, and questions. The body of the paper is for supporting details. The space at the bottom of the last page is for a summary.

2 points 2. Label your notes with subject, title, text page numbers, and date.

3 points 3. Use the margin to list headings, topics, questions, and examples. List supporting details in the main body of your page. Avoid complicated outlining.

3 points 4. Use telegraphic sentences. For example, rather than copy from your physics text, "The force of weight equals the mass times the force of gravity," write "F=ma." Do not write in full sentences or paragraphs.

2 points 5. Use symbols and abbreviations. For example, the first time you write "Archaic homo sapiens," write it out completely. The next time you write it you can abbreviate, "A.homo sap."

3 points 6. Skip lines when you come to a new heading. This will leave you space if you need to add more information later. Then put the new heading in the margin. Remember to write on only one side of the paper. To study your notes, spread all the pages in front of you, overlap the pages so only the margins (or summaries), can be seen. Then review headings, key words, and answer the questions you have written.

5 points 7. After you have finished the notes for a section, write a one paragraph summary (3-4 sentences). Write the summary for the section you have taken notes on, not a summary on the bottom of each page.

5 points 8. Review your notes. Using a different color, list key words and test questions in the margins. You may also highlight your notes using correct marking techniques.
