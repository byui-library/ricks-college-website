Course Description:

In this Nursing Program, Gerontological Nursing is the application of Brigham Young University-Idaho Nursing Department conceptual framework in caring for the aging person and persons needing long term care. This course is designed to help the student understand changes and specific pathological processes that may occur as one ages. Medicine has made great strides in adding years to life. The challenge to the nurse is to add quality years to the elderly's life by teaching prevention and health maintenance. Nurses can best meet this challenge through using functional helth patterns and the nursing process in planning care for elderly patients. Death is the inevitable consequence of birth. This course will assist the student in dealing with the feelings and fears of the patient, the family, and themselves when confronted with terminal illness, as well as providing techniques the nurse can use to bring comfort and peace.

Course Goals

1. Identify societal changes and demands in regards to the aging population

2. Recognize the common stereotypes and myths regarding aging.

3. Describe the physical and psycho-social care for a terminally ill patient.

4. Recognize the stages of the emotional response to approaching death or loss.

5. Assess the routine physiological and functional changes, developmental tasks and areas of potential self-care limitations of the elderly.

6. Apply possible nursing diagnoses, plan, and implement strategies to elevate the self-care level, eliminate or minimize self-care limitations, and act for or do for when the patient is unable to do for themselves.

7. Plan appropriate nursing care for elderly patients using the Clinical Reasoning Web and the nursing process.

8. Teach wellness concepts to elderly patients using the clinical Reasoning Web and the nursing process.
