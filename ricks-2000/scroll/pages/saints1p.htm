MORMON MYTHS DENOUNCED AT MAJOR FORUM

by April Murdock
scroll staff

Robert Marrott of the religion department examined and renounced common Latter-day Saint myths at the major forum Thursday in the Taylor Chapel.

He defined a myth as a "fiction or half-truth, especially one that forms part of an ideology." Three things that promote the formation of myths are imagination or zeal without knowledge, pride and intellectuality, and Satan, he said.

"[Myths are] things we may or might think are true that really are not true, or at least not verified," Marrott said. "Myths can be harmless and fun, whether true or false, but when they undermine gospel truth and testimony, they can be devastating or just plain stupid."

It is essential to get information straight from the source, Marrott said, and to not believe everything that others claim the general authorities have said.

Some common myths involving patriarchal blessings state that the one with the blessing will be presiding over the Church when the Savior comes or that there will be some living today who will see Christ when he comes.

"This is, of course, false," President Harold B. Lee said in the April 1970 General Conference. "The Master said that the time of His coming would be as a thief in the night, that of the time of His coming not even the angels of heaven would know."

Another rumor states that some missionaries' patriarchal blessings say they will be called home to fight in World War III or not be called on a mission at all because of pending global conflicts.

"We've never seen any reliable documented evidence of this claim. Similar stories have been around for many years," Marrott said.

One myth asserts that some missionaries will be called on secret missions to China. When they open their calls there will be a Salt Lake City phone number to call and President Hinckley will ask them to serve three years in China. When this rumor was brought up in a priesthood leadership session at Ricks a few years ago, Elder L. Tom Perry of the Council of the Twelve said he knew nothing about it, Marrott said.

"Though there have been service and welfare missionaries serving in China, no such 'special calls' have been issued as these rumors indicate," Marrott said.

A widespread quote says that today's youth were "generals in the war in heaven" and in some future day, they will be worshipped by other eternal souls when they hear that the youth lived in the time of Gordon B. Hinckley.

"None of the general authorities is the source of this quote. ... The original version of this statement originated with an instructor in an Especially for Youth class, but the statement has gradually been changed from its original form," Marrott said.

Another myth maintains that the Church has never taken a stand on evolution or confuses what that stand is.

"Twice the First Presidency has declared the position of the Church on organic evolution," President Boyd K. Packer said at a BYU Book of Mormon Symposium in 1988. One was published in 1909 and the other in 1925, Elder Packer said.

"It is my conviction that to the degree the theory of evolution asserts that man is the product of an evolutionary process, the offspring of animals - it is false! The so-called theistic evolution, the theory that God used an evolutionary process to prepare a physical body for the spirit of man, is equally false," Elder Packer said.

It is sad that often when a cherished myth is exposed as false, a person's testimony is dashed, Marrott said. Some people lose confidence in the Church or its members.

"Three components are necessary to assist us in becoming aware of the truth and its opposite, in all fields of study. First, the standard works; second, the teachings of the modern apostles and prophets, from Joseph Smith's day to now; and third, the Gift of the Holy Ghost," Marrott said.
