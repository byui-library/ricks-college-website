Writing: What It Can Do For You, How You Can Improve

Writing is a process of exploring meaning, a mode of thinking, a method of learning, and a means of organization and self-discovery . In other words, writing skills can have lifelong use and value for you.

Your best writing will come when you care about what you say and the people to whom you say it. For learning to occur you must be personally involved; the learning must be self-initiated; your attitude must undergo some changes; you must evaluate your own experience to incorporate the learning into your life; and what you learn must be more than just a gathering of facts.

Because this is a composition course, the emphasis will be on improving your writing skills. You learn to write by writing. To improve, you follow the same pattern as in learning to perform well at any sport: (1) Do it. (2) Evaluate your performance, usually with the help of a coach. (3) Revise, incorporating what you have learned.

You will learn the skills of writing, revising and proofreading short essays. The majority of your time will be used to learn revision skills--the skills to change and improve your writing until it is meaningful, focused, organized, supported, logical and stylistically correct and readable. You will learn to proofread--finding and correcting problems in spelling, punctuation and grammar.

Responses to your writing are most useful during the writing and revising process. Therefore, you will have opportunity to receive feedback from the teacher and your peers during the writing process to aid you in making the best choices about what to say and how to say it. Commenting and grading papers are separate steps. After you have received all the reponses you want and have completed the paper, the teacher will grade it.

This course will enable you to--
* Improve your reading, thinking, writing, revising and proofreading skills.
* Discover ideas and put them into words.
* Use feedback effectively.
* Revise for improved meaning, focus, organization, support, logic and style.
* Eliminate punctuation, grammar and spelling errors.
* Use a journal for writing improvement and for a personal record.

Requirements: Texts, Coursework, Grading

Texts
Miller: Writing by Choice
Hacker: A Pocket Style Manual
The Christian Science Monitor - ten-week subscription

Recommended: A hardbound college dictionary and a thesaurus

Coursework
Each student must complete the following:
* Write five papers to be revised and polished as final drafts to be graded (300 to 700 words each).
* Consult with the teacher and others to have your writing evaluated.
* Continue to revise two papers to be turned in as a final portfolio.
* Keep a personal and/or a writer's journal.
* Type all papers intended for grading.
* Keep all rough drafts in a folder and bring them with you each class period.

Grading
The final grade is based on a point system.
   Five papers                                 550 points
    [Late papers will lose 10% and will be accepted only until the class period followint the due date.]
    Five rough drafts                         50 points
    Portfolio                                        200 point
    Journal                                           100 points
    Quizzes & Daily Work                 varies
"A" = 90% of total possible points, "B" = 80%, "C" = 70%, "D"= 60%, "F" = 0-59%  (Plus and minus grades will be given: for example, 80-82% = B-  83-86%= B  87-89%= B+)
BONUS of 25 points for perfect attendance (You must be in class & prepared with each day's work.)

NOTE: Course texts, course requirements and point totals may vary from one semester to another and even from one section of the class to another.
