Ricks College News Release
November 8, 2000

Spori to be torn down by Dec. 22 deadline

REXBURG, Idaho —Work is expected to start immediately on razing the Jacob Spori Building at Ricks College.

R.E. Beck Construction of Idaho Falls submitted the low bid and was awarded the contract Wednesday by Ricks College.

It has until Dec. 22 to have the 28,000 square foot building removed, says Ricks Physical Plant Director Chuck Frost.

Workers will start with the roof and work down. The entryway facade on the north side will be salvaged. Some carved exterior stones and wooden pieces also will be saved. These pieces will be stored and evaluated for use on the inside of the new Spori Building that will be constructed on the same site.

Some pallets of cleaned rock will be set aside for future use by the college.

A rubble pile will be created from which local residents may take a rock or two as a keepsake. Frost says these rocks will not be cleaned and may be broken.

The first sign of work will be a protective fence that will be erected around the building and extend to the sidewalk at Second South Street. A construction road will be built from Second South to the work site.

An estimated 300 truck loads of debris will be hauled away from the 100-year-old rock structure. Frost says the contractor will have full salvage rights to the remainder of the building.

The building was constructed before asbestos became a common building material. But what little was subsequently added will be appropriately abated. All windows will also be removed so there is no glass in the rubble.

After the roof and upper story are removed, the walls will be pushed in, Frost says.

"Our pioneers did the best they could with their limited resources and we should be grateful for what they did – it has served us well," Frost says of the building. "There’s a lot of emotion in this. It’s now up to us to put together a new building that will last as long as the one they built."

Frost says architectural planning for the new Spori Building has resumed again this month. The design process had been stalled while college officials discuss implications of Ricks becoming a four-year university. He expects construction of a new building to start in June with completion during the winter of 2002-03.
