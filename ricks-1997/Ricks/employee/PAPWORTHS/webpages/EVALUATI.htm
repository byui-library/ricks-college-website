Instructions for Evaluation Paper

Now that you have completed your own research paper, you will have the chance to evaluate the research paper of a fellow student. This will demonstrate your editorial skills. Your evaluation consists of three tasks: 1) evaluate the research, 2) edit the text of the paper, 3) write the critique.

Evaluating the Research
First, evaluate the writer's choice of topic itself. Is it suited to the paper assignment? Is the topic large enough and significant enough to merit research? Yet, has the writer sufficiently limited the subject so that it can be covered in paper of the assigned length.

 
Next, look carefully at three or four quotations or paraphrases (of your choice) within the paper and compare them with the original source. Has the author of the research paper quoted exactly and given proper credit where due? Are the citations accurate and complete? Did you have any trouble locating the exact source?

Editing the Text

Next, read the text of the paper carefully and mark any errors in spelling, grammar, and technical content. Mark up the text with a pencil and write comments between the lines or in the margins.

Writing the Critique

Lastly, write a formal critique of the paper. This critique should summarize your evaluation of the research and your editing of the text (one or two pages in length). The easiest way to organize this evaluation is to use the following ten items as starting points for discussion:

1. choice of topic

2. depth of research

3. introduction and conclusion

4. focus

5. organization

6. transitions

7. audience adaptation (style and clarity)

8. support (case studies, examples, statistics, etc.)

9. mechanics

10. documentation (MLA or APA)

Each heading deserves a paragraph or so of explanation. You will probably also want to write an introductory and conclusion paragraph to your Critique. The ten headings should not necessarily be weighted equally in your evaluation. How much weight you give each heading is up to you, but you should explain how you weight the various factors and why. Lastly, assign the research paper a letter grade. The grade you give the research paper will depend not on how well you are able to guess what grade the instructor would give, but on how well you are able to justify the grade you assign the paper.
