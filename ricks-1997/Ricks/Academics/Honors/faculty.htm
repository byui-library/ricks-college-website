Our faculty work hard to keep the program running and care about the students they teach. We appreciate the excellent job they do for everyone they work with. Below is a quick biography of each of them.

Jeff Andersen

Jeff Andersen has taught Humanities at Ricks College since 1988, having previously worked for Saudi-Arabian Airlines in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia and International English Institute in Fresno, California. He completed his formal training at Brigham Young University. Brother Andersen currently serves as Chair of the Department of Humanities and Philosophy at Ricks. In addition to Humanities, he has taught German, English, and Religion courses. Brother Andersen has a passion for travel, having visited 29 countries in Europe, Asia, Africa, and the South Pacific. He taught for the BYU Study-Abroad Program in Vienna, Austria and has directed seven student tours to Europe for Ricks College. Climbing a pyramid in Egypt, scuba diving in the Red Sea, and riding an elephant in the jungles of Thailand are among his most memorable experiences.

Robyn Bergstrom

Robyn Bergstrom has been teaching at Ricks since 1994 in the Communication Department. She attended Ricks College, and moved on to Brigham Young University for her Bachelors Degree in Communication with a minor in Psychology and English. She later received a Resource Specialty from the University of Utah, and her Masters Degree is in Communication from the University of North Dakota. She loves teaching both Interpersonal Communication and Small Group Communication. She is the Assistant Coach to the Forensics team, and advisor to the Interpersonal Communication Club. She has worked with the Chicago Urban Studies program, the Humanities tour of Europe, and the Connection program for incoming freshman. She enjoys spending time with her family and friends. She is married to Scott Bergstrom and have four children. Mark, Angie, Alison, and Erik. She loves the statement made by Nelson Mandela. "Our deepest fear is not that we are inadequate. Our deepest fear is that we are powerful beyond measure. It is our light not our darkness, that most frightens us. We ask ourselves, 'Who am I to be brilliant, gorgeous, talented, fabulous?' Actually, who are you not to be? You are a child of God."

William Conway

Dr. Conway earned his Ph.D. in English from the University of Nebraska in 1971 where he attended school on a Tri-University Fellowship. He also has a Masters Degrees from BYU and the University of Hawaii. He taught high school in California and American Samoa before joining the faculty of BYU Hawaii. He has taught a Ricks College for twenty-six years. English 282 , Shakespeare, has been his specialty for the past sixteen years. He has attended the Cedar City Shakespeare Festival many times and has also visited Shakespearean sites in England. He loves teaching Shakespeare. His favorite saying is "So much to learn, so little time."

John Cullen

John F. Cullen, Jr. was graduated from the State University of New York at Stony Brook with a B.S. in chemistry. Born in southern New York and raised in Syracuse, NY, he received his Ph.D. from Cornell University (located in Ithaca, NY) in 1983. Dr. Cullen has taught chemistry for over 20 years at various levels ranging from high school to four year universities. Each summer he returns to Cornell University to either teach chemistry or assist in the department's graduate teaching assistant training program. He has been at Ricks as a member of the chemistry faculty since 1988 and has served as the chairman of the chemistry department for the past 5 years.

Rick Davis          Home Page

Rick Davis may have grown up in Rexburg but he has seen the world. His mission was in France and Switzerland where he gained his love for Europe. He holds degrees from the University of Idaho and Arizona State and was in law school when he was drafted into the Viet Nam War in ‘68. Favorite quote: "Men should be anxiously engaged in a good cause, and do many things of their own free will, for the power is in them."

Lynn Firestone

I sincerely enjoy being a part of the honors program because of the dedicated and motivated students who respond well to my philosophy of teaching students how to appreciate their ability to THINK and to become lifelong learners.

Murray Hunt

Although a native of Idaho, I grew up in the San Francisco Bay Area before serving a mission to Winnipeg, Canada. Eric Walz

Eric Walz has been teaching at Ricks since 1987 in the History Department. David Ward

Speak your latent conviction, and it shall be the universal sense; for the inmost in due time becomes the outmost, and our first thought is rendered back to us by the trumpets of the Last Judgment . . . Yet we dismiss without notice our thoughts, because they are our’s. In every work of genius we recognize our own rejected thoughts; they come back to us with a certain alienated majesty . . . Notwithstanding the fact that I have graduated from Brigham Young University with a Masters degree in American Literature and Composition Philosophy, and from The University of Michigan with a Ph.D in English and Education, I continue to feel like a kid in an adult world. Perhaps this is why I live to learn from a variety of sources. However, this zeal for learning didn’t begin in elementary school. In fact, I don’t think I ever experienced the excitement endemic to genuine learning until college–particularly during my graduate work at the University of Michigan. There my eyes were opened to the marvel of making connections between myself and a myriad of ideas. Those who shared their ideas in speech and in print were often on the cutting edge of their discipline of their time. Whether it was in science history, in art history, in language, in myth and culture, in the philosophy of education, in social psychology, in music, music history, or in religion, I became enamored of the transforming power of creative thought as manifest by the miracle of the spoken and written word. Consequently, in all of what I teach--in my writing classes, my literature class, my reading class, my C.S. Lewis class--I am moved to invite my students into the wonder of thinking for themselves while I teach to develop in them a commitment to pay the price necessary to connect authentically with wisdom greater than themselves.
