Religion 122 (Thomas) Take Home Lecture, 1 November

Alma 39-42

 

I.                    Please search out the answers to Study Guide 1, questions 25 and 34-45. This will give you a good grounding in the important teachings of Alma 39-42.

II.                 In addition, please consider the following items of instruction relevant to those chapters:

A.    Why did Alma teach a lustful son more about the plan of salvation than about chaste thoughts and behavior? Elder Boyd K. Packer (1986): ATrue doctrine, understood, changes attitudes and behavior.  The study of the doctrines of the gospel will improve behavior quicker than a study of behavior will improve behavior.@  (Ensign, November 1986, 17)

B.     What were Corianton=s doctrinal doubts and questions?

1.      ProphecyBAyou marvel why these things should be known so long beforehand@ (Alma 39:17) e.g., Can we really be sure about something yet to come?

2.      ResurrectionBAthy mind is worried concerning the resurrection of the dead@ (Alma 40:1) e.g., When will the resurrection start?  What doesAfirst resurrection@ really mean? (see Alma 40:15)

3.      RestorationBAthy mind has been worried also concerning this thing@ (Alma 41:1) e.g., If all will be restored to God=s presence, why worry about sin?

4.      JusticeBAsomewhat more doth worry your mind, which ye cannot understandBwhich is concerning the justice of God in the punishment of the sinner@ (Alma 42:1) e.g., Why would God consign one of his children to misery?  Why punish the sinner?

C.    What do we mean by “paradise” or “spirit prison”? Where do spirits go at death?

Joseph Smith (1843): “I will say something about the spirits in prison. There has been much said by modern divines about the words of Jesus . . . to the thief, saying, ‘This day shalt thou be with me in paradise.’. . . But what is paradise? . . . There was nothing in the original word in Greek from which this was taken that signifies paradise; but it was—This day thou shalt be with me in the world of spirits, then I will teach you all about it and answer your inquiries. . . . The righteous and the wicked all go to the same world of spirits until the resurrection. . . . The great misery of departed spirits in the world of spirits, where they go after death, is to know that they come short of the glory that others enjoy and that they might have enjoyed themselves, and they are their own accusers” (HC 5:424-26).

Joseph Smith (1843): “The spirits of the just are exalted to a greater and more glorious work; hence they are blessed in their departure to the world of spirits. Enveloped in flaming fire, they are not far from us, and know and understand our thoughts, feelings, and motions, and are often pained therewith.” (HC 6:52)

Brigham Young (1856): “Here the inquiry will naturally arise, when our spirits leave our bodies, where do they go? I will tell you. . . . They do not pass out of the organization of this earth on which we live. . . . It reads that the spirit goes to God who gave it. Let me render this scripture a little plainer; when the spirits leave their bodies they are in the presence of our Father and God, they are prepared to see, hear, and understand spiritual things. But where is the spirit world? It is incorporated within this celestial system. . . . Suppose the Lord should touch your eyes that you might see, could you then see the spirits? Yes, as plainly as you now see bodies” (Journal of Discourses 3:365).
