Banana and other Plants in the Green House

Musa X paradisca

 The Musaceae is an old world family spread from West Africa to the pacific. The family provides a major food crop......the BANANA. This family has 40 species and grows in manly wet tropical lowlands. All of the above ground growth is composed of leaves.  The stem is underground.  After flowering and fruiting the leaf material above ground will die.  New above ground growth will come from the underground stem.  In our greenhouse it takes almost 9 months for the fruit to ripen.

Strelitzia reginae

 The genus Strelitzia has four species and is found in South Africa. It has been exported world wide for ornamental uses. These plants are also known as the Birds of Paradise.  The flowers are bird pollinated.  Often this plant is put in the Banana family, but some times it is put into its own family Strelitziaceae.

Heliconia humilis

Heliconia psitticorum

Heliconias comprise about 50 species, found mostly in tropical America. This plant is often included in the family Musaceae.  Sometimes it is put into its own family; Heliconiaceae or included in the Strelitziaceae. The flowers are very similar to the Birds of Paradise.  The plant is herbaceous growing from a underground stem called a rhizome.
