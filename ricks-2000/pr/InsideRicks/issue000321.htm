ELDER HAIGHT SPEAKS AT DEVOTIONAL

"Look at who you are. We ought to be what we ought to be. We ought to be as good as we ought to be," encouraged Elder David B. Haight as he shared some of his feelings with those gathered for the Ricks College devotional on March 14. Now in his 94th year of life, Elder David B. Haight is a member of the Council of Twelve. He said he felt blessed to have had many experiences and opportunities to serve the Lord, to develop an ongoing testimony of the truth, and then to be able to reach out and help other people with their challenges. "All this has been a great blessing in my life because of the depth of understanding and knowledge that I have that God lives and loves us. . . He loves us so much that he sent his Son, Jesus Christ, to earth to establish an organization, to teach, and to give us the plan of salvation."

Elder Haight said, "You who are here now are here upon the earth for a purpose. That is not accidental. You have the ability, the talents, and the capacity to learn more of the gospel principles and to have the teachings of the scriptures be a part of your life ." He encouraged, "Be someone very special - someone who will stand out."

Elder Haight told the students that they should get all the education possible, get married, and raise a family, and help the Church to grow. "You are the ones here at this wonderful college that will go out in the world. . . . As the Lord looks down upon all of us, we are what he has. Everyone who is on the face of the earth is entitled to hear someone say 'I know that Jesus is the Christ, the son of the living God.' Bring that joy and happiness into your companionship, your life, and your community. . . We sing a little simple Primary hymn: 'I am a child of God. . . Teach me all that I must do' D-O. It is as simple, as sweet, as honest, as pure and as understandable as that. . . Our father lives! Do what you should do. The gospel is true. Love it. Live it. Be blessed by it. Bless your posterity by your knowledge and your actions and the lives that you will live."

EXEMPLARY WOMANHOOD AWARD

Lola Hillam Packer of Rexburg was honored with the Exemplary Womanhood Award at the concluding fireside of Women's Week. The award is given annually to one who has demonstrated righteous and worthy living. Packer has served in numerous ward auxiliary positions including president of Primary and Relief Society. She served three years as stake public affairs person. She taught early morning seminary and served with her late husband, Alden, as officiators in the Idaho Falls Temple. She recently served a family history mission in Salt Lake City and served as an adviser to the Chi Chapter in Lambda Delta Sigma for five years.

In presenting the award, Elder David A. Bednar said, "Sister Packer is a wonderful example of righteous womanhood and motherhood. Her bright smile and enthusiasm for life have blessed countless individuals - both friends and strangers; and her selfless service continues to bless the lives of her seven children, 37 grandchildren, and three great-grandchildren." Bednar quoted President David O. McKay who said, "She who can paint a masterpiece or write a book that will influence millions deserves the admiration . . . of mankind; but she who rears successfully a family of healthy, beautiful sons and daughters, whose influence will be felt through generations to come, deserves the highest honor that men can give, and the choicest blessings of God."

WOMAN OF THE YEAR

Brooke Bennion, a sophomore from Vicenza, Italy, was named Woman of the Year during Women's Week activities. Bennion is an elementary education major. She has been involved with the ASRC Academic Board, Tutoring Club, Phi Theta Kappa, and string orchestra. Humbled by the recognition, Bennion expressed gratitude to her parents who are in Italy and to the legacy of her grandparents and great-grandparents for her "drive in life."

WOMEN'S BASKETBALL TEAM ENDS SEASON RANKED 8TH NATION

With a season-ending record of 29 wins and only eight losses, the Ricks women's basketball team completed its fourth-best season ever. The Lady Vikings placed eighth nationally, winning two and losing two games at the NJCAA finals in Salina, Kans., March 14-18. It was the fourth time since 1993 that Ricks qualified for the national finals tourney. Ricks had placed third that year under Coach Lori Woodland and was fifth nationally two years ago. Ricks had another fifth-place finish under Coach Trent Shippen in 1995.

ASRC OFFICERS FOR 2000-2001

Election results were close for the newest officers of the Associated Students of Ricks College. Lance Taylor, an organizational communications major from Newport, Oregon, will serve as president with Charley Jenkins, a business management major from Roosevelt, Utah, as executive vice president; Autumn Southam, a home economics education major from Nashport, Ohio, women's vice president; Jason Franklin, a psychology major from Portland, Ore., academic vice president; Marci Clay, an organizational communications major from Blue Springs, Mo., programs vice president; and Russ Page, a communications/digital media major from Sandy, Utah, social vice president.
