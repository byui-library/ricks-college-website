Start semester off on the right foot

by Janelle Hart
HAR01019@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Leaving home for the first time, brings a lot of fear about the new people you are going to be living with.

Many of the off-campus student housing offer three to seven students sharing an apartment. This new arrangement could be a little strange to a lot of people.

As you move in to your new home, it is very important to be “open-minded” about the people you are going to meet. There are always going to be those who come from different backgrounds, and do not do everything the way you do.

“Patience is an important characteristic to have when dealing with new people, and having good communication from the very beginning will help,” Amy Bangerter, a freshmen from Tucson, Ariz., said.

Communication plays a very important role between the roommates. It is essential to share your views on things going on in the apartment; whether it be the mess in the kitchen, or someone always being on the phone. If you do not talk about your frustrations, then the ill feelings you have will keep growing as the problems never get fixed.

It is also valuable in the very beginning to get to know your roommates. When you move in try not to be shy, but open up and introduce yourself. Get to know them by asking questions: Where tare they from? Why they came to BYU-Idaho? Just ask things that will get them to open up.

“Getting to know their interests, such as their likes and dislikes [are very important,]” Tyson Taylor, a sophomore from Huntington, Calif., said

Subsequently, it is very important to spend time with your new “family” and spend time with them. Doing this you will get to know each of your roommates on a personal level, and be able to understand their needs and will be able to collaborate them with your own.

Another way to get to know your new roommates is serving them. You could serve your roommates in many ways, such as helping them out in cleaning, or just being a source of comfort when needed.

“Serve them and not yourself,” Josh Christensen, a sophomore from Ridgefield, Wash., said.

Serving is a great quality to have when dealing with new people. Often times, we will think of only our own needs and wants, and will not realize the importance of others happiness. Making another person happy will most likely make you happy as well. This is very important and very hard sometimes, but it does make your apartment experience run smooth.

Sometimes it can be hard to adjust to new roommates. The most important role a return student can play is to make the new students feel as comfortable as possible.

After spending a previous semester together with people you have gotten to know it can be hard to readjust to a new person in the apartment.

By taking a step back to when you yourself were the new person, realizing the comfort the new person will need will not only make him or her comfortable, but the whole apartment as a whole.

Moving in can be a completely new experience for everyone. It may take time to adjust to the new atmosphere, but if you come in with an open mind, patience, good communication, and the will to serve, life in the apartment will be exciting.
