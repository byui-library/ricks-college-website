Communications Department Mission Statement

As a communication department, we believe our students have something worth communicating, and we want to help each student find a unique voice. While we encourage theory and process, we focus on results and skills.  Through vision, we raise our students' confidence be improving their abilities in the wide facets of communication.   We glory, as Nephi of old, in plainness and truth, and it is our mission to help our students do likewise.

We accomplish these goals in the following ways:

As interpersonal communicators, we focus on skills and techniques that will serve students in widely varied careers and relationships from organizational behavior and public relations to marriage and family.

As public speakers and debaters, we believe we can help our students learn to effect positive change in their communities through clear thinking and organization, leading to competent, organized, persuasive and enjoyable public speaking.  We believe these skills are needed more than ever in a fast-changing world.

As broadcasters, we help our students learn technical skills like production, effective writing and advertising.  We teach them and train them to be competent, knowledgable, ethical and well-rounded broadcasters.  We believe this exciting, unique field will have great impact on the future of education at Ricks and in the LDS church and we want to help effect this vision.  We also believe that broadcast media can truly help make the world a better place to live.

As print journalists, we teach writing, reporting and editing first, but we do not neglect leading-edge technology.  We embrace change in the journalism field, but we remember that core skills like writing, editing and a reporter's passion will translate well into any emerging or traditional field.  We believe moral jounalists are needed now, perhaps more than ever before, to help bear up the casuse of truth in our modern society.

As speech pathologists and sign language educators, we believe each has a voice worth hearing and helping a person find that voice, however challenging the task, is of unequalled value to both speaker and listener.
