Ricks College News Release
February 14, 2001

Devotional: Elder W. Craig Zwick

REXBURG, Idaho — Elder W. Craig Zwick, a member of the LDS Church’s First Quorum of the Seventy, taught about navigating the roads of life at the Ricks College devotional Feb. 13.

"We each have our own onboard navigation system to give us turn-by-turn instructions," Elder Zwick said. "However, it has nothing to do with finding a certain street or a specific location. It has everything to do with our eternal destination. Our destination is clearly defined and a good navigation system will help us achieve exaltation and eternal life."

He said that for our navigation system to work effectively, we must have an understanding of where we are and where we are going. He also warned of distractions along the way.

"This gives us the point of reference and the tracking system to move us along identified routes to our destination," he said. "Often you can be on the right road, but will be assaulted from all sides with offers to take other roads or more attractive travel itineraries. The adversary will be doing his best to lead you off your desired course. He’ll stop at nothing to catch your attention, and then, ever so craftily, he will lead you into blind canyons and dead ends. Satan’s desire is for us to not know where we are and to not check in to confirm our location."

Elder Zwick told a story of a young man named Cody Parmer from Idaho Falls, who was involved in a serious accident and suffered many health problems on his long road to recovery. Zwick invited Parmer to share some words about overcoming his challenges and his feelings on the Savior.

As the young man finished telling how he overcame his adversity, Elder Zwick pointed out this principle: "Don’t allow yourselves to be discouraged by events which you cannot control. Overcome discouragement by losing yourselves in the service of others. This will also qualify you to receive more responsibility and to have added light and understanding."

He said that the navigation system has a detailed set of instructions with specific directions that must be followed in order for it to function properly. These instructions are the holy scriptures.

"There are so many blessings promised to those who know the scriptures," he said. "If we catch hold of the scriptures and cling to them, if we increase our study and application, and if we pray for understanding and live so the spirit can teach us, the scriptures will save us from temptations."

Another navigational tool Elder Zwick mentioned was hearkening to the voice of the living prophets.

He concluded, "You are powerful and will make a positive difference in the lives of thousands while here on this beautiful campus and well after you leave. You must always know precisely where you are, even when all that surrounds you is a background of darkness and confusion. You are well-marked path that leads to our eternal home."

Next Tuesday’s speaker will be C. Terry Warner, a professor of philosophy at Brigham Young University in Provo, Utah. He will speak at 2 p.m. in the Hart Auditorium. Devotionals are broadcast live and again at 9 p.m. on KRIC, 100.5 FM, in Rexburg.
