Former Ricks College professor overcomes handicap adversity

by Tammy McPherson
MCP00001@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Harold Neilsen talks about some of his many accomplishments in what he refers to as his “ego room.” Neilsen, who was born breech with a deformed right arm, didn't let the handicap hinder his goals. He was an instructor for years with Ricks College, was active in scouting and reared a family. photo courtesy Monte LaOrange / Post Register
The first room on the left in Harold Nielsen’s house is what he calls his ego room.

Pictures and plaques adorn his walls. The elk he shot at Leadore hangs there, as well as the one from Howe. A shelf is filled with handball, horseshoe, marble-shooting and tennis trophies. Nielsen tells of beating everybody out — with the use of only one arm.

Nielsen, 82, was born breech. He was almost 12 pounds. and “had a hard time getting here.” His mother was legally dead, so the doctors pulled him out to save her and in doing so, broke his shoulder. He has had limited use of his right arm his entire life.

However, Nielsen hasn’t let his disabilities slow him down.

“There are ways to get around your handicap,” he said.

In his garage, he has saws he uses to make birdhouses and birdfeeders. He plays the harmonica because “all you have to do is blow.”

His hobby is making charts. Nielsen has a chart of the timeline of the Bible, how long each king lived and who begat who. He has a chart on the different foods he eats and what vitamins and minerals are in each, and when he eats them, he said.

And Nielsen has scrapbooks. He has at least one scrapbook page for every year of his life. He has 10 pages left in the book he is working on right now.

“That puts me at 90 years old so, maybe that’s all I need,” Nielsen said.

Scrapbooks are spread all over his ego room and throughout the house. Every page tells a story of his life.

Nielsen can tell of each of the four missions he served for The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, three of which he went on with his wife. He has pictures of Singapore, Egypt, Samoa, France and Australia. He pulls out souvenirs from the three weeks he went to Baja to collect rocks.

Nielsen used to play tennis in jeans for BYU and was required to hit one square of a tennis net 100 times before going home

“If they’d paid me, I would’ve played tennis. I had a pretty good arm,” Nielsen said.

But instead, he taught at Ricks College. Although his degree was in biology, he taught math, photography and Optics. Nielsen was the first person to win the Outstanding Faculty Award at Ricks in 1968. He was the first to win Teacher of the Year Award, the Extra Mile Award, and the Exemplary Faculty Award.

Elder Henry B. Eyring, former president of Ricks College who is currently presiding over the Church Educational System of the LDS Church, wrote a letter about Nielsen when he was recommended for the Exemplary Award in 1982.

“There are people all over the world who know more about science, about optics, and about photography because of their good luck to know Harold Nielsen,” Elder Eyring said in his letter.

Nielsen is proud of his many accomplishments.

“More so than other people, because I’ve only got one hand to do things,” he said

He’s even had problems with that hand. Three years ago, Nielsen got his hand caught in a snowblower. The forefinger and middle finger on his left hand were severed at the knuckle.

Nielsen said he knows why they call it the forefinger.

“Because it’s worth four of the others. Give me this one back and I’ll trade the others,” Nielsen said. “The hardest thing to do is to pick something up off the floor. I don’t have anything to pick it up with.”

A year after his accident with the snow blower, his wife died. She used to sew his shirts and only leave the top button open so Nielsen could get dressed. She used to do the top button up for him. For a year after her death, he got up an hour earlier to get dressed.

Then, he and his daughter got the idea to velcro the top of his button-up shirts, one of the many improvisations that Nielsen has made. Nielsen can “get dressed up quick as anybody now,” he said.
Nielsen prays every day for wisdom and that he won’t fall down or get hurt. He has had many operations, so many that everybody at the hospital knows who he is, he said. But, he still keeps going.

After all, he still has 10 more pages to fill in his scrapbook.
