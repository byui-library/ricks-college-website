BYU-IDAHO IS A TWO-TIERED INSTITUTION

The transition to BYU-Idaho allows students to choose between associate or bachelor's degrees. Beginning Sept. 5, junior-level courses will begin at BYU-Idaho for the first time since the announcement that Ricks College would become a four-year university known as BYU-Idaho. The announcement added bachelor's degrees to the institution's offerings while retaining and consolidating the existing associate programs, creating a two-tiered institution.

"The notion of a two-tiered institution is simply that we will retain some specialized associate degree programs," says BYU-Idaho President David A. Bednar. "BYU-Idaho will be one of the only places in [The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints] for those specialized associate degrees, and we think it is important to retain them."

With a specialized associate degree, a student can be ready for employment in about two years. Students will learn skills in specialized areas such as culinary arts, criminal justice, welding, and agricultural management.

BYU-Idaho will offer these programs, which are traditionally found at community, vocational or junior colleges, in its unique academic environment due to its affiliation to The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.

During the 2000-2001 academic year, Ricks College offered 122 associate programs. Many of those programs will be shifted to baccalaureate degrees, others will remain as associate degrees and some programs will be phased out. In such a transition, the university is obligated to take care of its current students, allowing them to complete their current program. It is anticipated that by 2005 current programs will be transitioned or discontinued. Over the next five years approximately 50 integrated and specialized bachelor's degrees will be developed and phased in as time and resources make it feasible. Nineteen associate degrees will be offered.

ATHLETICS WILL REMAIN RICKS COLLEGE VIKINGS FOR FINAL YEAR

The school may be known as Brigham Young University-Idaho, but its athletic teams will continue to be called the Ricks College Vikings during their last season of play. Athletic Director Garth Hall says BYU-Idaho will retain the Ricks College Viking name as a tribute to the college, coaches and players that produced successful athletic teams. He says, "Student athletes and coaches felt it was important to retain the name for one last year. They wanted to compete as Ricks College."

Besides the emotional attachment to Ricks, it would have been expensive to change names on uniforms for one final year of play, Hall adds.

The football season will kick off Sept. 1 at 7 p.m. at Viking Stadium against Pima Community College from Tucson, Ariz. The volleyball season will begin Sept. 5 at home at 7 p.m. against Colorado Northwestern Community College.

A gala evening of paying tribute to athletics at Ricks College is planned for Friday, March 1, in the Hart Auditorium. Each sport will be honored and current and former athletes, coaches, administrators and staff will be recognized for their contributions. There will be awards and entertainment and the general public will be invited to participate. "March 1 of next year will be an evening you won't forget," Hall says. "We'll honor each sport that has been a part of Ricks College athletics. We'll reminisce, share stories and pictures from the past . . . in short, it will be a gala night to enjoy."

Additionally, as each sport concludes competition, former athletes and coaches will be invited to return to campus and will be recognized at the last home game or meet.

FACULTY RECEIVE ENCOURAGEMENT AS THEY BEGIN NEW YEAR

In two weeks students will enter the classroom for the Fall Semester at BYU-Idaho, and the faculty members are busy preparing for their arrival. Faculty met together Tuesday morning and were given instruction and encouragement. President David A. Bednar encouraged them to focus on the scholarship of learning and teaching. As the faculty plan their course work, they will also be rethinking university teaching, what is taught, and how students learn. Everyone at BYU-Idaho is a teacher. It was suggested that faculty be willing to learn from the staff and administrators. There should also be an open willingness to share insights with and from students.
