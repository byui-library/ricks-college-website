The Department of Welding Engineering Technology offers instructions in the techniques of welding. Students are taught to recognize and understand the processes involved. Why do certain temperatures affect the welding process differently? What are the chemical, physical, or metallurgical rules behind each process? Intelligent decision making is taught in terms of material usage and of the variables in processing. Jobs are always available for people who understand this type of information in addition to their actual skills.

Students interested in Welding Engineering as a major should see the Engi-neering section of this catalog and consult the Welding advisor.

Associate in Welding Engineering Technology (110)

Accredited by the Technology Accreditation Commission of the Accreditation Board for Engineering and Technology

This Associate Degree in Welding Engineering Technology is designed as a transfer program. It provides hands-on training in the Welding processes and skills, leading to Welder Certification. This program also provides a solid foundation in Welding Theory, Metallurgy, Welding Examination, Quality Control, Math, Physics, Chemistry and Computers.

Certificate in Welding Technology

This program is for the student who wants to enter the welding industry well trained and ready for this challenging and rewarding career. This course is hands-on and labor intensive. You will receive instruction and extensive training using the latest weldingtechnics on modern, up-to-date welding equipment. You will be trained in SMAW, GTAW, GMAW, and FCAW. This program also includes robotic welding, automatic thermal cutting, repair and maintenance welding.

Our goals include having you certified in pipe and plate and able to fit and weld in all positions.

For the best sequence and successful completion of the certificate program register as listed below. For students interested in running their own business we recommend a fourth semester of 15-18 credits with classes taken from the suggested classes for “proprietors list”.

Course Descriptions

Weld 105 Basic Welding 3-4
Two hours lecture, 7 hours lab per week
Lab Fee: $20 to partially cover material costs
A general Welding course which includes Oxyacetylene welding, Oxyfuel Cutting (OFC), Shielded Metal Arc welding (SMAW), Gas Metal Arc welding (GMAW), Flux Core Arc welding (FCAW), Carbon Arc Cutting (CAC) in the flat and horizontal positions and includes the safety procedures, electrode identification and focuses on theory and practice. Four credits for flat and horizontal position with three credits for flat only. (Fall and Winter)

Weld 117 Repair and Maintenance Welding 2
Prerequisite: Weld 105 or consent of instructor
One hour lecture; two hours lab/week
Lab Fee: $20 to partially cover material costs
Welding techniques, principles, theory, and practices used in the repair and maintenance of equipment. Includes brazing, bronze welding, arc welding, carbon arc gouging, flux core arc welding, and surfacing and its alloys. The study of the physical and mechanical properties, as well as chemical composition of the materials to be repaired will be included. (Winter)

Weld 123 Advanced SMAW, FCAW, and GMAW 3
Prerequisite: Weld 105 or concurrent enrollment
Two hours lecture; four hours lab/week
Lab Fee: $20 to partially cover material costs
Advanced pre-employment training which emphasizes developing manipulative proficiency in the use of SMAW, FCAW, and GMAW in the vertical and overhead positions in light and heavy gauge plate for the welding industry. (Fall)

Weld 127 GTA Welding CS/SS 4
Prerequisite: Weld 123 or permission of instructor
Two hours lecture; four hours lab/week
Lab Fee: $20 to partially cover material costs
Gas Tungsten Arc welding of carbon steel, stainless steel, aluminum and other alloys (titanium, nickel alloys and magnesium and copper used in the welding industry). Includes the study of their physical and mechanical properties and chemical compositions and weldability. Welding will be done in all positions. (Winter)

Weld 210 Blue Print Reading and Sketching for Welders 2
Two hours lecture; one hour lab/week
Sketching, blue print and welding symbol interpretation, plate and pipe layout measurement, bills of materials, dimensioning, notes and specifications, and abbreviations used in fabrication.

Weld 218 Welding Fabrication 2-3
Prerequisite: Weld 105
Two hours lecture; four hours lab/week
Lab Fee: $20 to partially cover material costs
The use and application of equipment, tools, fasteners, and processes used in fabrication practices in the welding industry. Interpretation of plans for the actual fabrication of small projects. Techniques for layout, fitting, squaring, tacking, and fabricating will be developed. (Winter)

Weld 221 Advanced Fabrication 2-3
Prerequisite: Weld 105, Weld 218
Two hours lecture; four hours lab/week
Lab Fee: $20 to partially cover material costs
Advanced pre-employment training used in the fabrication industry. The interpretation of drawing and symbols for the actual fabrication of large projects. Techniques for layout, fitting, squaring, tacking, welding, and fabricating will be practiced. (Fall)

Weld 225 Pipe and Tube Welding 4
Prerequisite: Weld 105, 123 127 or concurrent enrollment
Two hours lecture; six hours lab/week
Lab Fee: $20 to partially cover material costs
A variety of material and configurations using SMAW, GTAW and FCAW processes on subcritical pipe welding, critical pipe welding (pressure and power systems, cross country transmission pipeline) and water transmission pipe. The welding will be accomplished in the 1g, 2g, 5g, 6g, and 6gR positions using carbon steel, stainless steel, and aluminum pipe. (Fall)

Weld 229 Certification and Code Welding 3
Prerequisite: Weld 105, Weld 123, Weld 127, Weld 225 or concurrent enrollment
Two hours lecture; four hours lab/week
Lab Fee: $20 to partially cover material costs
Welding to AWS, API, and ASME codes to meet the entry level employment standards for Code work. Training in the procedures using SMAW, GTAW, GMAW, and FCAW processes for qualification of welders in all positions on plate, pipe and rebar. May be taken more than once. A qualification test will be given at end of course by a qualified Certified Welding Testing Lab. (Fall)

Weld 250 Welding Equipment Maintenance and Service 2
Two hours lecture; one hour lab/week
A study of the theory, application, and practices used in maintenance of welding equipment used in the Welding industry. (Fall)

Weld 254 Pipe Fitting and Cutting 2
Prerequisite: Weld 105
Two hours lecture; two hours lab/week
Lab Fee: $20 to partially cover material costs
Pipe fitting, measurements, patterns, marking, and layout tool use needed in the pipe welding industry. Techniques of fitting, layout, cutting saddles, and TKY branches will be practiced. (Winter)

Weld 256 Automated Welding Processes 3
Prerequisite: Weld 105, 123, 127, 210 or concurrent enrollment
Two hours lecture; two hours lab/week
A survey of the automated welding processes and their applications used within today’s welding industry. (Panograph, Robotics, Sub-Arc, Etc.) Field trips and hands-on learning will be used. (Winter)

Weld 258 Non-Destructive Testing and Inspection 2
Prerequisite: Weld 105, 123, 127, 225 or concurrent enrollment
Two hours lecture; two hours lab/week
Theory and application of non-destructive testing systems. Techniques for quality control. Includes magnetic particle, liquid penetrant, leak, visual and ultrasonic testing. (Fall)

Weld 290 Special Projects 1-3
Prerequisite: Consent of the Department Chair

Weld 298 Welding Technology Work Experience 1-3
Prerequisite: Consent of the Department Chair
Work experience with selected welding firms. Work period may be arranged during regular semesters. An acceptable written report on the organization and the work done will be required.
