COURSE DESCRIPTION

English 250 is a literature appreciation course. We will read a wide variety of poetry, fiction, and drama, and a novel you will read on your own. This course depends mostly on discussion, not lecture, so students may read more than the class discussion may cover. Though not designated as a writing course, you will log entries in a Journal for each assigned reading.

REQUIRED TEXTS

The Bedford Brief Introduction to Literature, Fourth Edition, Michael Meyer; a folder or small binder, a double-sided high density computer disk. As a college student, you are also encouraged to have a good grammar handbook, a small stapler, and a hole punch.

WHY READ LITERATURE?

"In the arms of language children will discover the family of humanity. They will learn what has gone before, and they will learn what is left to be done. In language they will learn to laugh, and to grieve, and to be consoled in their grief, and to console others. In language, they will discover who they are." Tobias Wolff

"Literature adds to reality, it does not simply describe it. It enriches the necessary competencies that daily life require and provides; and in this respect, it irrigates the deserts that our lives have already become." C.S. Lewis

"Fiction is like a spider's web, attached ever so lightly perhaps, but still attached to life at all four corners." Virginia Woolf

ATTENDANCE

Attendance counts. Most class sessions include a quiz or in class activity. Quizzes and activities cannot be made up. Lectures are not repeated.

READING JOURNAL

Your thoughtful journal responses will provide the basis for class discussions. Plan on three entries per week, written in preparation for class. Most often I will assign the type of entry: Dialogue Entry, Formalist Analyses, or Reader Response. Journal entries must be typed and will be turned in twice, at Midterm and Finals.

NOVEL REVIEW

You will read one novel of your choice, then write a report which considers some of the literary aspects of the novel. You will briefly report on your novel to a discussion group.

DRAMATIC SCENE

Each class member will act in and/or direct a scene from a play we read in class. Each group member will do a self evaluation, and a group evaluation.

EXAMS

1. Quizzes on reading assignments (5-10 points each).

2. Three exams (one on each unit, 100 points each).

GRADES

Journal 30%

Novel Review (paper and oral report) 10%

Exams 40% (4 @ 10%)

Dramatic Scene 8%

Poetry Reading 4%

Attendance, participation, quizzes 8%

Grades are determined on a strict percentage: 94+ = A, 90-93 = A-, 88-89 = B+,

84-87 = B, 80-83 = B, etc.

REASONABLE ACCOMMODATION

In compliance with applicable disability law, qualified students with a disability may be entitled to 'reasonable accommodation.' It is the student's responsibility to disclose to the teacher any special need she/he may have before the end of the first week of class.
