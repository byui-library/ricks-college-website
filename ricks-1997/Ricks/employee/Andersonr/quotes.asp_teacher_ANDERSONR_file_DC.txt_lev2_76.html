Temple Covenants-President James E. Faust
Every kind of Church council has at its focus the sacred work of bringing souls to the Lord.
President James E. Fausthas said, "We must do all in our power to ensure that no member of the Church departs this earth without having the necessary ordinances and covenants of the temple ... As we place the Savior at the center of our lives, develop greater love for others, recieve protection from evil influences, and obtain spiritual strength, happiness, peace of mind, and eternal life. (Ballard, Counseling with our Councils, p. 132.)
Definition of Councils-M. Russell Ballard
A council is a democracy of participation ... It should feature free and open discussion and clear, concise communication ... Come together in a spirit of loving cooperation ... We must be one ... Reasoning together ... Councils should always allow time for discussion and consideration of differing points of view ... There should be ownership. (Ballard, Counseling with our Councils)
Spiritual Synergism-M. Russell Ballard
Scientists call this process synergism, defined as a "united action of different agents of organismsproducing a greater effect than the sum of the various individual actions ... Individually we are weak, but togetherwe are strong. (Ballard, Counseling with our Councils, p. 10-11.)

Roles of Men and Women-M. Russell Ballard
Heavenly Father assigned different responsibilities in mortality to men and women. To His sons He gave the responsibility of fatherhood, and to His daughters He gave the responsibilities of motherhood ... God has revealed through His prophets that men are to recieve the priesthood, to become fathers, and with gentleness and pure, unfeigned love to lead and nurturetheir families in righeousness, taking for their pattern the way the Savior leads the Church. Men have also been given the responsibility for providing for the temporal and physical needs of the family. Women have the ability to bring children into the world and have been given the primary role and opportunity to lead, nurture, and teach their little ones in a loving, safe, and spiritual enviroment. In this divinely sanctioned partnership, husbands and wives work together. Why this pattern is the approved pattern is not entirly clear. The Lord has chosen to reveal only His will on the matteer, not His reasonong. Indeed, the reasons are unimportant as far as we are concerned, because the issue is not open to debate. Consensus and public opinion are irrelevant to a disscussionof the doctrine of God, because it is mandated through revelation, not legislation or negotiation ... It is an issue of faith --nothing more, nothing less. (Ballard, Counseling with our COuncils, pp. 55-57.)
The Purpose of Disciplinary Councils
Members sometimes ask why Church disciplinary councils are held. The purpose is threefold:
1. To save the transgressor.
2. To protect the innocent.
3. To safeguard the Church's purity, integrity, and good name.
Decisions of Disciplinary Councils
The council can reach one of four dicisions: 1. no action, 2. formal probation, 3. disfellowshipment, or 4. excommunication.
The council takes into consideration many factors, such as whether temple or marriage covenants have been violated; whether a position of trust or authority has been abused; the repetition, seriousness, and magnitude of the transgression; the age, maturity, and experience of the transgressor; the intrests of innocent family members; the time between transgression and confession; whether or not confession was voluntary; and evidence of repentance. (Ballard, Counseling with our Councils, p. 138-141.)
Procrastinated Repentance-Robert L. Simpson
The adversary places fear in the heart of the transgressor that makes it so difficult for him to do what needs to be done; and in the words of James E. Talmage, "As the time of repentance is procrastinated, the ability to repent grows weaker; neglect of oppurtunity in holy things develops inability." (Articles of Faith, p. 114.)
This simply means that doing what needs to be done will never be easier than right now. As in all other paths and guidposts that have been provided for us to achieve our eternal destiny of exaltation, there are no shortcuts ... (Robert L. Simpson, CR, April 1972, p. 32.)
The Things That Matter-The Leadership Training Emphasis Document-Russel M. Ballard
FAMILIES: Teach the preeminence of the home and family as the basic organizational unit of the Church. Encourage each family member, parents and children, to study scriptures, pray regularly, and follow the example of the Savior in all things.
ADULTS: Encourage each adult to be worthy to recieve the ordinances of the temple. Teach all adults to identify their ancestors and perform sacred temple ordinances for them.
YOUTH: Help prepare each young man to recieve the Melchizdek Priesthood to recieve the ordinances of the temple, and to be worthy to serve a full-time mission. Help prepare each young woman to be worthy to make and keep sacred covenants and recieve the ordinances of the temple.
ALL MEMBERS: Leaders, members, and stake and full time missionaries work cooperatively in a balanced effort to help convert, retain, and activate our Heavenly Father's children. Teach members to provide for themselves, their families, and the poor and needy in the Lord's way.
(Ballard, Counseling with our Councils, p. 76-77.)
An Interview with the Savior- President David O. McKay
In 1965, President David 0. McKay made the following statement to a group of Church employees:
"Let me assure you, Brethren, that some day you will have a personal priesthood interview with the Savior himself. If you are interested, I will tell you the order in which he will ask you to account for your earthly responsibilities.
"First, he will request an accountability report about your relationship with your wife. Have you actively been engaged in making her happy and ensuring that her needs have been met as an individual?
"Second, he will want an accountability report about each of your children individually. He will not affempt to have this for simply a family stewardship but will request information about your relationship to each and every child.
"Third, he will want to know what you personally have done with the talents you were given in the preexistence.
"Fourth, he will want a summary of your activity in your Church assignments. He will not be necessarily interested in what assignment you have had, for in his eyes the home teacher and a mission president are probably equals, but he will request a summary of how you have been of service to your fellow man in your Church assignments.
"Fifth, he will have no interest in how you earned your living but if you were honest in all your dealings.
"Sixth, he will ask for an accountability on what you have done to contribute in a positive manner to your community, state, country, and the world." (Reported by Cloyd Hofheins in a talk to the seventies quorum of Provo Utah Oak Hills Stake, May 16, 1982.)
The Purpose of Primary Document-M. Russell Ballard
The purpose of primary is to teach children the gospel of Jesus Christ and help them learn to live it. The objectives of Primary are to teach children that they are children of God and that Heavenly Father and Jesus Christ love them; help children learn to love Heavenly Father and Jesus Christ; help children prepare to be baptized, to recieve the Holy Ghost, and to keep their baptismal covenants; help children grow in their understanding of the gospel plan and provide oppotunitiesfor them to live gospel principles; help boys prepare to recieve the priesthood and be worthy to use this power to bless and serve others; [and] help girls prepare to be righteous young women, understand the blessings of the priesthood asd the temple, and serve others. (Ballard, Counseling with our Councils, p. 103.)
Purpose Of Relief Society Document-M. Russell Ballard
The purpose of Relief Society is to help women and families come unto Christ and to assist the priesthood quorums in achieving the mission of the Church; to help sisters build personal testimony, develop and excercise charity, strengthen their families, enjoy a unified sisterhood, and participate fully in the blessings of the temple while working to bless and lift the lives of the individual women of the ward. (Ballard, Counseling with our Councils, p. 104.)
Purpose Of Young Woman Document-M. Russell Ballard
The purpose of Young Women is to help prepare each young woman to be worthy to make and keep sacred covenants and recieve the ordinances of the temple ... We are daughters of our Heavenly Father who loves us, and we love him. We will stand as witnesses of God at all times and in all things, and in all places ... as we strive to live the Young Woman Values, which are: faith, divine nature, individual worth, knowledge, choice and accountability, good works, and integrity. We believe as we come to accept and act upon these values, we will be prepared to make and keep sacred covenants, recieve the blessings of the templ, and enjoy the blessings of exaltation. (Ballard, Counseling with our Councils, p. 104.)
The Mission Of The Aaronic Priesthood
The mission of the Aaronic Priesthood is to help each young man become converted to the gospel of Jesus Christ and live by its teachings; magnify priesthood callings; give meaningful service; prepare to recieve the Melchizedek Priesthood; commit to , worthily prepare for, and serve an honorable full-time mission; [and] live worthy to recieve temple covenants and prepare to become a worthy husband and father. (Ballard, Counseling with our Councils, p. 105.)
Purpose Of The Melchizedek-M. Russell Ballard
The Melchizedek Priesthood is responsible for the spiritual and temporal welfare of the men over whom they preside. They are charged to follow the Savior's example of righteous leadership ... When priesthood leaders lead as the Savior did,, they should help members develop greater love for others; share the gospel, do temple and family work, serve without thought of reward, assist the poor and needy, and minister to the lonely and distressed; recieve ordinances and make covenants with the Lord that will lead to eternal life; obey the commandments; become more humble, repentant, and forgiving; pray and study the scriptures daily; attend church meetings regularlyand partke of the sacrament worthily; achieve spiritual, emotional, and temporal self-relience; [and] attend the temple regularly. (Ballard, Counseling with our Councils, p. 105.)
