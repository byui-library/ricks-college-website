Santorini
audio
for L. Gee

 

 

 

 

 

 

 
Ancient Lady, Santorini,
Daughter of Violence and Heat,
Millions of years ago, you beat
Your way through boiling sea
And greeted air and sun.

You won the hot fight,
Rolling layer upon layer,
Building bright land,
Taking a stand
For deliberate living.

Legends whisper your dark past
When 3,600 years ago
Atlantis was buried and burned
By volcanic blasts.
Yet today, although you serenely speak,
Nothing about your freedom cry is meek.
You are Greek.

The warm, wet embrace
Of a soft, long-fingered, Aegean Ocean
Traces your firm, chaste body.

White houses, randomly rectangular,
With celestial-blue roofs and shutters,
Press against one another
Like comrades at a soccer game.

People shuffle through narrow streets
Having time
To know where they are going
To know what they want to do.

Some on docile donkeys loosely sit,
Shifting with the sway,
Enjoying a land where the clocks are
stuffed away.

Dusk brings a shimmering gleam
On the glassy ocean.
In the west, the slipping sun
Melts the heart's commotion
And starts the red horizon burning.

Brown, rocky Santorini,
When my own clock is pushed away,
I will come again and stay
With my wife.

Then, after an unhurried, chocolate day
Of sounds, sights, food, and fun,
We'll watch the setting sun.

When that is done,
Like amorous Greeks
We'll enjoy the rich, soft sheets
In our perfectly white hotel.
