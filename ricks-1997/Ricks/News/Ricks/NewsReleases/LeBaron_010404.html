Ricks College News Release
April 5, 2001 

Devotional: E. Dale LeBaron

By TRENT TOONE
Ricks College News Service

REXBURG, Idaho — Insights into the growth of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints in Africa were shared at Tuesday’s weekly Ricks College devotional.

E. Dale LeBaron, former mission president in Africa and a faculty member at Brigham Young University in Provo, Utah, said, "There is much that we can learn from our African pioneers."

During the 1950s church leaders were made aware of many devout Christians desiring church literature and baptism. The church leaders replied to the Africans that the time was not at hand. The people had to wait as the church made efforts to obtain entrance into various countries.

Then on June 9, 1978, church President Spencer W. Kimball announced that priesthood blessings could be given to all worthy male members, an announcement that was well received in Africa.

"The flood gates were now open for the gospel to go to Africa and to African ancestors," he said. "When the announcement came, my wife and I were presiding over the only mission in the continent of Africa in South Africa. The announcement brought feelings and stirrings impossible to describe."

LeBaron shared a brief history of one of the first baptized converts in Nigeria, a man named Anthony O’Binna.

O’Binna is the first black African branch president and witnessed first-hand many pioneering efforts in Nigeria. As the church was being established, it was recorded that in a 24-hour period more than 149 people were baptized. LeBaron said in the following years, West Africa became home to more than 1,700 members and 35 branches. About 9½ years later, Elder Neal A. Maxwell organized the first stake there.

Another pioneer is Joseph William Billy Johnson. He first read the Book of Mormon in 1964. Through many unique spiritual experiences, Johnson became a leader in the church as it was established in Ghana. He was the first branch president in Ghana and served many missions.

Another pioneer is named Moses Mahlangu, a preacher from Soweto, South Africa. LeBaron himself interviewed Mahlangu for baptism. He said it was an experience that he would never forget.

"To every question, I received the same answer: ‘I have been keeping that commandment for 14 years.’ I was deeply humbled in the presence of this great pioneer," LeBaron said.

The last pioneer mentioned by LeBaron is Julius Kasue, a man who helped the church grow in Kenya. His story was similar to the others as he was read the Book of Mormon, requested immediate baptism and was instrumental in helping others to become members.

In the last 22 years, African membership has jumped from 8,000 to 150,0000 and during the year 2000 sacrament meeting attendance in the West Africa Area was 54 percent, second only to the Utah South Area.

"This is impressive when we consider that most Africans have to walk a considerable distance to their meetings," LeBaron said.

In conclusion, LeBaron said, "We are truly brothers and sisters through our spirit’s birth and through our spiritual rebirth. May we always radiate pure love toward others regardless or race or culture as our Heavenly Father and the Savior do."

Next Tuesday’s speaker will be Elaine Jack, a former General Relief Society President in the LDS Church and currently a General Young Women’s counselor.

She will speak at 2 p.m. in the Hart Auditorium. Devotionals are broadcast live and repeated at 9 p.m. on KRIC-FM.
