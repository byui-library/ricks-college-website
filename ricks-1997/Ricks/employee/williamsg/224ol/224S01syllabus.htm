Welcome

Welcome to the Semester On-line version of Doctrine and Covenants 224. I am excited to participate with you in this experience. Dr. Keith Perkins, Emeritus Professor at BYU, wrote the course and I have been asked to monitor the class on this campus.

This course is unique and will prove to be very rewarding. First, it is about a unique book, the Doctrine and Covenants, which differs in some ways from all other scriptures. It is a book compiled not only for our day and time, but also recorded by prophets of this dispensation. It contains the word and will of the Lord for us in these last days. It includes many truths and doctrines which are not as completely revealed in other scripture. Second, while other books of scripture intermix some history with the doctrine, this is not the case with the Doctrine and Covenants. For this reason, at the beginning of each section we will describe the background, or what brought about this revelation. Without that background you will not fully understand the revelation. In addition, when we know the questions that were asked by the Prophet Joseph Smith and others it helps us better understand the answers.

As pointed out in D&C 88:118, "Seek learning, even by study and also by faith." You will find that you will get the most out of this course when you use both methods of learning. In addition, the Lord has commanded us to study items out in our minds and then ask if they are right. He will confirm or invalidate the answer we have arrived at (D&C 9:7-9). By this method we can learn "the truth of all things" (Moro. 10:5).

Course Objectives

After completing this course and pondering and praying about these great revelations, you should be able to do the following:

1. Understand that the Lord Jesus Christ is your personal Savior and Redeemer. As you gain a better understanding of the greatest Being to ever grace this earth, you will gain a better comprehension of His atonement. You will also learn what part repentance plays in activating the atonement fully in your life, as you seek for a remission of your sins.

2. Better understand and appreciate the Prophet Joseph Smith's role in the restoration of the gospel of Jesus Christ upon the earth. You will see why in the Church we emphasize the importance of the mission of Joseph Smith. It was he who walked and talked with God and Christ. It was by revelation from them, through the Holy Ghost, the voice of God, and the ministry of angels that he gained the understanding of the doctrines Christ taught on earth. What we know about God the Father, his Son Jesus Christ, and the Holy Ghost in a large degree we learn from the Prophet Joseph Smith.

3. Better understand the plan of salvation, or the great plan of happiness. If you know who you really are--a child of God--where you came from, why you are here, and where you are going, you will better see your place in the scheme of things on this earth. Much of this knowledge is revealed in the Doctrine and Covenants.

4. Realize that the heavens are no longer sealed and that God has once more spoken to living prophets again on this earth, especially as recorded in the Doctrine and Covenants. Then you will understand that "whether by mine own voice or by the voice of my servants, it is the same," and "whatsoever they shall speak when moved upon by the Holy Ghost shall be scripture, shall be the will of the Lord, shall be the mind of the Lord, shall be the word of the Lord, shall be the voice of the Lord, and the power of God unto salvation" (D&C 1:38; 68:4). That is why the Lord calls this "the only true and living Church upon the face of the whole earth" (D&C 1:30). It is alive because we continue to receive revelation by the means of the Holy Ghost.

5. Liken the scriptures unto yourself. As you do this, placing your name (where appropriate) in lieu of the names listed in the revelations, you will understand what the Lord is saying to you and how he wants you to live. Truly, when you read his words in the Doctrine and Covenants you will be able to hear his voice (D&C 18:34-36).

Lessons

Generally, each lesson in the course has four distinct parts: an introduction, lesson objectives (to guide your study), a "How to Proceed" section (steps to follow), and discussion material (background and commentary). These may be printed for future reference and study for assignments and the final exam.

The discussion material in each lesson is further divided into three parts: background, commentary, and thought questions. The background section will help you understand why the revelation came. The commentary, primarily from the prophets, seers, and revelators in this dispensation, will help you understand the material better and will clarify some of the scriptures you have read. The thought questions, which appear periodically throughout the discussion material, are designed to help you review the material and make it part of your understanding. Thought questions are for your own consideration. You do not need to write out the answers and submit them. These will help you prepare for the assignments. In additon to thought questions, there will be 'Discuss' questions. These questions will be posted on the discussion board located under the COMMUNICATION icon. Take the opportunity to integrate the facts you have learned with some applications for our times.

Of course, the greatest teacher of all is the Holy Ghost. As you search and ponder what you have read, you will gain a clearer understanding of how it applies to your life. Revelation is very personal and is available to all.

Assignments

Although there are fifteen lessons in this course, you will submit only eight assignments for grading (for lessons 1,3,5,7 9,11,13,15). The assignments consist of multiple-choice, true-false, and matching questions and constitute 46 percent of your grade for the course. Assignments are open book and can be printed and studied to prepare for the final exam. All assignments are graded automatically and you will receive immediate feedback on any questions missed. Remember, the assignments must be completed by NOON on the due date above in order for you to receive points! You may complete the assignments faster than the schedule -- this is even encouraged! Once you have competed all fifteen lessons and taken the assignments, you may then take the Final Exam.

Course Evaluation

Final Exam

At the conclusion of the course, you will take a closed book and timed [90 minute] final examination that consists of 122 multiple choice, matching, and true-false questions--similar in format to the submitted assignments. You can learn more about the final in the section of this course entitled "How to Prepare for the Final Exam." The final exam is worth 54 percent of your course grade. You can take the Final Exam once you have competed all fifteen lessons and taken the assignments. Further information concerning the Final Exam will be given as we approach the end of the course.
