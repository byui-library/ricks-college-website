Albanian student named Woman of the Year

by Emily Toy
TOY01001@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Evis Farka, a sophomore from Tirana, Albania, receives the 2002 Woman of the Year award from Robert Wilkes, Student Life Vice President.. The awards ceremony took place Thursday in the Taylor building. Sara Burt / Scroll
Evis Farka, a sophomore from Tirana, Albania, representing the International Student Association was selected as the 2002 Woman of the Year.

Students, mothers, faculty and administration gathered in the Taylor overflow Wednesday for the announcement.

“We’ve chosen a [person] to symbolize the good things we believe in and stand for,” Student Life Vice President, Robert Wilkes, said just before the announcement.

Farka is a psychology major, speaks English, Albanian and Italian and is learning French.

She has a desire to serve people, she said.

“I want to help people believe that life is beautiful, and that no matter what challenges they may have, they can still find happiness,” Farka said.

Farka has been a member of the Church since she was 14 years old, nearly as long as the LDS Church has been open in Albania. She was the first to receive the Young Womanhood Recognition Award in Albania.

Farka was originally leery of being nominated for Woman of the Year because she did not think the nomination would go anywhere, she said.

“I didn’t expect to win; I wasn’t even nervous,” Farka said.

Each club and organization on campus is given the opportunity to nominate a woman for the honor. Each of these nominees goes through an interviewing process, and the nominees are narrowed to 20 semi-finalists. These semi-finalists are again interviewed, and the female student population votes on the semi-finalists. This narrows the competition to six finalists.

Each of the girls represents the exemplary women of campus, Susan Bednar said.

“I wasn’t chosen because I am the best; I know that. I am moved and flattered [by the honor],”Evis Farka said in response to being named BYU-Idaho 2002 Woman of the Year. Scott Cameron / Scroll
The finalists are judged on their interviews, involvement, service and general example, Morgan Anne Green, Associated Women Students executive council member, said.

“I hope that no one will believe I won because I am better than other people,” Farka said. “I wasn’t chosen because I am the best; I know that. I am moved and flattered [by the honor].”

Although the honor is a nice distinction for the young women involved and they are worthy of the honor, perhaps it is not all about them but about the things and values the young woman symbolizes, Wilkes said.

“The lives of our people must become the only meaningful expression of our faith. The people are the symbol of our faith,” Wilkes said, quoted President Gordon B. Hinckley.

As followers of Jesus Christ, we can’t do some things without tarnishing his image, and we can’t do good without brightening it, Wilkes said.

“Our lives must be a symbol of him,” Wilkes said. “It is that simple and that profound. We should never forget it.”

“We like this opportunity we have to reverence womanhood,” President Bednar said.

The Woman of the Year not only represents herself and her personality but an entire generation, President David Bednar said.

“One of the blessings in partaking of this experience was getting to know the wonderful girls,” Farka said. “I am glad I got to meet them and develop a simple friendship with them. I will always remember what wonderful people they are, and I want to thank the girls for letting me be their friend.”
