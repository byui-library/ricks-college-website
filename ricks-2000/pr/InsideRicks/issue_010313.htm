RICKS HONORS SEVEN WITH ALUMNI AWARDS

A university professor in California, owners of a trucking company in Idaho, an agriculture educator in Maryland, a nursing specialist in New York City, a sculptor in Utah, and a high school teacher in Idaho were honored this month by the Ricks College Alumni Association at two luncheons on campus.

Dr. Thomas C. Timmreck, who graduated from Ricks in 1968, was presented the Distinguished Alumni Award for Professional Accomplishment. He is in his 16th year of teaching public health and health administration at California State University in San Bernardino. He is the author of the first major reference book on health care administration, now in its third edition, and has published numerous professional articles related to health care, including public health, behavioral health and gerontology.

Doug Andrus, a Ricks graduate in 1964, and his wife, the former Deanna Daw, a nursing graduate of 1961, were presented the Distinguished Alumni Award for Humanitarian Service. The couple operate Doug Andrus Distributing, a nationwide trucking company, out of Idaho Falls, Idaho. They also promote wholesome entertainment acts that include some of their 13 children, known as the Andrus Sisters. Through family-owned rental units they have helped the financially disabled. Through their trucking company they have provided transportation for food and clothing for overseas relief efforts. They also have organized benefit dinners and auctions to help those in need.

Dale Johnson, a Ricks graduate in 1981, was named one of two Outstanding Young Alumni. He provides educational programs in farm management, business planning, financial analysis, and computer applications for agribusinesses through the University of Maryland. He organizes workshops and seminars and has traveled abroad to develop dairies Moldova, advised state farms in Poland, and instructed the faculties of two agricultural colleges in former Soviet-bloc countries.

Debra Bingham, a nursing program graduate from Ricks in 1978, was also named as recipient of the Outstanding Young Alumni award. She is a registered nurse and perinatal nurse specialist at St. Luke's-Roosevelt Hospital Center in New York City. She has worked in the New York City area since the mid-80s, acquiring her four-year nursing degree from the University of the State of New York, and adding a master's degree in perinatal nursing from Columbia University in 1990.

Gary Price, a sculptor from Springville, Utah, was honored with the Eliza R. Snow Award for the Performing and Fine Arts. He left Ricks College in 1974 and graduated from the University of Utah. He sold his first sculpture in 1980. About six years later he began sculpting game birds. Since then he has concentrated on contemporary themes. His works can be found throughout the United States, including Ricks College, and in Hong Kong and Iceland, among other divergent places.

Charlotte Corgatelli Arnold, principal of Idaho's Shelley High School since 1991, was honored with the Outstanding Educator Award. She graduated from Ricks College in 1964 and began her teaching career in California four years later. She later returned to Idaho and began teaching at Shelley High School in 1979. A widow, she makes her home in Ammon, Idaho, and is currently pursuing a doctorate in educational leadership at Idaho State University.

 

RICKS COLLEGE ATHLETES ARE ACADEMIC NATIONAL CHAMPIONS

Ricks College athletes have once again proven themselves in the classroom, as well as on the field of competition. The NJCAA announced today that Ricks College has won Academic National Championships in Women's Cross Country, Men's Cross Country, and Football. The awards are based on cumulative GPA's.

In Women's Cross Country, the Lady Vikings earned a 3.75 GPA as a team. Runners Kathrine Anderson of Fruit Heights, Utah; Angie Benson of Orem, Utah; and Tasha Ellis of Hamer, Idaho; all posted GPA's of 4.0.

The Mens' Cross Country team earned a team GPA of 3.47, led by Josh Gunn of Provo, Utah, with a 3.94 GPA. This marks the second year in a row that the men's team has won the Academic National Championship.

The Viking football team was the only NJCAA football team to carry a team GPA over 3.00. Ricks came in at 3.11, led by Pat Williams of Bountiful, Utah, at 3.927, and David Cousins of Idaho Falls at 3.925. Ricks football was Academic National Champion last season as well.

SACRED MUSIC SERIES PREMIERES 'THE PASSION AND THE PROMISE'

The Ricks College Sacred Music Series will premiere a newly commissioned oratorio by American composer Daniel E. Gawthrop. Every other year Ricks College commissions a significant new composition of sacred music. "The Passion and The Promise of Our Lord Jesus Christ" utilizes scriptures in a musical setting to portray events in the Savior's life.

Conducted by Dr. Kevin Call, this year's oratorio features soloists Michael Belnap, Kristine Ciesinski, and Norman Bailey. The series has traditionally been performed in Utah and Idaho. This is first opportunity the college has had to share the experience of newly commissioned work with audiences in the Washington, D.C., area where Ricks musicians combine talents with Mormon Choir of Washington, D.C. and the Southern Virginia College Chamber Choir.

Tickets are available through the Ricks College Ticket Office at www.byui.edu/tickets or by calling 1-800-71-RICKS. Performances include Tuesday, April 3, in the Barrus Concert Hall, Ricks College at 7:00 p.m.; Thursday, April 5, at the National City Christian Church, Washington, D.C. at 8:00 p.m.; Friday, April 6, at Fairfax High School, Fairfax, Virginia, 8:00 p.m.; and Saturday, April 7, Tabernacle on Temple Square, Salt Lake City, Utah, 7:30 p.m. (No ticket required at the Tabernacle). There are five additional performances at Ricks College (Please see www.byui.edu/pr/performinggroups/sacredmusicseries.htm )

 

THREE HONORED AS EXEMPLARY EMPLOYEES

Three Ricks employees have been honored for their outstanding service. The Exemplary Employee award is given each semester to one administrator and two staff members selected for their contributions. The recipients for Winter Semester 2001 are Scott Hendricks, Leta Bates, and Michael Kunz. All three are past Ricks College students.

Hendricks is a lead custodian in Manwaring Center. He began working at Ricks in 1980. "I love working in Manwaring Center," he says. "I like getting up in the morning" -- a great attribute as his job starts at 3:30 a.m. Hendricks often speaks at youth conferences where he shares his story dealing with long-term affects of brain trauma caused by an accident at age six.

Bates has been employed at Ricks College as a clerk for Stores and Receiving since 1994. For Bates, the Ricks campus has strong family ties as her father Ted Erickson taught in the physics department and her father-in-law Orrin Bates taught psychology. "I love it up here," says Leta. "I am glad to be in a place where we all have the same values."

Kunz is the programming systems manager for Centralized Data Services. Kunz received a bachelor's degree in accounting from BYU with a minor in information systems. Since beginning to work at Ricks in 1987, he has had found his job to be "exciting and challenging." As he explores applications and determines if they will work in the college's system, he says there is solace in the scripture which advises: "And see that all these things are done in wisdom and order; for it is not requisite that a man should run faster than he has strength. And again, it is expedient that he should be diligent, that thereby he might win the prize; therefore, all things must be done in order" (Mosiah 4:27).
