Applicants entering the United States on a B visa are ineligible to attend CES schools.  

Instructions to the applicant:  All applicants who are not U.S. citizens or Permanent Residents must complete this form.  

The United States requires CES schools to verify international students' ability to finance the length of time necessary to pursue an education in the United States.  Evidence of financial ability, exclusive of travel expenses, may be furnished in one or a combination of the following ways:  

  • Proof of personal funds, including bank statements and tax returns.  

  • A contract of support from a parent or sponsor in the home country or U.S. who will pay education expenses.  (A bank statement or tax return is required to verify funds.)  

  • Proof of government loan.  

A realistic financial plan is required before admissions and scholarship consideration is given.  

When computing expenses, remember students holding F-1 (student) permits are not permitted to work off campus unless authorized by Immigration Services, and are allowed to work on campus only after completing one semester of studies.  Student employment cannot be used as part of your financial plan.  F-2 permit holders are not permitted to work under any circumstances.  

International students (excluding Canadians) entering the United States on an F-1 permit must deposit US $1,200.  This deposit is required of all new F-1 permit holders before issuance of the Form I-20, including those already in the United States.  The deposit belongs to the student and may be used toward the first semester's expenses.  If denied admission, a refund will be made by calling or writing Financial Services, Brigham Young University, D-151 ASB, PO Box 21128, Provo, Utah 84602-1128, telephone (802) 378-6643.  


Health Insurance

Full-time students must either enroll in a CES school Health Plan or provide verification of adequate insurance coverage.  The approximate cost of this insurance is $360 US for each year.
