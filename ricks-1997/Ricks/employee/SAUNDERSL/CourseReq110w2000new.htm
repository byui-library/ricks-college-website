Math 110 - Course Requirements — Winter 2000

Bro Larry Saunders, Romney 242, ext. 1466, Office Hours 11am Daily

Text: Precalculus and Trigonometry, 1997, Bittinger, Beecher, Ellenbogen, Penna

Either the TI-86 or the HP-G Graphing Calculator is required

Course Description: We will begin with a review of algebra centered on the complex numbers, functions and their graphs and solutions of linear and quadratic functions and inequalities. We then will examine polynomial, rational, and logarithmic functions. The course will conclude with a discussion of systems of equations and matrices, conic sections and the binomial theorem.

Course Outline: The chapters covered in the text are: review chapter, 1, 2, 3, 7, 8, and part of 9.

B- You the student will be expected to:

1. Memorize on the first day and say 4 or 5 times each day all semester:

"I seek to understand truth and teach it with clarity. As I listen and act quickly when moved by the Spirit and as I organize and teach properly each day . . . I answer my own questions."

--This is called a positive action statement (PAS), it includes four aspects: logic, detail, emotion, & overall perspective, you are to write one of your own within the next two weeks and repeat it all semester too.

      Group Structure:

    You will assist in selecting a partner the first day (if you are married select a partner of the same sex). As a partnership you will join with one other partnership to form a group of four. Each person will accept responsibility for one of the following group roles:

    Recorder Capture specialist, sees that each member has 100% of instructor's principles & structure

    Creator Expansion specialist, sees that each member has validations (examples) and applications

    Teacher Teaching specialist, sees that each member takes the opportunity to teach daily Evaluator Evaluation specialist, sees that each member writes and answers their own questions

    Course Requirements:

    1. A notebook containing for each lecture presented by your Instructor:

      a. 100% Capture of the Principles and Structure given by your Instructor.

      b. Insert within the instructor's notes 3 of your own examples (or more) for each concept and any connections plus a Real-life Application that helps you understand and want to use the concept(s) presented (identify your examples, connections and applications with a shaded triangle in the left margin).

      c. Include a list of personal questions with their answers, (title this portion Questions, and number each one) get in the habit of writing down your questions as you have them, and

      d. Write a paragraph concerning your daily teaching of the concepts, discuss how teaching each day has benefited your understanding, remember the PAS.

      For each lecture, each of the above parts is worth 10 points, for a total of 40 points per lecture.

      The Notebook may be typed and an equation editor used for a bonus of 25 points for the semester. If you choose to handwrite your notebook it must be very neat with all formulas and diagrams written as they are found in math textbooks, the same is true if you type your formulas, they must be written as found in math textbooks.

      Note: always keep your Notebook up to date, and always bring it to class.

      2. One additional Positive Action Statement for a habit or behavior you want to change. You should write this during the first two weeks of the course. Model it after the one you are asked to memorize and then two weeks before the class ends, report the benefits you have realized from memorizing both statements and reviewing them 4 or 5 times each day. (15 pts)

      3. Two or three one hour, in class, exams. (100 points each)

      4. Final exam. (150 points)

      5. Final Growth Report - a one page, typed, double spaced, carefully written report of your progress this semester in acquiring skills that will benefit you throughout your life. Have you come to understanding (have you received revelation?), do you see ways to benefit others with your knowledge? Include the grade you deserve and why, the rest of the page should support your claim with details. Rely heavily on your teaching paragraphs for details about mastery of specific concepts and teaching those concepts to others, and about the benefits gained from working with other students. Include comments about specific areas where you feel you made the greatest gains and why, and specific areas that still can be improved and how. (15 points)

      6. Daily report of homework (select a minimum of 5 textbook problems each day) and preparation (bring your notebook each day to verify). (1 point per class period, you must be present and prepared to get the point)

      Code of Honor is strictly complied with, any cheating on exams or plagiarizing on teaching reports will result in zeros for those assignments and could result in failure for the course.
