PROPOSED ACADEMIC PROGRAMS FOR BYU-IDAHO ANNOUNCED

A proposed list of baccalaureate and associate degrees to be offered by Brigham Young University-Idaho was announced November 15 by Ricks College President David A. Bednar. The president stressed that the proposed list of programs may be adjusted or refined, subject to a comprehensive accreditation evaluation by the Northwest Association of Schools and Colleges.

As Ricks transitions to a baccalaureate institution to be renamed BYU-Idaho, President Bednar explained the new university will offer both integrated and specialized baccalaureate degrees. Integrated baccalaureate degrees will require a maximum of 45 hours in the principal area of study while specialized baccalaureate degrees will require a maximum of 70 hours. Integrated degrees will require students to study in multiple disciplines and will a provide broad-based preparation for employment and life.

Beginning in the fall of 2001, the university anticipates offering upper-division classes leading to integrated baccalaureate degrees in accounting, English, history and recreation education, along with specialized degrees in business management, elementary education, interior design and nursing.

By 2002, the university expects to begin offering upper-division, secondary education programs in the areas of biology, English, foreign language, history, math, music, physical science, social studies and theater. Other secondary-education programs to be added later will be communications, consumer economics and economics.

While the programs and time line are subject to change, additional programs are scheduled to be added through 2005. BYU-Idaho will be a two-tiered institution which will offer specialized associate degrees. BYU-Idaho is expected to experience a modest increase in the size of the student body during the next few years. Tuition will be raised to $1,240 per semester. CROSS COUNTRY DYNASTY KEEPS GROWING

It takes continued achievement to make a dynasty grow and that is exactly what is happening with the Ricks College cross country program. On November 18, the Ricks women's team captured a sixth consecutive National Junior College Athletic Association championship at Levelland, Texas, some 30 miles west of Lubbock. The women won with a team score of 28 points, the fewest points ever for Ricks in the national finals of a sport where the lowest score counts the most. Utah Valley State College was second with 61points. The Ricks women also have the national individual champion in Kristen Ogden, a sophomore from Chico, Calif.

The men ran to a second consecutive national team title with 43 points, the fewest points ever for a Ricks men's team in the national finals. Central Arizona College was second with 71 points. The Ricks men, just like the women's team, can also claim the national individual champion in Jeff Davidson, a freshman from Burley.

The two team wins, combined with the two individual champions, are another first for the national junior college cross country finals. It is results like these from which the term "dynasty" is coined. (See: http://www.byui.edu/Ricks/Athletics/CrossCountry/CrossC.htm)


RICKS SET TO PLAY IN 14TH ANNUAL REAL DAIRY BOWL

The Ricks College football team will make its 14th appearance at the annual Real Dairy Bowl Friday, Dec. 1, in Holt Arena on the campus of Idaho State University in Pocatello, facing Snow College from Ephraim, Utah. The annual junior college gridiron classic is sponsored by the United Dairymen of Idaho. "We are pleased to offer fans in southeastern Idaho a chance to witness a rematch of a hard-fought Snow-Ricks game," said John Novosel, chairman of the bowl committee. "When these two teams meet, the outcome is always doubtful."

Tickets are on sale at Holt Arena and the Ricks College Ticket Office by calling 356-2230. A family pass is $20 for an immediate family. Single admission is $8 with a $2 discount with a Real Dairy seal. Children under 12 not attending with their family will be admitted for $4. (See http://www.isu.edu/real_dairy_bowl.htm or http://www.byui.edu/Ricks/Athletics/Football/Football.htm)

RAZING BEGINS ON SPORI BUILDING

Work has started on razing the Jacob Spori Building at Ricks College. R.E. Beck Construction of Idaho Falls submitted the low bid and was awarded the contract by Ricks College. It has until Dec. 22 to have the 28,000 square foot building removed, says Ricks Physical Plant Director Chuck Frost. (http://www.byui.edu/Ricks/News/Ricks/NewsReleases/spori_001109.htm)

Architectural planning for the new Spori Building has resumed again this month. The design process had been stalled while college officials discussed implications of Ricks becoming a four-year university. Construction of a new building should start in June with completion during the winter of 2002-03.
