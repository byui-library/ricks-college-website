Course Description: This is the first of a two semester series designed to give you a basic understanding of the structure and functions of the human body. This course is designed for students seeking a career in nursing or one of the many other professions in the allied health sciences. In this course we will learn the basic science necessary to understand the functioning of the human body. The basic chemistry of life and the functioning of the cell will be covered before we begin studying the various body systems. Body systems covered this semester include: the integumentary system, the muscular system, the skeletal system and the nervous system. There is a 2 hour lab accompanying this course in which there will be four main sections: Body tissues, bones, muscles, and the nervous system. Mastering the material in lab will require that you spend additional time outside of the regular lab periods, therefore you should expect to put in an additional 2-4 hours per week in lab.

Grading Policy:

  1. There will be six lecture exams, each worth 100 points. The exams be over material covered in lecture and selected material from the lab. The exams will consist of multiple choice, matching, fill in, short answer and essay type questions. If you miss an exam it may be made up within three days of the closing date, however, there will be a 10 point penalty for taking the exam late. If you do not make up a missed exam you will receive a 0 for that exam. It is your responsibility to inform me why you are unable to take the exam and to make arrangements to make up a missed exam. Being unprepared is not a valid reason for not taking an exam. Dates for exams will be announced in class.
  2. Since we all have an occasional bad day you may retake one exam, with the exception of exam #6. All retakes will be taken within one week of the closing date of the exam. The score for the retake will be the score recorded for the exam, even if it is lower than your original score.
  3. A final, comprehensive exam will be given during finals week. This exam will be worth 200 points.
  4. In lab there will be four units each worth 100 points. Unit #1 will be over the tissues, unit #2 will be over the anatomy of the skeletal system, unit #3 will be over the anatomy of the muscular system, and unit #4 will be over the anatomy of the nervous system. There will be quizzes each lab as well as an exam at the end of each unit. A comprehensive lab final will be offered for those who want to replace one of their lab unit exams scores. This exam will be given during finals week.
  5. There will be four vocabulary quizzes covering the prefixes, suffixes and combining forms found in the front inside cover of your book. Each quiz will be worth 15 points. There will be no make up for these quizzes.
  6. Extra credit (up to 15 points) can be earned by doing the "case study" discussed in class.
  7. Attendance will be taken in lab. Three or more absences from lab will result in a failing grade for the course. Attendance will not be taken in lecture, however, my experience has shown that there is a direct correlation between how well a student does on the exams and his attendance.
  8. There are approximately 1275 total points possible (1260 from exams and quizzes plus 15 extra credit). The highest cumulative score will be scaled to 100% and all other scores adjusted accordingly. Grades will then be assigned according to the following scale.

87-89=B+             75-79=C+             60-64=D+             Below 50=F

Study Guide

Chapter 1: The Human Organism

  1. Know the definition of anatomy and physiology.
  2. Be able to describe the different types of anatomy, i.e.; microscopic, gross, systemic anatomy, regional anatomy etc.
  3. Be able to describe the anatomical imaging techniques covered in class.
  4. Know the hierarchal organization of the body (chemical, organelle, cell, tissue, organ, organ system, organism)
  5. Know the four types of tissues found in the body.
  6. Know the definition of tissue, organ and an organ system.
  7. Know the 11 major organ systems of the body.
  8. Know the characteristics of living things.
  9. Understand the concept of homeostasis.
  10. Be able to explain how negative feedback maintains homeostasis and the components involved in a negative feedback system.
  11. What is positive feedback and what are some examples of it?
  12. Know the anatomical position.
  13. Be able to explain the directional terms used in anatomy.
  14. Be able to explain the different body planes.
  15. Know the anatomical terms used for the different regions of the body (cephalic, cervical, brachial, carpal, etc)
  16. Know the cavities in the trunk of the body.
  17. Be able to name and explain the different serous membranes of the body.
  18. Describe two ways to subdivide the abdominal cavity.
  19. Define mesentery and describe its function
  20. Define retroperitoneal and list examples of retroperitoneal organs.
Chapter 2: The Chemical Basis for Life
  1.  Be able to define matter.
  2.  What is an element and what are the 4 most abundant elements in the body.
  3.  Understand the structure of the atom including the description of the subatomic particles.
  4.  Be able to define atomic number, mass number, isotope, half-life, atomic weight, and mole.
  5.  What determines the characteristics of an element?
  6.  Given an atomic weight and mass number be able to tell how many electrons, protons, neutrons, and electrons in the outer shell and atom has.
  7.  Understand the nature of chemical bonds and be able to describe covalent (non polar and polar), ionic and hydrogen bonds.
  8.  Know the difference between a molecule and a compound.
  9.  Know the definition and characteristics of anabolic and catabolic reactions.
  10.  Be able to explain dehydration and hydrolysis reactions.
  11.  Know what oxidation-reductions reactions are.
  12.  Understand the concept of reversibility of chemical reactions and the concept of chemical equilibrium..
  13.  What factors influence the rate of chemical reactions?
  14.  Be able to define energy and know the differences between the different types of energy (Kinetic vs potential, electrical, mechanical, electromagnetic, heat, chemical).
  15.  Distiguish between exergonic and endergonic reactions.
  16.  Be able to describe the unique characteristics of water that make it suitable for sustaining life.
  17.  Know the definition of solvent, solute, ionization, electrolyte, colloid, suspension, molar solution, percent solution, osmolar solution.
  18.  Know what acids and bases are and the difference between acidic and basic solutions.
  19.  Know the definition of a salt and be able to give examples of important electrolytes found in body fluids.
  20. Understand the pH scale and the effect of buffers on pH.
  21.  What are organic molecules?
  22.  Be able to recognize and tell the properties of the important functional groups of organic molecules.
  23. Know the following about carbohydrates:
  24.     Basic structure
  25.     Definition of isomer
  26.     Characteristics of monosaccharides
  27.     Names of five important monosaccharides
  28.     Structure of glucose
  29.     Definition of disaccharides
  30.     Names and components of three important disaccharides
  31.     Structure of polysaccharides
  32.     Characteristics of starch, glycogen, and cellulose
  33.     Difference between bonds in starch and cellulose
  34. Know the following about lipids
  35.     The properties of lipids
  36.     Basic structure and function of fats (triglycerides)
  37.     Basic structure and function of phospholipids
  38.     Basic structure and function of steroids
  39. Know the following about proteins
  40.     The properties of proteins
  41.     Functions of proteins
  42.     Basic structure of amino acids and peptide bonds
  43.     Primary, secondary, tertiary and quaternary structure of proteins
  44.     How proteins are denatured
  45.     Functions and characteristics of enzymes
  46. Know the following about nucleic acids
  47.     The basic structure and function of nucleic acids
  48.     Components of a nucleotide
  49.      Types of purines and pyrimidines
  50.      Types of functions of modified nucleotides


Chapter 3: Structure and Function of the Cell

  1.  Be able to describe the structure and composition of the plasma membrane.
  2.  What are the lipids found in the membrane?
  3.  What types of proteins are found in the membrane and what are their functions?
  4.  What is the role of carbohydrates in the plasma membrane?
  5.  Be able to describe the fluid mosaic model of the plasma membrane.
  6.  Understand the role of tight junctions, desmosomes and gap junctions.
  7.  Know the structure of the nucleus including the role of each of the following: nuclear envelope, nuclear pores, chromatin, histone proteins, chromosomes, nucleolus.
  8.  Know the composition of the cytoplasm
  9.  Be able to describe the composition of the cytosol including the components of the cytoskeleton.
  10.  Know the structure and function of each of the following organelles and cellular structures: ribosomes, rough endoplasmic reticulum, smooth endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, peroxisomes, mitochondria, centrioles and spindle fibers, cilia, flagella, microvilli.
  11.  What is a selectively permeable membrane?
  12.  Know the difference between passive and mediated transport process in the cell membrane.
  13.  Be able to describe diffusion, the requirements for diffusion and factors that influence the rate of diffusion.
  14.  Be able to explain osmosis, osmotic pressure.
  15.  Know what is meant by hypertonic, hypotonic, and isotonic solution, and what happens to cells when placed in each of these solutions.
  16.  With respect to mediated transport understand the concepts of selectivity, competition, and saturation.
  17.  Be able to explain facilitated diffusion and active transport, including how the two are alike and how they differ.
  18.  Know what is meant by secondary active transport (know the difference between cotransport or symport and counter transport or antiport)
  19.  Understand the process of endocytosis and the difference between phagocytosis, pinocytosis and receptor-mediated endocytosis.
  20.  Explain exocytosis.
  21.  Know the definition of a gene and the nature of the genetic code.
  22.  Know the 3 different types of RNA and the role of each in protein synthesis.
  23.  What is transcription, where does it take place, what is the product of the process and what is the enzyme that controls it?
  24.  What happens to the pre-mRNA before it can be translated?
  25.  Be able to explain the basic steps in the process of translation including: the role of the ribosome, the function of the codon, the role of transfer RNA, the role of the anticodon, what happens during initiation, elongation and termination.
  26.  Know the stages of the cell life cycle and what happens during each.
  27.  Be able to explain the process of DNA replication.
  28.  Know the 4 stages of mitosis and what happens during each stage.
  29.  Understand the process of meiosis, how it differs from mitosis and what happens during each stage or meiosis.
  30.  With respect to meiosis know the meaning of each of the following: tetrad, crossing over, synapsis, haploid, gamete.


Chapter 25: Cellular Energy Production

  2.  Know the definition of and the difference between substrate level phosphorylation and oxidative phosphorylation.
  3.  Know the function of ATP and its basic structure.
  4.  Know the following concerning glycolysis:

  10.     What is the role of NAD in the process?

  12.     What happens to the products when oxygen is absent (anaerboic respiration)?
  13. What is the product of  fermentation and when and where does it occur in the body?
  14. What is the purpose for fermentation and how much energy is produced when this process is used?
  15. Know the following concerning the citric acid cycle (Krebs cycle)
  16.     Where does it take place?
  17.     How does pyruvate enter the cycle (the bridge)?
  18.     What is the role of oxaloacetate in the process?
  19.     How much energy is derived from the process?
  20.     What is the role of NAD and FAD in the process?

  22.  Know the following concerning the electron transport chain
  23.     Where does it take place?

  25.     What is chemiosmosis and how does it generate ATP?

Chapter 5: Integumentary System

  1. Know the functions of the integumentary system.
  2. Know the location, composition and function of the hypodermis.
  3. Know the structure and function of the dermis, including the differences between the papillary layer and the reticular layer.
  4. Know what dermal papillae, dermal ridges and cleavage lines are.
  5. Know the names and functions of the four cell types found in the epidermis.
  6. Be able to describe each of the layers of the epidermis.
  7. Understand the difference between thick and thin skin and where each is found on the body.
  8. Be able to explain the functions of the melanocytes and the factors that influence skin color.
  9. Know the structure of hair (shaft, root, cuticle, etc).
  10. Know the three different types of hair, their characteristics and when and where we find them.
  11. Know the structure of the hair follicle.
  12. Be able to explain the cycles of hair growth.
  13. Know the causes of hair loss.
  14. Know the function of the arrector pili muscles.
  15. Know the function of the sebaceous glands, apocrine sweat glands and merocrine sweat glands, the products of each, and their basic structure.
  16. Know the basic structure of the nails, including each of the following: nail root, nail body, mail matrix, nail fold, cuticle and lunula.
  17. Know the effects of aging on the skin.
  18. Know the three types of skin cancer, the characteristics of each, and the warning signs for the most serious type of cancer (ABCD’s)


Chapter 6: Skeletal System: Histology and Development

  1. Know the functions of the skeletal system.
  2. Understand the role of tendons of ligaments, their structure, the differences between the two and how they grow.
  3. Be able to describe the structure of cartilage, the composition of the matrix, the cell types found in it, the layers of the perichondrium, and how it grows.
  4. Understand the histology of bone, including each of the following: the composition of the matrix (collagen and hydroxyapatite), the structure of the osteon, the types of cells found in bone, the difference between cancellous and compact bone, the three different types of lamellae and the composition of the periosteum.
  5. For long bones be able to describe each of the following: diaphysis, epiphysis, epiphyseal plate, periosteum, perforating fibers, endosteum, nutrient canals.
  6. Describe the structure of flat bones, short bones, and irregular bones, knowing how they differ and where they are found in the body (examples).
  7. Be able to explain the process of bone ossification.
  8. Describe the process of intramembranous ossification and give examples of bones formed by this process.
  9. Describe endochondral ossification and give examples of bones formed by this process.
  10. Know the difference between appositional growth and endochondral growth in bone.
  11. Be able to explain the process of endochondral bone growth.
  12. Know how the following factors effect bone growth: Nutrition, vitamin D, vitamin C, steroid hormones, growth hormone, thyroid hormone.
  13. Understand the role of bones in regulating blood calcium levels and know the mechanisms and hormones involved in this process.
  14. Be able to describe the process of remodeling and give examples of when it happens.
  15. Describe the process of bone repair and tell what happens at each of the four stages.


Chapter 11 (p371-387): Membrane Potentials

  1. Know the relative concentration of the following electrolytes inside and outside of the cell:  K+, Na+, Ca++, Cl-, and proteins-.
  2. Understand the role of the sodium-potassium exchange pump and the selective permeability of the membrane in creating and maintaining the concentration differences of the electrolytes.
  3. Be able to explain the resting membrane potential and the factors responsible for it.
  4. Understand the concept of hyperpolarization and depolarization.
  5. Know the structure and function of the ion channels found in cellular membranes.
  6.     What leads to the specificity of the ion channels?
  7.      How do the gates control movement through the ion channels?
  8.      What factors effect the gates?
  9.      What are voltage-gated (sensitive) and ligand-gated channels?
  10. What tissues have excitable cells?
  11. Know the characteristics of local potentials (graded response, summation, decremental conduction).
  12. Know how action potentials are generated, their characteristics and how they are propagated along a nerve or muscle cell (threshold potential, depolarization phase, repolarization phase, afterpotential, all-or-none, absolute refractory period, relative refractory period, decrementless conduction)
  13. Be able to describe the synapse and how it works.
  14. Know how the frequency of action potentials relates to the strength of the stimulus.
  15. What is accommodation?


Chapter 9: Muscular System: Histology and Physiology

  12. Be able to explain what happens during tetany, incomplete tetany and treppe.

  17. Know the difference between psychological, muscular and synaptic fatigue.
