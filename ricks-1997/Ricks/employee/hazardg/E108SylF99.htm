Course Objectives:

The primary objectives of English 108 are:

1. To develop oral language skills

2. To help with acculturation

The secondary objectives of English 108 are:

1. To develop reading skills

2. To develop writing skills

Course Description:

English 108 is designed to help you improve your speaking, listening, reading, and writing skills. It is also designed to help you adjust to American and "Ricks College" life. You will meet in class (Clark 228) 3 times a week, and with your American partner (Clark 329) 2 times a week. The class environment is non threatening, and you and I will learn together. You will have the opportunity to improve your English by using it in authentic situations, not simply by rote memorization and reproduction. The best way to learn English is to use English. To this end, you will be given the opportunity to use English in the following five ways.

Listening

Participation in class discussions, group work, formal presentations, and the tutorial will provide authentic situations for you to practice listening. Lectures on cross-cultural communication will require good listening skills. Videos will also be used to practice listening skills.

Speaking

The purpose of class discussions is to provide you with an opportunity to practice speaking in a safe environment. The readings will be used as springboards to discussion. Sharing your personal experiences and insights will be accepted and encouraged during discussions. There will be three oral presentations during the course of the semester. As you participate in discussions and presentations you will gain confidence in speaking English. Reading

Most reading will be assigned for homework. You should come to class prepared to discuss the readings. You will be asked to read out loud in class. Writing

You will write five papers during the semester. They will each be typed, double spaced, and one to two pages in length. Writing is thinking put on paper. If you are thinking coherently and know how to write effectively, I will understand what you are thinking. Whether you are thinking in English or in your primary language is often revealed by your writing. Much of the writing in English 108 will center around your own experiences, and should not cause undue stress.

Grammar

Grammar skills are the building blocks of good writing. We will spend a small amount of time talking about basic grammar in class. Tutors may also work on grammar with students during tutoring sessions.

Vocabulary

Academic vocabulary will be very important to you in college. You will keep a weekly vocabulary list. The words on your list must be new to you. Most of them should come from the classes you are taking this semester. A few can be words or phrases you hear from friends or around campus. You will be quizzed each week on your list of words.

Grading Policy

Each assignment will be given a point value. Grades will be based on a weighted percentage as indicated above. The oral presentations including the teaching assignment must be done in order to pass the class. The final examination must be taken in order to pass the class.

100-90 = A

89-80 = B

79-70 = C

69-60 = D

Below 60 = F

Attendance Requirements

Attendance is very important. You have one free absence. After that, every absence will result in a 2% deduction from your final grade. For example, if your final grade was a B+ (87%), but you were absent twice from class, then your grade would be adjusted to a B (85%). If you were absent three times, your grade would be adjusted to a B- (83%), etc.

Course Policies

All policies in the "R" book will be followed. Late assignments will not be accepted, if you are going to miss a "turn in date", it is your responsibility to make arrangements to get the assignment to me. There may be "pop" quizzes at any time during the semester.

"In compliance with the Americans with Disabilities Act, all qualified students enrolled in this course are entitled to "reasonable accommodation." It is the student's responsibility to disclose to the teacher any special need she/he may have before the end of the first week of class."

ELD Program Exit Requirements

Regardless of your grade in English 108, you must pass the IPT, Woodcock, SOLOM, and writing assessments at appropriate levels in order to exit the ELD program. You may not register for English 111 or above without exiting the ELD program.
