Policies

  1. "... they have become free forever ... to act for themselves and not to be acted upon" - 2 Nephi 2:26.
    • You are responsible for your education.
    • Take responsibility to complete homework and tests as assigned.
    • Be prepared to accept the consequences if you do not.
  2. Learn how to learn! Don't expect someone else to give you the answer. (It will probably be wrong anyway). Learn to develop alternative solutions and implement the best one.
  3. For me, assisting students has first priority. Grading has a lower priority than assisting students. Grading exams has a lower priority than grading homework. I apologize if I do not grade and return homework and exams quickly.
  4. The best way to get on my bad list is to interfere with a student's learning. This includes, but is not limited to, preventing me from helping students and causing disruptions in the computer labs.
  5. My grading philosophy: If you cause me extra work, you pay for it.
  6. Since no one, including me, pays attention to my office hours I have decided not to declare any. My office hours start when I arrive and terminate when I leave. If my office door is open, and it usually is, please come in.
  7. I do not track attendance except in Ethics classes. Regardless, it is important that you come to class and that you arrive on time. I often give homework hints during class. Material covered in class (but not in the text book) appears on tests. I tend to give test reviews at the very beginning of class. I reserve the right to give unannounced quizzes at the beginning of class. I also reserve the right to require the completion of unannounced assignments during class time.
  8. Study assigned material before class. This may require multiple readings and experimentation if you do not initially understand it. I would prefer to spend class time answering your thoughtful questions.
  9. I confess that I am more prone to assist students who attend class. I have little patience for the student who skips class and then asks questions that were answered in the class he/she missed.
  10. While I encourage you to work with your classmates, all submitted work must be original. Share ideas, but DO NOT SHARE CODE! The penalty for copying is a negative score equal to the maximum number possible points for that assignment.
  11. I do not have set lectures and I do not keep lecture notes. If you miss a lecture, obtain lecture notes from a classmate. Please do not take time away from classmates by asking me to repeat material.
  12. When I teach multiple sections of the same class, I endeavor to keep the classes in synch (i.e., I cover the same material in each of the classes on any given day). Consequently, you are free to attend any section.
  13. You may start homework assignments early (i.e., before the assigned date in the course outline), but you are on your own. Do not take my time away from your fellow classmates by asking me to explain concepts we have not yet covered in class. All will be revealed at the appropriate time.
  14. There is a bowl of Dum-Dums in my office. Help yourself. Since I don't refill the bowl until it is empty (or almost empty), sometimes you may have to take a flavor that is not your favorite. Life is like that.
  15. Please don't embarrass me by putting me into a position where I will embarrass you with regard to the Honor Code (including dress and grooming standards).
