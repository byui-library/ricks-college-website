The text below comes from the British Museum:

"Iron Age, mid-1st century AD, Lindow Man was found in 1985 in Lindow Moss, Cheshire, England.   Victim of a Druidic sacrifice?

This man met a horrific death in a bog in north west England at about the time of the Roman Conquest of England. He was 25 years old, and had probably done very little hard, manual work. His finger nails were well manicured and his beard and moustache were cut by a pair of scissors. He suffered from a severe case of worms.

Before he was killed he had eaten unleaven bread made from wheat and barley, and drank a drink containing different plants, including mistletoe. He was taken to the bog. First, a couple of blows from a heavy object to his head smashed his skull. He was then strangled with a thin cord. By now he was probably dead, but then his throat was cut and he was allowed to bleed for a time. Finally, he was placed face down in a pond in the bog. This horrific and elaborate sequence strongly suggests that he was sacrificed as part of a ritual. The drink he drunk before he was killed contained mistletoe. Mistletoe was sacred to the Druids, and it is recorded that Druids carried out human sacrifices.

He was found in 1984 when workmen cutting peat to be used in gardens discovered a leg. Archaeologists were called to the scene and the upper part of a body was found, still in the ground. It was boxed and transported to the British Museum, where it was carefully excavated and thoroughly examined by a team of scientists. Their research has allowed us to make a reconstruction of what he may have looked like.

His official name is Lindow II, since a skull (Lindow I) and a fragmented headless body (Lindow III) have also been found at the same place. He is sometimes also affectionately known as 'Pete Marsh.'"
