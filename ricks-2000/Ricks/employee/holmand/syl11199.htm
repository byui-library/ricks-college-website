Introduction

In a society that has so much to say, so much information to communicate, often little time or attention is devoted to how well it is said, i.e. to the quality of communication. Com-munication is complicated still more by the varieties of idiom, jargon and slang, as well as of values, that distinguish and various groups (such as Baby Boomers and Generation X-ers; Mormons and Catholics; inner-city Los Angeles and rural Rexburg). Almost all of us want to have our say, but it is not enough to be heard. It really comes down to whether our target audience understands what we mean.



Purpose and Approach

In this class we will emphasize reader-oriented writing at the college level. More than just fulfilling assignment criteria to the letter or reading the teacher's mind, it will be your responsibility to inform and persuade your audience in accordance with the real purpose of each essay. If the reader does not feel adequately informed or persuaded, the essay has failed. The reader is the judge, and you will learn to approach each assignment accordingly.



A Word on Responsibility

As the teacher, it will be my responsibility to make all expectations and assignments as clear as possible, as well as to help you acquire the skills necessary for meeting those expectations. I am not, however, responsible for your education. Your education is your responsibility. Within the context of this course, that responsibility entails the following:

  • regular class attendance (barring illness or real emergencies);
  • conscientious fulfillment of all assign-ments;
  • active, thoughtful participation in all group activities and class discussions;
  • a determination to master the central concepts and skills of the course.


Papers

You will be required to write four essays, each one four to five pages long. These essays should be typed, double-spaced, and should conform to MLA format guidelines. The kind of essay you write will be assigned; the topic, however, is up to you, as long as it is appropriate to the assignment. The following are brief descriptions of the essays you will write:





The personal experience essay demands reflective meditation upon an experience you have had which lends itself to self-characterization. You will narrate a personal experience - using vivid description, specific details, and a climactic moment - and structure the essay so that it conveys a significant theme about the experience.



Your expository research essay will, explain a concept or idea. It will require clear, coherent and well-organized writing. You will also research you topic and integrate secondary sources into your essay. This will require documentation according to the MLA style.



The argumentative essay will present reasons and evidence in support of a debatable position on a particular issue. You will write about an issue that has real relevance to you. In your argument you will need to establish your credibility and fair-mindedness; provide adequate reasoning and support (statistics, examples, authorities, personal experience); and appeal to the emotions and values of your audience.



The interpretive essay will analyze and create meaning out of a poem. The essay should include a brief summary of the text (demonstrating your comprehension of it), an interpretation of one or more related themes, and some personal response to the signifi-cance or implications of your interpretation.



Process Work

40% of each paper is determined by your efforts in the writing process. You must complete invention activities, drafts and participate in peer response groups and evaluation groups. All process work must be completed and turned in on the day it is due. Failure to complete work on time is failure in the writing process.



Quizzes

Comprehension of reading assignments and writing skills will be monitored by three unannounced, short-answer quizzes. The quizzes will be given randomly throughout the term, always at the beginning of a class period. There will be no make-up quizzes for those who are tardy or absent. The only exceptions will be in the event of an excused absence (illness, family emergency, or school-sponsored activity), in which case a note will be required from the appropriate authority. If you are absent for an excused reason, please leave a message on my voice mail prior to class.



Examinations

A final exam will be held as noted in the syllabus and in the Winter Class Schedule. You must take it on the scheduled date. If you can't take the final exam on the date noted in the Winter Class Schedule, you should drop this class now. A marriage - your own or that of a friend - or family vacation plans will not be considered legitimate excuses.



Conferences

You will be required to meet with me at least twice in my office (at least once before November 1) to discuss your drafts and review your essay grades. At least one of the conferences must be devoted to going over a draft. In these conferences I will offer suggestions on how to improve your drafts and discuss the peer comments on and peer evaluations of your essays. You are, however, encouraged to come and see me anytime you have concerns about your writing or your status in the class. Ten points will be deducted from your semester total for each required conference that you fail to schedule and/or attend.



Evaluation and Grades

The audience to whom you will be addressing your papers will be your peers--other English 111 students, not just the instructor. After the deadline for each final paper, you, along with other members of a peer group, will read, comment on, and evaluate the essays written by students in another class. Your papers, too, will be evaluated by such a group. If you are satisfied with an evaluation, it will stand as your grade. If you are not satisfied, you may bring the paper to me for reevaluation. You may do this as often as you wish.
