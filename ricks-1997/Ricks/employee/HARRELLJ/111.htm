COURSE OBJECTIVE

The Ricks College Catalog describes English 111 as such: "Emphasis on learning to manage the composing process and to write proficiently on a college level." The best way to learn to write clear, coherent, college-level essays is to write and evaluate writing frequently. In this course you will write your own papers, evaluate them with peers, and learn how to evaluate the writings of others, all in an effort to improve your current writing skills.

WRITING FOR AN AUDIENCE

Almost all writing is written for an audience, and almost all writing has clear communication as its goal. But the audiences for writing can differ significantly. For example, articles in Glamour magazine are written mainly for women between the ages of 18 and 30; articles in Boy's Life are written for boys; articles in The Ensign are written for North American, English-speaking Latter-day Saints. In the working world, business executives write to employees and other executives; advertisers write ads, technical writers write guidelines for manufacturing. All of these people write with at least two things in mind: a specific audience, and a desire to communicate clearly.

The audience for everything you write in this course will be other Ricks College students. The five major papers you write in this class will be read and evaluated by other students in supervised writing groups. Anyone dissatisfied with the grades on their papers may appeal to the teacher for a higher grade.

WRITING AS A PROCESS

Even when we know our audience good writing does not simply appear polished and effective. In order to produce writing that communicates well, all good writers go through three basic stages-pre-writing, writing, and rewriting.

You will be required to complete five essays of three to five pages, double-spaced. Your success in this class depends almost as much upon your participation in the writing process as it does upon the final product. The process of writing and evaluating each essay determines 40 percent of its grade; 60 percent depends upon the product, the completed essay.

ATTENDANCE AND WORK DUE

Absences will be excused only in the following three cases: 1) Illness or physical injury documented by a letter from a physician or health professional. 2) College-sponsored activities documented by a letter from an appropriate school official. 3) Family tragedies documented by the Dean of Students Office. In these cases, students should talk with the teacher about completing assignments, since no absence relieves the student of assigned work.

In-class work missed due to an un-excused absence cannot be made up.

CLASS ATMOSPHERE

In this class we will follow the Ricks College Code of Honor and Dress and Grooming Standards. In class discussion, we will follow the pattern outlined in Doctrine and Covenants 88:122: ". . . let not all be spokesmen at once; but let one speak at a time and let all listen unto his sayings, that when all have spoken that all may be edified of all. . . ."

Class atmosphere can be influenced- positively or negatively-by a few people. Students who are frequently absent, repeatedly late to class, insistent on testing the limits of the dress code; who talk when the teacher or other students are talking; who dominate class discussion to the point that others don't get a chance to speak-those students become a burden to everyone. On the other hand, students who participate enthusiastically in class discussion; who bring interesting ideas to the class and help maintain civil order; and who just get into the spirit of the course-those are the ones who help insure that everyone in the class has a good experience.

An essential characteristic of good writing is "critical thinking," which involves honestly asking questions and being willing to seek answers. Few things in writing impress an audience as much as an honest attempt to communicate well. As a reader, you will be obligated to submit yourself to the goal of trying to understand the writings of others. As a writer, you will be obligated to communicate well, so that understanding will be easy-and hopefully even enjoyable. The goal of this class is to teach you to understand your audience and communicate well with them.
