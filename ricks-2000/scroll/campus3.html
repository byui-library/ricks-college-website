Heating Plant suffers minor fire

by Emily Toy
toy01001@byui.edu
Scroll Staff

Rexburg’s notorious wind played a significant role in producing flames at the BYU-Idaho heating plant Oct. 21.

While the wind is a force beyond the control of any contractor, inspector or building operator, precautions could have been taken to prevent the fire, Kyle Williams, BYU-I safety director, said.

The contractors left wood too close to the temporary coal chute, and it caught fire, Fire Chief Spence Larson said.

A temporary chute was installed to move the coal until a permanent chute could be installed.

The building operator noticed flames around 7:30 p.m., and fire crews were dispatched by 7:40 p.m.

“It is like a big fire next to a gas tank — we’ve got to be careful,” Police Captain Garth Gunderson of the Campus Division of the Rexburg City Police said at the scene.

The heating plant has been under major renovation since June and was scheduled to be complete by the end of the year. The project includes replacing the 100-foot “R” smokestack and the smallest of four coal-fire boilers with a natural gas boiler.

Because of the renovations, a portion of the roof where the fire started is also being replaced. The bulk of the damage occurred on the old roof and the pile of debris left from its removal. Only a few boards of the new roof were slightly burned and will need to be replaced, Bud Robbins, project supervisor for Arnell West, the general contractor of the renovation project, said.

It is difficult situation because the school has to continue to use the facility as it is being renovated, Williams said.

Workers were unaware of how hot the smokestack could get. Had they been more aware of the circumstances, they could have taken more precautionary measures, Robbins said.

Better housekeeping by the department and by the contractors also could have helped in preventing the fire, Williams said. The roofers, who left combustible items on the roof and welders, who had been working on the project earlier that day, should have been more safety conscious, Williams said.

The temporary chute should have worked, but due to high winds and combustibles being too close, things got a little hotter than usual, Dave Davis of the Rexburg Fire Department said.

Still, the building was inspected to identify the potential problem areas.

“The majority of the problems were with the contractor,” Williams said.

However, Robbins said that there were out-of-date fire extinguishers and clean-up that should have been done by sub-contractors.

All of the deficiencies that Larson pointed out during the inspection have been corrected, Robbins said.

“[No one],” Williams said, “is sure who to point their finger at.”

The fire was controlled quickly. Fire crews spent time cleaning up and moving combustible items away from the chute and coal stack. Although the fire was under control, if it reached the coal the building would have been in severe danger, Gunderson said.

“It’s lucky [the fire] didn’t amount to much and that the operator found it when he did, Robbins said. “Good action was taken by [the building operator].”

No one will be cited for violations, Davis said.
