Mothers hold divine roles in family life

by Emily Toy
TOY01001@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

The names, duties and image of mothers have changed over the years, but the divine role has not.

“Before the world was created, in heavenly councils the pattern and role of women were prescribed. [Women] were elected by God to be wives and mothers in Zion. Exaltation in the Celestial Kingdom is predicated on faithfulness to that calling,” President Ezra Taft Benson said.

Beginning with our first earthy mother, Eve, and continuing on through the years to Sarah, the miraculous mother of Isaac; Mary the mother of Jesus; Naomi the mother of Ruth; Sariah the mother of Nephi; the mother’s of the stripling warriors and into the latter days to Pioneer mothers who buried their babies as they pressed onward to bring their surviving family members to Zion, mothers have been relied on to fulfill a divine calling.

This scriptural list should not exclude the heavenly work of modern mothers.

It is not uncommon for young mothers to strive to teach their children about the gospel of Jesus Christ and provide them with a loving home and ample quality time as she and her young husband are finishing school and establishing themselves.

There is the unforgettable mother who stays up all night baking brownies for her fifth grade son’s entire class. He mentioned the field day he promised to bring treats to the next day as his mommy tucked him into bed.

Or how about the mother who patiently listens to her 17-year-old daughter cry when she did not get asked to prom?

Do not exclude the mother that President Thomas S. Monson alluded to in the April 1998 Liahona magazine — the mother of each fallen soldier who has died in sacrifice for this country.

“The work of a mother is hard, too-often unheralded work,” Elder Jeffery R. Holland said. “Mothers, we acknowledge and esteem your faith in every footstep. Please know that is worth it then, now and forever.”

Mothers are reminded of the nobility of their work.

“There is no more noble work than that of a good and God-fearing mother,” President Benson said.

This divine role extends to grandmothers, step-mothers and adoptive mothers. Heavenly father placed the divine role of motherhood upon all women.

What is this divine role? Is it that of sacrifice?

Victor Hugo wrote:

“She broke the bread into two fragments and gave them to her children, who ate with eagerness. ‘She hath kept none for herself,’ grumbled the sergeant.

“‘Because she is not hungry,’ a soldier said.

“‘No,’ the sergeant said, ‘because she is a mother.’”

Is this divine role that of teaching faith in God? It is just as the stripling warrior’s mothers in teaching their sons, inexperienced yet unafraid to enter the battlefield and proclaim their faith in God: “We do not doubt our mothers knew it.”

Is love the divine call? It is all incorporated.

“The ability and willingness to properly rear children, the gift of love and eagerness, yes, longing to express it in soul development, make motherhood the noblest office or calling in the world,” President David O. McKay said.

We’ve been taught since we were children in primary to love our mothers, as we sweetly sang the words, “I know a name a glorious name, sweeter than any other. Listen, I’ll whisper that name to you, it is the name of Mother.”

An example of the love we should offer to mothers comes from our Savior, Jesus Christ.

“What power, what tenderness, what compassion did our Master and Exemplar thus demonstrate,” President McKay said.

The words of our Savior, Jesus Christ, when he suffered on the cross are given in command, “Behold, thy mother.”
