WHAT YOU SHOULD EXPECT FROM THIS COURSE:

 This course is designed to introduce you to the world of floral design.  You will have opportunities to learn:
the history of floral adornment
the principles and elements of design
bouquet designing methods
corsage construction technique
floral industry opportunities
identification of floral materials
Primary Text: The Art of Floral Design, by Norah Hunter (2nd edition, Delmar)


 Regular attendance and careful attention to the lectures and other classroom activities in this course  Most of the learning in this class comes through "hands on" experience and active participation.

There is some subjectivity to grading a “hands-on” course.  As time allows, part of the instruction will include feedback evaluation for you to know how you stand regarding lab grades.  If you feel the need for more specific information, please contact the instructor during office hours.  Due to the fact that this is a beginning level course, you will be graded in labs mostly by attendance and participation.  You will usually be given a satisfactory mark.  If your work is above average or highly outstanding, a plus will be indicated.  If you have neglected to complete the lab properly, followed instructions poorly, or demonstrated an attitude of non- cooperation, you will be given a minus mark.  If you fail to attend the exercise, you will be given no mark.

Lab exercises are graded by your participation.  Half of your grade comes from lab experiences.  Most materials needed are provided, with the exception of the final project.
 There is a lab fee automatically assessed for this class ($45.00).  This is applied to the majority of lab expenses such as the use of tools, foam, fresh flowers.  There may be additional materials required that you will need to provide on your own as indicated by the instructor.
 Those of you who are majoring in the Floral Design Management program may want to begin purchasing some of the “tools of the trade,” such as a knife, wire cutters, stem cutters, scissors, glue gun, which can be ordered through the department.  If interested, please ask the instructor for current prices and availability.

Final design Project:
A final design project will be completed The last week of class.  You will be responsible for providing the materials (including your own container and principle permanent botanicals (artificial flowers) that will be used to create it in your choice of style , incorporating the skills learned in the class.  Glue guns, floral foam, moss, dried filler flowers, wires, & ribbons.will be provided for you.  You will have the time in class to complete the design.

Floral Design Notebook:

 A pictorial notebook will be required which will include a minimum of twelve photographs, each mounted on a separate page with a detailed explanation of its content.
 These pictures should be of various floral arrangements in a variety of room settings, such as would be found in home & garden and interior decorating magazines.  There are some magazines in the media lab at the library that can be cut up, as well as some magazines in the floral lab.  You may need to purchase some of the pictures you choose to use.

The detailed explanation of each arrangement, in outline form, must include:
 1:  description of the style, shape, & origin of the arrangement
 2:  identification of the flowers used
 3:  color harmonies within the design
 4:  discussion of how the bouquet relates with the setting
 5:  reference to the source for each picture (ie.page 34, Elle decor, May’00)
 This is an exercise in observation and judgement.  The purpose is to expose us to a broad variety of styles and designs, learn to recognize the elements of good, quality design, and develop a vocabulary of floral terminology.
 

Weekly reading assignments:

 Ten reading assignments in current periodicals will be required .  A brief review of the article read must be turned in to receive credit for the assignment.
Many florist friends have told me that one of the most important, and most neglected skills is that of having good penmanship.  You could use this assignment to practice your handwriting, or if you prefer, just send it to me via  romneyb@byui.edu

 Magazines could include:  Professional Floral Designer, Florist, Flowers &, Florist’s Review, Floral Management, House Beautiful, Better Homes and Gardens, Sunset, Supermarket Floral, Architectural Digest, Elle Decor, Metropolitan Home, and any others that you may happen to find that have articles about the use of flowers and plants.  Also, the internet has some excellent information that you may find both interesting and helpful.

[The reading reports should be no more than one full page, and should include a specific reference to the name of the magazine, title of article, author, page(s), date, volume, etc.  Or include a web address URL or link if you look on the internet.  You may choose a particular format (for example, outline versus a paragraph form) that you feel is most appropriate.  The purpose of these assignments is to expose you to the world of horticulture and floral design, not to become an exercise in verbosity, so keep it simple.]
