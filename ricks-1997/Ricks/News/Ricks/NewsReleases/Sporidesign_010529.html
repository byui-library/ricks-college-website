Ricks College News Release
May 29, 2001

Design of new Spori Building
captures spirit of original structure

    
REXBURG, IDAHO - The public got its first glimpse of the new Jacob Spori Building at Ricks College during ground-breaking services May 29.

The new 44,000 square foot building, which will sit at the exact location of the old building that was torn down last year, has a striking resemblance to its predecessor and will feature a similar size and shape, rock exterior, tall windows and pitched roof.

Although the new building is reminiscent of the old building, it is not a replica, explains Ricks Administrative Vice President James Smyth, who serves as chairman of the building committee. “There is a beauty to the building and its beauty comes by way of the harmony and simplicity of its design,” he says.

Unlike the old structure, the three-story building will feature a 12 by 30 foot skylight that will allow natural light to flow down through the center of the building to the first floor, Smyth says.

The interior design also features efficient use of space for classrooms and offices of the Communications and Art Departments, as well as an art gallery on the main floor. Unlike the old building, it will have a full basement.

The earth-tone color of the building will be slightly different than the original gray rock and feature a veneer of split-faced walnut travertine from the Medicine Lodge area near Dubois.

Some rock and woodwork from the original building were preserved and are incorporated into the design of the new building. A central focus of the interior will be the triangular wooden pediment from the front of the original building that will be mounted on the third floor and visible from the first floor by looking up the skylight. The pediment will be located at approximately the same elevation and location as it was in the old building.

An outdoor terrace in the front of the building will be bordered by a wall of  rock from the original building. 

Some other rock pieces from the original building will be used throughout the new building, including some bricks from the basement that had names of former students scratched into their surface.

Jim Lewis, principal architect from FFKR in Salt Lake City, says there are many similarities, but also distinct differences between the two buildings. “It just kept getting better and more exciting,” he says of the design process. He credits other architects at FFKR and members of the college building committee for their ideas and interest in the project.

“I hope people are utterly surprised when they come inside. It’s a very small center space, but very tall,” he says.

Lewis also likes the idea of using local stone on the exterior, saying. “I like the fact there’s a link to something from the area.”

FFKR designed both of Ricks College’s most recent buildings – the Spencer W. Kimball Student and Administrative Services Building and the John Taylor Religion Building. The architectural firm also designed a replacement of the historic Nauvoo Temple in Illinois, the BYU Jerusalem Center and the Delta Center in Salt Lake City.

The bid opening for the Spori Building will be held in June and construction is expected to start in early July and be completed in 2003.

Excavation of the foundation already is under way to expedite the construction schedule. Edstrom Construction Inc. of Rexburg is excavating the site where the original Spori Building stood.
