An Offender Oriented Culture

It is difficult for a woman to grow up in a culture that is not safe.  It is sad that such a condition exists in this culture.  The fact that a woman has to remember multiple safety guidelines to survive and not be blamed is a reflection of a society that blames the female if she dresses a certain way, or is out alone at night when she should know better.  The following guidelines are shared in the hopes of making females more safe and at the same time making the point that it is the masculine gender that is to blame for offending and preying upon the feminine.

Some General Guidelines to Avoiding Rape

  • Learn to be assertive.
  • Look and act alert at all times.
  • Be aware of your environment.
  • Be aware of your nonverbal communication.
  • Avoid dangerous places.
  • Think through what action you would take if confronted by a rapist.
  • Know how to use nonconventional weapons.
  • Trust your instincts when you sense danger.
  • Know how to defend yourself.
  • Carry an alarm device whenever you leave your residence.

  • ON CAMPUS
  • Avoid working late at night or on weekends in isolated offices or labs.

  • AT A PARTY OR A BAR
  • Whenever you go out with a male friend, be sure you know who he is, tell a friend his name, your destination and when you will return.
  • On first dates, either meet at or go to a public place. Consider dating with another couple who you know until you get to know your date a little better.
  • Go to activities with a friend(s) or date and return home with the same person(s).
  • Pay attention when you have mixed feelings.
  • Set limits early.
  • When you return to your dorm or apartment with a date, always give the impression that there is another person within calling distance.
  • Don't drink too much.
  • Avoid situations which involve heavy drinking or use of drugs.

WHILE WALKING

  • Walk briskly.
  • If you feel someone following you, walk toward a store, a lighted area, a classroom building or a busy street.
  • Don't let yourself be surprised.
  • If you think someone in a car is following you, cross the street and walk on the opposite side. If he persists, record the license number.
  • Walk on the side of the street facing traffic, close to the curb.
  • Keep one hand free when walking.
  • Don't ever hitchhike.
  • Avoid walking alone at night.
  • If you travel a distance at night, consider taking a cab. It is expensive, but much safer.
  • If a stranger approaches you and asks for the time or directions, make eye contact. Don't look down or glance away. Don't stop to look down at your watch or open your purse. Keep your stride and ignore the person.
  • Vary routes on campus.
  • If your route requires the use of elevators, don't enter the elevator by yourself if a person on the elevator makes you feel uneasy.
  • Know the locations of public telephones on routes to and from class.

WHEN USING PUBLIC TRANSPORTATION

  • Wait for buses at well-lighted stop and/or wait with groups of people.
  • If anyone bothers you on a bus or subway, let the other passengers and drivers know what is happening. In a loud voice say, "Leave me alone!"
  • Notice who else gets off at your stop. Be sure you aren't being followed.
  • Avoid falling asleep on public transportation if you are alone.
  • When you use public transportation at night, always try to stay near other people or near a ticket booth or police officer or security guard.

AT HOME OR IN A RESIDENCE HALL

  • Do not open the door to strangers. Be suspicious of door-to-door solicitors.

If someone comes to your door and says there is an emergency, don't let the person in (male or female). Tell the person you will call for help.

If a stranger comes to your dorm or apartment looking for your roommate, do not let the stranger in.

  • Never reveal your number to a wrong number caller.
  • Never listen to heavy breathers or obscene callers.
  • Keep emergency numbers near the phone.
  • Use your first initial and last name on your mail box and when listing your number in the phone directory.
  • Lock your apartment or dorm doors as soon as you enter or leave.
  • Always have your keys out and ready when you come to your door.
  • Don't lend your keys to anyone, including roommates and friends.
  • Be sure your doorway is well lighted at night.
  • If you think someone is watching you when you go to your door, go to a neighbor's door instead.
  • Never leave messages on your door.
  • Close your blinds and shades at night.
  • Have peephole installed in your door with a light outside so that you can clearly see who is at the door.
  • Never go outside unless you are fully clothed.
  • In residence halls, take security seriously.
  • If you live in a home or apartment, develop an escape plan for use in case of an intruder.

WHILE DRIVING

  • Have your keys in your hand as you approach your car.
  • Always look inside the car before you get in.
  • Keep your car doors locked and your windows up when you drive.
  • Keep your purse and valuables out of sight, under the seat, in the glove compartment or in the trunk.
  • When you are driving somewhere at night, get very specific directions and, if you can, locate the place during the day.
  • Always carry appropriate maps with you.
  • Don't let the gas tank approach empty.
  • Maintain the car in good running condition.
  • Park in areas that are well lighted and busy.
  • Never leave the keys to your home with your car keys when your car is left at a parking lot or service station.
  • Never pick up hitchhikers.
  • Always carry enough money for an emergency phone call and to purchase gas if you should need it.
  • If you suspect that your car is being followed, drive into a busy, well-lighted business establishment, a police or fire station, hospital emergency entrance, gas station or campus security headquarters.
  • Know what to do if your car breaks down.
  • If another motorist stops to help.
  • If your car won't start.
  • In cold weather.

FINALLY

  • Attend a rape prevention seminar.
