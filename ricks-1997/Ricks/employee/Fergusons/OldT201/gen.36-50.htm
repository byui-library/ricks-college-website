Gospel Symbolism- Bruce R. McConkie

The Hebrew word kethoneth can be translated as "garment," "tunic of skin," "worn next to the person," "garment with long skirts and sleeves," "a holy linen tunic, of a High Priest." Kethoneth finds its first use in the Old Testament in the account of the "coats of skins" made by God for Adam and Eve (Genesis 3:21). The King James translators could just as well have rendered this "garments of skin" or "robes of skins." The Hebrew word for skins (Or) has a homonym which means "light." The oral reading of the phrase "garments of skins" in Hebrew conveys the meaning "garments of light." Jewish legends refer to the garments given by God to Adam as the "celestial garments," the "priestly garments," or "high-priestly garments," described as having "super-natural qualities" and to be the garments worn by Adam and his descendants when offering sacrifices. Such traditions also hold that these were "the priestly garments worn by the firstborn who performed the priestly service before Aaron's time." (Legends 5:103-4, 283.) Joseph's coat was more than a mere expression of his father's favoritism. It apparently announced that the coveted birhtright was to be his. The "coat" given to Joseph was a priesthood garment, adn may have been related with Joseph's ability to dream dreams and speak by way of revelation. (McConkie, Gospel Symbolism, pp.32-33.)



Understanding Who We Are Brings Self-Respect- President Harold B. Lee

One's activities in his premortal life had an influence on his being born into a particular situation on this earth. Thos born in the lineage of Jacob (Israel) were born into the most illustrious lineage of any of those who came upon the earth as mortal beings. All these rewards were seemingly promised, or foreordained, before the world was. Surely these matters must have been determined by the kind of lives we had lived in that premortal spirit world. Some may question these assumptions, but at the same time they will accept without any question the belief that each one of us will be judged when we leave this earth according to his or her deeds during our lives here in mortality. Isn't it just as reasonable to believe that what we have received here in this earth [life] was given to each of us according to the merits of our conduct before we came here? (President Harold B. Lee, "Understanding Who We Are Brings Self-Respect," Ensign, Jan. 1974, p.5.)
