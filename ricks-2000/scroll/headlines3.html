Reeve joins 900 club

by Nathan Hathaway
hat97013@byui.edu
Sports Staff

JoAnn Reeve began coaching volleyball in 1964.

Thirty-seven years later, Reeve notched her 900th junior college volleyball win Thursday as Ricks beat Colorado Northwestern Community College 30-17, 30-12, 30-9.

Remarkably, all those wins, making her the second winningest volleyball coach in NJCAA history, came at Ricks.

Reeve came to Ricks in 1970 as a physical education teacher after teaching and coaching in public schools in Utah and California for six years. In 1973, the head volleyball coach went on leave and Reeve was asked to fill in until she returned.
The head coach never did return.

Reeve immediately made her presence felt as her volleyball team went 29-13 in her first season as coach, winning the Region 18 championship and placing fourth at the NJCAA tournament. In her second year as coach of the volleyball team, Reeve led the Vikings to another Region 18 title and took the team all the way to a national championship.

The 25 years since that national championship season have not been much of a letdown. Reeve has never had a losing season as head coach of the Vikings and got close to another national championship in 1990, losing in the championship game of the NJCAA tournament to Miami-Dade Community College Wolfson.

Reeve was selected as the AVCA/Tachikara West Region Coach of the Year for Junior College/Community College Women’s Volleyball in 1996. Reeve’s career reached its apex in 1998 when she was inducted into the NJCAA Volleyball Coaches Association Hall of Fame.

Of course Reeve would downplay the accomplishment and deflect the praise to others.

“I’ve always said if you live long enough, you’re going to get a few wins,” Reeve said. “I’m proud of all the girls.”

This achievement comes as no surprise to those who know Reeve.

“She deserves it. When you think of Ricks volleyball, you think of JoAnn Reeve. I’m so proud of her,” Larry Stocking, an assistant to Reeve from 1985 to 1999, said. “It’s very fitting that she’s the one here when athletics are out. If they don’t put her in the hall of fame within a couple of years, something’s wrong.”

And who could know Reeve better than her own family?

“She’s never been satisfied until she’s had all that’s available. She’s had a great desire to be at the top,” Reeve’s father, Rex Reeve, said. “It’s the same feeling she’s had about her whole life, about all the things that lead up to it. She’s wanted to be the best.”
