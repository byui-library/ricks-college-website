Prepositions
To recognize the most common phrase, the prepositional phrase, you must learn to recognize prepositions.  This simple idea may help.

Most prepositions are anything that a rabbit can do to a log.
 
It can go through, around, under, across, by, near, to, from, into, over, in, or along the log.
 
The words just listed are prepositions.  A preposition is a connector.  It will be used with a few other words, words which make up the phrase.  (There are more prepositions than those just listed.)
 
Here are some examples of prepositional phrases. The first word is the preposition (blue); the last word is the object of the preposition (red).  Each line makes a prepositional phrase.
 
through the house
around the corner
under the table
across the lawn
by the tree
near the fence
to the store
from the garage
into the car
over the hill
in the army
along the canyon
 
Note these prepositions also: by, with, and for.  They too are prepositions.

Intimations of Immortality
by William Wordsworth
 
1  Our birth is but a sleep and a forgetting:
2  The Soul that rises with us, our life's Star,
3  Hath had elsewhere its setting,
4  And cometh from afar;
5  Not in entire forgetfulness,
6  And not in utter nakedness,
7  But trailing clouds of glory do we come
8  From God, who is our home:
9  Heaven lies about us in our infancy!
10  Shades of the prison-house begin to close
11  Upon the growing boy
12  But he
13  Beholds the light, and whence it flows,
14  He sees it in his joy;
15  The Youth, who daily farther from the east
16  Must travel, still is Nature's Priest,
17  And by the vision splendid
18  Is on his way attended;
19  At length the Man perceives it die away,
20  And fade into the light of common day.
