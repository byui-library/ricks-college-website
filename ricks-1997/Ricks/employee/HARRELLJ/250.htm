COURSE OBJECTIVE: The Ricks College Catalog describes English 250 as an "Appreciation course in various types of literature: short story, novel, poetry, and drama, with careful reading and analysis of significant examples." What does it mean to appreciate something? A standard dictionary definition outlines four aspects of appreciation: 1) recognizing quality or significance; 2) being fully aware of, or sensitive to; 3) having thankfulness or gratitude; and 4) admiring greatly, seeing the value in something.

When dealing with things of quality and depth-such as good literature-our ability to appreciate can increase as we learn more about the subject. The purpose of this course is to increase your ability to maturely judge, value, and admire writings that are described by the term literature.

There will be three tests in this course, one at the end of each unit-literary fiction, drama and popular fiction, and poetry. Tests will be based on assigned readings and class lectures.

PAPERS

You will write three papers of three to four double-spaced pages, due at the end of each unit. Guidelines for papers will be distributed beforehand. Papers will be graded by supervised student groups. Anyone dissatisfied with the grades on their papers may appeal to the teacher for a higher grade.

CLASS PRESENTATIONS

Each student will participate in a group presentation. The presentation categories are five genres of popular fiction-detective, western, romance, sci-fi, and horror; the lives of two American fiction writers-Nathaniel Hawthorne and Flannery O'Connor; and the lives of two poets-William Wordsworth and Robert Frost.

Each presentation will consist of an introduction, a performance of a short drama, and a question-and-answer period. The drama may be performed live, or videotaped beforehand and shown on TV to the class. Groups will be determined on the second class day of the semester, and a guideline sheet for each group will be distributed at that time.

CLASS ATMOSPHERE

In class we will follow the Ricks College Code of Honor and Dress and Grooming Standards. Class atmosphere can often be influenced-positively or negatively-by a few people. Students who are frequently absent, repeatedly late to class, insistent on testing the limits of the dress code; who talk when the teacher or other students are talking; who dominate class discussion to the point that others don't get a chance to speak-those students become a burden to everyone. On the other hand, students who participate enthusiastically in class discussion; who bring interesting ideas to the class and help maintain civil order; and who just get into the spirit of the course-those are the ones who help insure that everyone in the class has a good experience.

LITERATURE AND BELIEF

As students of literature, we don't have to agree with everything we read; but we should read sympathetically, knowing that people we don't agree with are probably more like us than unlike us. Possibly the greatest benefit of reading literature is vicarious experience. Through reading the writings of others, we can "live" experiences with them; we can suffer, rejoice, learn, and laugh. And the more experience we have, the more we will understand ourselves and others.

Great literature-especially the literature of the 20th century-often lacks the typical "happy ending." Some Latter-day Saints are disappointed by this. But before condemning literature, we should consider the scriptures. In the scriptures we find stories of tragedy, trial, sin, and suffering alongside stories of joy, triumph, and righteousness. Even the greatest story of all-the story of the life of the Savior-involves struggling, suffering, and dealing with wicked people as well as glory, righteousness, and love. We should keep this in mind as we deal with the circumstances and characters we will discover in literature this semester.

My goal is not to tell you what to think or believe, but to encourage you to think and believe for yourself. If you have a problem with any aspect of this course, please come to me first so we can discuss your concerns and learn together how to balance faith and study.
