BACKGROUND STUDY GUIDE

This study guide will lead you to sources which:
(1) provide background information on your topic;
    (a) search terms
    (b) authorities
    (c) recommended sources
(2) help you narrow your topic and form an issue question; and
(3) help you locate appropriate research materials.

II. NARROW YOUR TOPIC/FORM AN ISSUE QUESTION

Background reading will suggest issues and sub categories that could focus your research. For example:
    Historical perspective,
    Relationships between topics,
    Terminology (possible search terms), and
    Definitions.
Look back at the research topic ideas you listed in section I.
Select a focus that interests you.
Formulate an "issue question".

SAMPLE ISSUE QUESTION: "Are sanitation standards sufficient to prevent further escherichia coli contamination of beef meat products?" Opposing Views are collections of essays with examples of questions.

III. FIND RESEARCH MATERIAL TO USE IN YOUR PAPER

Consult the Library Catalog and periodical indexes found on any library computer to find research materials.

    A. BOOKS & GOVERNMENT DOCUMENTS Using the Library Online Catalog

 
Before you do a subject search, return to the Library of Congress Subject Headings list with your specific issue in mind.
Review the subject headings from  part I  and add others to find those most appropriate for your narrower topic (see.

        1. Perform subject searches. Write down or print out the complete information about each relevant title, including the call number so that you will be able to locate it.

        2. Next try a general keyword search.
If you get too few hits or too many hits, go to the multi-index feature by clicking on advanced search.
The advanced search screen will help you broaden or narrow your search by using Boolean Logic (and, or, not).
Boolean Logic is a computer and mathematical method of using AND to narrow, OR to expand, and NOT to exclude.
        3. Perform title and author searches to find specific titles from the encyclopedia bibliographies and books written by authorities (see General Encyclopedia or Subject Encyclopedia.).

    B. Articles - Using Periodical Indexes

    1. At any library computer, click on the Start button and go to Magazine Indexes General. Choose one of the following and perform searches with the same keywords you used in the Online Catalog. Narrow or broaden your search as needed. Use the help screens as needed or ask a librarian for assistance.
        ProQuest  an Internet database with many fulltext or image articles
        Academic Abstracts an Internet database with some fulltext artilce
        Newsbank an Internet database for newspaper articles in full text.
        SIRS an Internet database with fulltext articles

    2. Choose Magazine Indexes Special. See if any of these databases would apply to your topic.

    3. Check the printed indexes located at the index tables:
        New York Times newspaper is available on microfilm from 1851 to the present.
        Each field of study will be covered by a printed index.
