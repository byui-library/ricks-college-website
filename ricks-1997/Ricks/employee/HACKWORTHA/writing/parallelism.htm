Parallel Structure
 
Good writing consists of two parts: thought and structure. With writing, you may have good ideas yet a faulty sentence, paragraph, or essay structure. Consequently, you would lose effectiveness. Or you may have well-organized sentence, paragraph, and essay structures which contain no worthwhile ideas. Here, too, you would lose writing effectiveness. Again, good writing must have both good thoughts and good structure.
 
Your task as a writer is to be able to recognize structure in writing and to be able to produce your own effective writing structures at will.  At other places in the workbook, we will deal with the structures of the paragraph and essay.  Here we will consider only the sentence.
 
When you find the parts of speech in a sentence, you are dealing with sentence structure.  When you determine which sentence pattern is being used (SV, SVDO, etc.), you are dealing with structure.  And when you determine if a sentence is simple, compound, complex, etc., you are dealing with sentence structure.
 
Two additional words relating to sentence structure are balanced and parallel.  Here is an example of a balanced sentence.
 
To know her is to love her.
 
"To know her" (an infinitive phrase) is the subject.  "To love her" is the subject complement (predicate noun).   The one phrase is balanced with the other.  
 
Here is an example of parallel structure:
 
To work part time, to earn a 4.0 grade point average, and to run for a student body office are my goals.
 
Note the parallel structure:
 
To work part time,
to earn a 4.0 grade point average, and
to run for a student body office
are my goals.
 
The next example of balanced, parallel structure is by Samuel Johnson.  The quote was taken from a letter addressed to the Earl of Chesterfield, written in 1754.
 
The notice which you have been pleased to take of my labors, had it been early, had been kind; but it has been delayed till I am indifferent, and cannot enjoy it; till I am solitary, and cannot impart it; till I am known, and do not want it.
 
You will see the ordered structure of this sentence as I retype it in a new way.
The notice
which you have been pleased to take of my labors,
had it been early,
had    been kind;
but
it has been delayed
till I am indifferent,     and cannot enjoy        it;
till I am solitary,          and cannot impart      it;
till I am known,            and do not want        it.
 
Study a few more examples:
 
If there exists a good and wise God, then there also exists a progress of mankind toward perfection.  Plato
 
If
there            exists a good and wise God,
then
there also    exists a progress
of mankind toward perfection.
 
Additionally, a writer can make one sentence parallel to another.  The writer can, thereby, establish repetitive patterns with a good effect.  Here is an example:
 
Youth is not a time of life -- it is a state of mind.  It is not a matter of ripe cheeks, red lips, and supple knees; it is a temper of the will, a quality of the imagination, a vigor of the emotions; it is a freshness of the deep spring of life.  Samuel Ullman
 
Again, retyping this will help you see the parallel, balanced structure.
 
Youth is not  a time    of life--
it is         a state        of mind.
it is not   a matter      of ripe cheeks, red lips, and supple knees;
 
it is         a temper     of the will,
a quality                    of the imagination,
a vigor                       of the emotions;
it is       a freshness    of the deep spring of life.
 
You now see what is meant by the term, parallel structure.  With writing, which includes both good thought and structure, it is not just what is said but how it is said.  We respond to order and can understand information better if it is presented in a logical, balanced fashion.
 
Good writing, like any art, "is the imposing of a pattern on experience, and our aesthetic enjoyment in recognition of the pattern."   -- Alfred North Whitehead
 
Sometime in our writing, we violate a sense of order in our sentences by following illogical structures.  We produce, in fact, sentences which have faulty parallel structures.  Here is an example:
 
To read recommended books and watching educational TV are helpful in gaining an education.  Can you see the problem?
 
To read recommended books and
watching educational TV are helpful
in gaining an education.
 
The following revisions correct the above problem.
 
Reading recommended books and
watching educational TV are helpful
in gaining an education.
                       
To read recommended books  and
to watch educational TV
are helpful in gaining an education.
 
Here is another example of faulty structure:
 
Dr. Mills had neither a knowledge of human nature and he did not like people.
 
Here is a possible correction:
 
Dr. Mills had
neither   a knowledge   of human nature
nor         a liking           for the people.
 
You now understand some theory.  Apply your knowledge by doing the exercises on the next page.
 
Faulty Parallelism Drill
 
When revising your writing, avoid faulty parallel structures.   Note the words that are parallel.
 
1.  Linda was both sensitive and responsive.
2.  Either Marge arrives on time or we will leave without her.
 
3.  Jim and Margo must decide to rent an apartment or to buy a house.
 
4.  The students wanted to sketch outdoor scenes, to paint still life, and to enamel copper jewelry.
 
5.  Frank Yamada is the man who has visited us several times and who has shown an interest in buying our home.
 
6.  Bill is tall, muscular, and handsome.
 
7.  Biff Rosen argues constantly, hates school, and distrusts everyone.
 
8.  Trustees, managers, and employees attended the opening session on Thursday.

9.  Jed sat all day eating and having a conversation.
 
10.  Ms. Nelson is a teacher and who writes stories.
 
11.  The art course included drawing, to paint, and sketching.
 
12.  The building designed by the architect and which Bingo built has large cracks in the walls.
 
13.  The class has three projects: writing a term paper, to read three novels, and two plays.
 
14.  His suggestion is brilliant, original, and will work.
 
15.  That painting is gaudy and without meaning.
 
16.  Midway through the examination, Peter found his mind  wandering and that his vision had become blurred.
 
17.  While the children were swimming, Loni and I made the  salad and set the table, and then the steaks were grilled.
 
18.  The animals not only require a lot of attention, but Madelene finds that caring for them is expensive and she gets very tired.
