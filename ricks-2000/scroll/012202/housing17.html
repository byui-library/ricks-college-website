Olympic housing prices go through the roof

by Carl Neil Ekman III
EKM98001@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Lodging for the Olympics is becoming a profitable market. Property owners are raising prices for high rolling spectators.

Salt Lake City and other nearby cities that are hosting Olympic events are being bombarded with an entourage of people. Spectators from all over the world are going to Salt Lake City to be a part of the history.

The closer in distance you get to the events, the more expensive it becomes.

The prime places to stay in Utah are Salt Lake City, Park City and Snow Basin, according to Eventhousing2002.com.

Already a highly visited tourist town, Park City has many places for lodging. Now that many of the Olympic events are located there, it is possible to find hotels and other housing within walking distance of the Olympic venues. This kind of convenience does not come at a low price.

At Marriott Vacation Club Summit Watch in Park City, a two-bedroom apartment-style condominium with a maximum occupancy of six to eight people costs $795 plus tax per night, according to marriott.com.

Papineau Properties, a small family-owned property management company, normally rents a house called Old Town Original for $550 per night. They have raised the price to $2,000 per night during the Olympic period. They are also providing an optional catering service for a price, according to 2002winterrentals.com.

“It’s a shift in demand of the housing for the Olympics,” Kurt Knight, a senior at BYU, said. “It’s a great deal for the home owners in Utah.”

If renting a house, there are some larger houses for as low as $600 per night. There is a two-bedroom house priced at $1,200 per night or a six-bedroom house with 5.5 baths at $14,900 per night. Prices start high and climb fast, according to 2002winterrentals.com.

Making $14,900 per night, houses being rented every day over the 17-day, Olympic Games will accumulate $253,300 before taxes.

The house going for $14,900 is 7,200 square feet, according to 2002winterrentals.com.

Renting out a house for thousands of dollars per night can bring a lot of money, but some residents, such as Duane Burton, from Bountiful, Utah, feel that renting out apartments and houses is not going to make as many wealthy as expect to be.

There are many risks involved in letting other live in your house.

“You’ve got a heavy risk of damage and [cigarette] smoke,” Clint Gurney of Salt lake City said.

Utah.com even advertises a rental house that is non-smoking.

The Internet is helping those who want to rent their houses and other lodgings. The Internet is helping in ways that weren’t possible in the past.

“Information is power,” Kevin Twitchell, of the computer science department at BYU-Idaho, said. “[And] the Internet can help you find information.”

But the Internet can hold unwanted surprises.

“The Internet has no central point of control,” Twitchell said. “Be very ,very careful that [information] comes from a valid source.”

Buying a house can also be pricey, as Chris Callis, Olympic speed skater, found out.

“[The market is plentiful, but] the price is not right,” Callis said.

For a one-room, one-bath condominium in Park City, the price range is around $100,000.

Though house renting has gone up for the Olympic period, it has not greatly effected house buying, Kay R. Ashton, Utah district manager of Home Loan Corp., said.

The house prices are staying at a flat rate overall because of the recession last year.
