Ricks College News Release
September 28, 2000

Proposed building at Ricks
to retain name of Jacob Spori

REXBURG, Idaho — A proposed building that will replace the historic Jacob Spori Building at Ricks College will retain the name of the college’s first administrator.

Jacob Spori’s name and legacy will live on at Ricks College, said President David A. Bednar in making the announcement Wednesday night at the President’s Club banquet in Rexburg. Those in attendance included members of the Executive Committee of the Board of Trustees and donors to the college who belong to the President’s Club.

Although the design of the new building has not been finalized, Bednar says the new building will reflect the "spirit of Ricks" and be a fitting tribute to the school’s first principal.

Matt Geddes, dean of the Division of Performing and Fine Arts, explained the building will not be a replica of the existing building. "I am excited about this new building and the concept of preserving the legacy while moving to the future."

Geddes said making a replica would be like having your father, who just lost his wife, "come home and tell his children, ‘I found another wife and she looks just like your mother.’ "

He said the new building will have a similar appearance. It will be a vertical building with formal, symmetrical design and pitched roof. Unlike other buildings on campus that face inward, he said it will face toward downtown Rexburg "as an acknowledgment of our community." While Geddes did not say what the exterior walls will be made of, he said it will not be built of buff-colored brick like most other building on campus.

The president added that some exterior features of the existing building will be removed and likely incorporated into the design of the interior of the new building.

Bednar said the college has undertaken several projects to preserve the memory of the original building. It commissioned several pieces of artwork focusing on the building, commissioned a model that has been built by retired Ricks architect Reed Wyatt, filmed the interior and exterior of the buildings, and even created a digital tour of the building that is available on CD ROM.

The president added that a historical photograph will be taken on Saturday, Oct. 14, at noon during a special Spori Celebration in front of the Spori and he invited anyone wanting to be in the picture to come.

Because of the decision that Ricks will become a baccalaureate-granting university, architects and campus planners are taking a second look at the final design of the new building. This has caused a delay of when work on the new building will begin. However, the college still intends to begin construction sometime in 2001.

The existing Spori Building is now vacant except for a few rooms that are occupied by the college’s two radio stations and Graphic Services. They too will soon move out so work can begin on tearing down the structure that was built in the early 1900s.

The Spori Building had been a major concern to college leaders for several decades. Determining the appropriate course of action was particularly challenging because of the historic value of the building, and much time and effort was expended in trying to find ways to preserve and/or restore the facility. After extensive research and deliberation, it was determined that restoring the building was not a feasible option.

Ricks officials selected the architectural firm of FFKR of Salt Lake City to design the new building because they believe it has the vision and capability to design a building that will reflect the heritage and legacy of the aging building. Ricks officials were impressed with FFKR’s extensive expertise in historical work.

Currently, FFKR is designing a replacement of the LDS Church’s historic Nauvoo Temple in Illinois. It also did the designs for the conversion of the Vernal (Utah) Tabernacle into an LDS temple, the conversion of the Hotel Utah into the Joseph Smith Memorial Building in Salt Lake City and the restoration of the Noyes Administration Building at Snow College in Ephraim, Utah.

Jacob Spori set an example of selfless service that has been characteristic of many people who have contributed to the college over its 112-year history. He joined the LDS Church in Switzerland and immigrated to the United States in 1879. He and his family arrived in Rexburg in 1888 and later that year Spori was named principal of the church’s new academy in Rexburg.

After teaching all day, Spori performed janitorial work in the evenings. With the help of some of his students, he sawed and chopped the wood that was used to heat the school, which was held inside the local church building. Uncounted hours were spent visiting area residents to find the means to send their children to the school. People were urged to pay their tuition with any produce they could spare from their farms.

Spori served as principal of Bannock Stake Academy for just three years. During financially difficult times, he suggested to the Board of Education that one way to cut the deficit was for him to work on his farm and not draw his salary while still teaching and administering at the school. In addition to applying his salary toward the debt, he worked on the railroad for a time, using some of his earnings to help pay salaries of other teachers.
