Summary  Guidelines

1.    The summary is to be typed, with the chapters and verses highlighted just like the example summary at the end of these guidelines!  You will be allowed to single space and possibly shrink your essay so as to fit on one page (front and back).  I am into saving paper and ink (your hard earned money).  Just don't shrink it so small that I can't recognize anything.

2.    Somewhere at the top of your page, you need to list your Name, class and hour, summary number, and the scripture block.  You are responsible for any mix-ups due to not putting the summary number (or wrong number) and scripture block at the top of the page.

3.     Using the scripture block assigned in your syllabus, you will read a certain amount of verses at a time and then summarize what you read in typewritten form. Book of Mormon students will  summarize approximately every 10 but no more than 15 verses.  You will have better success if you can try to divide the verses up in logical expressions of thought.  New Testament students will summarize according to the outlined paragraphs. Paragraphs are marked by a paragraph indicator ( ¶ ) (The first verse of every chapter is an assumed paragraph indicator.)    An example summary is provided at the end of these guidelines.  Generally, two to three lines is acceptable for each block of verses.  Some will do more (just because you are wonderful) but don't get carried away.  However, a bunch of "one liners" will result in the loss of points according to my discretion.

4.    At the conclusion of your summary, you will respond to what you have read.  This response should be 3 to 4 lines minimum and should reflect the personal thoughts and insights that were developed during your preparation.  Failure to include the response results in the loss of 3 points.  Quick, thoughtless responses will also result in the loss of points according to my discretion.

5.    If your summary consists of more than one page, it must be stapled or it will not be accepted.  I know this sounds quite harsh, but I have had too many mishaps where folded corners or tape caught another summary and the other summary was not recorded because of it.  So invest in a stapler.

6.    The summaries are due at the beginning of class.  Place them on the counter at the front of the classroom as you walk in..  Any summaries received after class begins (due to tardiness) will result in the loss of 3 points.  If you are late, please don't hand me your summary when you walk in.  Just be seated and write "late, -3 points" on the top of your summary.  Then wait to turn it in at the end of class.  Better yet, get here on time.  Any summaries turned in after I leave the classroom will result in the loss of 5 points.

7.    I repeat, the summaries are due at the beginning of class.  Computer problems, faulty alarm clocks, roommates, etc. are not excuses for late summaries.  Be sure to give yourself plenty of time before class to allow for any mishaps that need extra attention.

8.    Due to the hectic nature of returning your summaries every day, the summaries will not be returned to you.  That is why I want you to type and save these for yourself.  Every week or so I will pass around a score sheet to let you know how you did on your summaries for that week.  The score you receive will reflect the quality and content of your summary.  Lack of effort or failing to finish the summary will obviously result in the loss of points according to my discretion.

9.    In the event that you miss class or find that you were not able to finish the summary, be smart and turn in what you have.  Some points are better than no points.  You will be allowed to turn in late summaries with 5 points being the maximum.  Points can still be deducted.  For instance, if you forgot your questions on a late summary, you would receive 3 points.  The five point score  fails the assignment, but it is five more points than you would have had. They must be full quality and must be turned no later than one week immediately following the original due date.  For example, if you missed a summary for class on a Wednesday, you have until the beginning of class the following Wednesday to turn in that summary for late points.  After that, there is no making up the summary.

10.    Don't make the mistake, as some have in the past, as seeing these summaries as mere busy work.  I promise that if you will prayerfully and thoughtfully prepare these summaries for class each day, you will find the spirit more willing to reveal to you a greater understanding of the doctrines of the kingdom because of your intense preparation.  Also, some of you will immediately see ways you could cheat on these summaries.  Please read the end notes of your syllabus again.

Luke 15-16
Luke 15

1-2 As tax collectors and sinners approach Jesus to be taught, Pharisees and scribes chide the Savior for associating with the      lower class of society.

3-7 The Savior hurls three parables of rebuke at the Pharisees and Scribes denouncing their self-righteousness. The first parable concerns a man who lost one sheep of his ninety and nine. In the parable the Savior used sarcasm to denounce the Pharisees when he said God rejoices over one soul who repents than "over ninety and nine just person, which need no repentance."

8-10 The next parable deals with a woman who lost a coin. Just as the woman searched the house to find her lost coin, so should the Pharisees and Scribes be as diligent helping the lost souls of Israel.

11-32 The final parable is the parable of the lost son (prodigal son). The parable equated the publicans and sinners with the lost son while at the same time the Pharisees and Scribes were represented by the son who disfavored the return of his brother.

Luke 16

1-12 Having denounced the Pharisees and Scribes the Savior now began to teach the publicans and sinners. He gave the parable of the unjust steward hoping to encourage the new converts to continue in the same creativity, resourcefulness, and forethought in the church as they did in the unjust activities they participated in before their conversion. He then encouraged them to use wealth to help build up the kingdom of God rather than to build up themselves.

13-18 The Savior taught that we cannot serve two masters. The Pharisees, "who were covetous," began to deride the Savior's teachings. Jesus then reproved the Pharisees because of their misguided lifestyle.

19-31 The Savior then taught the parable of the rich man and Lazarus condemning the covetous nature of the Pharisees.
