Hecate is most often portrayed as a dark & evil manifestation who wanders in graveyards or haunts dark nights with terrifying hounds of hell. This distorted image comes from the twisted minds of those who fear Her power: Those sad souls who have lost their connection with the chthonic, who shun their own shadow, & fear what they do not understand.

The Christian Church, & those it serves, fear Her, for She has power they cannot comprehend. Hecate is one of those ancient deities who come to us from a primordial time before the Olympians & the Solar gods of the patriarchs. She is patron to all who stand on the boundary between life & death; midwives, healers & witches.

Some say that Hecate is the daughter of Nyx, ageless Goddess of the night. Others believe that She is born of the Titans Phoibe & Koios. Her name means 'most shining one', & early representations show a Goddess of both beauty & power, carrying a torch & wearing a head-dress of stars. She is a moon Goddess, & as 'Hekate Triformus' represents the lunar phases of New, Full & Waxing. The Olympians 'adopted' Her after they had defeated the Titans, but She was not of the same kind, & never lived amongst them. During this time Hecate's power was still recognised. Zeus gave Her dominion over Heaven, Earth & the Underworld, & they shared the right to grant or withhold gifts from humanity. Hecate was worshipped as Goddess of abundance & eloquence, & She is still generous to those who recognise Her.

But Hecate's power was to fade. In later myths She is a daughter of Zeus who rules the Underworld & the waning Moon. The Greeks began to emphasise Her darker aspects; Hecate as Goddess of the Dead & Queen of Witches.

As the power of the Solar Gods rose, Hecate became demonized, until by the Middle Ages She was reduced to a parody of an evil crone.
Yet beneath the distortions of history there lies a deeper truth. Hecate is awesome & can be terrifying, for She rules all that is outside our ken: Death, & the dark intuitive wisdom that is beyond the conscious mind. Such wisdom comes through dreams & whispers, mediumship & divination. It is the inspired vision of artists & seers. For some it may be too much & bring the madness of lunacy: Hecate's power can poison as well as heal.

Our culture denies Her realms. Death is a taboo subject, & the old are hidden away. Hecate is the Wise Old Crone who knows death & does not fear it: Death is a transformation & brings renewal through the fertility of decomposition. Hecate's torch guides the soul to the Underworld, into the dark womb, the cauldron, a place of regeneration & change.

Hecates' Companions.

All wild animals are sacred to Hecate & She sometimes appears three headed as dog, horse & bear or dog, snake & lion. But the creatures of darkness & of the earth are most sacred to Her; ravens, owls, crows, snakes & dragons.

Both the yew & the black poplar are also sacred to Hecate. The leaves of the black poplar are dark on one side & light on the other, symbolising the boundary between the worlds. The yew has long been associated with the Underworld. It is the longest living creature on earth, which may be why it is so often found in graveyards as a symbol of eternal life. Yew berries carry Hecate's power, & can bring wisdom or death. They are highly poisonous, but if prepared correctly can bring inspirational visions, & the potion in Hecate's cauldron contains 'slips of yew'.

Hecate has close links with Hermes. As messenger of the Gods, it was Hermes who would sometimes guide the dead to the Underworld, & some say that Hecate & Hermes were lovers.

Sacred times & places.

Hecate is most properly worshipped at a crossroad, the place where three roads meet. The crossroads symbolise Hecate's triple nature & Her all seeing ability. Here travellers may ask for protection on their journeys, or witches meet to learn Her mysteries. Hecate is traditionally worshipped on the eve of the full moon, but you may find a waning or dark moon appropriate. Samhain is especially significant to Hecate, but several Festival days are celebrated in Her honour: The 13 th August is the time to ask for her blessing on the coming harvest, for as Goddess of Storms Hecate has the power to destroy the crop before it can be cut.

November 16 th is Hecate Night. If you only honour her once in the year, this is the time to do it! In the distant past a black dog was seen as a suitable sacrifice, but personally I would recommend an offering of food, traditionally known as 'Hecate's Supper'. She is partial to honey, & magic mushrooms if you have them. (Well, it is supposed to be a sacrifice!)
