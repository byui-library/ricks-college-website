Ricks College News Release
January 31, 2001

Devotional: Elder Harold Brown

REXBURG, Idaho — Elder Harold Brown, the LDS Church’s director of Welfare Services, taught about "the marvelous gift of choice" during the weekly devotional at Ricks College Jan. 30.

"Choices will affect who and what we are in the future," Elder Brown said. "Agency is as basic as life itself and cannot be taken from us. Even in the most dire and difficult circumstances, we may still choose our course. As students, your choice to study, learn and master a body of knowledge will significantly increase your range of choices after you graduate."

He added, "The power to choose is within each of us and nothing can take it from us. We have the power to choose our course in life – to choose good or evil when the choice is placed before us."

Elder Brown illustrated his points on making choices by sharing some examples of associates who felt they had lost their power to choose.

"Individuals suffering even from the strongest addictions still have agency and power to choose righteousness. However, their desires increasingly diminish with a continuation of sin," he said. "Every act of disobedience takes away light and truth and gives Satan more power over us. (But) each time you exercise your agency and will to make correct choices, even in small things, you gain more light and truth and are more able to make the right choice with even greater temptations and challenges."

He continued, "It is helpful to remember when faced with a wrong choice, the Holy Ghost will prompt us to do right. To partake of sin, we must first resist or reject the spirit, then partake of the sin."

Elder Brown counseled listeners to seek advice from those whom they trust, love and respect, especially those in authoritarian positions when making big decisions.

"Listening to those who are in a position of authority or who have had experience beyond our own or who know the dangers we may face will help us choose wisely," he said.

Elder Brown concluded, "Yes, we have the power to choose our course in life. We must accept the consequences of what we choose. When your challenges are difficult and your burdens hard to bear, remember this: God will never forsake you nor forget you."

Next Tuesday’s speaker will be Sandra Rogers, the associate academic vice president at Brigham Young University in Provo, Utah. She will speak at 2 p.m. in the Hart Auditorium. Devotionals are broadcast live and repeated again on KRIC-FM.
