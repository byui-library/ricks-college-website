Winter can provide students with many activities

by Katie Mcmurray
mcm00005@byui.edu
Scroll Staff

Snowmobiles head down a groomed trail in Duxbury, Vt., Wednesday, last winter. Students can participate in many activities, including snowmobiling in the Rexburg area during the winter.
Approximately eight months out of the year BYU-Idaho students find themselves snowed in.

Because of Rexburg’s long winter, the anticipated typical “snow day” may seem endless and BYU-I students look for new things to do.

Those who are not fans of being cold and wet can find things to do indoors.

Renting a movie or going out to a local theater only costs a couple dollars.

Sipping hot chocolate, making microwave s’mores, eating soup and leaving the door of a heated oven propped open are ways to keep warm indoors.

Playing card games, board games or Nintendo can distract from the snowfall outside.

Gathering together at someone’s house can be just as fun as playing outside in the snow.

Combine apartments and keep warm — the more, the merrier.

For those who can’t wait to build their snowmen and sled down hills, there are many activities outdoors, including a new ice-skating rink which is being built on campus.

“I enjoy sledding and skiing outside and then going inside for a nice mug of hot chocolate,” Ashley Blackburn, a freshman from Crofton, Md., said.

Put the powder to work by building snow forts, igloos and snowmen and making snow angels. These are activity anyone can enjoy.

The intramural fields, dorm hills and sand dunes act as sledding hills for BYU-I students.

Sleds can be an inexpensive investment that provides opportunities to make fun memories during these cold winter months.

“Buying a sled last year was probably the best investment I made. I had so much fun going to the intramural fields to go sledding. There were so many great memories of flying off the jumps,” Kimberly Hayes, a sophomore from Summerville, S.C. said.

Snowmobiling and tubing are activities that can be participated in nearby at places such as Island Park and Yellowstone and require a little more money.
