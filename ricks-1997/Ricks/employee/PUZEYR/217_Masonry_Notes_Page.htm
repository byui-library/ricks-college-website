• Describe how to power mix mortar.

• Describe how steel reinforcing bars, metal anchors, and K-web or truss type design reinforcement
   is installed.
• Describe how and where flashing is used.
• Where and how are control joints installed?
• Our masonry lab mortar does not include cement to prevent it from getting very hard. The lab mix
   is a ratio of 1 part lime to 3 parts sand and enough water to provide proper adhesion.
• What is the difference between Portland Cement and masonry cement?
• A good mortar mix would consist of the following:
   1. 1 bag Portland Cement (1 cubic foot or 94 lbs.)
   2. 1 bag hydrated lime (50 lbs.)
   3. 6 cubic feet of sand (7 shovels per cubic foot average)
   4. Water (enough to make firm paste)
• Power mixer procedure:
   1. Add 1/2 water to empty running mixer
   2. Add 3 cubic feet sand
   3. Add 1 bag of Portland Cement
   4. Add 1 bag of hydrated lime
   5. Add 3 cubic feet sand
   6. Add additional water to right consistency
   7. Mix for a minimum of 7 minutes
• Galvanized wall ties are typically placed 16 inches horizontally and 16 inches vertically.

Lab Performance

1. Become familiar with power mixer and mortar materials in preparation to mix a batch of mortar.
2. Mix a batch of lab mortar in power mixer.
3. Mortar in the masonry lab is mixed without cement. Mix 1 part line to 3 parts sand.
4. To develop your basic manipulation skills, practice the following techniques:
   a. Loading a trowel
   b. Troweling the mortar, first on the edge of the mud board and then on an 8 foot 2 x 4 bed
   c. Furrowing the bed
   d. Laying brick
   e. Cutting off excess mortar from the bed joint
   f. Buttering
5. Practice using the spirit level to check the horizontal level and vertical plumb of the brick in a
    course.
6. Lay brick on a 2 x 4 bed with a line.
7. Clean all tools and power mixers.

Lab Performance

1. Build a single wythe masonry wall 64 inches long with a 16" corner. Lay masonry units 10 courses
    high. All horizontal joints should be 3/8 wide. This wall can be built on top of the 3 course CMU
    wall from Chapter 7 lab performance. Running bond should be used.

2. Dry bond the horizontal course and mark layout.

3. Build leads at least 4 courses high and then use a line to fill in brick units. Watch closely the vertical
    height of wall.

4. Use a spirit level to keep wall straight, level, plumb, and square.

5. Use a rack joint on mortar joints.

Lab Performance

1. Build a concrete block wall 64" high with a 16", 90º corner. Begin with a full mortar bed laid on tar
    paper directly on the concrete floor.

2. Dry bond the beginning course. Use a running bond.

3. Lay block three courses high. Total wall will have 12 full block and three 1/2 block.

4. Begin by building leads. Use a spirit level to keep leads straight, level, plumb, and square.

5. After leads are built, use a mason line to keep masonry units straight.

6. Measure the vertical height of the leads. Keep all joints 3/8".

7. Use a jointing tool for concave joints.

8. Brick lab assignment from Chapter 5 can be constructed on top of this wall.
