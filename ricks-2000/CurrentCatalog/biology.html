The Department of Biology has designed courses that address the needs of students in all three major areas of study at Ricks College. For those who are in the General Education program, biology courses will satisfy the requirements for graduation; for those who choose to study biology outside of their major, classwork will allow them to acquire knowledge which can assist them in the raising and teaching of their families.
Those with majors that stress the biological sciences, as well as other students, will find that the Department of Biology provides the very best training possible in undergraduate study.

Due to the increased stress on science expressed in the continuing national debate about quality education, students are encouraged to explore the biological sciences, even where it is outside of their major interest.

Elective courses in these subjects give credit toward graduation and also transfer credit to other institutions. General Education requirements include eight to nine hours of classwork in the Natural Sciences. Of these, four can be satisfied in Biology.

For those who have the majors listed below, recommended course work will be listed on the following pages, however, students are encouraged to plan their program with the aid of an advisor.

Biology Education, Botany, Ecology, Genetics, Marine Biology, Medical Technology, Microbiology, Pre-Dental Hygiene, Pre-Dentistry, Pre-Forestry and Pre-Range Science, Pre-Medicine, Pre-Optometry, Pre-Pharmacy, Pre-Physician Assistant, Pre-Veterinary Medicine, Range Management, Wildlife Management and Zoology

Biology students majoring in other areas of biology may graduate with an Associate in Arts and Sciences Degree with a major in General Studies Advised in Agricultural and Biological Sciences.

Since the curricula for these majors varies with programs and prospective transfer schools, students are urged to write to transfer institutions for catalogs and guidance. The General Education requirements and advisor consultations will determine graduation requirements at Ricks College.

You must write to the various PA Schools you are considering for their specific prerequisites. Most PA schools require that you have either a BS or BA degree prior to application, so you must plan a degree program, such a as Microbiology, Nursing, Paramedicine, Respiratory Therapy, or some other major. All PA Schools require you to visit with and shadow a practicing PA. In addition many hours of either volunteer or paid work in a health care field are required prior to application. These hours must be documented by the supervisor with whom you have worked. It would be to your advantage to begin this exposure prior to your arrival at Ricks College, and continue during subsequent summers and while at school.

The purpose of an Associate in Arts and Sciences degree with a major in Pre-Physician Assistant (PA) is to guide the prospective student toward the completion of the prerequisite course work and those non-cognitive requirements needed prior to application to professional school. Since there are over one hundred PA schools in the USA, each with their own specific set of entrance requirements, and because the applicant pool if far greater than the number of seats available, it is essential that the Pre-PA student work closely with the advisor to select the PA schools to which they would apply and what is needed to become a viable candidate.

It is important for the student to visit with the Pre-PA advisor during the first two days after arrival on campus

The Botany, Pre-Forestry and Range Science majors are designed to prepare students to transfer to a four year institution where the student can complete a B.S. degree in one of these sciences. The required classes for the majors are the required prerequisites for upper division classes at most four year schools.

 

COURSE DESCRIPTIONS

BS 100* Principles of Biology 3
Three hours of lecture per week

Not open to students having completed BS 200 or 208. (When taken with BS 102, Principles of Biology Laboratory, these two classes will fill the General Education Biology requirement.) An introduction to biology, designed around the areas of cell structure, function, laws of heredity, evolution and ecology. There are several other classes offered in the department that will fill the GE requirements. They may fit your specific needs better than BS 100

BS 102* Principles of Biology Laboratory 1
Lab Fee: $5 lab

This is a three hour independent study audio tutorial laboratory experience in general biology for non majors. This laboratory is designed to accompany BS 100, Principles of Biology, and may be taken concurrently with BS 100 or anytime after completing BS 100. This laboratory counts as credit when taken with any other biology course. Laboratories involve hands on experiences in science investigation, the microscope, the cell, classification, ecology and characteristics and examples of the kingdoms Monera, Fungi, Protista, Animalia, and Plantae.

BS 118* Field Biology 4
Three one hour lectures and one two hour lab per week
Travel Fee: $50

Field identification techniques, ecological relationships, field study methods, and conservation/ preservation practices of natural populations of living species.
Four different approaches to this course may be offered: (1) Field Botany, (2) Field Zoology, (3) Environmental Biology, (4) Wildlife Management.

BS 120* Biology of Cultivated Plants 4
Three one hour lectures and one two hour lab per week
Lab Fee: $15

Plants grown in homes and gardens are used to introduce biological principles. Laboratory work emphasizes individual projects utilizing living plants.

BS 130* Plant Life 4
Three one hour lectures and one two hour lab per week
Lab Fee: $15

Major plant groups and their impact on man are studied. Four different approaches to this course may be offered: (1) Wild flowers, ( 2) Trees and shrubs, (3) Survey of the plant kingdom, (4) Plants and Society.

BS 150* General Zoology 3
Three one hour lectures per week

An exposure to the variety of animals in the world with emphasis on diversity, structure and function, economic importance and behavior.

BS 150L* General Zoology Laboratory 1
One two hour laboratory per week
Lab Fee: $5

Selected activities that reinforce the concepts introduced in lecture

BS 151 Principles of Wildlife Management 3
Two one hour lectures and one two hour lab per week
Travel Fee: $30

Emphasis is on the life histories and management of fresh water fisheries, big game waterfowl, upland game birds, furbearers, and predators.

BS 152 lnsects and Man: Harmful and Beneficial Aspects 3
Two one hour lectures and one two hour lab per week

A practical study of insects and their affects on man and animals.

BS 154 Parasites of Man 1
Block plan only
Two one hour lectures per week

The occurrence, life cycles, pathology and emphasis on prevention of protozoan, helminth and arthropod parasites common to man.

Recommended for students entering the health professions and fields of education, and also those who may travel to other parts of the world.

BS 176* Heredity 4
Three one hour lectures and one two hour lab per week

A General Education course covering Mendelian genetics, interactions, sexlinkage, probability, chromosome aberrations and change in chromosome number, linkage, population genetics and molecular genetics. Students will be required to solve genetic problems in the above areas.

BS 199 Pre-Professional Orientation 1
Fifteen hours of orientation, counseling, and guest lectures

A program to orient and counsel prospective students interested in pre-professional (Pre Chiropractic, Pre-Dentistry, Pre-Medicine, Pre-Optometry, Pre Osteopathic, Pre-Pharmacy, Pre-Podiatric, and Pre-Veterinary).

BS 200* Introduction to Biology 4
Five one hour lectures per week
Previous credit in Chemistry 105 or equivalent

An intensive introduction to biology, including cell structure and function, basic metabolic pathways, genetics and ecology.

BS 200L Introduction to Biology Lab 1
Prerequisite: Concurrent or previous enrollment in BS 200

 

*Gives GE credit in the Biology area of the Natural Science requirement.

BS 202* Natural Resource Management 4
Three one hour lectures and one three hour lab per week
Travel Fee: $50

The management of natural resources such as wildlife, fisheries, forests, range, and recreational lands. The orientation of the course will be ecological with emphasis on ecosystems, energy budgets, and interrelationships .

BS 204 Vertebrate and Invertebrate Strategies 4
Three hours of lecture and one three hour lab per week
Lab Fee: $10

Comparative organization and evolutionary significance of adaptive morphological, physiological, behavioral, reproductive and ecological differences in vertebrates and invertebrates.

BS 208* General Botany 4
Three one hour lectures and one two hour lab per week
Travel Fee: $15

For students majoring in Botany, Agriculture, and Forestry. An introduction to cell structure, plant anatomy and physiology, reproduction, heredity, evolution and ecology.

BS 209 An Evolution Survey of Plants 4
Prerequisite: BS 208 or permission of instructor
Three one hour lectures and one three hour lab per week (Winter only)
Travel Fee: $15

Structure, function, evolution and diversity of procaryotes, algae, fungi, and higher plants.

BS 210 Plant Classification 4
Prerequisite: BS 208 or permission of instructor
Two hours lecture and two two hour lab per week
Travel Fee: $30

Identification, naming and grouping of vascular plants emphasizing families of native flowers. (Fall only)

BS 212 Invertebrate Zoology 4
Three hours lecture and one three hour lab per week
Lab Fee: $10

Emphasis on classification, anatomy, physiology, behavior, ecology and the medical and agricultural significance of the invertebrates.

BS 221* General Microbiology 3
Prerequisite: Knowledge of the structure and function of the cell
Three one hour lectures per week

An introduction to the fundamental biological processes as they are seen in bacteria and viruses. Emphasis is placed on the role of these micro organisms in causing disease in man and methods used for the prevention and control of these diseases.

BS 222* Microbiology Laboratory 1
Prerequisite: Previous or concurrent registration in BS 221
One two hour laboratory per week
Lab Fee: $5

A series of laboratory exercises demonstrating the basic principles of microbiology.

BS 234* Appreciation of Nature 3
Two one hour lectures and one two hour lab per week
Lab Fee: $10

This course will have major emphasis upon Ecology, Biological Conservation, Biodiversity and Behavior. Fourteen laboratories will use the scientific method to teach the students how to experiment.

This class is designed for Elementary Education majors.

BS 240 Neurobiology 3

Introduction to neurobiology, covering the nervous system cellular communication, neural basis of behavior, neural systems in learning and development and hormonal influences.

BS 250* Environmental Biology 3
Three hours lecture per week

Global and local environmental issues and their effect on ecosystem structure and function.

BS 250L* Environmental Biology Lab 1
One two hour lab per week

Selected activities that reinforce lecture material

BS 264* Human Anatomy and Physiology I 4
Three one hour lectures and one two hour lab per week
Lab Fee: $5

First part of a two semester course to prepare students for further study in the health and medical fields. Includes basic biochemistry, structure and function of the cell, tissues, skeleton, muscles, and nervous systems of the human body. Both BS 264 and BS 265 must be taken to receive credit at BYU.

BS 265*Human Anatomy and Physiology II 4
Prerequisite: BS 264 or permission of instructor
Three One hour lectures and one two hour lab per week, Lab Fee: $5

Second part of a two semester course to prepare students for further study in the health and medical fields. Includes structure and function of the circulatory, lymphatic, respiratory, urinary, digestive, endocrine and reproductive systems and embryology.

BS 266 Gross Anatomy 1
Prerequisite:BS 264, BS 265
Consent of the instructor
One two hour lab per week
Lab Fee: $10

Structure of the human body systems with emphasis obtained through cadaver dissection.

 

*Gives GE credit in the Biology area of the Natural Science requirement.

BS 275* Genetics 4
Prerequisite: BS 200, BS 208, or high grade in other biology courses with instructor's permission
Three hours lecture per week plus one two hour lab per week
Lab Fee: $10

Genetics of procaryotes and eucaryotes including DNA structure and synthesis, Mendelian genetics, genetics of sex linkage and genetic mapping, quantitative inheritance, effects of chromosomal aberrations, and population genetics.

BS 281 Pathophysiology 3
Prerequisite: BS 264, 265, Chem 101 or equivalent
Three one hour lectures per week

Designed for nurses and allied health professions. An application of basic principles of physiology to pathological conditions.

Fills requirement for Zoo 361 at BYU.

BS 284* Ecology 4
Three one hour lectures and one three hour lab per week
Lab Fee: $50

Interrelationships between plants and animals characteristics of aquatic, mountain, and desert ecosystems with emphasis on structure and function.

BS 286 Nursing Pharmacology 3
Prerequisite: Concurrent enrollment in the nursing program or BS 264, BS 265, Chem 101 or 105 or permission of instructor
Three one hour lectures per week

This course is designed to help nursing students understand the basic principles of pharamaco kinetics, pharmaco dynamics, and the clinical application of drugs. The mechanisms of drug action are emphasized to correlate physiological and pharmacological principles.

BS 290 Special Problems 1-3
Prerequisite: Consent of instructor and Department Chair prior to registration

Faculty student consultation will determine a special area of study and/or research problem that will give students greater preparation for advanced work in their field of interest. Term of enrollment, credit and other details will be arranged with instructor.

 

*Gives GE credit in the Biology area of the Natural Science requirement.
