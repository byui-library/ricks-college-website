Students and faculty react to terrorism attack

by Marie MacKay
mac00005@byui.edu
Religion and Special Sections Editor


Students and faculty have been asked to remain calm and proceed with business as usual despite the terrorist actions that have occurred in our nation's capitol and New York City today.

"All of us at BYU-Idaho are deeply concerned about the apparent terrorist actions in our nations' capitol and New York City," President David A. Bednar said. "We feel it best, however, to proceed with business and classes 'as usual' on our campus."

All classes will remain in session and all offices will remain open. If a concern for safety on campus does arise, students and faculty will be notified, President Bednar said.

Emotions and feelings have been expressed during classes all over campus and students and teachers discuss the events have taken place today.

"We just wanted to pray for families, our country and the leaders to handle everything that is going on," Robyn Bergstrom said of her small group communication class. "It was totally somber and quiet."

Other classes have offered prayers in behalf of the many people struggling from today's events including Jason Flora's Humanities 201 class.

"This is just surreal. Talk about the last days," Flora said to his class.

Students gathered around the Manwaring Center TV engulfed in the breaking news shown by CNN.

"I think this situation is a tragedy. I just hope that nothing else will happen to the country," Carl Reese, a freshman from Salt Lake City, said.

"To me this is a tragic thing. It's a shock to the world is what it is! It was heart-wrenching watching it -there's not much more you can say than that," Teresa Goates, a sophomore from Gillette, Wyo. said.

"This reminds me of when we stood with horror around the television at BYU when Desert Storm started. I'm concerned about peoples' lives," Dean Cloward, Assistant Dean of Students, said.

"It seems like it's a conspiracy, but it's hard to imagine how passenger planes could be taken over with the security we have today," Carolee Coy, a sophomore from Eagle River, Alaska, said.

Around 9 a.m. planes crashed into each of the twin towers of the World Trade Center in Manhatten and an aircraft crashed into the Pentagon about an hour after the crash in New York.
