Registration Procedures:


Q:                  When can I register?  Is there a deadline for registering?


R:                   BYU-Idaho registration is done by credit-level check.  Credit level includes credits earned and currently enrolled classes.  Transfer credits are not counted into the credit level until processed by the Registrar’s Office.  The schedule for registering can be found by clicking on “Registration,” entering ID and PIN numbers.  A student can register for classes until the last day to add classes.

Adding Full Classes:


Q:                  The classes I want to get into are full, can I still add somehow?


R:                   Students can go to the class on the first day and take a chance of someone dropping or they can call the teacher directly at his/her office to inquire about openings.  Most teachers do not keep waiting lists, and leave it to the student to check the registration page for openings.

Adding Instructor Signature Required Classes:


Q:                  I tried to add a class, but it says I need a signature.  How can I get in?


R:                   You must contact the instructor and see if you meet the pre-requisites of the course.  The instructor will add you to the class if you qualify.

Tuition and Payment Deadlines:


Q:                  How much is tuition and when is it due?


R:                   LDS tuition of 12 or more credits is $1,240.00 - not including class fees and books.  You can print out a Fee and Schedule Form by clicking on “Registration” - use your ID number (SSN) and PIN (birth month/birth year).  Click on add/drop classes - pick semester and Action (Display my schedule) and then print.   The tuition deadline is shortly after the beginning of the semester, and will be charged interest if late.  Unpaid fees will block you from making academic transactions.

Tuition Deadlines:


Q:                  Do I have to pay the $60 registration deposit for each semester or summer term?


R:                   For students who are on the Fall/Winter track, your $60 will remain in your account until you leave BYU-Idaho.  If you are on the track system where you will miss a major semester - Summer/Fall or Summer/Winter, your $60 will be returned to your home and you will need to pay it again before you can register for the next term.

Continuing Endorsement:


Q:                  What does “Continuing Endorsement” restriction mean?


R:                   During the Winter Semester, students meet with their ward bishops on campus and renew the Code of Honor with him.  The bishop will fill out a paper ensuring the university that the student is still worthy to attend BYU-Idaho.  If the student fails to have this interview with his/her bishop, a restriction from the Dean of Student’s Office will be placed on the student’s record, restricting the student from adding or dropping classes from that time on until an endorsement is submitted.

Full-time status:


Q:                  How many credits do I need to take during summer/fall/winter to be full time?


R:                   Summer 6; Fall and Winter 12

Credit loads:


Q:                  How many credits is an average load during the summer, and how many are too many? 


R:                   The average load for summer is six per term.  Use this example.  Each credit is multiplied by two which will give you total class time and outside homework you will have.  Students are restricted from taking over ten credits per summer term unless permission is granted through the Registrar’s Office or Assistant Academic President.

Dropping Classes:


Q:                  How do I drop a class?


R:                   A student can drop a class by either using the touch-tone phone or using the Internet.  However, a student must be aware of deadlines, which could charge them $5 per drop or put a “W” on their transcript or both.

Drop Deadlines:


Q:                  I missed the drop deadline.  Is there any way I can remove the “W” from my transcript?


R:                   Generally, missed deadlines are not an acceptable reason for removing a “W”.  There must be some extenuating situation, which must be approved by review of the Exception To Policy Committee.  You can call or visit the Registrar’s Office, KIM 190, to answer any specific questions about this procedure.  Verification is required for medical reasons or circumstances requiring absence from attendance.  These must be attached to a petition form, which may be obtained at the Registrar’s Office.

“W”s (withdrawal):


Q:                  If I drop a class with a “W” – how will that affect me?


R:                   A “W” signifies that you have significant investment into a class but did not complete the course to obtain a letter grade.  “W” grades DO count against you in the 75% rule.  The 75% satisfactory progress policy states that you must complete at least 75% of the credits for which you register each semester.  Failure to obtain a 75% progress completion will result in placing a student on academic probation.  “F” and “W” grades are non-progress grades.  In the next semester, if your progress rate were 75% or higher, you would be removed from academic probation; if it were lower than 75%, you would be placed on academic suspension.  “W” grades are not calculated into your GPA and they will not disappear if you re-take the class at a later date.  If you are receiving Federal Financial Aid, you must check with that office to determine how “W” grades affect your eligibility.
