Blodeuwedd

Welcome. This place is a shrine dedicated to the Welsh Goddess Blodeuwedd. The graphic in the background bears Her totem animal -- the owl. In the Welsh mythologies, Blodeuwedd was transformed into an owl as punishment for having transformed Her husband, Lleu Llaw Gyffes,  into an eagle (in an attempt to murder him).

Blodeuwedd was created by Her husband and his uncle, after Arianrhod (Lleu's mother) swore he would never have a wife of the race of humans. (Visit Arianrhod's shrine for more information about Her.) And so, She was created, by two men, from the blossoms of nine plants -- all chosen for their magickal properties. Of these nine, Oak, Broom, and Meadowsweet were the most abundant.

In the Avalonian tradition, Blodeuwedd's season is Spring. She is honored by the rites of Spring that are seen between the Moon after Imbolc until Beltane Moon. She is the Maiden, a Goddess of Air. Her gift is transformation, and the lesson She teaches is knowledge of one's true self.
 

"The Ballad of Blodeuwedd"
          by Dana Aine

It happened when the world was young
and yet the days were ages old --
The story of a lovely girl (a bride
and Goddess) will here be told.

               

        A woman made of bud and flower,
        Her intended husband cursed before.
        She came afresh to the wedding bower
        not knowing yet that she'd soon want more.

              But wait, I jump ahead too far.
              Her story needs more time to share.
              The song is sad and sweet, and yet
              the lessons learned are true and fair.

                 

                    Arianrhod, a virgin mother,
                    cursed the son that She begot --
                    a geise of three upon the lad
                    for grief he and his kin had wrought.

               

                          The curses have been named as this:
                          no name, no arms, no human wife.
                          But Gwydyon usedskill and cunning
                          to bring a name into Lleu's life.

               

Weapons he would also have,
and so tricked Arianrhod again.
The bride he made of flowers and magick.
So Blodeuedd's story here begins.

               

        Human She could never be,
        but flowers, they thought, would serve as true.
        Gwydyon was strong in magic,
        and made a girl as bride for Lleu.

              Nine flowers -- buds and leaves and petals --
              used to make a woman there.
              Each with its own special purpose:
              to make Her strong and sweet and fair.
               

                    Eight flowers, though, were male in gender.
                    Only one was feminine.
                    What other should have been expected?
                    A woman can't be made by men!

               

                        She came to Life one fine Spring morning --
                        living doll for Lleu to hold.
                        She didn't know that She had meaning,
                        and never asking was never told.

               

For a season Her love and duty
and contentment waxed and grew.
But as each Summer turns to Autumn,
so She longed for something new.

               

        It was around this time that Blodeuwedd
        met a man who took Her heart.
        So it came that She and Goronwy
        made a promise not to part.

              But Lleu Llaw Gyffes, still Her spouse,
              would to Her no freedom give.
              She knew Her love would find no refuge,
              long as Llew Llaw Gyffes lived.
               

                    Together they had formed a plan,
                    Blodeuwedd and Goronwy.
                    For the flower bride to keep Her lover
                    She had to find Her own way free.

               

                          On a hill and near his bath
                          Lleu would have to place his feet
                          upon the bath's edge and a goat,
                          or Death he would not ever meet.

               

Blodeuedd arranged it thus --
the bath, the goat, peculiar stance --
knowing that behind the rock
Her love was waiting with a lance.

               

        The weapon made in one full cycle --
        made in one year and one day --
        to pierce the breast of the Sillful Handed --
        was wielded by Goronwy.

               

            When thrown, the spear struck Lleu's strong breast
            but Death that moment he did not find.
            An eagle he became before them
            and stretched his wings and then did fly.

               

                  When Lleu was found, a full wheel after,
                  retribution he would seek to have.
                  So using the same peculiar posture
                  and same spear, smote is bride's true love.

                     

                        His uncle again used magick's power
                        to change the girl to another shape.
                        No longer bride of Spring's fair flowers,
                        but now a bird of flowered face.
                         

Gwydyon saw this change as punishment
for the wrong that She had caused,
but Blodeuwedd knew She was now free
from man's dominion and his laws.
 

      She flew the night and usually
      kept solitary counsel, too.
      She asked Herself a thousand questions --
      and all were prone to start with "who?"
       

            "Who are you to have created
            a girl for Lleu, oh Gwydyon?"
            "Who are you, Lleu, to have taken
            a girl whose heart you did not win?"
             

                Eventually the questions turned
                away from Lleu and Gwydyon.
                A better question She now asked --
                "Who am I?" She asked within.
                 

                    Thus "Blodeuwedd" has come to mean
                    "owl" in the Welshman's tongue.
                    And free from men and Self re-claimed
                    our flower girl's story here is done.
