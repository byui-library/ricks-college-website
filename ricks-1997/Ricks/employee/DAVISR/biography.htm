One of the best ways to help you understand both my teaching philosophy and how I think is to read a little bit about me.

 

In 1910, as a young man of twenty five, my grandfather (whose parents migrated from Wales) came to Idaho and homesteaded on the Canyon Creek foothills about 20 miles east of Rexburg. During the long winters he would move to town, living in a house just a block from the Ricks campus. My father settled here too, so I grew up in Rexburg in the 50’s, days when campus was only a couple of buildings, the student body measured in hundreds, and everyone knew everyone in town.

 

My high school days gave me a breadth of interest that I’ve kept ever since. Fearing a Russian takeover, I, like so many others in the late 50’s, majored in physics so America would have enough engineers to stop the Russians. My spare time was taken up with debate and music, the latter being something I’ve always enjoyed. I started piano lessons when I was eight and kept them up until age 19, practicing two hours each day, usually from 5-7AM (my parents and five other siblings learned how to sleep through scales and Chopin). Playing the piano got me into a dance band and we played almost every weekend, earning enough money, along with a part-time job in a gas station, to put me comfortably through high school.

 

Late in my junior year of high school I tried out for a DJ position with a local radio station and got the job. It gave me a huge boost in my education for in addition to playing Top 10 requests I had to read a five-minute newscast every hour. Those newscasts suddenly expanded my teenage views, making me very aware of what was going on, not in my high school, but in the world.

 

My mission was to France, the eastern and southern regions, and also the French-speaking portion of Switzerland. It proved to be far more than just a mission; it was a wonderful 2½-year education abroad with baptisms tossed in. I quickly fell in love with Europe.

 

I finished school and decided to go into law, not physics—not that I didn’t love physics, it’s just that you could make more money in law and I was feeling a bit mercenary. But at the close of my first year in law school I became one of the many who received a letter from Uncle Sam; I was drafted into the Viet Nam war. Late ‘68 saw the Tet offensive—things weren’t going well for us in Nam, nor at home. This was the 60’s and riots, marches, long hair, and drugs were commonplace; I witnessed a major building, on the campus where I was attending, burned to the ground by student protesters. Not that I agreed. Raised as a hard-core conservative Republican, I was (still am) a firm believer in what we should have done over there. Simply put, it was American politics that lost that war, not the will or abilities of those fighting there. But that’s another story .  .  . 

 

Two years later, and with my skin still very much intact, I returned, but not to law school. Two years of thinking and a phone call had changed my direction. Just as I was coming home I received a call from Ricks, asking me to come in for an interview to teach humanities, a subject which had always been a hobby of mine. A year later, 1971, I stood before my first class and have been here ever since. I love teaching!! I love helping you see the difference an education can make. Since coming to Ricks I’ve taught classes in humanities, music appreciation, piano, Book of Mormon, and even a couple in computers. I served as chairman of the Humanities Department for 15 years, and am currently the Director of the Honors Program. I’ve been around the world and to Europe lots of times; I’ve seen what I teach.

 

Oh yes, I got married to a wonderful lady from Phoenix, Arizona, and we have a daughter and three sons. The two oldest sons and I used to spend many a summer day on nasty dirt bikes, roaring up trails and through streams in the mountains around here.

 

Because life is varied and opportunities are many, I have two other occupations. I own a professional photography studio and spend many weekends photographing weddings and family groups. My other hat is a public one; I’ve served as the county coroner for over twenty years. Yes, I deal with dead bodies on a regular basis, and since I’m on 24-hour call there have been more than a few times when I’ve been called right out of class.

 

There is much more but by now you’ve got a general idea. One final comment: my father always told us kids to “Think!” and he was correct. I appreciate people who think and know where they stand.
