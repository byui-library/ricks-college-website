Course Description and Objectives: This course is designed to introduce students to both financial and managerial accounting principles with exposure to basic accounting statements, processes, and management applications. It is designed mainly for nonbusiness majors and certain specialized business programs. The main objective of the course is to help students develop the skills and confidence necessary to apply accounting concepts and techniques in analyzing, planning, and controlling operations of various kinds of organizations.

Mid-term Exams: There will be 3 mid-term exams given during the course of the semester. They will all be administered in the Testing Center. Please take note of the dates for these tests in the tentative course outline. There are no grade improvement projects (extra credit) and no retakes of tests. If you have a scheduling conflict, please arrange with the instructor to take the test before it is scheduled to be given. It is the student's responsibility to be aware of the Testing Center hours and to make sure that adequate time is allowed for completing the exams. Although no time limit penalties will be assessed, students should be aware that speed is important too, not just accuracy. (The CPA Exam is a timed exam) Each of the 3 mid-terms is worth 100 points.

Final Exam: The final exam will be held on the final day of class in the regular classroom. The final will not be comprehensive. It will cover the material from the last few chapters that are discussed in class. The final is worth 100 points.

Calculators are permitted while taking the exams. However, programmable calculators should not be programmed with notes and other aids for taking the test. Only the basic functions should be used. The exams consist of multiple choice questions and work problems. The questions are both subjective and objective in nature. The exams test student memory, understanding, and ability to analyze and apply concepts covered in the course.

Homework: The course revolves around the preparation, completion, and explanation of homework. Homework problems are assigned to help students learn and experience the principles covered in the course. Although some memorization is essential, accounting usually has to be experienced before a thorough understanding can be achieved. Be prepared to spend many hours in solving homework problems. Time and effort are just as important as the correct solution.

The questions, exercises, and problems that are assigned as homework are found at the end of each chapter in the text. Homework assignments should be completed prior to the day that they are discussed in class based on the tentative schedule included in this course outline. There are 12 homework assignments and 5 points possible for each assignment, regardless of the number of questions, exercises, or problems assigned. The lowest homework score will be dropped for grading purposes.

The homework assignments are to be done in pencil or with a computerized spreadsheet application and should be done very neatly (both form and content are important). Clearly label each problem and problem part in the left margin. The homework will normally be corrected by the students in class. Any corrections made to homework should be done in pen in order to give the instructor a good impression of where mistakes are being made. The homework will not be graded based on accuracy. It will be graded on being complete and timely. Any sloppy, negligent, or incomplete work will receive at least a 1 point deduction.

Quizzes: Quizzes are used as stepping stones in helping prepare students for the exams. There will generally be a quiz after each chapter. Quizzes will be taken and corrected in class. There will be 12 quizzes worth 5 points each. The lowest quiz score will be dropped for grading purposes.

Special Projects: Two of the most common complaints against recent accounting and business graduates is their inability to express themselves in writing and their inability to work in groups or teams. Accountants and other business people are frequently called upon to express opinions and findings in an accurate, clear, thorough, and concise manner. They are also regularly expected to work with a variety of individuals in groups or teams. The special projects in this class are designed to address these areas of concern.

During the first few days of the semester, the instructor will divide you into groups of three or four to work on these special projects. It is expected that all group members will actively participate in their group. The projects will consist of ethics cases, internet activities, research projects, and other assignments. The specific topics, instructions, and due dates of each project will be discussed in class.

The projects will normally require a written paper. These are formal papers and should be treated as such. Logical thought development along with good grammar and spelling are important. You will be given considerable freedom in your approach to the projects. Be creative!!

Late Assignments: Any homework or quiz submitted after the assigned class period will receive a 1 point per-school-day deduction. Special projects will not be accepted if they are late. No homework will be accepted and no quiz can be taken after the test for that material has been administered. Tests may be taken late only under extreme extenuating circumstances and with prior permission from the instructor and will receive a 5 point per-school-day deduction.

Textbooks: Use the textbook to its fullest potential. Underline key statements. Follow and work through the examples given. Work extra exercises and problems from the book. You can check your work with me on any extra work you do. A Study Guide is also available in the bookstore.

Fellow students: Group learning can be beneficial. Form study groups if you wish. You can work on homework assignments together if you wish, although each person must submit homework for grading purposes. Be willing to share your knowledge if someone asks you a question. Explaining a concept to someone else helps reinforce the concept in your own mind.

Class: Attend regularly and on time. Take good, accurate, readable notes. Refer to examples in the notes. Ask questions if you do not understand.
