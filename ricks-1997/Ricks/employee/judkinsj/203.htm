  • Course Description:  Many of you are interested in Comm 203 because you are interested in becoming a communicative disorders professional, and thought a course in phonetics may be beneficial to you. You are right. Some of you are looking into phonetics simply because you thought it sounded interesting. You are right too.  Whether you are aware of it or not, you know how to think and behave graphemically. I know that because you are reading the letters, or graphemes, in this paragraph. The graphemes of English, or the "ABCs" roughly represent the sounds of the English language. However, there is a more accurate way to represent sounds.  It is with a system that uses phonemes instead of graphemes.    In this class, you will learn how to accurately write things as they are pronounced--no matter how they are pronounced--using the International Phonetic Alphabet and diacritic symbols.   Hopefully, however, more than that will occur. Hopefully you will learn to think phonetically.  This is the objective of this course.  Thinking phonetically is not easy--especially when dealing with phonology which is disordered, foreign, or unfamiliar to you. It requires a solid knowledge and understanding of phonetics and related concepts (gained through study), and the development of a finely-tuned auditory perception mechanism (gained through practice).  Furthermore, thinking phonetically ought to have a "point."  We will practice applying your knowledge in many ways, but most importantly, we will practice applying it clinically, for those who struggle to produce understandable speech.
