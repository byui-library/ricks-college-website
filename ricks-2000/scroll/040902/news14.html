World's richest get poorer

by Allison Jenks
REE00013@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Forbes magazine released their sixteenth annual rankings, and the elite list had fewer members than last year.

The new list reported 83 fewer billionaires this year, and the group’s combined worth fell to $1.54 trillion from $1.73 trillion last year.

The Microsoft co-founder Bill Gates topped Forbes’ list as the world’s richest billionaire for the eighth year, even though his net worth fell 10 percent from a year ago to $52.8 billion.

Wal-Mart founder Sam Walton left five heirs in the top 10 with over $20 billion each.

John R. Simplot was one of the oldest living billionaires at 97 and is on the list at number 112. Simplot made his $3.3 billion fortune in potatoes.

It was the second year of decline in the number of billionaires since the downturn in technology. The largest drop in number of billionaires is last year’s drop — 91 people lost their place on the list, according to CNN.

Only 25 people on the list are under the age of 40, led by the 37-year-old computer founder Michael Dell at number 18. The highest ranking woman was Alice Walton, number eight, with assets of $20.5 billion.

The United States had more people on the list than any other country, with 243 billionaires, down from 272 last year. Their combined net worth fell by $111 billion, according to CNN.

Of the 497 total billionaires from 43 countries, 260 inherited some or all of their wealth, and the rest made their money themselves, according to CNN. Twenty-seven are college dropouts,according to Forbes.
