BYU freshmen skip dorm life, head off campus for housing

Brittany Candrian
The Daily Universe (Brigham Young U.)

(U-WIRE) PROVO, Utah — Despite the amenities of living on campus, which are enough to entice most freshmen, about 2,000 students at Brigham Young University skip life in the dorms and head straight to off-campus housing.

While most freshmen have the convenience of three meals prepared for them on a daily basis, free high-speed Internet connection and a less than five-minute walk to campus, those freshmen living off-campus go without such benefits.

Although on-campus living makes life easier for some freshmen, off-campus housing provides different needs to other freshmen. This includes such things as living closer to family members, less rules, and the option of having more roommates.

Brittany Thackeray, a sophomore at BYU from Kansas City, Mo., majoring in social work, said, “I don’t regret not living in the dorms and I have enjoyed the fact that since I was thrown in with an older crowd right off, I wasn’t labeled as a stereotypical ‘ditzy’ freshman.

She chose to live off-campus her freshman year because she planned on living with her older sister who was a sophomore, Thackeray said.

Having family off-campus played a big role in his decision to not live in the dorms because “living closer to my sisters made it easier for me,” Dave Smith, a freshman from Delta, Utah, said.

By living off-campus as a freshman, he has an advantage over other male freshmen because he believes living with returned missionaries will help him be better prepared to serve a mission, Smith said.

“I’ve already learned so much about what a mission is like because all five of my roommates have served,” Smith said.

The returned-missionary roommates also set an example that helps in preparation for a mission, Jason Alleman said, a freshman from The Colony, Texas.

“Although I feel I missed out on the freshman interaction of the dorms, I’m glad I lived in an apartment, because with five other roommates you feel like a family,” Alleman said.

Steve Nielsen of the BYU off-campus housing office, said roughly 5,100 students who are mainly freshmen lived in either Deseret Towers, Heritage Halls or Helaman Halls during fall semester 2001. The remaining 2,000 freshmen reside at home or in BYU-approved off-campus housing.
