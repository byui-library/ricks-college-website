Ricks College News Release
January 8, 2001

Ricks winter entertainment to range from classical to pop

Please note: The dates in this news article that tickets go on sale are for the general public. Tickets generally go on sale to Ricks students two days before. Cost to Ricks students for all of these shows is $1. We have included cost for the general public in this news release.

REXBURG, Idaho — Winter entertainment at Ricks College will range from classical to country and jazz to pop. It includes such names as the Osmond Brothers from Branson, Mo., the Beatles group known as 1964, jazz singer Kevin Mahogany and Emmy-winning actors Roscoe Lee Browne and Anthony Zerbe.

The Center Stage Entertainment Series offers something for just about everyone. "Our intent is to bring a variety of very fine, nationally recognized entertainment to campus for both our students and the community at large," says Don Sparhawk, who arranges the series.

Tickets for the first three events go on sale Jan 8. Other tickets will go on sale about three weeks before each event begins. For more information, call the Ricks College Ticket Office at 356-2230. All shows will begin at 7:30 p.m.

For the third consecutive January, Ricks will bring an acclaimed string quartet to Rexburg to brighten up the long winters. The American String Quartet from New York City is this year’s featured quartet and will work with Ricks music students for three days while on campus. The public is invited to hear the American Quartet in the Barrus Concert Hall on Wednesday and Thursday, Jan. 17 and 18. Cost is $6.

Students, parents and even grandparents can relive the music of Sixties as the group known as 1964 takes to the stage in the Hart Auditorium. The group that claims to be the No. 1 Beatles show in the world will return to Ricks College Friday, Jan. 26, after a 10-year absence. Cost for the concert and dance is $8 and $6. To make the evening complete, a British dinner for an extra $10 will be served before the show in the Manwaring Center.

A Shakespearean tale of jealousy, betrayal and repentance will open the winter theater series. The Ricks College Theatre Department will present "The Winter’s Tale" Jan. 18 to Feb. 3 in the Kirkham Arena Theater. Tickets are $6.

Nationally acclaimed organist Richard Morris will return to Ricks College to play the Ruffatti organ on Wednesday, Feb. 7. The Atlanta native is one of but a few organists to ever have performed as a soloist at New York City’s Carnegie Hall. Tickets are $6 and go on sale Jan. 24.

An evening of classical music played on Russian folk instruments will be presented by the Russian group Trio Voronezh. Discovered playing Bach in a German subway station, Trio Voronezh made its debut in the United States during the 1996 Oregon Bach Festival. The concert will be held Saturday, Feb. 24, in the Barrus Concert Hall. Tickets are $6 and go on sale Feb 7. A Russian dinner will also be served for an extra $10.

 

Emmy-winning actors Roscoe Lee Browne and Anthony Zerbe will present a performance of 20th century poetry and drama entitled "Behind the Broken Words" Thursday and Friday, March 1 and 2. The New York Times writes "Behind the Broken Words sings; Brown and Zerbe are a two-part harmony." Tickets are $6 and go on sale Feb. 14.

 

One of Gilbert and Sullivan’s greatest works, "The Pirates of Penzance," will be revived by the Ricks College Theater and Music departments.The show runs Feb. 22 to March 3 in the Snow Drama Theater. Tickets are $6 and go on sale Feb. 7.

Internationally acclaimed jazz vocalist Kevin Mahogany will perform Saturday, March 10, with the Ricks College Sound Alliance jazz band during the third annual Ricks College Jazz Festival. With a blend of blues, soul, gospel and jazz, Mahogany presents the soul of jazz music. Tickets are $8 and $6 and go on sale Feb. 21.

Among one of the most celebrated families in the music industry, The Osmond Brothers will entertain during Women’s Week. The show will feature Wayne, Merrill and Jay Osmond who perform most of the year in Branson, Mo. Shows will be at 3 and 7:30 p.m. Saturday, March 17. Tickets will be available beginning Feb. 28 and are $8 and $6.

The Ricks College Theatre Department will close out the season with the classic heart-warming story of Louisa May Alcott’s "Little Women" April 12 through 21 in the Snow Drama Theater. The story of a mother and her children brings hope, cheer and love. Tickets are $6 and go on sale March 28.
