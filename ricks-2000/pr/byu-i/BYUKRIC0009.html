Ricks Issues - BYU-Idaho Transition

KRIC Radio Report, September 2000

The following was prepared by Mark Bailey, KRIC Radio news director. It includes sound bites that you can click on and hear audio recordings:

As Ricks College continues to make the transition to BYU-Idaho, some questions have been posed, such as "Will the Rexburg campus become a clone of the Provo campus?"  More specifically, will Ricks College adopt the BYU Honor Code, which is a little different than Ricks College's.  Student Life Vice President Robert Wilkes says Ricks College will retain its individuality . . .

"We will be following our own rendition or version of the Honor Code as the Board's instruction allows and supports. So we will not be adopting the BYU Honor Code or any other aspect of the Honor Code that is not currently part of what we are doing."
[Click here to listen]

As time goes along, Wilkes says a review of the Honor Code will be made, but the intent is to be true to the identity and tradition of Ricks College. 

With the announcement that Ricks will be going to a four-year school, some students have the idea to just "hang around" Ricks so they can graduate with a four year degree.  College President David Bednar says that's premature to be even considering that idea . . . 

"What we said publicly is that we will gradually phase in four-year programs.  And they will come, if you will, in little clusters or bundles – some at a particular time – some will follow a year after that. For a student to think that they are going to hang around and kind of catch on to a four-year program – well maybe one of the ones they want may be in that first wave, maybe not. Therefore it could be an incredible waste of time to think that, 'I am just going to be in limbo waiting for my four-year program to come along.' "
[Click here to listen]

Bednar says it will be the same program for this school year as it always has been since discussions are still going on to determine what classes will be carried for a four-year program.  Once it's been decided which classes to carry, then it will need to be determined which ones will be offered first, then which ones will be offered the second year and the third.  Then academic space and other resources will have to be adjusted, which may take several years to do.  It will take time to complete the transition from Ricks to BYU-Idaho.  

Wilkes says it's important for students to catch the vision of the transition and for them to initiate ideas now that can be implemented later . . . 

"The real delivery point of this change where it is doing the thing that it was intended to do in June when President Hinckley made the announcement – we don't know where that is down the road, but it is down the road. But if you'd see yourself now helping to mold whatever that's going to be, you really have the way to do it. We have a wonderful and affective student government organization. We have all kinds of committees and service volunteer opportunities through our Leadership and Service Institute. So we hope you don't feel disconnected from this whole process."
[Click here to listen]

Wilkes says channels are available for students to offer ideas and suggestions to get in the mix of all what will happen in the future.
