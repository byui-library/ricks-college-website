Course Outline
For the official syllabus, please print out my .pdf version.

Instructor: Lane Williams, Spori 104, 356-2921; hours: Immediately after class each day or by appointment. E-mail williamsl@ricks.edu (I will respond to all e-mails and will accept written assignments sent to me that way.)
Text: Speaking With a Purpose, 4rd or 5th ed., Arthur Koch.
Other materials: One videotape, note paper.

Somebody once said the human mind starts working the moment we are born and doesn't quit until we die or get up to give a speech. If you can identify with this problem, this fun class may be just the class for you.

In this class, we're all friends here! You will practice some speeches in a non-threatening environment. You are encouraged to have fun, maybe even get a little crazy once in a while, when it's appropriate. The really important thing is that you get involved with the classroom activities, study hard for a semester and emerge a better speaker.

We have four objectives in this class:

1. Help improve your confidence as a public speaker and as a communicator.

2. Help you understand what goes into making an excellent public speech.

3. Help improve the clarity of your writing and the ability you have to tell a story.

4. Help you understand the importance of public speaking, communication and persuasion in society.

Here's what I expect:

· Learn the names of the other class members. We want to become friends.
· Tell about your classmates. Your first assignment will be to introduce a classmate.
· Show your sense of humor. Your second assignment is to share a well-told, clean joke.
· Help teach a chapter. You will work with others and teach a chapter in the book.
· Give a personal experience speech. You will learn about story-telling.
· Give an informative speech. You will be videotaped. Provide an outline.
· Give a persuasive speech. You may choose a topic. Provide an outline.
· Deliver a religious speech. You will write this speech out and turn in the written speech.
· Help with a group speech. This is a discussion and will be if we have time.
· Pay attention. Part of this class will involve watching great speeches. Watch and discuss!
· Attend the final. This will be an impromptu speech.
· Evaluate your peers. This will be part of their grade.
· Don't be late. A late speech cuts your grade on that speech one full letter.
· Produce three out-of-class assignments. This will count about 15 percent of your grade.
· Analyze the speech of a professional speaker. This is your first paper.
· Critique your own performance. This will be from the video. This is your second.
· Analyze your progress. This is your final paper.
· Be clear in writing. Mistakes in grammar, spelling and mechanics will hurt.
· Be concise. Two papers will be no more than 3 pages, but no less than 2.
· Attend devotionals. This will help your speaking.
· Be prepared for quizzes. We will give four quizzes on the text and lectures.
· Quizzes matter. They will count about 15 percent of your final grade.
· Speaking is the bulk of your grade. This will count about 70 percent of the final grade.
· Attendance matters. I will take roll and mark off about one half grade for three absences.
· One possible change. In lieu of the dramatic reading, we may do a short informative talk.

Notes on Grading:

· Starting with the long informational speech, expect to submit outlines with complete sentences.
· I will provide an evaluation of each speech to you directly.

Here is a copy of the evaluation form in .pdf version that you will need.
· I will provide two or three graded overall evaluations of your status in class.
· It is possible that the religious speech may take too long to get to. You will still be asked to write the speech.
· Expect the speeches, with teaching of a chapter being considered a speech, to count about 70 percent of your final grade.
· One of my favorite questions is: What percentage of the text's assigned chapters did you read?
· Expect quizzes to count about 15 percent.
· I will drop your lowest quiz score.
· Expect two papers and one non-paper evaluation form.
· I will provide specifics on each assignment.
· Expect papers to count about 15 percent of your grade.
· Attendance matters. If I notice a pattern of more than two unexcused absences or more than three unexcused tardies, I may drop a grade a half step for each additional absence or tardy. My philosophy, however, is that you are college students and can take care of yourselves. So, unless absence becomes a huge problem, don't worry about it -- unless you have an assignment due or a speech to be given. This affects others and will affect your grade.
· We may do a group speech . . . that is discussion group . . . if we have time at the end of the semester.
· Expect speeches to be weighted in value based on their length.
· Expect late assignments to lose approximately 10 percent of their value per class period they are late.

· If I find grades dipping substantially, I may look at using a modified curve. That is, I will look at your evaluations of each other and the average scores to determine where A, B, C, etc. are through natural grouping. Don't count on this, however.
· I reserve for myself the right to count assignments at the end of class more than earlier ones. That is, if you give a speech at the end that is so good . . . good enough for an A++ . . . then I might raise your grade more than I might otherwise if I were just to count the speech at its normal value. This is my best equivalent of extra credit for this class.
· As for the papers, I will give you a "Tips on writing" paper. Since one of my objectives is effective writing, I do focus on mechanics heavily. Indeed, I have been told that I grade more strictly than some English teachers. I have been a writer by profession, and it seems to me that I should help you in this area, if I am able to, especially since the best speeches are well-written ones. This tip sheet is the one I will use to mark points off your written assignments. I will also look at spelling errors. Expect one five percent off (1 point out of 20) for the first three errors and an additional point for every error thereafter.
· I am willing to discuss any problem you have with my grading, but if you are to persuade me I made an error, and I do make some occasionally, your arguments must be based firmly in the text, on the tip sheets or in class discussions.
· Accuracy in scores and final grades is partly your responsibility.
· Hold onto everything I give back to you, so you can show me I made a mistake in recording your grades. Paperwork is a good habit to start now.
