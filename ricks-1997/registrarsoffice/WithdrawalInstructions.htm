Withdrawal Instructions and Information

 

THE DECISION

TDetermine the impact of the withdrawal.  Remember that this can effect your student employment, housing contract, financial aid, scholarship, and admission status for the next semester, insurance coverage, and academic career.  You can determine the impact of your decision by viewing the below information, or by contacting the individual departments.  

TInternational Students: This could impact your visa status.  Be sure to contact the International Student Office prior to making a decision.

TIt is also important to consult with parents, spouse, or other significant persons prior to making a decision.  

TLook for alternatives.  For example, you may be eligible for financial assistance or on-campus employment that may relief financial pressure.  The Counseling Center or your bishop may be helpful in helping you sort out homesickness or other overwhelming situations.  Visiting the Career and Advising Center can offer guidance and direction regarding academic and career decisions.

HOW TO WITHDRAW

You can withdraw in one of three ways:

1.  Dropping all classes on the web.  Remember, as soon as you drop all of your classes you are considered as withdrawn from school.  Be careful not to drop all of your classes unless your intent is to withdraw.  If you are changing classes, first add your desired class prior to dropping the undesired class.  Call the Registrar’s Office, if you need help.

2.  By telephone.  You may drop all classes by using the “Touch Tone Registration System” at 496-1006.  You may also call the Registrar’s Office at 496-1003.  You will be required to give your Student ID number and PIN.

3.  Withdraw in person at the Registrar’s Office in Kimball 190.  

WITHDRAWAL DATE

The withdrawal date is the date you drop your last class to zero credit.  Changing your last class to an audit is a withdrawal.  

If the date of withdrawal is different from the date that you dropped your class, you must contact the Registrar’s Office (208 496-1003).  Verification is required to enter any date other than the date of the last class dropped.

ENROLLMENT DEFERMENT

 

If you desire to return the next semester, you must submit an “Enrollment Deferment”.  Otherwise, your enrollment status will discontinue and you will be required to reapply.  To defer enrollment without reapplying, the following conditions should be met:

1.  Be admitted as a regular day school student the semester prior to requesting deferment

2.  Be admissible to BYU- Idaho.

3.  Submit the “Continuing Student Enrollment Deferment Application” before the application deadline of the semester you are waning to return.

Note: This deferment is for one semester only.  You must re-enroll a t the university in the Fall or Winter semester immediately following your absence, (i.e., if you withdraw winter semester 2002, you must re-enroll Fall Semester 2002.  If you withdraw fall semester 2001, then you must re-enroll for Winter Semester 2002).  

Students who plan to interrupt their studies for more than one semester should contact the Admissions Office for appropriate re-application materials.  Students leaving to serve LDS missions should ask the Admissions Office should ask for a “LDS Missionary Admission Deferment Application”.                                                                                                

FINANCIAL AID

ARE YOU WITHDRAWING BECAUSE OF A LACK OF MONEY?  If so, be sure you visit with the Financial Aid Office.  You may qualify for some financial help (MONEY!)  from us.

HAVE YOU RECEIVED FINANCIAL AID THIS TERM?  Withdrawing from school may require you to repay some of the aid you received.  You may want to check with the Accounting Office at (208) 496-1057.  Withdrawing could also jeopardize your future eligibility for federal student aid at BYU-Idaho and may require you to petition for a reinstatement of your eligibility.

EXIT COUNSELING IS REQUIRED!  Exit Counseling is required when you withdraw if you have received a student loan.  This will explain your rights and responsibilities regarding your loan.  You may receive this counseling on line by going to You will generally have a six month grace period before you must begin repaying your loan.  If you return to school at least half time, you may request a deferment.

           

CONTACT THE FINANCIAL OFFICE!   The Financial Aid Office is located in Suite 100 of the Kimball Building, telephone number (208) 496-1014 or email .

ACCOUNTING INFORMATION

Refunds and Charges:

A $60 processing fee will be charged to all students who withdraw from school.  The Registration Deposit will be used to pay this fee.

After the first week of school, students will be charged for the classes for which they are registered until they officially withdraw from school, even though they may not have been attending the classes.  Then if payments for Tuition and Fees exceed the charges, the overpayment will be used first to pay any outstanding Federal financial aid and school debts.  Then the refund will be returned to any scholarships and grants received by the student, with the remaining balance mailed to the student within 30 days of withdrawal.  

Refer to the Catalog under Financial Policies for further detail or visit the Accounting Office for more information.

Federal Title IV Financial Aid:

If you have received a Pell Grant, the date of your withdrawal from BYU-Idaho will determine if you have to pay some of that grant back to the government.  Your withdrawal date will also affect the amount you will owe on Stafford Loans you may have received.  You may also owe money to BYU-Idaho.

You will have to pay any unearned balance owing on the Pell and the balance owing on you Stafford Loans.  The date of your withdrawal will determine the amount of Federal Aid, which has been earned by yourself and by the school.  Any refund (unearned amount) from the school will be applied in the following order: (1) Stafford Loans which will be mailed directly to the bank, (2) unearned amount which you owe for the Pell Grant, (3) debts still wing to BYU-Idaho, (4) scholarships/grants, and (5) balance to the student.

To see how your withdrawal will personally affect you financially, come to the Accounting Office before you make the decision to withdrawal.

STUDENT EMPLOYMENT

Students withdrawing from BYU-Idaho lose their eligibility to be employed in any on-campus positions.  Students withdrawing from classes, but continuing their internship position, are not affected by the withdrawal.

Students with financial difficulties should consult with the Student Employment Office at Kimball 244, to explore possible options rather than withdrawing from school.

STUDENT HOUSING

Procedures for withdrawing students living in on-campus housing:

• Check out of dorms within 72 hours of the withdrawal date.

• Pick up checkout information from the head resident and arrange a time to do checkout.

• Students are charged for the time resided in on-campus housing and a termination fee.

Procedures for withdrawing students living in off-campus housing:

• Immediately inform manager of the withdrawal.

• Examine closely the terms of the housing contract, and pay particular attention to responsibility for selling of the contract.

• If the student decides to remain in their housing unit, a “Non-student Commitment” must be signed.  These forms are available in the Housing Office (Kimball 196).
