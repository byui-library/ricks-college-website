What I Believe

 
1.  Good and evil exist.

I love good and despise evil.  This is because I have learned by hard, painful experience that wickedness never is happiness, and I have felt the joys of proper choices.  To the extent we follow goodness, that is, the light of Christ, to that extent peace comes into the world, that wide lake of human existence.

Each violation of moral law causes reverberations and shock waves throughout the lake. As the waters become troubled, sensitive people discern the storm. And the storm affects us all because all nature and mankind are interconnected. Every action produces a reaction.

We may not notice, but even our footsteps have a consequence on the blades of grass.   One cannot claim his or her deeds of misconduct hurt no one.  When a man or child robs, he hurts me; he hurts us all.  When a couple chooses divorce over reconciliation, I reap the consequences; we all reap the consequences.

When a man becomes an alcoholic, all suffer. John Donne knew this when he said "No man is an island, entire unto himself.  But each man is a part of the continent, a part of the main."  Conversely, when we choose good over evil, when our behaviors are positive, we help sooth life's troubled waters.  We indeed make the world more fit, more peaceful, more beautiful.
2.  Our common fight is against numerous social evils.

I am a religious man. I take values and gather insights from the holy scriptures.   But for me, to fight others over doctrinal issues is stupid. (Debate is not stupid, but alienation from one another, over doctrine, is.) All religious people must help in the fight against evil.  We should realize that the Catholics, Methodist, Baptists, Mormons, Jews, etc. are mighty allies.

Our common fight is against numerous social evils including pornography, trashy movies and vulgar television shows, ignorance, poverty, racism and discrimination. Of course, the list goes on.  Religious people should seek to understand others' points of view, and then to respect those views.   Our own arrogance and ignorance help make enemies.  We would all do well to attend different churches, not of our own persuasion, until we learned to understand one
another.
3.  God gives His light to every man and woman.

To the extent we are true to that light, that is, to the extent we live God's word, we acquire more light. To the extent we disregard God's light, we grow gradually into darkness.  As we grow towards darkness, we lose our ability to discern the light.  At that point we may begin to call darkness light and light darkness.  We, in fact, become what we choose to follow.  If we follow the light, we become creatures of light; if we follow darkness, we become children of darkness.
4.  Toleration for divergent views is extremely important.

Society should always have room for and a welcoming hand for the atheist and agnostic. The religious and non religious alike can define and agree on common social problems. Christians can become allies with non believers in making the world a better place.   Our world must make room for all who honestly arrive at differing religious convictions; however, if some ideas are found destructive or anti-social, they should be opposed. For me, an example of an anti-social idea is abortion.

5.  Mankind can not improve our most fundamental rules for human behavior, that is, the Ten Commandments.


The atheist may not follow all of the commandments. Yet, ideally, the Ten Commandments should be followed by all. This divine code, if lived by all, would cause the world to be more prosperous, peaceful, productive, and safe. 

Pain is mitigated to the extent we collectively and individually live the Ten Commandments.  The commandments against lying, adultery, dishonoring the Sabbath, and killing are particularly important and should be followed by agnostics, atheists, Jews, and Christians alike.  Honoring one's parents is also highly important.

6.  Each must accept responsibility for his or her own life, circumstances, and happiness.

This sense of responsibility should be taught to children and adopted by them as early in life as possible. Yet at the same time, when men and women are learning to accept responsibility for their own lives, they should surrender to God, asking for his guidance and inspirations in making their decisions. This allows men and women to follow the light.  My faith is that the light enriches our lives, making us more than we otherwise would be.

7.  Moral achievements are the highest and most important.

Of all the things we can achieve in this life, moral achievements are the highest and most important.  As one become more moral, one becomes more like Christ.   This condition produces a sweetness and joy that is worth more than money, fame, or temporary pleasure.

8.  Give attention to the soul.

When one works to cure the body, one must also give attention to the soul. Assuming one has a conscience, a sick soul can be reflected in bodily ailments. Yet one must also know that just because the body is sick, that does not mean the soul is under a heavy burden. Yet, if the soul is healthy, bodily ailments will be lighter and more bearable.

9.  Television programming damages our society.

Television programming, although marvelous in many ways, damages our society.  Thinking of our body, what we don't use, we lose. And television is the great thief.  It robs us of our will and of our mental powers. Television gradually anesthetizes a man's and woman's mind because TV requires no mental exertion.  It dulls one's imagination and corrodes one's zest for reading, writing, and study.  TV makes zombies out of the best of us.

Plus, television robs our children of their innocence.  Once, certain topics were taboo, only talked about when little ears were in bed.  Now, all the adult themes, problems, and weaknesses of our age are thrust upon our tender children.  Consequently, they experience the death of innocence, of childhood.  Additionally, some children grow up to act out the violence which has been modelled for them over and over again on the television screen.

10.  All mature men and women now suffer or will suffer.

They suffer because they feel empathy for their friends and relatives who hurt. They suffer because mankind is blotted and stained. They suffer because they grow old, because they lose their sight, their hearing, their youth. They suffer for their weakness and mistakes. But through all of their suffering, if men and women turn to God, striving to do His will, the suffering becomes a mechanism which helps purify one's personality and conscience.

11.  Religion can produce bigots in every denomination.

A bigot is someone who is intolerably convinced that he or she is right. Often bigots are not honest, that is, they won't call a spade a spade.  They become intolerant of anyone or anything that threatens their point of view. While they claim to follow Christ, they become great practitioners of mental coercion.

12.  Great men and women are around us.

Great men and women are around us, as great as any Eliza or Jefferson or Lincoln or Harriet or Hyrum or Brigham.  Because of time's distortions and because of the historian's rosy lens, we mistakenly concluded that only past ages had truly great men.

Or we conclude that greatness is only found with people who amass wealth or leadership fame.  It is forever true: the prophet is without honor in his own country.  Many of us take our neighbors, the quiet people, for granted. But they live serenely, independently, filled with sweetness and conviction. They are often the salt of the earth.  The first shall be last, and the last shall be first.
