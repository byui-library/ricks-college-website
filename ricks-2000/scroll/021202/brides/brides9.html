Wedding traditions from across the world

by Laura Bohon
BOH00002@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Bride Wang Min-Reay wipes the face of Bridegroom Chen Li-Zen. The couple, dressed in ancient Chinese wedding costumes, participated in the ceremony to highlight the holiness of marriage in a mass wedding in Taiwan. AP Photo Archive
Making arrangements for a wedding is a daunting task for any couple. On top of all of the routine things to worry about with a wedding, an element of creativity must be added.

Members of the present generation will not be satisfied with a completely traditional wedding; they need something new!

Or do they?

Brides and grooms searching for that certain something for their receptions and ceremonies can turn to their ancestry for ideas.

In southern Italy, for example, there is a long-standing custom wherein the bride and groom shatter a vase or glass at the end of their wedding day. Folklore maintains that the number of pieces of glass will be the number of years the couple is happily married.

An old tradition from England, dating back to the Tudor reign, dictates that the guests at a wedding must throw their shoes at the married couple as they ride away from the chapel. If the couple or their carriage was hit, it meant good fortune would follow them.

This tradition has even deeper roots. In Anglo-Saxon times, grooms would symbolically strike their bride with a shoe as a way of establishing their authority. The brides would then throw a shoe at their bridesmaids to see who would be married first.

If throwing shoes around does not sound appealing, couples can look to Asian traditions for less destructive customs.

In Japan and Korea, ducks play an important part in wedding ceremonies. Ducks are symbols of fidelity since they mate for life ; they were also included in the wedding processional.

In Korea, the custom is carried further by introducing cranes into the ceremony, since they are a symbol of long life. Korean brides wear embroidered cranes on their sashes.

Asia is not the only continent in which birds play an important role in wedding ceremonies.

In Armenia there is an old tradition in which two white doves are set free on the day of the wedding to symbolize love and happiness.

In the Czech Republic, friends of the bride sneak into her yard to decorate a tree with painted eggshells and ribbons. The tradition goes that the decorated tree will live as long as the bride.

Generations of German couples participated in an old tradition. During the ceremony the groom must kneel on the bride’s hem to show that he will keep her in line. Consequently, the bride may step on his foot as she rises to reassert herself.

In Holland and Switzerland, newly-married couples plant a pine tree— a symbol of fertility and luck— outside their home.

As a symbol of patriotism, engaged couples can look to early America for intriguing ideas. One colonial American custom dictated that the bride pin a small pouch to her petticoat. She would put a small piece of bread, cloth, wood and a dollar bill inside the pouch. These items ensured there would be enough food, clothing, shelter and money for future years.

There are myriad customs and traditions for engaged couples to discover to make their own wedding unique. The traditions listed here are just a sample of all the many options available.
