What has been suggested about
The Mount of Transfiguration?

========================================================

There is much we do not know about this occasion (D&C 63:21).

Peter, James and John had been asking questions. (JST Mark 9:1) Christ takes them upon an unnamed mount to pray. It is suggested by most that Mt. Hermon, was the mountain (Jesus the Christ, p. 376; DNTC 1:402; Bible Dictionary, p. 786) The separation of these three from the rest of the Apostles occurred on two other occassions: the raising of Jairus' daughter (Mark 5:22-24, 35-43) and Gethsemane (Mark 14:32-42).

Not only was Christ transfigured, but also Peter, James, and John (Teachings, p. 158). The initial purpose of Christs trasfiguration seems to center on comforting and supporting him regarding the prospects of his future crucifixion (Jesus the Christ, p. 373).

Moses and Elias (Elijah) appeared as traslated beings. The word Elias is the Greek (New Testamemt) form of the Hebrew word Elijah (Bible Dictionary, p. 663). There seems to be no question that this was Elijah (DNTC, 1:404).

John the Baptist was also present in spirit personage, perhaps representing the last legal administrator under the Old Covenant that is about to be fulfilled (DNTC, 1:404). The curious wording of JST Mark 9:3 does not imply that the Elias at the Trasfiguation was John the Baptist, but that in addition to Elijah the Prophet, John the Baptist was present (Bible Dictionary, p. 663).

Peter, James, and John, like many prophets of old, saw the earth in its' trasfigured state (D&C 63:20-21). They also recieved the keys of the kingdom from Christ, Moses and Elijah, the singular most important event between Christ's baptism and Gethsemene (Teachings, p. 158; Studies in Scripture, 5:305-308).

Peter, James, and John saw the majesty of Christ (2 Peter 1:16) and were instructed upon his death and resurrection (JST Luke 9:31).

Some believe that Peter, James, and John recieved their endowment upon the mount and could possibly have received the more sure word of prophecy, or their calling and election made sure (DOS, 2:165; JST 2 Peter 1:19).

The Father's voice came out of a cloud and bore record of His son (Luke 9:34-35). This could have been a possible visistation of the Father (Mortal Messiah, 3:61; Teachings, pp. 150-51).
