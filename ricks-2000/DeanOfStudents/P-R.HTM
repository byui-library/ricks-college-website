Parking Overnight
Overnight parking on campus streets and parking lots, as well as city streets is prohibited from November 1st to March 1st every year. This regulation is necessary to facilitate snow removal. Vehicles parked in violation of this regulation will be cited regardless of weather conditions and towed at the owner’s expense when impeding snow removal equipment.

A few designated lots are exempt from this regulation. They are the lots used by the on campus residence halls; Biddulph Lot, Taylor Lot (general parking area only), Pioneer Lot (between the girls on-campus residence halls), North Stadium Lot, and the Manwaring East Lot (general parking area only). Contact Campus Police at Kimball 150, ext. 2448 for information.

Parking Permits
A BYU-Idaho Parking Permit is required to park in all on-campus lots. General Parking Permits are available at Campus Police, Kimball 150, for a $5 fee and are good for the academic year in lots designated "General Parking." Faculty and Staff parking lots are reserved and require a Faculty/Staff Permit. Permits and parking regulations can be obtained from Campus Police.

Payroll Office
All hiring of student employees must be processed through the Student Employment Office, located in Kimball 244. This process must be completed before students work for any departments. Plagiarism
Academic dishonesty is defined by BYU-Idaho as an unacceptable act by any student, such as plagiarism or falsifying documents, that would gain that student an advantage in admission, grading, graduating from the university, or qualifying for entrance into any academic program.

Academic dishonesty and plagiarism undermine the foundation of a gospel centered educational experience. Honesty in test taking and writing assignments should be a fundamental commitment of each student. Every member of the campus community should be united in their effort as a community to help one another maintain their commitment and maintain their personal integrity without fail.

Plagiarism is defined as the attempt to pass off as ones own the unacknowledged and extensive appropriation of material which is not original to the student. It can consist of material taken from the text of a book, of an electronic publication, or of a fellow students work. Refer to the catalog under Academic Honesty for information in regards to possible penalties.

Police
Law enforcement and emergency services for the campus are provided by the BYU-Idaho Division of the Rexburg City Police. Members of the division are duly sworn municipal police officers. All violations of the law and requests for emergency services occurring on campus should be reported directly to the Campus Police by dialing ext. 2448 or 911 for emergencies. The division office is located in Kimball 150. Although Campus Division Officers have city wide jurisdiction, their specific area of responsibility is the campus of BYU-Idaho. Off-campus housing units are not university property; therefore, all requests for police service off-campus should be referred to the Rexburg Police Department by calling 356-5426.

Post Office
The Rexburg U.S. Post Office, 356-5031, is located one-half block north of the campus at 140 South Center Street. A full range of postal services is available. Some tips to assist you in sending and receiving mail are as follows:

1. Use correct and proper addressing and return address procedures with the proper two letter state abbreviation and zip code.
2. Use proper packaging materials for all parcels.
3. Students should provide all persons with whom they will correspond with their current, correct, and complete address. Students should check with their apartment manager or head resident for the proper address. The City of Rexburg has a single digit street number system. For example, 1st West cannot be used as 100 West. This will create mail receipt problems.
4. Students should learn and follow their apartment complexes procedure for sending and receiving mail.
5. Residents of off-campus housing units will need to file a change of address with the local post office at least two or three days before moving. On-campus residents will need to leave their forwarding address with their head resident, otherwise their mail will be returned to sender. All students residing in a unit which has a head resident or manager should provide that person with their proper forwarding address.
6. Stamps are available at the Cashiers Office and Manwaring Center stamp machine.

Posters
Prior to being displayed or circulated on campus, posters must be approved at the Manwaring Center Information Desk and stamped with the "Approved For Campus Display" stamp. Posters must be no larger than 14" x 22", well done, and be of poster board quality (no flyers), not of a commercial nature, and meet the standards of decency of BYU-Idaho. They must pertain to the university or relate to a university-sponsored activity or event. To find out where authorized areas for hanging posters are, pick up the brochure, "Approved Placement For Posters On Campus", at the Information Desk.

Probation--Academic
Students should check with their faculty advisor or the Registrar's Office on how to avoid academic probation or suspension.

Probation--Behavioral
Violations of the Honor Code, dress and grooming standards, housing regulations or criminal law may result in disciplinary action being taken through the Dean of Students Office. Disciplinary action can range from counsel and education to suspension, depending on the nature and seriousness of the violation. Probation, restrictions, and stipulations will be used to strengthen students’ commitment during the probationary period. For example, probation may make students ineligible to hold a campus job or to participate with an athletic team or performing group. Additionally, academic loads may be reduced, fines may be levied, curfews established, or restrictions placed on visits to other apartments. Terms of the probation are established between students and the Dean of Students or one of the assistant deans.

See Honor Code in the Standards Section

Repeating Classes
Students may repeat classes in which they have previously earned a poor grade. In calculating GPA's, the first grade will not be included. The first grade is not taken off the transcript, but is marked as "repeated," and is no longer calculated into your GPA. It is the second grade which counts. Updating student transcripts for repeated courses is automatic; students need not request the change. Refer questions to the Registrar's Office in Kimball 190.

Right of Access
BYU-Idaho is a private institution; and all grounds, walkways, roads, parking areas, and buildings owned by the university are private property. As such, the university retains the right and legal authority to limit or otherwise restrict access to the campus for any reason it may deem appropriate. As an educational institution, BYU-Idaho affirms its right to guard or protect employees, students, and guests from any interference which would disrupt any class, program, activity, event, or permitted function. This includes stalking or other conduct which is threatening or disruptive to students, employees, or guests on campus properties.

Individuals determined to fall within the purview of this policy will be banned from campus and if banned will be subject of arrest and prosecution for Criminal Trespass in violation of Idaho Code 18-7008(8) if they return to campus without authorization.
