MAX CRANER NAMED AS MAN OF EXCELLENCE

A lifelong dedication to service was recognized at the Men's Week fireside. Elder Max W. Craner was presented with the Man of Excellence Award by President David A. Bednar. Elder Craner is a former Area Authority currently serving as president of the Logan Utah Temple. He worked for 40 years with the Church Education System, including supervision of seminaries and institutes.

"You must learn to be guided by personal revelation," Elder Max W. Craner told students in his fireside message. He shared four guidelines concerning the young people of the Church today. "You were held back to come forth for a specific purpose," he told his audience in the Hart Auditorium. "You were foreordained to be on the earth at this time." Secondly, he asked his listeners to "share the Gospel" by being missionaries throughout their lives. "Marry in the temple and learn to raise a family," he said was the third guideline; and finally, "make and keep covenants made in the temple of the Lord." In closing he urged the students to "build a Zion setting in your apartments."

A native of the Declo-Burley area, Elder Craner graduated from Ricks College in 1953 when it was a four-year school. While employed by CES, Brother Craner organized some 300 seminaries in the Northeastern United States and Canada. He has been institute director at Snow College and Idaho State University and was area director for CES for southern Idaho. After retiring from CES in 1989, Brother Craner made his home in Rexburg and taught for two years as a volunteer part-time instructor in the Religion Department at Ricks. He served as president of the Philippines Quezon City Mission. He and his wife, the former Evelyn Parke, are the parents of six daughters and currently have 47 grandchildren.

 EMMY WINNING ACTORS TO PERFORM AT RICKS

"Behind the Broken Words," an evening of 20th century poetry, drama and comedy, will feature Emmy Award- winning actors Roscoe Lee Browne and Anthony Zerbe. Presentations will be Thursday and Friday, March 1 and 2, in the Kirkham Auditorium at 7:30 p.m. Tickets are $6 for the general public. "Behind the Broken Words," devised in the early 1970s has toured to 90-plus theaters across the country. The program will consist of two acts of mixed spoken-word poetry and dramatic verse.

Browne is an OBIE and Emmy Award winner and a Tony nominee. He launched his theatre career with the inaugural season of the New York Shakespeare Festival. He has appeared both off and on Broadway and in theatre festivals throughout the United States and Europe. He has thrice received the L.A. Drama Critics Award for Best Actor and has also received the Helen Hayes Award for distinguished work in the theatre. He has worked extensively in television include his Emmy winning role in "The Cosby Show."

Zerbe is recognized as one of the country's most versatile actors with extensive credits in film, television, and theatre. He received an Emmy Award for his role as Lt. Trench in the popular series "Harry-O." His stage work includes appearances both on and off Broadway and with leading resident theatres across the country including five summer seasons at the Old Globe Theatre. He is widely respected for his work in developing new plays.

 

JAZZ FESTIVAL TO FEATURE RECORDING ARTIST KEVIN MAHOGANY

The third annual Invitational Jazz Festival at Ricks College on Saturday, March 10, will feature Warner Brothers recording artist Kevin Mahogany, currently the most-in-demand male jazz vocalist. The evening performance at 7:30 p.m. in the Hart Auditorium will begin with the outstanding high school jazz band selected from the participants. Then Ricks College's Sound Alliance will take the stage along with vocalist Mahogany; Robert McCurdy, professor of trumpet and directors of jazz bands at the University of Idaho; and Chris Brady, free lance composer and producer, on piano. Concert tickets are available from the Ticket Office for $6 and $8 for the general public.

Mahogany has been in high demand since 1996 when he appeared in the film "Kansas City" portraying a character inspired by Big Joe Turner. He also appears on the current Malpaso release "Eastwood after Hours," a Clint Eastwood ensemble project performed and recorded live in Carnegie Hall.

The Ricks festival is designed to provide jazz education for local high schools and junior high schools. The guest artists will be holding instructional workshops throughout the day. Beginning at 8 a.m. in the Snow Drama Theater, schools will perform and receive adjudication from respected performers and jazz educators dedicated to presenting and education about jazz - one of the only truly original American art forms.

 

BELARUSSIAN OCTET TO PERFORM IN A FREE CONCERT

The Belarussian Wind Octet is scheduled to perform Wed., March 7, at 7:30 p.m. in the Snow Recital Hall. Admission for the event is free. "This has been in the works for two years, and we are now able to allow them to perform at Dixie State College, Utah State University, Ricks College and BYU," said BYU Chairman of the Performing Art Series Kenneth Crossley who has been instrumental in the organization of the Belarus visit. "I am very excited to see this come through."

The repertoire of the ensemble consists of classic compositions of West European, Russian, Soviet and Belorussian composers. As part of their program, the octet will also premier a piece written by BYU faculty member Murray Boren. The ensemble includes two flutes, two oboes, two bassoons and two clarinets. Since its formation in 1993, the ensemble of the soloists have successfully participated in 20 international festivals. In 1997, the ensemble won first place in the International Competition of chamber ensembles in Rovno in the Ukraine.
