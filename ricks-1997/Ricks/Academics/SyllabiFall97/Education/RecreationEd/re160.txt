I. COURSE DESCRIPTION:  Camping techniques required for harsh environment 
	penetration, including snowshoe and cross-country ski skills as 
	well as shelter and meal preparation.

II. STATEMENT OF PURPOSE:  The purpose of this course is to provide 
	students with the principles and practice of the advanced camping 
	and navigation skills required to negotiate the special concerns 
	one finds in a winter environment.  The course will focus mainly 
	on the needs of the individual or family camper, but will also apply 
	to leaders who will be responsible for short-term winter outings.
	With changes in the severity of winter weather, one would assume 
	that those who venture into this paradise below zero would do so 
	with educated caution. However, within rising trends in winter 
	recreational activities (i.e.: skiing, mountaineering, snowmobiling, 
	hunting) we find a parallel rise in the deaths and injuries 
	attributed to lack of experience and preparation of participants.  
	By training skilled users we increase the level of judgement and 
	environmental ethics which make the winter wilderness an enjoyable 
	and safe place to be.

III. REQUIRED PREREQUISITE: None, however, basic camping experience should 
	be gained by participants, and backpacking is recommended.  
	RE 160 is a Level II course.

IV. COURSE OBJECTIVES:    
	A. To experience the winter world in a safe, enjoyable and clean 
	manner.
        B. To develop an ability to plan and participate in a winter 
	camping experience.
        C. To gain basic exposures to various methods of locomotion 
	and navigation in winter terrain.
        D. To develop an awareness and respect for the severity of 
	hazards and safety procedures found in winter camping.
        E. To qualify students as skilled and concerned users of winter lands.
