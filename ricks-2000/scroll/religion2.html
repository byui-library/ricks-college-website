‘Light of the World’ musical production prepares for opening show in February

Christina Rivers
riv01002@byui.edu
scroll staff

The reflection of the spirit of man and the glory of God will radiate from inspirational music, stories, dances and international costumes into the audience of the Salt Lake City Conference Center during the month of February for the production “Light of the World: A Celebration of Life.”

The musical production is sponsored by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. Intended to create a more positive experience for visitors to the 2002 Winter Olympics, the show will be the first major cultural production put on by the Church.

“One of the key elements we’re going to try to convey in this show is the light of Jesus Christ reaching across the world. It is the same light that touches everyone, that lights up our souls and enhances the inner light that is already there,” John Featherstone, production lighting designer, said.

The production was first announced by Elder Robert D. Hales, the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles, on Sept. 19, shortly after the terrorist attacks in New York and Washington D.C.

“In the wake of last week’s tragic events, we believe this light has burned even more brightly and intensely in the hearts and minds of Americans and others throughout the world,” Elder Hales said.

“We have seen acts of bravery, courage and compassion that cause us to pause and reflect on the sanctity of life. It is this light which reflects the glory of God and the spirit of mankind that we wish to celebrate with the world,” he continued.

“Light of the World” will include a cast of more than 1,500 participants who will represent countries from all over the world in an attempt to bridge the gap between cultural, ethnic and language boundaries.

Five award-winning Latter-day Saint musicians who wish to remain anonymous have donated their time and talents to compose the original score for the “Light of the World” production, most of which will be set to music either through lyrical song or underscoring.

Featherstone has provided lighting for concerts of prominent music groups and attractions at Walt Disney theme parks and Universal Studios.

It will take Featherstone and his crew one hour to program each minute of the show.

Over 100,000 tickets will be sold for the musical production at $5 per person. There is a ticket limit of 10 tickets per person.

For more information regarding the show, log on to the Web site, www.events.lds.org.
