October 23, 2001

Former mission president  

speaks at BYU-Idaho devotional


REXBURG, Idaho –Sander L. Larson, former president of the Washington Seattle mission and former Rexburg resident, addressed the importance of being a light to others during Tuesday’s devotional Oct. 23.

Larson was employed at Ricks College from 1975 to 1998, where he worked as the associate director of public relations.

The native of Southern California explained that inside the mission boundaries where he presided, 10 lighthouses could be found.

"It was a constant reminder that at one time all ships needed to pay heed to those lights that would give them their location, and lead them safely into shore or out to sea as the case may be," he said. "We are beacons ourselves, bulwarks that people can look to, a way that they can find safe passage back to something perhaps that they don’t even have."

He then noted that while the light of righteous living attracts others toward it, so it can also work in reverse. "If we are weak or confused or not sure of our position, we sometimes can be misled," he said.

As a teenager, Larson said he began to notice and get involved with an outstanding group of students. "Something about them made me want to be around them more and more. This group was LDS, and soon they were inviting me to meetings. They were beacons for me, and after attending church meetings and firesides with them for some time, I decided to be join their church."

Larson continued to share experiences where the example set by Latter-Day Saints made the difference in someone’s life. "One of my sons served in the Sydney Australia Spanish-speaking mission, and received a letter after he had returned home," he said. The letter was from a young missionary, whose mother had been taught the gospel by Larson’s son. "Because of you and your companion, and your decision to serve the Lord, my mother and my grandparents have been sealed in the temple. Now I am serving a mission, and the people that I am teaching thank you as well," quoted Larson from the letter.

As lights to others, Larson counseled, "Do not drift. Stay close to the things the Lord would you do. How? By personal prayer and study of the scriptures."

He shared five steps to happiness. "First: keep the commandments. Second, use the scriptures. Third, always be an example, be happy. Fourth, Be prayerful, and to thine own self be true. Fifth and last, look for opportunities to let your light shine."

Before working for as the associate director of public relations, Larson was also the dean of students at Ricks College. Prior to his employment with the college, he had a career in teaching and education administration. Larson received his bachelor’s degree in political science with a minor in history from BYU, and his master’s degree in educational administration from the University of Utah.

The devotional speaker for next week is Kevin Shiley, department chair of business management at BYU-Idaho. Devotionals are broadcast live on KBYI 100.5 FM and rebroadcast every Tuesday night at 9 p.m.
