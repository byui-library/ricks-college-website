Ricks College News Release
May 22, 2001

Vice President leaving Ricks College
 for mission assignment

REXBURG, Idaho — Ricks College Vice President Brent Kinghorn has lived in his comfort zone long enough. "My car pulls out of the garage and automatically heads in the direction of Ricks College," he says. Before long the lifetime Sugar City resident will leave his familiar surroundings that he's known since childhood to become president of a church mission in the relatively poor and unknown eastern European country of Albania.  "It's a land we've never visited and a language we've never spoken," he says.  The 60-year-old Community Services vice president will be taking a three-year leave of absence from the college with the prospect of a job upon his return if he so chooses.

Employees and community friends will say goodbye to Kinghorn and his wife, Donna Jean, on his last day of work, May 31, from 2-4 p.m. in the executive offices reception area located in the Kimball Building.

The couple will have a few weeks at home before checking into the Mission Training Center in Provo, Utah, June 24. After a week of intensive training, they are expected to reach the capital city of Tirana where they will live July 3.

In preparation for the three-year assignment for The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, he and his wife (who has taught part time in the English department for the past several years) have been learning the basics of the Albanian language over the phone. His wife visits for an hour each morning with a tutor from the Mission Training Center and he visits each afternoon at 5.  Having learned German on a mission he served as a young man, Kinghorn says Albanian is unrelated to any other language and they are learning from scratch.  While he's committed to learning the language, he says the population is considered 100 percent literate and many younger Albanians know English because it's taught in school.

They've also been learning everything they can about the little known country that would stump anyone on "Who Wants To Be a Millionaire?" The country of 3.5 million people is about the size of Maryland. It's directly east across the Adriatic Sea from Italy, north of Greece and south and west of the politically troubled spots of Kosovo, Serbia and Macedonia.  Kinghorn has been told there are only four traffic lights in all of Tirana with a population of 350,000. There are few paved roads and only one railroad that crosses the country.

About 70 percent of the people are Moslem, 20 percent Greek Orthodox and 10 percent other Christian denominations. Latter-day Saints number about 700 in the seven branches in all of Albania. Because there is no organized stake, he will also serve as the presiding church officer in the country and have other church duties besides serving as mission president.

The first Latter-day Saint missionaries went into Albania in 1991 and the mission was organized in 1996. Today there are 34 missionaries, including three older couples, but the force is expected to grow to 40 by fall. The Book of Mormon was only translated into Albanian in 1999.

"We understand they are very friendly, very family oriented and quite a moral people," he says. "It will be a privilege to serve them."  Interestingly enough, eight young Albanians attended Ricks College during the past year so the Kinghorns have learned many things firsthand. "They're wonderful people," he says.

Leaving home for three years requires plenty of preparation. There are doctor visits, shots, dental work, extra eyeglasses and prescriptions to be prepared. There are vehicles to be sold and household items to be stored.

The church has allowed them to ship 1,800 pounds of belongings to Albania. This includes everything from pictures for the walls and books for their new home to scarce food items like cream of mushroom soup, chocolate chips and popcorn. These items take about two months to be shipped and should be there when they arrive.

Their son Brian, who lives in Idaho Falls and teaches at Ricks, plans to sell his home and move into the family home in Sugar City for the next three years.

All but one of the Kinghorn children are married and have families of their own. Their youngest, 19-year-old Michael, just left on his own two-year-long mission to Chile May 16. They will also leave elderly parents and 18 grandchildren behind.

Besides getting their household affairs in order, they are gearing up for missionary work by reading a pile of guidelines and handbooks supplied by the church. They must also be prepared to be spiritual leaders of a host of missionaries who will depend upon them for guidance and support.

Kinghorn is in his 34th year of working at Ricks. For the first 11 years he worked for the BYU-Ricks Center for Continuing Education and eventually became its director. He also was director of the Ricks College Summer and Evening Schools for several years.

In 1978 he was appointed to serve as vice president of the newly created Community Services area by Ricks President Bruce Hafen. He will have been the only person to ever serve in this capacity because the Community Services area is being dissolved and transitioned into a new area called Advancement. His area included oversight of admissions, alumni relations, athletics, continuing education, development and public relations.

During his years at Ricks he served with seven presidents, beginning with President John Clarke and including Henry B. Eyring, Bruce C. Hafen, Joe J. Christensen, Steven D. Bennion and David A Bednar. He also worked with Harry Maxwell who served as acting president for a year.

"I always wanted to work for Ricks College," he says. "I've had a wonderful job - I can't think of a better one. It's been an honor to spend my career here."

Kinghorn adds, "It's also been a great opportunity to work with the greater Rexburg community. We have been truly blessed to be a part of the growth and development of this area." He's been involved with many community organizations, including the Sugar-Salem District Board of Education, Madison Memorial Hospital Board, Rotary Club, Madison County Coordinating Council and Rexburg Chamber of Commerce.
