ELDER L. ALDIN PORTER HONORED AS MAN OF EXCELLENCE

Elder L. Aldin Porter was honored with the Man of Excellence Award at the Men's Week Fireside Sunday night. Elder Porter serves as the senior member of the Presidency of the Seventy and on the Board of Trustees for Ricks College. Presenting the award was the president of Ricks College, Elder David A. Bednar. President Bednar shared comments collected about Elder Porter. The now-grown Porter children said they felt their father had an "eternal impact on their lives" as they grew up knowing their father loved them, that he loved the truths found in the scriptures, and that he expected them to learn to make correct choices. Elder Boyd K. Packer said that "Elder Porter is a wise and spiritually powerful man. . . . I count him as a dear and close friend and value his counsel. Perhaps the best recommendation for Aldin Porter becomes apparent when you meet his wife, Shirley. They are a wonderful team, equally yoked together."

Following the presentation of the award, Elder Porter addressed the audience of approximately 6,000 students gathered in the Hart Auditorium, Taylor Chapel, and Kirkham Auditorium. He also gave tribute to his wife who was willing to set aside her degree in accounting to be a full-time wife and homemaker. "Her education has had a marvelous impact on our family." Speaking directly to the female students, he said, ">I encourage you, as Brigham Young did, to get all the education you can."

Elder Porter explained the connection between love and the willingness to serve and to be self-sacrificing. He said, "One way to measure love is by the amount of sacrifice you are willing to give." The feelings of love need to be put into action. The concepts of love and service applies into all types of relationships. "This principle is true for parents, friends, missionary companions, spouses, and for the building up of the kingdom."

"If someone followed you for a day, would they say that they had followed a disciple of the Lord Jesus Christ?" Elder Porter asked for introspection as to whether personal actions reveal a love of the Lord, neighbors, wives, husbands, and children. He said, "We can control who we love. True love grows out of caring, kindness, and self-sacrifice."

According to Elder Porter, the youth of today live in a great time of opportunity. "The question is what will you do with it," he said. "These are your days of preparation to step forward and take the responsibilities of [leadership] assignments that come in the kingdom. . . . Go forth with confidence and assurance."

He said that the youth of today have often heard they are "chosen" to be on the earth during these last days. "You are chosen. You are chosen to love all the days of your life; chosen to serve all the days of your life; chosen to pray. . . to think; and chosen to give, because the Lord knows that in that way you will find the greatest happiness."

FOSTER NAMED RICKS MAN OF THE YEAR

Rob Foster, a sophomore from Raleigh, N.C., was named Ricks College Man of the Year Thursday night. Foster, age 23, graduated from Milbrook High School in Raleigh in 1994 and is majoring in sports medicine. He was sponsored by the Black Students Union of which he is the vice president. He was also recently the chairperson for the college's Multicultural Festival. He is a returned missionary from the California Arcadia Mission.

FAMILY HISTORY CONFERENCE WILL HELP TO DISCOVER ROOTS

The Ricks College Family History Center and the Continuing Education Department are co-hosting the annual Spring Family History Conference Saturday, March 11. The daylong conference will run from 8 to 5 p.m. Anyone with a genealogical interest is welcome. Pre-registration fees for the conference will be $13 per person until March 8, or one may register Saturday morning in the McKay Library foyer for $15 on a space-available basis. For more information, contact (208) 356-2336 or 356-1040, or e-mail campus@ricks.edu. Organizers expect approximately 250-300 people from eastern Idaho and adjacent states to be in attendance at the conference. DJ Simpson, area advisor on family history, will be a guest speaker representing the LDS Church Area Presidency.

WINTER CHOIRFEST SET FOR SATURDAY AT RICKS

The Ricks College Music Department will present the Winter Choirfest Saturday, March 4, at 7:30 p.m. in the Barrus Concert Hall. Tickets are $2 for the public and $1 for Ricks students. The concert will feature four Ricks choirs, including Collegiate Singers, Women's Glee, College Choir and Concert Chorale. "Selections performed will be representative of the very best that the choirs have learned to this point in the semester," director Clyde Luke says. Luke directs the Concert Chorale. Collegiate Singers and College Choir are under the direction of by Eda Ashby. The Women's Glee is directed by Mary Beth Jones.
