The Department of Computer Aided Engineering Technologies prepares students for a variety of career opportunities in today's technologically oriented society including CAD/CAM Technology, Computer Aided Design Technology, Manufacturing Engineering Technology, and Welding Engineering Technology. Graduates enjoy a high level of job satisfaction, excellent salary range, and good job placement.

The major in CAD/CAM Technology (325), gives graduates an introductory education into engineering technology relating to Computer Aided Design (CAD), Computer Aided Manufacturing (CAM), and Advanced Welding Processes Technology while completing the General Education course requirements for the Associate of Arts and Sciences degree. The program is designed for students who have an interest in discovering what technology is about and how it is applied in today's world. This major will help focus a student's interest in the area of technology.

The major in Computer Aided Design Technology (048), involves graduates in the process of translating creative ideas and concepts into models, computer simulations, or working drawings used to produce new machines or products. Emphasis is placed on traditional design concepts coupled with strong Computer Aided Design (CAD) skills. The curriculum includes Computer Aided Design (CAD), Solids Modeling, Computer Rendering and Animation, CAD Customization, Computer Aided Manufacturing, (CAM), and Computer Numerical Control (CNC). The program is designed for transfer to a university or immediate employment.

The major in Manufacturing Engineering Technology (087), involves the graduates in selecting the methods of production, operations, machine selection, and tooling. They deal with the actual production processes through which designer's concepts are translated into manufactured goods in a cost efficient manner. The curriculum includes Computer Aided Design (CAD), Computer Aided Manufacturing (CAM), Computer Numerical Control (CNC), Quality Control, Tool Design, Process Planning, and Hydraulics & Pneumatics. Program is designed for transfer to university or immediate employment.

The Associate Degree in Welding Engineering Technology (110), provides hands-on training in the Welding processes and develop skills leading to Welder Certification. They are taught to recognize and understand welding processes. The curriculum includes a solid foundation in Welding Theory, Metallurgy, Welding Examination, Quality Control, and Computer Aided Processes (Robotic Welding). The Program is designed for transfer to a university or immediate employment. The Associate Degree in Welding Engineering Technology is easily transferred for those students continuing on to a University and pursuing a career in Welding Engineering, Welding Engineering Technology, Manufacturing Engineering Technology, Metallurgical Science, etc. It fulfills many of the requirements of General Education, math, chemistry and physics required at the University level.

Certificate in Welding Technology (322), is a one year program designed for the student who wants to enter the welding industry well trained and ready for a challenging, rewarding career. This course is hands-on and labor intensive. You will receive instruction and training using the latest welding techniques on modern equipment. You will be trained in SMAW, GTAW, GMAW, and FCAW. Instruction also includes robotic welding, automatic thermal cutting, repair and maintenance welding.

For the student going on a mission, this may be the course for them. It can be completed in one year enabling them to leave on a mission with a certificate of graduation and return job ready.

Our goals include having you certified in pipe and plate and able to fit and weld in all positions.

This program which will start in September and end in May.

For more specific information, please contact the department head.

COURSE DESCRIPTIONS

COMPUTER-AIDED DESIGN

CAD 112 Intermediate Engineering Graphics 3
Prerequisite: CAD 172 or consent of Instructor
Three one hour classes per week

The purpose of this course is to build the students design skills by introducing them to more in-depth design techniques and skills with CAD as the tool. Topics include engineering graphics, precision dimensioning and tolerancing (GD&T), pictorial layouts, introduction to manufacturing materials and forming processes, threads and fasteners, and detail drawing layout. Use of 3D CAD will be heavily stressed. Students should continue developing their portfolio.

CAD 172 Engineering Graphics: Principles and Applications 3
Three one hour classes per week

Review of engineering graphics principles. Fundamentals of CAD engineering drawing principles, descriptive geometry, and geometric dimensioning and tolerancing.

CAD 211 Advanced Engineering Graphics 3
Prerequisite: CAD 172 and CAD 213 (CAD 213 can be concurrent)
Three one hour classes per week

This course teaches advanced design skills using 3D CAD and rendering tools. Subject matter includes working drawings (detailed and assembly), introduction to belts, gears, and cams, sheet metal drawings, structural steel, electrical manufacturing tooling, presentation quality output. Emphasis on industrial quality output will be stressed. All design work will be done on 3D CAD tools.

CAD 212 Descriptive Geometry 3
Three one hour classes per week

Fundamentals of CAD engineering drawing principles. Special relationships and design principles relating to point, line, plane, curved lines, and surfaces, vectors, intersections, and developments with application to practical problems.

CAD 213 Introduction to Computer Rendering and Animation 3
Three one hour classes per week

An introductory course covering fundamental principles of computer generated illustrations, still renderings, and full motion animations using 3D Studio Max software. Topics include understanding 3D work environment, object creation and modification, materials, lighting, cameras, rendering, animation, lofting, morphing, boolean operations, space warps, hierarchal links, inverse kinematics, and dynamic simulations.

CAD 234 AutoCAD 2
Two two hour classes per week

Computer aided drafting design AutoCAD software. Class includes 2D and 3D drawing generation, solids modeling, and other software applications.

CAD 238 Advanced AutoCAD 3
Three one hour classes per week

A study of advanced Computer Aided Design applications using AutoCAD software. Topics include advanced solids modeling, drawing
layout, menu & toolbar creation, slides, script files, linetype & crosshatching creating, attributes.

MANUFACTURING ENGINEERING TECHNOLOGY

MT 131 Machine Tool Operation 3
Five instruction hours per week

Operating principles of basic machine tools. Major emphasis is on metal lathe operation and techniques and acquaintance with precision measuring instruments.

MT 132 Manufacturing Processes 2
Prerequisite: None
Two instruction hours per week

Introduction of modern manufacturing, methods and processes; shows possibilities and limitations of these processes and their application to fabrication of industrial products.

MT 132L Manufacturing Processes Lab 1
Prerequisite: Concurrent enrollment in MT 132

Enrolling for this optional lab will make it possible for Engineering majors to transfer the class for MFE 232 at BYU. MFE is required for all Mechanical and Manufacturing majors at BYU.

MT 134 Machine Tool Operation II 3
Prerequisite: MT 131
Six instruction hours per week

Planning and machining of metal parts to print dimensions, cutting feeds and speeds, surface finish, tool geometry, horsepower requirements and tool materials.

MT 222 Engineering Materials 3
Prerequisite: Chem 105
Five instruction hours per week

Physical properties, composition and constituents of metals and metal alloys. Laboratory work consists of preparation of metallographic samples for microscopic examination and also the effects of heat treatment on steels. (Fall)

MT 231 Tool Design 3
Prerequisite: CAD 172, MT 131 and 132
Five instruction hours per week

Design and evaluation of jigs and fixtures for manufacturing processes. Introduction to design concepts of pressworking tools. (Fall)

MT 234 Computer Numerical Control (CNC) 3
Prerequisite: MT 131 & 134, Math 110 & 111
Six instruction hours per week

Automated machine control through Computer-Aided Manufacturing (CAM) and Computer Numerical Control (CNC) will be the main emphasis of the course, with a brief introduction to Automated Program Tools. (APT). In the laboratory, students gain practical experience on CAM and CNC software and equipment. (Winter)

MT 236 Hydraulics and Pneumatics 3
Prerequisite: Math 101 or equivalent
Five instruction hours per week

A study of the fundamental principles governing the transmission and control of fluid power (hydraulic and pneumatic) . The course includes a study of the basic components of hydraulic and pneumatic systems and how they are combined to build up various circuits. (Winter)

MT 252 Quality Control 3
Prerequisite: Math 101 or equivalent
Three one-hour lectures per week

Fundamental coverage of quality control concepts. Teaches the student the quality concepts and problem solving techniques associated with statistical process control.

Emphasis will be placed on statistical methods. Computers will be used for analyzing data and plotting charts.

MT 291R Professional Seminar 0

Attendance required for all manufacturing majors. Held three times each semester to discuss technical developments, career opportunities, advisement, etc. Pass-fail determined by attendance.

WELDING ENGINEERING TECHNOLOGY

Weld 105 Essentials of Welding 3-4
Two hours lecture, six hours lab per week
Lab fees, $25 to partially cover material costs

A general Welding course which includes Oxyacetylene welding, Oxyfuel Cutting (OFC), Shielded Metal Arc Welding (SMAW), Gas Metal Arc Welding (GMAW), Flux Core Arc Welding (FCAW), Gas Tungsten Arc Welding (GTAW), in the flat and horizontal positions. Includes the safety procedures, electrode identification and focuses on theory and practice.

Welding majors should take the four credit class (Fall and Winter semesters).

Weld 117 Repair and Maintenance Welding 2
Prerequisite: Weld 105 or consent of instructor
One hour lecture;Two hours Lab per week

Welding techniques, principles, theory, and practices used in the repair and maintenance of equipment. Includes brazing, cast-iron , aluminum welding, carbon arc gouging, hard surfacing and other process. The study of the physical and mechanical properties, as well as chemical composition of the materials to be repaired will be included. (1st Fall Block)

Weld 123 Advanced Weld Processes 3
Prerequisite: Weld 105
Two hours lecture; four hours lab per week
Lab Fee: $25 to partially cover material costs

Advanced pre-employment training which emphasizes developing manipulative proficiency in the use of SMAW, FCAW, GMAW, and GTAW in the vertical and overhead positions in light and heavy gauge plate. (Winter)

Weld 218 Welding Fabrication 2-3
Prerequisite: Weld 105
Two hours lecture; four hours lab per week

The use and application of equipment, tools, fasteners, and processes used in fabrication practices in the welding industry. Interpretation of drawings for fabrication of small projects. Techniques for layout, fitting, squaring, tacking, and fabricating will be developed. May be repeated for credits. (Fall and Winter)

Weld 225 Pipe and Structural Welding 4
Prerequisite: Weld 105, 123, or concurrent enrollment
Two hours lecture; six hours lab per week
Lab Fee: $20 to partially cover material costs

A variety of material and configurations using SMAW, GTAW, FCAW and GMAW processes on pipe and structural shapes. The welding will be done in all positions on carbon, stainless steel and aluminum. (Fall)

Weld 229 Certification and Code Welding 3
Prerequisite: Weld 105, Weld 123, Weld 225 or concurrent enrollment
Two hours lecture; four hours lab per week

Interpreting and writing welding procedures and welder qualifications and specifications. Welding to AWS, API, and ASME codes to meet the entry level employment standards for Code work. Training in the procedures using SMAW, STAW, GMAW, and FCAW processes for qualification of welders in all positions on plate, pipe and rebar. May be taken more than once. A qualification test will be given at end of course by a qualified Certified Welding Testing Lab. (Winter)

Weld 250 Welding Equipment Maintenance and Service 2
Two hours lecture; one hour lab per week

A study of the theory, application, and practices used in maintenance of welding equipment used in the Welding industry.(2nd Fall Block)

Weld 256 Automated Welding Processes 3
Prerequisite: Weld 105, 123, or current enrollment
Two hours lecture; two hours lab per week

A survey of the automated welding processes and their applications used within today's welding industry. (Panograph, Robotics, Sub-Arc, Etc.) Field trips and hands-on learning will be used.

Weld 258 Non-Destructive Testing and Inspection 2
Prerequisite: Weld 105, 123, 225 or current enrollment
Two hours lecture; two hours lab per week

Theory and application of non-destructive testing systems. Techniques for quality control. Includes magnetic particle, liquid penetrant, leak, visual and ultrasonic testing.

Weld 290 Special Projects 1-3
Prerequisite: Consent of the Department Chair

Weld 298 Welding Technology Work Experience 1-3
Prerequisite: Consent of the Department Chair

Work experience with selected welding firms. Work period may be arranged during regular semesters. An acceptable written report on the organization and the work done will be required.
