AUTO 165 - DRIVE MECHANISMS

TUESDAYS & THURSDAYS, 7:00 A.M.

INSTRUCTOR: Bro. Dondavid S. Powell
TEXT: Erjavic, Jack; Today's Technician - Manual Transmissions and Transaxles, 2nd Edition, Delmar, 1997.

Objective: The purpose of this course is to give you a working knowledge of the automobile drive train including clutches, manual transmissions, transaxles, transfer cases, drive lines, u-joints and differentials. All work in the class is to be restricted to these areas unless specially assigned by the instructor.
    Your grade will be determined as follows:
                Class assignments         40%
                Lab Work                    40%
                Attendance/Tardies      10%
                Subjective/attitude        10%

Chapter assignments are due as assigned on the Daily Schedule. You will be expected to have studied those chapters by that date. Read the schedule often so you aren't surprised when they are due! The Fill In The Blanks in the Classroom Manual and the ASE Style Review Questions from the Shop Manual are to be answered and turned in on the day the chapter will be discussed according to the Daily Schedule.

Professional Articles: You are expected to turn in a total of 10 professional articles this semester, worth 5 points each. They should only include topics covered by this class.  Copies of articles or Internet articles should be highlighted, (NO ORIGINALS) and only turn in one each Tuesday and/or Thursday until finished.

Research Paper: You are required to write a research paper for this class. Pick a topic relating to the course objectives. It should contain a text of about 10 pages again, plus your table of contents, footnotes, bibliography and appendices. Type it double spaced, single side of page only. It is to be completed and turned in ON OR BEFORE March 15, 2001. It is worth 100 points and will be graded on content and structure (including spelling!) I'd suggest your doing it on a word processor so it will be easy to correct your mistakes. If you would like it checked over before it is graded, turn it in at least a week ahead of time and I will go over it and return it so you can make the changes to improve your grade. If you would rather do a class presentation, it must be cleared by March 1st and a date for presentation established.

Shop Work: You are responsible to line up your own work. Many people call the division secretaries and request work. You should check with the secretaries regularly to find the type or work you need for the class. As you do the job, you should complete the lab reports and turn them in. You will not be given credit for a job unless they are completed and turned in, including flat rate time!

Labs: You are expected to be at your scheduled lab on time and ready to work. Tardies or absences from lab will affect your class grade separately from the class ones. They really hurt, so be there ON TIME! You need to learn to work alone, as you will be alone when you are working in the industry. You are expected to clean up thoroughly and on time.

Outside Work: Because everyone doesn't have the same opportunity or time schedules, there will be no credit for work done outside of class.

Lab Work: Do not give a completion date. When you line a car up the customer must understand that the car will be tied up for awhile. If he is not willing to leave it for the time it takes to fix it, tell him to take it somewhere else and pay labor. Don't guarantee your work beyond redoing it. Remember, you are here to learn! If you were already the best in the business, you wouldn't need to be here. You will probably not be able to find all of the jobs nor have time to do them all. Concentrate on the ones that you need the experience on and will get good points for. Look up the procedures on each of these jobs before you start them so that you can do them as you follow the steps in the service manual. Make sure you give me the completed pink copy of the work order when you are finished.

Parts: All work will be paid for before the car leaves the shop. This includes work done on ANY car, including your own! If you buy your parts here it is cost plus 10%. Customers pay LIST PRICE!

Be Careful: If you are involved in an accident with another car, you must pay ½ of the total deductible of $100, or $50. If you do not report it to the instructor immediately, you will pay the full $100. Believe it! Be especially carefully moving cars around in the compound.

Work orders: They are to be filled in AS YOU DO THE JOB! Don't finish and leave, figuring you can do the work order later, as this frustrates customers, irritates instructors, and is poor business practice. If the car isn't done, leave the work order and keys where they belong in the tool room. If it's done, complete the work order, give me the pink copy, and turn the rest and the keys in to the office. Park the customer's car in the lot across the street, NOT ON THE ROAD. Make sure the work order is fully filled out and signed by the customer when they leave the car. Some of the biggest problems we've had resulted from failure to have the work order signed. DON'T LOSE A WORK ORDER. YOU WILL BE CHARGED $15 FOR ANY WORK ORDER YOU LOSE, plus the parts billed to that work order!

Computer: Enter all parts on the computer as you use them, not later. You'll forget something if you don't, and it comes out of your pockets.

Appearance: Learn to look, think, and act like a professional. Keep your coveralls, clothes and tools clean. Use the paper floor-mats on all cars before climbing in. It will save greasy footprints. Make sure that you check the cars over for hand prints, footprints, etc. Remove them before the car goes out the door. Strive to do the best work you are capable of.

It is the responsibility of the student to be aware of these changes.

It is the student’s responsibility to disclose to the teacher any special and/or learning disabilities he/she may have before the first week of class is over.
