Outstanding Educator Award
CHARLOTTE L. ARNOLD

From student leadership at Ricks College to leading students as principal of Shelley High School since 1991, Charlotte is described as "an outstanding administrator. . .ethical in her dealings with students, faculty and the community. . .a fine role model, both in her behavior and her appearance. She is uncompromising in doing what she thinks is right. . ."

Charlotte grew up in the Lost River Valley on the family farm and ranch, and attended Mackay High School. She graduated from Ricks College in 1964, attended Utah State University, and graduated from Idaho State University in secondary education in 1968. She lives in Ammon.

At Ricks, Charlotte was the drum majorette her freshman year, a member of Kappa Chi, and was student body vice president of Support, and head cheerleader her sophomore year. "Ricks offered countless opportunities for students to be of service to others," she says. "Student leadership became an important part of the two years I spent at Ricks. I had the opportunity to meet and work with many students that served as role models as we worked together."

Charlotte started her teaching career in Visalia and San Jose, Calif., in 1968. After a four-year break, she returned to Idaho and taught at Butte High School in 1978 and 1979 and then moved to Shelley High School where she taught English. A fellow teacher recalls that "her teaching methods (were) effective, and her love of students is obvious."

Professionally she has served as president of the Six District Athletic Association, is a member of the Advisory Board for Counseling Education at ISU, and serves in other district positions. She is working towards her Doctor of Education degree in education leadership at ISU.

She is the mother of two children and stepmother of two. There are nine grandchildren. Her late husband, Blair, operated a farm and a cattle feeding operation in Idaho Falls. Charlotte is the first counselor in her ward’s Relief Society and a visiting teacher.
