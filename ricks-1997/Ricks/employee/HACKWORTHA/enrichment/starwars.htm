Lessons from
Star Wars Episode One - The Phantom Menace
© 2000 Allen Hackworth

Born of imagination and skill, the Star Wars
Episode One - The Phantom Menace presents
themes of mythic proportion.  Understanding these themes can enhance the quality of our own spiritual journey and development.  The Star Wars themes can be understood as: (1) lessons which teach the meaning of life, and (2) lessons which show us how to obtain a more abundant life. The lessons deal with eternal truths.

First, the Star Wars heroes have been called to a great adventure, a quest, which includes solving hard problems. These problems or ordeals usually start after someone brings bad news. Such ordeals are symbolic of situations in our own lives, yet our problems are on a smaller scale.

We hear that someone is sick and needs special attention. We hear that a pipe broke in our basement. We encounter sin, our own or others.  We hear our friend has misunderstood our intentions, and we must correct the emotional damage.  Or we aspire to lofty goals which require great work and sacrifice.

Second, because the participant becomes equal to the challenges, the Star Wars hero becomes more capable than earlier.  He or she grows and becomes more than he or she otherwise would have been. This same process occurs when each of us works to achieve our goals.  An inspired leader once said, "That which we persist in doing becomes easier.  Not that the nature of the thing has changed, but our ability to do has increased."

Third, the film acknowledges the presence of the Force.  The Jedi knights explain the Force as an "energy field that sustains all living things." The Jedi knights learn to trust this energy as something bigger than themselves, something bigger than their combined skills, wisdom, and understandings.

Using the Force, the Jedi knights absorb information which comes from beyond their five senses. This is necessary because the magnitude of the knights' challenges is greater than the knights' skill and insight.

That George Lucas acknowledges such an energy field gives Star Wars significance beyond the ordinary.  In more traditional terms, the Force would be called intuition or the Holy Ghost. Yet the Force is not only the Holy Ghost but also the spirit of Christ.

In our own traditions, enlightened scriptures teach that Christ's power is in all things, animates all things, and sustains all things.
We learn from the movie that if we go inside ourselves and listen to the inner voice and go outside ourselves and acknowledge a reality beyond our five senses, we will gain insights and convictions which can change our lives forever.  By reading scriptures, poetry, significant stories, and by watching good movies, and by coupling their messages with our own meditations, we can feel the spiritual intimations of the Force.

Fourth, gathering in earlier times as a "theological and philosophical study group," the Jedi knights discover values which are higher than themselves. Then coupled with the guidance of the Force, the knights live out their cherished principles and dreams. 

In Robin-Hood fashion, they fight to make the world a better place. They use their "might for right" in much the same way as did Arthur and the Knights of the Round Table.

Such a practice also describes the quest of thousands of LDS missionaries who serve as spiritual physicians for a soul-sick world. As young missionaries discover a cause greater than their own egocentric needs, and as they
struggle to advance this cause, missionaries are silently and slowly transformed into creatures of excellence, having an expanding personality and a growing sense of spiritual power.

The missionaries are enriched in much the same way as was Jedi Master Qui-Gon Jinn and youthful apprentice, Obi-Won Kenobi.  Again, both groups become aware of the needs of others; and both groups live to help others.

Fifth, we learn that coming from a mundane existence, regular people can be part of great events.  For young Anakin Skywalker, it started as a local race.  But Anakin is called to the hero quest and in time, his skills and insights, coupled with whisperings from the Force, help save Queen Amidala's planet from siege by the Trade Federation.

This situation is often repeated daily as ordinary people, our friends, prepare in ordinary ways for service.  Not knowing how, where, or when, they are eventually called to help calm the earthquakes in people's lives. Their quest may be to save a brother's or sister's personal planet from siege and destruction by the Dark Side.

Sixth, in Star Wars we observe the importance of the master-apprentice relationship. Before power is gained and granted, either for good or ill, training must be strictly followed. The quest is long and arduous, and many trials are presented before success is obtained.

Both Anakin and Obi-Won Kenobi require teachers, wise ones who give vital information for successfully completing the quest. Anakin Skywalker, who eventually fathers Luke and later becomes Darth Vader, will be trained by Obi-Won Kenobi. Obi-Won Kenobi is trained by Jedi Master Qui-Gon Jinn.  Additionally, all of these people receive guidance from the High Council and particularly Yoda.   Considering the Lord's way, He first tests and proves before He admits a candidate into His glory.

Seventh, we learn the dangers and enticements of the Dark Side.  Although it does not happen in this episode, even a creature of light like Anakin Skywalker, bright as "a son of the morning," later succumbs to the powers of
darkness.

Eight, at some point in the quest, the hero strikes rock bottom. While there, he or she is confronted by the Dark Side, face to face.  At this point, all good could be lost.

Ninth, finally we learn the importance of working as a team. No single act of courage could be strong enough to dislodge the Dark Side after it becomes enmeshed in the lives of hundreds of people, all pleased to follow
their baser instincts. Christ too enlisted the aid of millions to help fight against the destruction of Satan.

When you watch Star Wars Episode One - the Phantom Menace, enjoy yourself.  Eat the popcorn; drink the Pepsi.  But when you get home, ponder some of the themes which George Lucas has consciously included to make the movie more than just another science fiction flick.

It is more because it symbolically teaches truths which help us on our own hero quest of becoming like the greatest Jedi knight of all, Jesus Christ.
