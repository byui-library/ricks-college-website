Budget Carryover (3-1)

I. PURPOSE

To establish a policy for carrying over unused or deficit general fund budget amounts from one fiscal year to another.

II. DEFINITIONS

A. Calendar Year

For financial purposes BYU-Idaho is on a calendar year.

B. Unallocated Account

An unallocated account is an expense account code which is to be used only for carrying a budget surplus or deficit over into the next fiscal year. No expenditures are charged to this account.

C. Encumbrance

An encumbrance is an obligation in the form of orders, contracts, and similar items that will become payable when goods are delivered or services rendered. This term is synonymous with commitment.

D. Free Balance

Unused amounts left in an expense account after deducting expenses and encumbrances from the budget are free balances.

III. POLICY

A. Unused budgets or deficits will be carried over into the next calendar year in an amount up to one percent of the total departmental budget.

B. For purposes of computing the maximum one percent carryover, all salaries, wages, and non-personnel (i.e. supplies, equipment, computer, and other expense) budgets are to be included.

C. The "Budget Carryover" will be set up in an unallocated account in each division or administrative department. Division chairmen and administrative department heads will be responsible for distributing surplus or deficit budget carryovers back to their departments.

D. In no instance may the budget carryover be used to employ additional teaching personnel, administrative, or staff employees; or to increase salaries or wages; unless specific approval for such employment is authorized by the University Administration.

E. Encumbrances remaining at year end and budget balances equal to the encumbrance will also be carried forward into the new calendar year.

F. All free balances from salaries, wages, (except part-time temporary wages, overtime, and student wages) and phone equipment rental will be removed before computing unused budgets or deficits.

Budget carryover ceiling: 1% X $52,000 = $520

Memberships, Dues, and Subscriptions (3-3)

I. PURPOSE

To identify guidelines under which University funds may be appropriately used to pay for institutional memberships, professional dues, and subscriptions to magazines and professional journals.

II. DEFINITIONS

Institutional memberships are memberships in organizations with broadest campus impact, or specialized functions which ought to be centralized for the benefit of the entire University, or which require the public relations support of the University as a whole.

III. POLICY ON INSTITUTIONAL MEMBERSHIPS

Institutional memberships are appropriately paid from general University funds under the following guidelines:

A. When the University has a legitimate accreditation function and an appropriate membership is required.

B. Where appropriate advisory services are received such as salary information, guidance to meet governmental regulations, and similar items not readily available elsewhere.

C. Where organizations in which the University has a public relations or public service responsibility, or where it needs to have appropriate exposure among other institutions with similar problems and objectives.

D. When the request is signed by the appropriate vice president and approved by the President’s Staff.

E. An annual review will be conducted by the President and his staff.

IV. POLICY ON DEPARTMENTAL MEMBERSHIPS AND SUBSCRIPTIONS TO PROFESSIONAL JOURNALS AND MAGAZINES

A. Memberships

1. Departmental memberships are appropriately paid from departmental funds.

2. Memberships for a department should have the broadest departmental application.

3. The division chairman or administrative division director will be required to approve all departmental memberships.

B. Subscriptions

1. Frequently, in order for employees to keep up-to-date with developments in their areas of concern, departments should subscribe to journals and magazines.

2. Departments should avoid duplicating subscriptions already purchased by the library. Possible exceptions to this should meet the following criteria:

a. The publication should be of general use to many members of the department or students. Use of the publication by department members and students cannot be readily accommodated by a single copy in the library.

b. Having a copy of the publication in the department affects a considerable savings in time, as well as a great convenience for both department members and students in consulting the publication.

c. The publication primarily is a reference work that is used more than once.

C. Implementation

1. Memberships

Persons justifying total participation by the department for memberships should supply their appropriate division chairman with the following:

a. The objectives to be accomplished with the particular membership or subscription.

b. Reasons the objectives cannot be reasonably attained by alternative means.

c. The names of the department members who feel that the particular membership or publication will be an aid to them in carrying out their work assignments.

2. Subscriptions

a. Publications should be circulated so that they can be systematically read by those for whom they are purchased.

b. Publications should not become the private property of the department members after they have been distributed. Publications should still be accessible to all department members and students.

c. The expense of subscriptions should come out of the customary budget allocation to the department.

V. POLICY ON INDIVIDUAL MEMBERSHIPS AND/OR SUBSCRIPTION TO JOURNALS AND MAGAZINES

A. The department may participate in memberships and subscriptions for each individual, up to one-half of three memberships, journals, or magazines with the maximum participating not exceeding $300 per year.

B. When appropriate professional literature is more economical for an individual to order than when ordered by a department, permission may be granted by the division chairman to have the costs borne by the department. (See Section IV B)

Travel (3-4)

Also refer to Financial Services Travel Policies: www.byui.edu/FinServices/Policies_Procedures/Atravel.htm

I. PURPOSE

To summarize the university travel policy.

II. POLICY

A. Advanced authorization is required for all travel. Generally such authorization is obtained from the traveling employee’s supervisor. Such authorization is obtained by preparation of the estimate column of the BYU-Idaho Travel Authorization/Expense Form (TA) with signatures of the traveling employee as well as the employee’s supervisor. Instructions for completion of the form TA are found on the back of the form.

B. The university will pay reasonable costs of approved business travel. The employee is expected to make an effort to conserve the resources of the school in making travel arrangements. Air travel arrangements should be made using an approved travel agent. For information on current approved agents and other travel related questions, call the travel office at 2310.

C. Airfares will be charged by approved travel agents directly to BYU-Idaho. A TA number and account number will be requested by the travel agency. Registration fees for seminars etc. are generally paid by check. Other expenses are generally paid by a corporate charge card or in cash.

D. If travel is to be by automobile, a vehicle should be requested from vehicle dispatch at 2461. If none is available or if travel is for distances less than 75 miles, and employee may use their private automobile and will be reimbursed at the current motor pool rate. If a BYU-Idaho vehicle is available and the employee elects to use their own vehicle they will be reimbursed at one half of the motor pool rate. Private vehicles used for University travel should have a minimum insurance coverage of:

· $100,000 for personal injury to, or death of, one person.
· $300,000 for injury to, or death of, two or more persons in one accident
· $100,000 for property damage.

Proof of insurance may be requested by the accounting office before reimbursement is made.

E. Employees who take spouses or family members on official business trips must pay for all additional costs associated with the family members travel. Any exceptions to this policy must be approved by a vice president in writing.

F. All receipts must be attached to the form TA when submitted for reimbursement except for food which is paid on a per diem basis as indicated on back of page two of form TA.

G. Travel advances may be received from the cashiers office by submitting the form TA with approval signatures. Travel advances should be for more than $50.00. Travel advances in excess of $2,000 will be disbursed by check unless advance arrangements are made with the cashiers office. Preparation of a check will require one working day. The travel advance is considered a loan to the employee. When an advance exceeds the reported expenses on the TA, the difference must be deposited at the cashiers office with the final TA. The completed TA with receipts must be submitted within five days of completion of the trip. The university encourages employees to use a corporate charge card rather than using large travel advances. A charge card application may be made at the financial services office, Kimball 290. For details call 1144.

H. More requirements and information regarding travel arrangements and procedures are available on the intranet through the BYU-Idaho Home Page. Questions may also be directed to the travel services office at 2310 or through accounts payable at 1060.

I. Special procedures apply to group travel, tours and expeditions. Information for this type of travel is available on the intranet and by calling 1060.

Development Funds (3-5)

I. PURPOSE

To outline the university’s policy with respect to donating, approval for expending, and expending restricted and unrestricted development funds, including those used for travel purposes.

II. DEFINITIONS

A. Unrestricted Development funds are gifts from donors who have not specified how they are to be expended.

B. Restricted Development funds are gifts from donors who have specified how they are to be expended.

C. Gifts-in-kind are gifts from donors made in property or articles rather than money. Gifts-in-kind can be designated as unrestricted or restricted.

D. Endowment funds are gifts from donors, the principal of which is not expendable by the university.  Only investment income and gains can be expended.

III. POLICY

BYU-Idaho, as a part of the Unified Church Educational System Development Program, encourages contributions from both internal and external sources to be used for the enhancement of its educational purposes.

IV. IMPLEMENTATION

A. Unrestricted Gifts

Wherever possible the giving of unrestricted gifts to BYU-Idaho shall be encouraged.

1. Procedure for Giving. All unrestricted gifts shall be processed through the BYU-Idaho Development Office.

a. This office shall ensure that said gifts are properly accounted for, receipts are made, and that the donor receives appropriate acknowledgment.

b. This office will be the only office authorized to acknowledge tax deductible, charitable donations made to BYU-Idaho.

c. BYU-Idaho employees make charitable contributions through the university "Together for Greatness" program.

2. Procedure for Obtaining Approval to Expend

a. Requests for the use of unrestricted funds shall be channeled through the academic division dean and/or administrative directors to the appropriate vice president who will present said requests to the President’s Council.

b. The President’s Council is comprised of the president, who serves as chairman, and the four vice presidents. The committee meets during the first President’s Council meeting of each quarter and considers requests for the use of unrestricted funds at that time.

3. Procedure for Expending

a. Upon receiving notice of proper approval, the BYU-Idaho Financial Services shall transfer funds from the unrestricted fund account to a special expenditure account.

b. The department is then notified of the account number at the time authorization to expend from it is given.

c. Accounting statements will be mailed monthly to the account custodian.

B. Restricted Gifts

On those occasions where donors choose to restrict their gifts to especially approved programs or areas, it will be the responsibility of the BYU-Idaho Accounting Office to see that the stipulations of the restrictions are met.

1. Procedure for Giving

a. Whereas donors, generally, shall be encouraged to give unrestricted gifts, any desiring to restrict a gift shall not restrict said gift to any unit below an academic division or an administrative services area level except for specifically approved university-wide programs as outlined below:

(1) President’s Club
(2) Thomas A. BYU-Idaho Associates
(3) Viking Boosters
(4) Student Financial Aids
(5) Established university development projects

b. All restricted gifts shall be processed through the BYU-Idaho Development Office which is the only office authorized to acknowledge tax deductible, charitable donations made to the university. This office shall ensure that the gifts are properly accounted for, receipts are made, and that the donor receives proper acknowledgment.

(1) Upon receiving notification from the Development Office of the gift, the Accounting Office is authorized to notify the recipient (division dean or area director) of the gift and the amount. (Note: This notification does not authorize the recipient to expend the gifted amount. Approval to expend is outlined below.)

(2) BYU-Idaho employees desiring to make restricted contributions to the university may do so through the "Together for Greatness" program as described in B. 1. a. above.

2. Procedure for Obtaining Approval to Expend

a. Requests for the use of restricted funds, up to $500, shall be channeled through the appropriate academic division chairman or administrative director for approval and authorizing signature.

b. Requests to expend more than $500 must be approved by the appropriate vice president and the president. A memo from the division chairman or administrative director to the vice president will serve as a request. This approved memo should be attached to the requisition, CPO, etc.

3. Procedure for Expending

a. Expenditures from restricted accounts are generally made in the same manner as other expenditures including the use of check requests, departmental hand checks and purchase orders.

b. Disbursements for less than $500 will require only the signature of an employee authorized on the restricted account.  Any disbursement over the $500 limit must include the signature of the authorized employee and the vice president through whom he/she reports.

c. The steward over each restricted account is responsible to ensure that the funds are spent in accordance to the restrictions placed by the donor.  The supervisor of each steward is responsible to review these expenditures.

d. The Accounting Office is responsible for maintaining proper records of donations, expenditures, and restrictions.  Monthly statements are sent to the steward of each account and their supervisors except for scholarships, which are sent to the Scholarship Office.

C. Gifts-in-Kind

1. Procedure for Giving

a. All gifts-in-kind shall be processed through the BYU-Idaho Development Office. This office shall ensure that the gifts are properly accounted for, receipts are made, and that the donor receives proper acknowledgment.

b. All gifts-in-kind will be recorded and become a part of a file available for future reference on a computer listing. This file will be accumulated by academic year and will contain information pertaining to the donor and the gift.

c. The valuation placed on gifts-in-kind, for the internal records of the university, will be determined by the department receiving the gift in conjunction with the Development Office. The university assumes no responsibility for valuations claimed by donors.

d. Certain gifts-in-kind will be inventoried from year to year until converted to cash. The following types of gifts will receive this treatment:

· Works of Art
· Stocks, bonds, partnership interest, and other investment accounts
· Trusts
· Real property
· Other - where the interest of the university is to convert the account to cash, and the account has a value in excess of $1,000.

2. Procedures for converting gifts-in-kind to cash

a. Those gifts listed in IV. C. 1. d. above cannot be sold or converted to cash without the approval of the appropriate vice president.

b. Gifts other than those listed in IV. C. 1. d. may be converted to cash at the discretion of the appropriate division chairman or director if not otherwise restricted by the donor.

3. Procedure for Obtaining Approval to Expend

After the gift-in-kind has been converted to cash per paragraph IV. C. 2. above, see Unrestricted Gifts - Procedure for Obtaining Approval, IV. A. 2. or Restricted Gifts - Procedure for Obtaining Approval, IV. B. 2., depending upon the type of gift.

4. Procedure for Expending

See Unrestricted Gifts - Procedure for Expending IV. A. 3. Or Restricted Gifts - Procedure for Expending, IV. B. 3., depending upon the type of gift.

D. Endowment Funds

1. Procedure for Giving

a. The minimum amount for an initial endowment gift is $10,000. After the minimum has been established, additional amounts may be added in any increments.

b. See Restricted Gifts - Procedure for Giving, IV. B. 1.

2. Procedure for Obtaining Approval

See Restricted Gifts - Procedure for Obtaining Approval, IV. B. 2.

3. Procedure for Expending

See Restricted Gifts - Procedure for Expending, IV. B. 3.
