Doctrine & Covenants Section 105
Zion's Camp
The Prophet Joseph Smith told the brethren of Zoin's Camp: "Brethren, some of you are angry with me, because you did not fight in missouri; butlet me me tell you, God did not want you to fight. He could not organize His kingdom with twelve men to open the Gospel door to the nations of the earth, and with seventy men under their direction to follow in their tracks, unless He took them from a body of men who had made as great a sacrfice as did Abraham'" (History of the Church, 2:182).
Although some members felt that Zion's Camp was a failure, many benifited and grew from the experience. The Lord's purposes were accomplished. From this group of men, the Prophet selected those whom the Lord wanted as leaders in the Church. Of the 207 men in Zion's Camp, 27 men were eventually called as General Authorites.
Zion's Camp-Obedience-Joseph Smith
On another occasion the company camped on the Mississippi River. the Prophet recorded the following:
"'Some of the brethren went on to the sand bar and got a quantity of turtles' eggs, as they supposed. I told them they were snake's eggs, and they must not eat them; but some of them thought they knew more about it than I did, and still persisted they were turtles eggs. I said they were snakes' eggs-eat snakes eggs, will you? The man that eats them will be sorry for it; you will be sick. Notwithstanding all I said, several brethren ate them, and were sick all the day after it'" (History of the Church, 2:82-83).
Zion's Camp-Obedience-Joseph Smith
The Prophet Joseph Smith gave this account of the march of Zion's Camp: "In pitching my tent we found three massasaugas or prairie rattlesnakes, which the brethren were about to kill, but I said, 'Let them alone--don't hurt them! How will the serpent ever lose his venom, while the servants of God possess the same disposition, and continue to make war upon it? Men must become harmless, before the brute creation; and when vicious dispositions and caese to destroy the animal race, the loin and the lamb can dwell toether, and the sucking child can playwith the serpent in safty.' The brerhren took the serpents carefully on sticks and carried them across the sreek. I exhorted the brethren not to kill a serpent, bird, or an animal of any kind during our journeyunless it became necessary in order to preserve ourselves from hunger.
"I had frequently spoken on this subject, when on a certain occasion I cam up to the brethren who were watching a squirrel on a tree, and to prove them and to know if they would heed my counsel, I took one of their guns, shot the squirrel and passed on, leaving the squirrel on the ground. Brother Orsen Hyde, who was just behind, picked up the squirrel and siad, 'We will cook this, that nothing may be lost.' I percieved that the brethren understood what I did it for , and in their practice gave more heed to my precept than to my example, which was right" (History of the Church, 2:71-72).
Zion's Camp-Angels Attend-Joseph Smith
One of the most dramatic events of Zion's Camp occured while the company was stopped on a hill between the two main branches of Fishing River between Richmond and Liberty, Missouri. A mob of over three hundred men had gathered opposite the camp. Five men representing themob rode into the camp and swore terrible oaths against the brethren, threatening that they would kill them. The company had stopped there to repair their wagons and were hemmed in on two sides by the steep banks of Fishinng River. This location appeared to be a natural trap. Heber C. Kimball recorded the events as follows:
"'Soon after these men left us we discovered a small black cloud rising in the west, and not more than twenty minutes passed away before it began to rain and hail; but we had very little hail in our camp. All around us the hail was heavy; some of the hailstones, or rather lumps of ice, were as large as hens' eggs. The thunders rolled with awful majesty, and the red lightenings flashed through the horizon, making it so light that I could see to pick up a pin almost any time through the night. The earth quaked and trembled, and there being no cessation it seemed as though the Almighty had issued forthHis mandate of vengence. The wind was so terrible that many of our tents were blown down. We were not able to hold them up; but there being an old meeting house close at hand, many of us fled there to secure ourselves form the storm. Many trees were blown dow, and others were twisted and wrung like a withe [a slender, flexible twig]. The mob came to the river two miles from us, but the river had risen to that height that they were obliged to stop without crossing over. The hail fell so heavily upon them that it beat holes in their hats, and in some instances even broke the stocks off their guns; their horses, being frightened, fled, leaving the riders on the ground. Their powder was wet, and it was evident that the Almighty fought in our defense. This night the river raised forty feet'" (Orson F. Whitney, Life of Heber C. Kimball, 3rd ed. [Salt Lake City; Bookcraft, 1973], pp. 52-53; see also History of the Church, 2:102-5).
Unity-Joseph Smith
On several occasions, the Prophet had to rebuke some of the brethren for dismay and lack of brotherly love. Sylvester Smith causeed problems several times. The Prophet said that the following incident happened on 12 May 1834 just west of the Ohio-Indiana border:
"This evening there was a difficulty beteween some of the brethren and Sylvester Smith, on occasion of which I was called to decide in the matter. Finding a rebellious spirit in Sylvester Smith, and to some extent in others, I told them they would meet with misfortunes, diffficulties and hindrances, and said, 'and you will know it before you leave thisplace,' exhorting them to humble themselves before the Lord and become united, that they might not be scourged. A very singular occurance took place that night and the next day, concerning our teams. On Saturday morning, when we arose, we found almost every horse in the camp so badly foundered that we could scarcly lead them a few rods to water. The brethren then deeply realized the effects of discord. When I learned the fact, I exclaimed to the brethren, that for a witness that God overruled and had His eye upon them, all those who would humble themselves before the Lord should know that the hand of God was in this misfortune, and their horses should be restored to health immeadiatly; and by twele o'clock the same day the horses were as nimble as ever, with the exception of one of Sylvester Smith's, which soon afterwards died." (History of the Church, 2:68-69.)
Unity-Joseph Smith
This location was also the site where the revelation known as Doctrine and Covenants 105 was recieved.
Near Liberty, Missouri, members of Zion's Camp were stricken with cholera as Joseph Smith had earlier prophesied would happen. Concerning these difficulties, George A. Smith wrote:
"The Prophet Joseph took a full share of the fatigues of the entire journey. In addition to the care of providing for the Camp and presiding over it, he walked most of the time and had a full proportion of blistered, bloody, and sore feet, which was the natural result of walking from 25 to 40 miles a day in a hot season of the year. But during the entire trip he never uttered a murmur of complaint. While most of the men in the Camp complained to him of sore toes, blistered feet, long drives, scanty supply of provisions, poor quality of bread, bad corn dodger, frouzy butter, strong honey, maggoty bacon and cheese, etc., even a dog could not bark at some men without their murmuring to Joseph. If they had to camp with bad water it would nearly cause rebellion, yet we were the Camp of Zion, and many of us were prayerless, thoughtless, careless, heedless, foolish or devilish, and yet we did not know it. Joseph had to bear with us and tutor us, like children. There were many, however, in the Camp who never murmured and who were always ready and willing to do as our leaders desired." ("My Journal," Instructor, May 1946, p. 217).
Taught More Perfectly- Joseph Smith
Just east of Atlas, Illinois, Joseph Smith and some of the brethren discovered and ancient burial ground. Joseph wrote in his journal: "The brethren ... discovered the skeleton of a man, almost entire, and between his ribs the stone point of a Lamanitish arrow, which evidently produced his death ... the visions of the past being opened to my understanding by the Spirit of the Almighty, I discovered that the person whose skeleton was before us was a white Lamanite, a large, thick-set man, and a man of God. His name was Zelph. He was a warrior and cheiftain under the great prophet Onandagus, who was known form the Hill Cumorah, or eastern sea to the Rocky mountains. The curse was taken from Zelph, or, at least, in part- one of his thigh bones was broken by a stone flung from a sling, while in battle, years before his death. He was killed in battle by the arrow found among his ribs, during the last great struggle of the Lamanites and the Nephites" (History of the Church, 2:79-80).
Taught More Perfectly- Joseph Smith
After crossing the Grand River in Missouri, Martin Harris decided to "show off" the power of God and met with a problem. Joseph Smith told of the incident:
"Martin Harris having boasted the hecould handle snakes with perfect safety, while fooling with a black snake with his bare feet, he recieved a bite on his left foot. The fact was communicated to me, and I took occasion to reprove him, and exhort the brethren never to trifle with the promises of God. I told then it was presumption for any one to provoke a serpent, he might have faith, or his brethren might have faith for him, so that the Lord would hear his prayer and he might be healed; but when a man designedly provokes a serpent to bite him, the principle is the same as when a man drinks deadly poison knowing iut to be such. In that case no man has any claim on the promises of God to be healed" (History of the Churvh, 2:95-96).
