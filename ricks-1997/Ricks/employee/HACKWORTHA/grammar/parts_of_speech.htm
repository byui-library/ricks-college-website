Parts of Speech
English has eight parts of speech: noun, verb, preposition, pronoun, adjective, adverb, conjunctions, and interjections.
 
Noun  
A noun is a word that symbolizes a person, place, or thing.  A noun will name anything in this room to which you can point.  Also, a noun can be a word that represents an idea.  The words "greed, ambition, and socialism" are examples of such nouns.  Put the words: a, an, the (articles)  in front of a word and see if it makes sense.  If it does, that word will be a noun.  For example, the car, a mountain, an ideal, a love, a freedom, the government, an egg.
 
Pronoun
A pronoun is a word that takes the place of a noun.  Pronouns include words such as: you, they, them, those, these, his, her, their, she, me, I, who, whom.  In the following sentence, the pronoun "she" takes the place of the noun, Nancy.
 
My friend, Nancy, is a faithful runner.  She runs four or five miles per day.
 
The word that the pronoun refers to is the antecedent.  In this case, Nancy is the antecedent of she.
If there is any question about which word or idea is the antecedent, the pronoun is considered vague.
Pronouns have case.  A pronoun will be either subjective (nominative), objective, or possessive.
 
Adjective
An adjective is a word or group of words that modifies a noun. An adjective clarifies and strengthens a noun.  Adjectives answer the questions: how many, which, and what kind.  An adjective can be a single word, a phrase, or a clause.  Examples of adjective clauses will be given later.  First, consider the single word adjective and then, an adjective phrase.
 
Single Word Adjectives -- "The blue, fast, 1985 Corvette was stolen."  
 
In this example, the words "blue, fast, and 1985" are all adjectives.  They modify (clarify) the noun "Corvette."
 
Adjective Phrases -- "The man with the torn shirt threw the football."  In this example, the words, "with a torn shirt" make an adjective phrase.  These words modify and clarify the noun, "man."  However, within this adjective phrase, the word "torn" is also an adjective.  This word modifies the word, "shirt."
 
Adverb
An adverb is a word or a group of words that modifies a verb or another adjective.  Adverbs answer the questions:
 
how
when
why
where
 
Often, a single word adverb will end in "ly."  Following are some examples of adverbs:
 
Gifford carefully selected his target.
"Carefully" is an adverb.
 
Carla moved quickly to the other side.
"Quickly" and "to the other side" are adverbs.  "Quickly" tells -how- and "to the other side" tells -where-.  "Quickly" is a single word adverb and "to the other side" is an adverbial phrase.
 
Article.  (Not a part of speech)  Articles include only the words:  a, an, the.  These words have also been called determiners because they signal the reader or listener that a noun will follow shortly.  Often, the pattern will be -- article, adjective, noun.  Here are some examples: the large elk, the broken wheel, a small bird, an early date.
