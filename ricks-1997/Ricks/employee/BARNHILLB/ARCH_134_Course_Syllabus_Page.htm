I. COURSE DESCRIPTION

Introduction to architectural computer-aided drafting. The class will use microcomputers along with AutoCAD 2000 software to give introductory experiences with architectural drawing creations: floor plan fundamentals, dimensioning, text, hatching, drawing storage and retrieval, drawing output: plotting and printing, and drawing modifications (trimming, stretching, moving, erasing, etc.). The architectural templates and symbols will also be introduced. The construction of basic details using various symbols and line types will also be covered. Basic Windows (NT) will be emphasized with no computer language application considered.


II. INTRODUCTION

When a student seeks to gain knowledge and develop new skills in a selected subject, it is best to have a clear understanding of the purpose and objectives set forth to learn this information, particularly the way in which the course is organized, the work he/she is expected to do, and how the work is to be done. This syllabus is to give students this information at the very outset of the course. Refer to the syllabus and schedule regularly and follow them closely!! Highlight important areas as you would while studying your text.

Due to the increased application of the computers in making technical drawing in industry, it is recommended that you gain as much exposure to computer aided drafting as possible. Computer-aided drafting, CAD as it is called, is used today by architects, interior designers, contractors, electronics, engineers, and drafters. Virtually every conceivable technical field is using CAD. It must be emphasized that skill on a computer does not make a successful draftsperson! A strong architectural background and understanding of construction elements are essential.

Because many beginning drafters have little or no knowledge of computers or architecture, this course will provide and aim towards developing a working knowledge of computer systems as a whole and give an architectural slant and application. The student will learn and use the components that make a total computer system. The student will also explore AutoCAD software and develop practical skills in using this software, thus using the computer as a tool to create architectural and construction documents.

ARCH 134 is an introductory course. It is recommended that students be skilled or familiar with conventional drafting fundamentals and/or be concurrently enrolled in an architectural drafting course to learn the drafting fundamentals to assist in course assignment completion. It is also recommended that students have an understanding of basic Windows commands and operations.

III. PURPOSE OF THE COURSE

To develop introductory skills and knowledge using the computer as a tool to complete architectural drawings and solve graphic problems. Learning curve (to learn and achieve the purpose of the course) is enhanced when following these steps:

1. Read text before class lecture.

2. Take notes during class lecture -- NO COMPUTER WORK IS TO BE DONE WHILE LECTURE IS IN PROCESS UNLESS DIRECTED BY INSTRUCTOR.

3. Spend time at the computer going over text material to familiarize yourself with the computer keyboard and procedures.

4. Read through text assignments before actually doing the CAD assignments.

V. COURSE OBJECTIVES

When the student completes the requirements of the course, the student will be able to:

1. Identify the components making a complete computer graphics system and know the essential set-up.

2. Demonstrate configuration procedures, making the software work with the hardware.

3. Demonstrate using essential periphery routines using Windows.

4. Apply drafting fundamentals in creating architectural drawings on the computer graphics system using AutoCAD software.

5. Develop and demonstrate the use of tool bars, shortcut menus, the keyboard, and icons.

6. Develop an understanding of computer "Paths" for filing and retrieving drawings.

7. Apply conventional practices in creating, storing, and retrieving drawings.

8. Demonstrate using existing templates/symbols files in drawing creation, also modify or create new symbols for drawing use.

9. Obtain hard copy output of CAD drawings using the plotter and printer.

10. Develop user familiarity with the AutoCAD software commands, options, and variables.

11. Establish the start or continuation of a professional portfolio using the best CAD drawings of the course as the basis.

VII. EVALUATION AND GRADING

What you learn in class supersedes by about ten times the simple grade you receive. A student learns by doing and this CAD class requires a lot of doing to become proficient. During lectures, students are expected to listen and take notes. NO CAD WORK DURING LECTURES IS ACCEPTABLE UNLESS INSTRUCTED OTHERWISE. In evaluation of each student's performance and mastery of assignments and course work, the following criteria will be used for student evaluation.

1. ATTENDANCE, CLASS PARTICIPATION, ATTITUDE AND PUNCTUALITY: The college attendance policy is in force. Regular attendance and punctuality will influence the final grade and the level of achievement. Studies have indicated that grading and attendance have a direct relationship:

a. Missing lectures significantly lowers comprehension of course material, thus performance is decreased.

b. Class participation and involvement is essential to the goals of the course.

c. In past years, students with more than a few absences had the lowest grades.

  • To teach the importance of attendance, a point system is used. At the end of the semester, your attendance points will be tallied and added to your accumulated points for a final grade. The point system for attendance is as follows:

a. Each student is given 100 points at the beginning of the semester.

b. Five points will be deducted for each absence.

c. Two points will be deducted for each tardy.

  • Two points will be deducted for leaving class early
  • One point will be deducted for sitting idle, waiting for class to be over, and wasting valuable class time.

3. QUIZZES, UNIT TESTS, AND FINAL EXAM: During the course, each student will be evaluated for learning comprehension of reading assignments, CAD exercises, lectured material, and acquired CAD skills. There will be no make-up of quizzes. Unit tests and final exam will be given as scheduled and quizzes will be given at random during the course, see class schedule.

4. CAD LAB EXERCISES: Computer drawing assignments are given throughout the course. These laboratory problems will be progressive in CRT terminal skills and will be based on accumulative computer knowledge, so it is strongly encouraged to keep current with the class schedule of assignments. The computer drawings are to be turned in as original drawings to be evaluated, or you will receive a screen evaluation as directed in class. For each CAD drawing assigned, A BRIEF SUMMARY OF YOUR CAD EXPERIENCE WITH VARIOUS COMMANDS, INTERRUPT KEYS, ICONS, ETC., IS TO BE TURNED IN WITH THE DRAWING. (See example from instructor.)

5. PORTFOLIO OF CAD PRESENTATION DRAWING: CAD drawings representing student work AT THEIR BEST, will be compiled into a presentation portfolio booklet. All drawings for portfolio display must comply with the requirements set forth in the portfolio project of the text.

6. GRADING SYSTEM: To satisfactorily complete the course and reach an acceptable level of CAD mastery, a student must obtain a minimum of 70% of all course work. Without this level of mastery, the student is at a disadvantage and should retake the class.

Evaluation of all work required in the course is by a point system. No drawings will be accepted after the given due date. So follow the schedule and keep up with current assignments.

All points for attendance, CAD assignments, quizzes and tests will be totaled and equated, based on the total points possible in the course which will be set by a class curve. Grades are then given based upon the student's score verses the points possible on a curve and converted to a percentage. The grade percentages are as follows:

IX. STUDENT CONDUCT

1. Horseplay or general rowdiness is not acceptable behavior. Students are responsible for proper care of computer equipment and are expected to act responsible while in the computer lab, reporting any misconduct or computer failure to the instructor.

2. The following activities are not considered to be consistent with laboratory objectives: 1) DRINKING OR EATING in computer area, 2) keeping lab students waiting when scheduled to use computer, and 3) unnecessary or disturbing conversation.

3. Honesty. Students must do their own work. Students may not copy each others disk of drawings or turn in another student's drawing for a grade. Should this happen, both students will lose the points for the drawings.

4. Each student is responsible for housekeeping of the computer labs. Cleanliness is a key to computers working properly. Always work at a clean computer station with an uncluttered reference area. A mature student never leaves his/her computer area cluttered with books, papers, etc., for someone else to clean up. CLEAN UP BEHIND YOURSELF! Always leave the computer area neat and clean, even if the mess is not yours.
