BIBLIOGRAPHIES

 
Use a bibliography to find: 
  • bibliographies
  • book publishers
  • books
  • periodicals in a subject

Bibliography comes from the Greek words "biblion" for "book" and "graphein" for "to write".
The scribes who copied books were the first bibliographers.
In the 1700's the definition for "bibliographer" changed from one who copied books to one who wrote about books.

Books and periodicals often contain a list of sources used or recommended readings called a "bibliography".
The library catalog can be considered an index to the collection or a bibliography of the collection.
Some bibliographies are published as numerous volumes in a set.
Briefly a bibliography can be any list of sources.

A bibliography can be a complete list of all works of a particular kind.
A subject bibliography gives only works on one subject.
The United States government publishes subject bibliographies of its publications.
Andriot wrote a guide that also assists in locating government publications.

The Library of Congress at one time had to have access to every book that was currently copyrighted in the United States.
The National Union Catalog is a bibliography of not only the books at the Library of Congress but also other large libraries. Union catalogs give the combined holdings of several libraries.
OCLC and RLIN are examples of electronic union catalogs.

A trade bibliography is a list used to sell books.

Publishers often have a line of books for school use, textbooks, which may not be listed in the trade bibliography.

A bibliography may list the works of an author.
Contemporary Authors (Ref Z1224.C..) is a bio-bibliography which gives a biography and bibliography.
Use the most recent cumulative index.
The first pages of the index will explain abbreviations.
The oldest volumes are plain brown; newer volumes will have an orange or green stripe.
The index also gives reference to other titles by the same publisher.
The publisher also has critical series for the nineteenth century and earlier.

A selective bibliography is a list based on quality.
Eugene Sheehy edited Guide to Reference Books for the American Library Association.
This ALA publication selectively lists reference books based on quality.
A newer edition is has a Cutter number for the title rather than the editor.

An evaluative bibliography contains criticism.
Booklist and School Library Journal are magazines published by the American Library Association
The ALA periodicals evaluate and recommend books for purchase.
Guide to Reference Books is based on reference books that have been reviewed in evaluative bibliographies like Booklist.

A descriptive bibliography contains notes or annotation.
Guide to Reference Books also describes many of the reference materials covered in LS 111.
A book can be a selective, evaluative, and descriptive bibliography.

Bibliographies are useful in research.
Master's and doctoral degree research usually require a review of all the research done in the field.
Bibliographies can serve as recommended reading lists for those unfamiliar with the field.
English majors should be familiar with a publications by the Modern Language Association.
An international bibliography is produced on publications on modern languages, literatures, folklore and linguistics.
MLA International Bibliography (Ref Z7006.M64)

Union catalogs and trade bibliographies may help a researcher locate information.
Periodical and newspaper directories can be used to obtain copies of these types of publications.
