JUNE 5 -- A DAY OF HISTORY

June 5, 1976, is a day imprinted on the memory of Ricks College. It has been described as a "day like no other in Idaho's history." Twenty-five years later, it becomes a day to remember not only heartbreak but also "hope, courage, and love."

A pleasant sunny summer Saturday became a flood of destruction and distress at 11:57a.m. as the Teton Dam collapsed releasing nearly 80 billion gallons of water downstream in a 30-foot wall of churning water. The17-mile long reservoir spread into a 75-mile long pathway of destruction. Homes, businesses, machinery, and rich agricultural top soil were swept away or submerged in muddy water and muck. More than 2,100 families were driven from their homes that were washed away, destroyed, or left uninhabitable. Affected families and individuals were overwhelmed by the restoration and cleanup work from the flood.

The Ricks College campus became a "haven on the hill" with flood waters ebbing on the athletic field north of the Hart Building. College administrators and The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints offered resources to the refugees and responding aid. President Spencer W. Kimball came to campus to offer support and counsel. He encouraged the victims to meet the challenges of rebuilding that still lay ahead.

Campus facilities were used as command posts for not only LDS Welfare but also government and private agencies (Idaho State Employment, Red Cross, USDA, Department of Health and Welfare). The Physical Plant building was used as a distribution point for tons of clothing, bedding, and food sent from Church headquarters. Concerns dealt with on campus and by the community at that time included clean-up and technical assistance for homes, commercial and public buildings; passes to community and outlying homes (the general public was road-blocked out of the flooded area); garbage including trash and dead animals; water samples and testing; volunteers from campus and community; nursery; heavy equipment, road construction, missing persons, medical, disaster communications, and press releases. Even banks set up temporary offices in campus classrooms.

Volunteer services were extended by the college. Some 386,250 meals were served to volunteers and those in need of aid. In addition approximately 1,800 people were housed on campus using 190 apartments for two months in the Women's Residence halls and about 400 guests were housed in the Men's Halls for approximately 50 nights. Other services donated were laundry service for bedding; washer/dryer services for clothes; new pillows, sheets, towels; and support of head residents and resident assistants. While the offering of academic courses continued, other college summer programs were canceled.

For those impacted so greatly, Ricks College became the "special friend" who offered hope and assistance. In her own account of the "Teton Saga" written just one month after the flood, Ruth H. Barrus expressed gratitude to the many volunteers and agencies that brought relief. Of Ricks College she said, "How marvelous has been the role of Ricks College with President Eyring at the helm in its service to this valley. I realize it has been an extension of the arm of the Church, and yet it has played a unique role. . . . This is my 43rd year of teaching at Ricks College, and every year has been memorable and rewarding, but 1976 will stand out, I think as the greatest year of its history. Quietly and inconspicuously, the hungry were fed, given shelter, provided recreation and counsel, and facility was provided that the great system of rehabilitation could carry on. I am deeply grateful for Ricks College! . . . Most of all, I am grateful for the Gospel of Jesus Christ and its representatives here on earth. What comfort it has given in time of travail, peace in time of emotional and mental conflict, hope in a time of despair, and light in a time of darkness."

EDUCATION WEEK INCLUDES A QUESTION-AND-ANSWER FORUM

Structured classes will naturally fill the schedule of Education Week on the Ricks College campus June 6-9. However, one of the most interesting features may well be the opportunity for those coming to participate in an open question-and-answer forum with President David A. Bednar. It is anticipated that the main focus will be on the process of Ricks College becoming BYU-Idaho at the session on Thursday, June 7, at 4:30 p.m. in the Manwaring Center West Ballroom. In addition to those registered for Education Week, alumni and any others interested in attending are invited.

The format of open communication through question-and-answer sessions has become a campus tradition initiated by President Bednar. Under his direction, the President's Council holds monthly discussions for anyone interested in attending. Answers are given to previously submitted questions with time also give for inquiries from the audience. Times for these sessions rotate to enable people with various work schedules to attend. In fact the next regularly scheduled Q&A will be on Tuesday, June 19, at 7 a.m. in the Manwaring Center Little Theater.

FINAL SEASON TO BE A TRIBUTE TO INTERCOLLEGIATE ATHLETICS

The final season of intercollegiate athletics at Ricks College starting this fall promises to be one of spirited action as well as one of reflection and tribute to former athletes and coaches, says Garth Hall, athletic director and recently appointed vice president of advancement. By the start of the final athletic season in early September, Ricks College will be known as BYU-Idaho. It will continue, however, to compete athletically against two-year junior colleges for one final season.

A gala evening of paying tribute to athletics at Ricks College is planned for Friday, March 1, in the Hart Auditorium. Each sport will be honored and current and former athletes, coaches, administrators and staff will be recognized for their contributions. There will be awards and entertainment, and the general public will be invited to participate. Additionally, as each sport concludes competition, former athletes and coaches will be invited to return to campus and will be recognized at the last home game or meet. The final women's volleyball game on Oct. 20 against Snow College will kick off the celebrations. The final home football game will be a week later on Oct. 27 against Arizona Western College. That same day the regional finals in cross country will be held in Rexburg and former cross country runners will be invited to return to campus. Track and field athletes will also be invited to participate. The regional wrestling tourney on Feb. 1 will bring former wrestlers to campus, and both men's and women's basketball will be celebrated with the final home games March 2. The final women's fast-pitch softball game will be held April 20, and the final baseball game will take place May 4.

RICKS PIANIST STEPHEN ALLEN PERFORMS IN JAPAN

Ricks College music faculty member Stephen Allen recently returned from Japan where he gave a concert, a master class and judged a junior music festival. He was invited by the Music International-Japan and Yokohama/Yokosuka Music Association.

Allen, a pianist, played with two Japanese musicians, a violinist and a cellist, whom he had never met until the day of the performance. "It was interesting how music spanned linguistic barriers and how three people could get together with no preparation and make music," Allen said.

The concert was originally a community concert, but arrangements were made to perform as part of an open house for the newly-acquired property for the Yamate-cho Ward building. The location is of historical significance to members of the Church in Japan because it is the approximate location where Heber J. Grant and three other companions stayed as the first missionaries in Japan in 1901. "I think there was a unique purpose in that performance," Allen said. "I think we were given heavenly help. It really was an inspiration to play."

NEW GALLERY OPENS IN MCKAY LIBRARY

A new display gallery is open on the first floor of the west wing of the McKay Library. Appropriately, the first display is entitled "The Art of Books" which is scheduled to remain until June 22. Eleven showcases exhibit items depicting both the practical and beautiful side of books and the process of book making. If the display sparks a creative thought or two for a summer project, there are even step-by-step instructions for making simple books and journals. Display items were contributed from private collections of Scott Samuelson, Donnell Hunter, Jim Papworth, George Barnhill, and Sam Nielsen. Plans are to exhibit a wide variety of items and memorabilia in the McKay Gallery reflecting history, progress, and insights of knowledge.
