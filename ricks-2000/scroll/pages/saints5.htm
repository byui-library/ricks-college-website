CHURCH SENDS HUMANITARIAN AID TO INDIA AFTER EARTHQUAKE

by Jennifer Yates
scroll staff

Over 600,000 pounds of food, blankets and other supplies have been sent by the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints to aid in the magnitude-7.9 earthquake that devastated India's western Gujarat state on Jan. 26 according to Church News.

The supplies had already been en route when the earthquake occurred. The relief supplies were part of a long-term aid project in India, but were redirected to the devastated area.

"We tailor what is sent based on the assessment of those in the country," Rich McKenna, manager of planning and administration for LDS Humanitarian Service said.

Government officials have currently counted at least 12, 000 dead and 29,000 injured. Many others are still being pulled from the rubble. The Church currently has no missionaries serving in the affected area, nor any organized units, but a few members were living in the same quake zone.

The Church has donated $25,000 to the Prime Minister of India's Relief Fund to assist in the search and rescue operations, according to McKenna. The money will help provide temporary shelter for those who lost their homes.

In addition, local relief agencies were sent $40,000 to aid in supplies for refugee camps, McKenna said.

The donations will be distributed by charitable organizations such as Diwaliben Mohanlal Mehta Charitable Trust, and the Share and Care Foundation.

The aid will continue for a long time, according to Church News. For the next several months, the Church will provide aid in a long term feeding program. It will also participate in helping provide for temporary shelters.

Relief has also been sent to El Salvador, who also experienced an earthquake Jan. 13 that killed hundreds and left many homeless.
