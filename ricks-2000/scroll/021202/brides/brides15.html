There’s more to diamonds than what meets the eye

by Aubrey Oman
 OMA00001@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Something that takes millions of years to create must be special.

Diamonds, though often referred to as a girl’s best friend, are more. Much more. They are a symbol of fidelity, of eternity, of love.

It has been said that Cupid’s arrows are tipped with diamonds.

Because every girl is different, it is important when shopping for the diamond that the purchase is personal.

There is much more to diamonds than many people think.

The first thing to do before beginning the diamond search is to research what diamonds are all about.

Become acquainted with the 4 Cs: cut, color, clarity and carat.

Cut is so important to the value of a diamond that it can affect the value from 25 percent to over 50 percent, according to www.ags.org.

A high-quality cut unlocks the luster and fire of a diamond and lets it shine. Light reflects off the facets in the diamond and is dispersed through the top of the stone. If the diamond is too shallow, light will escape through the pavilion. If the diamond is too deep, light will reflect out the opposite side of the pavilion. Either way, it reduces the brilliancy.

A diamond’s sparkle can often be very important to a girl. A diamond’s brilliance refers to the white light reflected; dispersion refers to colored light. Scintillation is a mix between the two.

Diamonds come in all colors of the rainbow. Diamonds that display enough of a color to be desirable are called fancy-colored diamonds.

The color of the diamond depends on the traces of elements within the diamond. Nitrogen will cause the diamond to appear more yellow, while boron colors the diamond blue. Fluorescence will cause the diamond to reflect blue colors when exposed to ultraviolet wavelengths and can cause the diamond to appear oily.

Rare diamonds come in colors of red, blue, yellow, pink and purple. Most diamonds are graded on a color scale ranging from colorless to fancy yellow.

Clarity, the diamond’s birthmark of nature, is really crystals that were trapped inside the diamond during its creation. Clarity can affect the diamond from 5 to 20 percent.

Fewer than one percent of women will ever own a two carat or larger diamond, according to www.adiamondisforever.com.

Large diamonds are more rare and consequently more expensive. The cost of diamonds is calculated geometrically instead of arithmetically. For instance, a one carat diamond will cost approximately four times as much as a half carat diamond, according to www.asg.org.

It takes a lot of work from the earth to make a diamond. Usually, 250 tons of rock,

sand and gravel must be processed to yield one carat of diamond, according to www.diamonds.be.

Once the diamond is purchased, for protection purposes, it is wise to get a diamond grading report and go through it with a gemologist so the buyer fully understands it.

The report will include the authenticity of the selected diamond, from the depth perception to the polish. Some insurance companies may require a report in order to insure a diamond.
