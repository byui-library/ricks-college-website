Wisdom at a Price

You lie there
brave beyond the fear
of going it alone,
stretched out
on our living room couch
when hours earlier
you were
stretched out
in a dentist's chair
paying the price of wisdom
inherited, but not earned.

Your wisdom was extracted
bit by bit,
some more beligerant than others,
hanging tight and unrelenting,
deep rooted, bone-secure:
a sure anchoring it seemed.

But even these were extracted
as the physician used his silver skill
to cut resistence from nerve
letting blood run free
to cover the hollow of extraction,
gauze-soaked, reddened
against the bared nerve,
protecting against a drying socket
the swell of resolve resenting
the injustice of tried wisdom not being
adequate enough--over crowded.

Your head rests now
on a towelled pillow
protected against stubborn bloodstain
that may hint at
a trauma you are aware of,
but not interested in thinking about
let alone, sharing.

Removing bloodpacks from mump-like cheeks
I wonder at the price of wisdom:
wisdom of one life extracted,
but only at the price of blood
--always blood--
if not our own, then someone's
crimson assurance of trauma
not quite understood, but real nonetheless,
exacting the cost of change possible
only by wounds inflicted
before the rythmns of living
and the miracle of regeneration
cover with scar
to remind us of blood shed
for wholeness now enjoyed.

David L. Ward
December 21, 1995
