Computer Science Major
The purpose of the Computer Science major is to provide a solid background in computer science that facilitates transfer to a four-year university or college. To accomplish this objective, the student is given experience in the following areas:
  • Programming. Students are taught procedural programming using C and Perl and object oriented programming using C++ and Java. Client-server and CGI (Common Gateway Interface) programming are taught using the World Wide Web. Students also learn assembly language.
  • Algorithm development. Students are taught how to develop algorithms for stand-alone and Web-based software. Topics include iteration, induction, recursion, and algorithmic analysis. Software engineering practices and structured programming techniques are introduced.
  • Data structures. Students are introduced to linked lists, queues, binary trees, and other structures. They gain experience in implementing abstract data types using an object-oriented approach.
  • Computer architecture. Students are taught the fundamentals of computer architecture and organization. They are introduced to number systems, memory structures, registers, instruction formats, addressing modes, and CPU structure and function.
  • Theoretical foundations. Students learn the basic theory of computer science, including topics such as relations, automata, grammars, and propositional and predicate logic. This, coupled with science and mathematics courses, prepares students to transfer to many accredited universities and colleges.
The program for the Computer Science major accommodates students having a diverse background. Some students have not had any previous computer science education or programming experience. Others have had significant training in high school. Almost all computer science students continue their education at four-year institutions.
