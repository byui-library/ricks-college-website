Ricks College News Release, October 13, 1998

Bells chime once again on Ricks campus

By Doug Anderson
Ricks College News Bureau

REXBURG, Idaho--As another college hour begins, bells are heard in the not-too-far-off distance. Their tolling breaks a silence that has lasted for more than four years.

A new digitalized bell system was installed on the college campus in the Manwaring Center in late September. The electronic tolling is designed to sound like Westminster Chimes, says Randy Parkinson, assistant director of the Manwaring Center.

The bells sound every hour on the hour from 9 a.m. to 9 p.m. The system is even programmed to play song selections from Handel's "Messiah" 10 minutes before Tuesday's campus devotional, for example, or songs from Rogers and Hammerstein about 5 p.m. during weekdays. During the holiday season, Parkinson plans to install both traditional and religious Christmas songs for the apparatus to play.

Four new speakers were replaced on the Manwaring Center roof, which can carry the sound of chimes as far away as downtown Rexburg at times, Parkinson says. A local resident even called campus to say how the bells had been missed on the day they were installed and tested for the first time, he said.

Parkinson says the sound of the bell was so realistic that a person can even note the sound of the clapper (the instrument to toll the bell) as it rings and the actual decaying sound of a bell when the sound fades. The sounds of the bells and song selections are electronically recording onto auto-cards, a computer-disc like device, and are loaded onto a four-foot mechanism, which acts as a computer to store and play requested items. When the computer is programmed to play a selection from a certain auto-card, it randomly chooses an item to play from that specified menu.

"The computer picks what it wants to play," Parkinson says.
In other words, while faculty and students are scurrying to leave campus around 5 p.m. or so, they could very well encounter a rendition of "Do Re Mi" from "The Sound of Music."

But whether they are happy or annoyed to hear the cheery chimes may very well depend on their disposition, Parkinson says. "Sometimes if you've gone through an extremely stressful situation, there might not be anything in this world that helps you out," he says. "Generally, people will enjoy hearing them."

The new electronic system was installed by Shulmerich Carillons Inc, a company from Pennsylvania that also installed the electronic chimes that lasted from 1983 to 1993.
