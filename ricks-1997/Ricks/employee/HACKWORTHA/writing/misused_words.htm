Misused Words
 
Consider the proper use of words.
 
Too, To, Two
The word "too" is an adverb.  Use it when you mean "also" or "how much."  Example.  I want to go too.  The price is too high.  The word "to" is a preposition or part of an infinitive phrase.  Example.  He went to the store.  To win the race is my goal.  The word "two" is a number.   Example.  I want two cookies.
 
There, Their, They're
The word "there" is an expletive or an adverb.  It is often only a filler in a sentence and will never be a subject.  Example.  There are several reasons why I want to go.  The subject of the first clause is "reasons" not "there."  Here is an example of "there" as an adverb.  Please stand there for one minute.  The word "their" is a possessive pronoun.  Example.  Their house took ten years to finish  The word "they're" is a contraction for "they are."  Example.  They're coming home this weekend.
 
Personal, Personnel
The word "personal" relates to a person or to the body.  Example.  Solving the disagreement will require your personal attention.  The word "personnel" relates to the workers in the office, business, or factory.  Example.  The manager was seeking personnel for the new store.
 
Principal, Principle
The word "principal" means the head man.  It also means a sum placed at interest, the main
actor, a person from whom an agent's authority derives, and a matter of primary importance.
Example.  He collects his interest but does not wish to disturb the principal.  Our principal source of income is rentals.  The principal of our school called the meeting.   When we sell our home, we want to deal with the principals only.
The word "principle" relates to a code of conduct, a law, or a doctrine.  Example.  The plan goes against my principles.
 
Its, It's
The word "its" is a possessive pronoun.   Example.  I gave the dog its last bath.  The word "it's" is a contraction for "it is."   Example.  It's too late to go for dinner.
 
Bad, Badly
The word "bad" is an adjective.   Example.  Sue had a bad day at school.  Sue feels bad.  (The verb feel is a linking verb.  Consequently, bad is a predicate adjective.)  The word "badly" is an adverb.   Example.  Sue was beaten badly in her tennis match.
 
Lose, Loose
The word "lose" is a verb.   Example.  If you lose, try again.  The word "loose" is an adjective. Example.  The loose screw was in Bertha Sue's head.
 
Passed, Past
The word "passed" is a verb.  Example.  Sue passed all her classes.  The word "past" is an adjective.  Example.  The past year has been rewarding.  Also, you should make a distinction between the following words as adjectives and adverbs.  In each example, the word listed first is the adjective.
 
Slow, Slowly.  The car is slow.  The car moves slowly.
 
Good, Well.  It was a good show.  Frank plays basketball well.
 
Sure, Surely.  His win was sure.  He surely played well.
 
Real, Really.  A real ghost came into our yard.  It was really large.  (Don't use really and very in this class.  They are both examples of intensifiers that don't intensify.  They are dead words.)
 
Near, Nearly.  The treasure is near.  Sue nearly lost her chance to date Larry.
