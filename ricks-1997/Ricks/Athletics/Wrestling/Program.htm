2001-2002 Season Review

    When head wrestling Coach Bob Christensen enters the workout room, he needs name tags on his squad to remember who they all are. New faces are everywhere. Athletes returning with college experience are few and far between. But that doesn’t mean this team will flounder. Christensen says, "I feel good about this group. It’s well rounded. There is balance through the weights, and I expect to be surprised by a few of them, too."

    At 125 pounds, Christensen will look to two true freshman, Tyler Price and Jake Summers. Price comes in with over 100 career wins and Utah Wrestler of the Year honors. Summers is an Idaho state champion. Both will need to mature quickly to help the Vikings.

    At 133, Noah Cinquini of Oregon has looked good early. He is an Oregon state champion and runner up. Two 141 pounders look to battle for a starting spot. Doug Ireland, a state champion out of South Dakota, red-shirted as a freshman and is now home from his LDS mission. He is the brother of former All American Jeff Ireland, and is "a tough kid." Ireland is getting plenty of competition from Kristian Beckett who red-shirted last year. Beckett was an All League and All Section performer in California.

    Coach Christensen has found plenty of talent at the Wright family home in Payson, UT. This year, B.J. Wright is back from his mission to compete at 149 pounds. B.J. was an All American his freshman year, finishing third at nationals. Younger brother Jake Wright is the latest family member to become a Viking. He’s a true freshman, but has the talent and tenacity to take over at 157 pounds. Jake was a three time Utah state champion at Payson High, and an All American.

    A number of solid athletes have gathered at 165 pounds. Pete Mecham, a two time state champion out of Shelley, ID, will battle with David Anderson, a returned missionary who is transferring in from Dickinson State. Anderson was an All State wrestler at Colstrip High in Montana. Cody Fehlberg of Utah is also in the mix. "And there’s probably going to be others," says Christensen. "This is a tough weight with lots of talent. Some of these guys might have to move a weight." At 174 pounds there is Kimball Cluff and Ryan Shurtz, Cluff was a two time runner up in Utah. Shurtz is another prep star from Oregon with solid credentials. Returning from a mission is Steve Babcock who will come in at 184 pounds. Babcock was an All American as a freshman in 1998-99. 

    At 197 pounds is Renn Christensen of Ashton, ID. Christensen came off his mission a year ago. He’ll compete with two time state champion Braden Hansen of Layton, UT. Heavyweight has always been the ‘spotlight’ weight at Ricks with numerous national champions in Viking blue. "Time will tell," says Christensen about this years group. They include Robert Perkes of Arlington, WA, and Erik Freeman who is currently a starting offensive lineman on the football team.

    Team wise, "I think we’ll be balanced. Kids that are unproven will have to step up if we are to be as good as previous years, but I like what I see." Competition wise, it’s still North Idaho in the ‘team to beat’ slot. Competition is solid throughout the region, but Ricks and North Idaho remain the most balanced and respected nationally.
