EDUCATION WEEK SET FOR JUNE 6-9

More than 60 speakers and 270 classes will highlight the fourth annual Education Week at Ricks College set for June 6-9. The three-day event is expected to draw nearly 3,000 people to the Rexburg campus to participate in classes focusing on such topics as the gospel, family, self improvement, homemaking and fine arts. Although most classes are aimed at adults, specific classes are held for youths between the ages of 14 and 18.

Vickey Pahnke will be the keynote speaker at a pre-conference session Wednesday, June 6, at 6 p.m. in the Taylor Chapel. Her talk will be centered on the theme of Education Week: "What Is Man that Art Mindful of Him."

A concert featuring Michael McLean will be held on Thursday, June 7, in the Hart Auditorium at 7:30 p.m. and is open to the community.

Two special evening events will be held for those who are registered for Education Week. The musical group Heartbound will perform on Thursday at 7 p.m. in the Snow Recital Hall, and Marvin Goldstein, Janice Kapp Perry and Johanne Freschette Perry will unite in a musical journey on Friday at 7 p.m. in the Barrus Concert Hall.

Morning devotionals are free of charge and open to the public at 9:45 a.m. in the Hart Auditorium. Speakers will be Ricks President David Bednar and his wife, Susan Bednar, June 7; Elder Dallin H. Oaks, a member of the Quorum of the Twelve of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, June 8; and Elder F. Enzio Busche, an emeritus member of the Church's Quorum of the Seventy, June 9.

Applications are being taken through the Division of Continuing Education and are due by May 29 for mail-in, fax and online applications. In-person registration deadline is June 1 and applications need to be turned into Continuing Education at the Auxiliary Services Building on campus or at the BYU/Ricks Center in Idaho Falls. At-the-door registration will be available. Cost is $38 for the full three days or $16 for one-day or evening passes.

Any questions can be directed to Continuing Education at (208) 356-1040. Information can be accessed on the Internet at http://www.byui.edu/educationweek.

THEATER DEPARTMENT STAGES FOLKLORE REVIVAL

The Kirkham Arena Theater is filled with sounds of laughter and cheers as audiences of school children delight in the Folklore Revival. "They seem to have a blast," says director Omar Hansen.

The Folklore Revival is under the guidance of the Performing Arts Council as a prelude to a possible future performance tour for the Theater Department. The committee recommended that the department first give a presentation on campus to help gauge public response. Invitations were mailed to schools through the college's public relations office. The results were phenomenal, and the 20 shows between April 30-May 11 were quickly booked with more schools disappointedly turned away. Over 3,500 students were scheduled to visit from Blackfoot, Driggs, Howe, Idaho Falls, Rexburg, Rigby, Ririe, St. Anthony, Sugar City and Terreton. "It is a great community/college liaison," says Hansen.

Hansen finds great value in the program for the Ricks College students enrolled in theater education courses. "It has honestly been one of the best educational things we could do for my students," he said. "They get a chance to work with elementary school children and to interact with them plus a chance to perform every day."

The Folklore revival is a win-win situation. Early grades get perhaps their first taste of live theater. Upper grades and drama students get a skilled demonstration in acting and staging. Behind-the-scenes tours helped teachers discover ways to incorporate presentation ideas into their own classrooms. "It is a top-notch educational experience," evaluates Hansen.

WOMEN'S HALL SWITCHING TO FAMILY HOUSING

Work has started on converting a single women's residential hall at Ricks College into family housing. Lamprecht Hall, also known as Dorm 5, will be switched from housing for more than 200 single women to 37 units of family housing. Ricks Housing Director Sharon Tuckett says the project is designed to provide additional family housing for students who will be attending Ricks. "As we transition to BYU-Idaho, we recognize there will be a larger percent of students who will need this type of housing," she says. College officials estimate there will need to be about 1,000 such units within five years as Ricks begins offering bachelor's degrees and attracting more older students who may be married or have children.

Work has been contracted to Double E Construction of Ririe and the units are expected to be ready for occupancy for Winter Semester of 2002.

Tuckett says Lamprecht Hall was one of two non-cooking-style dorms for single female students and did not have kitchens. Remodeling will involve converting the current three-bedroom units into two-bedroom units with a kitchen and living area.

In April, Ricks officials announced the college is prepared to address the need of more family housing for college students if such housing is not provided by the private sector. Officials hope that private developers will consider the family market, but as a prudent action, the college has received permission from the Board of Trustees to begin feasibility studies necessary to develop plans to build more family housing on an "as needed" basis.
