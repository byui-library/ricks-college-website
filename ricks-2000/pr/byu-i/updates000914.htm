The Transition: Updates September 14, 2000

A general question-and-answer session open to employees and the general public was conducted by President David A. Bednar with members of President's Council on September 14, 2000.  Several of the questions concerned the transition of becoming BYU-Idaho.  Below are responses which give new or updated observations:

What new is happening towards the transition to becoming BYU-Idaho?

President David A. Bednar: President's Council and Academic Council have met with the academic deans and department heads. The deans and department heads are working with their faculty and staff to develop proposals. Ideas are being generated. The transition is in progress. The Executive Committee is scheduled to meet with us on campus on September 27, at which time we will share some of the initial proposals and receive feedback and direction.

Students are so optimistic that they can stay for four years. How can they wisely plan?

President Bednar: Every student should have two plans of action. The first plan should be to obtain an associate degree and transfer to another four-year university. The second plan should be to stay at Ricks if in a timely manner a desired four-year major comes on line.

Are efforts being made not to duplicate programs within the Church Educational System?

Don Bird, Academic vice president: We will duplicate a lot. There are some programs in high demand, and it would be impractical to expect one campus to admit all the students desiring to be in the program. At the same time there are some programs that are consistently under enrolled, and it does not make sense to duplicate those programs.


How much communication on programs has there been with the other CES campuses?

Bird: We met two weeks ago with their academic vice presidents and put a lot of things on the table. The preliminary discussions were mostly with BYU-Provo. We went through every program and talked about which ones might be offered here and which ones might not.

Will more courses be offered in the evenings and early mornings to make it more favorable for our employees [and community patrons] to take courses towards a bachelor's degree?

Jim Gee, assistant Academic vice president: One of our past limiting factors has been that we could only offer associate degrees. We have approval to schedule 7 a.m. classes but have not done so in the past. This change opens great possibilities that could be a benefit for all employees and the community who want to get bachelor's degrees.

What will happen to the BYU-Ricks Center in Idaho Falls?

Brent Kinghorn, Community Services vice president: We anticipate it will become the BYU-Idaho Center in Idaho Falls. Ron Campbell, our director over Continuing Education, has a good relationship with the administrators for the other universities that are at the Center of Higher Education. There is a consortium between the three institutions that work together. A lot of people in the Idaho Falls area like the opportunity to take classes from LDS faculty. We feel that the BYU-Idaho Center there is an opportunity to grow again.
