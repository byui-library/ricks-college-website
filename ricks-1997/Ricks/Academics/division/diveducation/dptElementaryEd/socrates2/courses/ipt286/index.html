Course Purpose

This course focuses on the instructional process using technology in general, and computers in particular. ISTE standards will guide course curricula with emphasis on WWW development and implementation for classroom use. Students should take ELED 150 in order to obtain experience with word processors, spreadsheets and databases.

Course Materials

  • A Zip Cartridge
  • A 3.5" floppy for email (if desired)
  • An E-mail and Internet Account (Through Ricks College or Personal)

Course Objectives

This course will give you the basic knowledge and skills required to develop, produce, and use soundly designed instructional courseware based on ISTE standards as adopted by many state departments of education. A two hour course cannot make you an expert in these areas, but it can give the motivation necessary for you to seek other courses and programs in this exciting discipline (continuing to improve will be a lifelong experience). Specifically, in this course you will:

  • Establish and use E-mail accounts for personal and educational use on a regular basis.
  • Demonstrate competency with a variety of software applications.
  • Evaluate and install software in a variety of categories.
  • Review and practice four basic instructional strategies and implement technology to enrich these strategies.
  • Design and build stand-alone and online presentations using PowerPoint and Hyperstudio.
  • Practice and use basic lesson design using the ASSURE model.
  • Research Copyright laws, Privacy and Fair Use guidelines and their applicability to selecting and using teaching materials.
  • Research and develop instructional learning materials that meet design and best practices guidelines using a variety of applications.
  • Develop strategies for implementing technology into both classroom and lab settings in schools.
  • Demonstrate use of a computer connected to a network.
  • Develop strategies for and conduct troubleshooting exercises with computers, software and other devices.
  • Evaluate the strengths and weaknesses of various projection devices.
  •  
  • Demonstrate skills and knowledge of application of the Internet to:
    • communicate using e-mail
    • search for educational materials
    • publish classroom materials
    • distribute instructional resources to students or others in distant locations, using:
      • html
      • sound, text and graphics
  • Prepare an instructional supplement using HyperStudio
