HS 203 (3 Credits) Introduction to Occupational Therapy. Roles and Activities in Occupational Therapy.

Instructor: Fred R. Pearson, Ph.D.

Office: Clarke 116 F

Phone: 356-1332

Course Description: Occupational Therapy is one of the 10 top fields in the United States. There are currently many professional openings with few people training to fill these positions.

When the occupational therapist graduates, he/she will find many well-paying positions available in hospitals, rehabilitation centers, mental health facilities, nursing homes, home health agencies and public and private schools. More than half of the graduates have more than 10 job offers. Because of the high demand, good jobs are available in almost every area of the United States. The range of starting salaries are between $36,000 and $55,000 annually.

This course will explore what the student can expect from a degree in Occupational Therapy, how to apply to professional programs, understanding of the changes in the Allied Health Careers and experience with an occupational therapist.

Textbook: Introduction to Occupational Therapy, Barbara Sabonis-Chafee and Susan M. Hussey, Mosby, Second Ed., 1998.

Assignments: Papers: Each student will be required to write 4 papers for this class. The first paper will be about your life history and what makes you unique. The second paper will be why you want to become an occupational therapist. The third paper will be a position paper on one of the many aspects of Occupational Therapy. This paper may be any topic that has a bearing on the field of Occupational Therapy. The fourth paper is a major research paper on one of the major areas of Occupational Therapy explaining the expectations and the rewards of this area.
