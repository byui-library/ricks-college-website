Important Information for Students and Their Parents

Tax Benefits for College Students

The Taxpayer Relief Act of 1997 introduced two types of educational tax credits along with other educational tax benefits. The scholarships and grants received from Ricks College, the government, and other sources can have an impact on the amount of tax you or your parents may have to pay. Below is a summary of some of the benefits available. This summary touches on issues of primary interest to students at Ricks College. Students and parents should not rely on the following summary of information. Ricks College makes no warranties of any kind regarding the accuracy of this information.

The HOPE Scholarship Tax Credit

This credit will impact many students at Ricks College.

  • The credit equals the first $1,000 of tuition and fees paid during a tax year plus 50 percent of the second $1,000. (The maximum credit is $1,500 per year for each eligible student.) For purposes of computing the credit, tuition and fees paid are reduced by any scholarships or grants received by the student which are excluded from the students taxable income. (A credit provides a dollar-for-dollar reduction of income tax, not merely a reduction of taxable income.)

    The credit is only available for the first two years of college education.
  • The credit is available only to the taxpayer who claims the student as a dependent.
  • The credit will be available for the tax year in which the expenses are paid as long as the education begins or continues during that year or begins during the first three months of the next year.
  • The student must be enrolled at least half time in at least one academic semester, block, or term during a tax year.
  • The credit phases out for those with a modified adjusted gross income between $40-50,000 for single taxpayers or $80-100,000 on joint returns.
  • This credit is not available for students who have been convicted of a drug-related felony.
  • The credit is nonrefundable, i.e., if the credit is more than your precredit tax liability, the excess is not refunded to you.

The Lifetime Learning Credit

This credit will be of less interest to Ricks College students because it generally provides a smaller benefit during the first two years of college education than the Hope Scholarship Tax Credit. The Lifetime Learning Credit may benefit students who are not eligible for the Hope Scholarship Credit.

  • The Lifetime Learning Credit is available for students in any year but cannot be used for a student in a year that the Hope Tax Credit is claimed for the student.
  • The credit equals 20 percent of the first $5,000 of tuition and other qualified expenses paid per year. The maximum annual credit is $1,000 per family.
  • There is no limit on the number of years the credit can be claimed for each eligible student.
  • The credit applies to tuition and fees for undergraduate, graduate, and continuing education course work irrespective of the number of classes taken.
  • The credit is available only to the taxpayer who claims the student as a dependent.


Summary

Because of the complexities of the law, you should consult your tax advisor. For more complete information, students may request a copy of Publication 970 from the IRS. It can also be found on the Internet at: http://www.irs.treas.gov/prod/forms_pubs/pubs.html. The publication not only contains information on the above credits but also contains information regarding other educational tax benefit programs.
