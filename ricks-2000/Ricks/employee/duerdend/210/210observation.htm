Child Development Observation/Interaction Assignment


Pick any 4 below and provide the requested information in four, 1-pg (typed) reports. For full credit you must deal with each question/issue in each observation completely and specifically. Numbers 3-6 can be done easily using the Ricks College Child Labs observation booths, Clarke 258 and 292 during lab hours: Preschool (ages 3-5) is MWF 8:20-10:40am and 1:20-3:40pm, Toddler class (ages 1-2) is TTH 9:30-11am [Note: You may do additional write-ups of these for up to 5 points extra credit each, up to max]

1. Prenatal - Interview some new parents (preferably with a baby under 6 months). Ask about the size of their own families of-origin, and the ideal size of their family-of-procreation. What were the most difficult aspects of pregnancy, and how did they cope with these? What is the biggest adjustment to parenthood? What were the fathers roles and feelings during pregnancy and delivery? How did they decide on their method of feeding the baby --bottle or breastfeeding?
2. Infancy - Spend at least 1 hour having complete responsibility for an infant under 1 year old who is awake most of the time you are there. Describe what you believe to be normal or somewhat abnormal (according to what your text describes) in the following areas: size and weight, motion and activity, sociability, distress from stranger and/or separation anxiety. Describe your own feelings and frustrations as you cared for the infant.

*Note: #'s 3-6 Need to be done with a supervised group of children, such as our Child Labs

*3. Toddler (1-3 yrs old)- Observe a group of toddlers (Toddler Lab or a Ward Nursery), note how they play and interact with each other. Which behaviors seem more mature (like preschoolers) and which seem more immature (like infants). Describe a behavior you observe which indicates a toddler's drive for autonomy (as Erickson suggests). What would be rewarding or difficult for you about working with toddlers?
*4. Preschool Interactions - Observe one child for 10 min. Describe how he interacts, and communicates with the other children and adults. Observe a group of children playing together and determine the child who appears to be the leader. What is this child doing that makes him/her the leader? Observe one child who is in a very happy or a very negative mood. Describe the behavior and how it is affecting other children and adults.
*5. Preschool Characteristics - Give specific examples of at least 3 of these 4 characteristics as you observe: 1) Short attention span, 2) Sex-role stereotypes, 3) Egocentrism (being unable to consider any other viewpoint than his own), 4) Enjoyment of fantasy/pretending. Observe the teachers helping the children to develop self-control. Give examples of this kind of guidance from the teachers.
*6. Preschool Play Patterns - Your text (pp. 292-293)describes several types of social and cognitive play preschoolers may engage in. Observe and briefly describe an example of at least 4 of the following types: 1) Onlooker Behavior,
2) Solitary Play, 3) Parallel play, 4) Associative Play, 5) Cooperative Play, 6) Pretend Play. Did you see any examples of formal games with rules? Were they teacher or child initiated?

7. Middle Years - Spend at least one hour with a child age 6-11 years, doing an activity of the child's choice. Report the child's name and age, the activity you did and your feelings while doing this with the child. Report the child's responses to these questions: 1) What do you want to be when you grow up? Why? What will you have to do to achieve this? 2) What kind of behavior is acceptable for boys to do and what is acceptable for girls to do. Why? 3) What things are you good at? What things are you not good at? (Determine how you think the child feels about him/herself as a person.)
8. Adolescence - Observe a group of adolescents (13-18 years, not Ricks College Students ). State where you observed them, and their approximate ages. Compare their physical development. How are boys and girls relating to each other? If they are all of the same sex, is the other sex mentioned? How? What need for group approval and conformity do you see? What were your feelings as you observed? (Relate to your own adolescence.)
9. Influence of Television - Watch a television show developed for young children, and one for older children. State the name, and main characters of each program and what you think was the purpose of each program. Identify what prosocial and anti-social behaviors might be being modeledin each program. What are some of the popular-culture messages, politically correct images, or things you might be concerned with. How would you talk to your child about these?

Rubric for Peer Evaluation of Observation/Interaction Assignments
25 pts Meets all the criteria, answers all the questions and/or discusses all the issues completely and clearly.
20 pts Meets all the criteria and answers most of the questions/issues completely and clearly.
15 pts Mostly OK, but doesn't meet some of the criteria, and doesn't completely answer the questions/issues.
10 pts Not a very good job, but did make an attempt to deal with some of the criteria and questions.
5 pts Not even close to what was assigned. A hurried attempt to get something on paper.
