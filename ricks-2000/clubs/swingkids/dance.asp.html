Introduction to the Lindy Hop

In the mid 1920s, a strange new dance was emerging that combined the very popular Charleston with the less mainstream Break-A-Way (aka the Texas Tommy or the Mooch and Sugar).
The famous Savoy Ballroom

In 1927, George "Shorty" Snowden (yes, that's the Shorty George) gave this emerging dance its name and its start towards widespread recognition. In honor of Charles "Lucky Lindy" Lindbergh's thirty-three hour "hop" across the Atlantic, the dance was named the Lindy Hop and the name took.

Stemming primarily from Harlem dancers, the Lindy was/is a work in progress. New "moves" were always being developed and displayed on Harlem's dance floors. Among the popular places to dance, The Savoy Club was said to be the most popular.

Opening in 1926, The Savoy was home to many of Lindy Hop's most famous dancers, including "Shorty" George and Frankie Manning. Additionally, her stage was graced by some of the most famous artists of the day, including Duke Ellington, Count Basie, Billie Holliday, and Benny Goodman, the King of Swing.

With the passage of time, the Lindy Hop came to be known as Jitterbug. This term eventually came to include all forms of swing, but the original Lindy Hop still remains. Savoy Style, characterized by a bent-over stance with a more pronounced bounce and lots of swing, is said to be the original form of Lindy Hop. Other forms, such as Hollywood (smooth) Style, also gained popularity.  These other forms of the dance also led to other dances such as the Shag, Balboa and others.
"Bobby Soxers" watch Benny Goodman

We hope you have a good time participating in this truly American dance that has brought people of all ages, races, and social classes together in dance since its conception.
