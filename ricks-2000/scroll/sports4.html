Goof Troop beats Madison 18-6 in intramural championship

Riana Jevne
jev01001@byui.edu
Jim Higgins
hig98002@byui.edu
scroll staff

Editor’s Note: The following are the intramural championships played the week of Oct. 23-25. Dominating the entire first half of the game, Goof Troop out fought Madison 18-6 in the women’s independent flag football championship game Wednesday night.
A Granma running back eludes SIPA defenders in Men’s Viking League Intramurals. The Granmas beat Sipa 33-26 to stay in the tournament.

“It feels great to win the championship,” Jenny Rossi, a sophomore from Dallas, Texas, said. “Madison was an awesome team to play. It was great competition. We felt, as a team, we really pulled together.”

Ana Belle Gutierrez, a junior from Dallas, Texas, and Rossi teamed up early in the first possession, resulting in a Rossi to Gutierrez touchdown.

“All the girls who played had great sportsmanship, and they put a lot of hard work into it,” Goof Troop’s Cherie Bloss, a junior from Rome, N.Y., said. “I appreciate their spirit.”

Gutierrez labeled the ball as Madison’s, intercepting it on the very next play, 20 yards outside the Goof Troop end zone.

Rossi pulled a quarterback sneak and ran into the zone herself, putting the score at 12-0.

Just before the half, Goof Troop scored again on a rush.

“It was overall a really awesome season. It was a good way to start our last year here together,” Trisha White, a sophomore from Pleasanton, Calif., said. “The best part was warm-ups right before the games in our apartment with our warm-up CD.”

Madison got past Goof Troop in the last half to put points on the board but then had their two-point conversion attempt intercepted by Rossi.

“We had fun, and we had our bishop here,” Gutierrez said. “We harmonized as a team, and we had each other.”

“We have a wonderful, tight-nit ward, and they’re the heart of it,” Bishop Phil Sompson said about the Goof Troop.

•Elena Brooks wins women’s raquetball

After three long matches, Elena Brooks, a sophomore from Tremonton, Utah, defeated Geneva Beazely, a sophomore from Alberta, Calgary, to claim the girls racquetball intramural championship title.

“After a long, stressful day, it’s nice to forget about homework and come in and play and hit really hard,” Brooks said.

Beazely won the first game 15-6, but Brooks came back to outscore her in the final two games, 15-5 and 15-9.

“Geneva was the hardest player I’ve played,” Brooks said. “I had to put effort in before, but not as much as I had to with Geneva. If there were more girls involved, there would be more varying levels of competition.”

•Jason Carpenter wins men’s raquetball

Jason Carpenter, a freshman from Idaho Falls, beat Justin Nielsen, a sophomore from Shelley, Idaho, 15-2 and 15-8 in the men’s racquetball intramural championship game Thursday night.

“I’ve been playing my whole life, and up here a lot of people can’t beat me,” Nielsen said. “Jason is a great player and he deserved to win. I enjoy it because it’s the only sport I’m good at, and it keeps me in shape.”

“When I competed at a national level in the past there was a lot more stress and competition,” Carpenter said. “I enjoy playing intramurals more because you meet some really cool people, and it’s a lot more fun.”

•3 Lions beat Pime 1-0 for men’s soccer championship

The 3 Lions defeated Pime 1-0 for the men’s league intramural soccer championship Wednesday. Defense and goal tending dominated the hard fought match.

Felipe Moreno, a freshman from Santiago, Chile, scored the only goal on a breakaway.

“You can’t think that much or you’ll miss it,” Moreno said of his game-winning goal.

With Moreno’s goal and the stingy goal tending of Oscar Ibanez, a sophomore from Barcelona, Venezuela, the 3 Lions were able to hang on for the victory and the championship.

Pime had many opportunities to score in the first half, with four shots missing high. In the second half, however, the defense of the 3 Lions held Pime to only to shots.
