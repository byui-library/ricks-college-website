RICKS COLLEGE SIFE TEAM CAPTURES HONORS

 

by Munro Murdock
scroll staff


A group of nine students representing Ricks College competed at the 2001 SIFE USA National Expostition, sponsored by the Kansas City Business Community, May 13-15 in Missouri.

The Ricks College SIFE Team earned the First-runner-up award in their league and ranked among the top 12 in the two-year division.

Students In Free Enterprise is a non-profit organization that was founded in 1975 and is active on more than 1,000 college and university campuses in 20 countries. SIFE partners with business and higher education to provide college students with the opportunity to make a difference through learning, practicing, and teaching the principles of free enterprise.

"I honestly am not aware of a more devoted group on campus that is as willing and diligent to help so many people, just for the gratification and satisfaction of knowing that they have made a positive difference," Brigette Stallings, a sophomore from Blackfoot, Idaho, said.

The Ricks College SIFE team competed against more than 150 other teams represented by nearly 2,500 students, in the Kansas City competition. Earlier this spring, each team competed at one of 20 regional competitions. The SIFE team from Ricks was named regional champion at the Los Angeles, Calif. SIFE Exposition held April 3.

The SIFE team at Ricks attracted several students who wanted to get involved and make a difference in the lives of others. "Where else can you find a club that has done over 30 projects in one year that have benefited over 1,000 people?" Brady Apostolo, a sophomore from Randleman, N.C., asked. "If you're not involved in doing something other than school work while here at school, then you have missed the whole Ricks experience," Apostolo said. "You grow by getting involved and not by sitting at home doing nothing."

During national competition, Ricks SIFE students presented a 24-minute multi-media summary of the 34 educational outreach projects they developed and implemented throughout the year. Participating teams were judged by a panel drawn from more than 750 of the nation's top business and community leaders.

Mark Nygren, a professor in the Business Management department at Ricks, serves as advisor for the school's SIFE team. He was named a Sam M. Walton Free Enterprise Fellow in recognition of his leadership and support of the Free Enterprise program at Ricks College.

"SIFE has become an integral part of the business program at our school and has provided hands-on learning experience as well as career opportunities for all of the students involved," Nygren said. "Several of these students will continue throughout this next year to build upon the foundation of Free Enterprise education that they have worked so hard to establish."
