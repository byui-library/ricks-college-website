There are three verbals:  gerunds, participles, and infinitives.  In each case, a verbal is a verb form used as an adjective or a noun.
 
Gerunds
A gerund is a verb with -ing added to it.  The word is then used as a noun.  For example, start with the verb, ski.  Then add -ing to the word and form, skiing.  Then use this word as a noun.
 
Examples.  Skiing is fun.  Running develops and strengthens the heart.  Clear writing is essential in business.  Seeing is believing.  With the last example, both words, "seeing and believing," are gerunds.  "Believing" is a predicate noun.
 
Participles
A participle is a verb usually with -ed -en or -ing added to it.  When the word is used as an adjective, it is a verbal.  For example, start with the verb, run.  Then add -ing to the word and form, running.  Then use this word as an adjective.  
 
Examples.  Running water remains pure.  Broken hearts can heal with time.  The laughing clouds share my joy.  The words, running, broken, and laughing, are participles.  (Also note examples like "kept woman, known fact, sprung door," etc.).
 
Infinitives
An infinitive is a verb with "to" in front of it.  The phrase is then used as a noun, adjective, or adverb.  Examples.  To run is useful.  To ski is relaxing.  To write clearly is essential. (These examples are all nouns.)  The man to beat is Harry.  Here the infinitive is an adjective.  I waved to show I was willing. The infinitive is an adverb.
 
Infinitive and Gerund Phrases
The infinitive and gerund can also have an object.  This object, like a direct object, will also answer the questions, whom or what.  Study these sentences:
 
"Running the store is hard work."  "Running the store" is the subject of the sentence.  It is a gerund phrase used as a noun.  "Store" is the object of the gerund.  Running what?  The store.
 
"Moving the furniture took more time than we had expected."  "Moving the furniture" is the subject of the sentence.  It is a gerund phrase used as a noun.  "Furniture" is the object of the gerund.  Moving what?  The furniture.  We can get the same structure and situation with "to run the store" and "to move the furniture."  Here is another example:
 
"To know her is to love her."  "To know her" is the subject; "to love her" is the subject complement (predicate noun).  In both cases, the word "her" is the object of the infinitives. "to love" and "to know."
