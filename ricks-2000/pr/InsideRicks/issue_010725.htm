LAST RICKS COLLEGE ENROLLMENT IS OFFICIALLY COUNTED

The last classes are now under way at Ricks College before it becomes known at Brigham Young University-Idaho.

Enrollment statistics show the overall number of students studying at Ricks increased for the entire summer, but is down slightly for the third summer term that runs from July 16 to August 17.

The combined number of students enrolled during any of the summer terms was 7,826 students compared to 7,685 during 2000.

The first and second summer terms showed increases, while the third summer term showed a decrease from 3,349 last year to 3,021 this year.

Jeff Sorensen of the Admissions Office wasn't surprised by the third term decline, explaining that last year a large group of entering freshmen were required to attend at least one summer term. The requirement was dropped for this year's incoming freshmen, resulting in fewer freshmen starting their college careers during the summer.

In keeping with the college's plan to expand enrollment and class offerings in the summer months, Sorensen expects to see more students at BYU-Idaho next summer. Next year the university will offer two eight-week summer terms, instead of the traditional three five-week terms that have been offered at Ricks in the past.

This term's statistics include 1,865 females and 1,156 males; 115 foreign students from 29 countries; 1,081 from Idaho; 312 married students; and 671 returned missionaries.

The final term of summer school ends Aug. 17 with fall semester getting under way Sept. 5.

NEW TELEPHONE PREFIX IS ASSIGNED FOR BYU-IDAHO

A new telephone prefix has been applied for and received for BYU-Idaho. Beginning August 27, access to the university telephones will be through a 496 prefix rather than 356. All telephone and fax extensions previously assigned to a 356 prefix will remain the same. For example, operator assistance (open weekdays from 7 a.m. to 11 p.m. and weekends 7 a.m. to 1 a.m.) will change August 27 from 208-356-2411 to 208-496-2411. E-mail fax numbers previously beginning 359 will be changed completely.

For the first month, either prefix 356 or 496 will work. After October 1, a recording will inform callers to use the new 496 prefix. As of February 1, the 356 prefix will no longer work nor will it result in a recording. 356 and 359 numbers previously used on campus will be turned to Qwest for reassignment in the Rexburg area.

The university has its own telephone office and system. The system currently handles over 2,000 phone devises (telephones, e-mail modems and faxes). The demand for services continues to steadily increase with expanding faculty and student body. Gaylen Jensen, Director of Telecommunication Services explains, "We were running out of phone numbers and knew we would need more. Since we are becoming BYU-Idaho, we felt it was the right time to ask for an entire prefix of our own."

According to Jensen, the administration felt the change would be a good neighbor approach to avoid interference with community phone numbers. In the past there has been some confusion caused by sharing a prefix with the Rexburg community.

With the high volume coming through the system, a new prefix will add distinction. Telecommunication Services handles calls that are internal, incoming, outgoing local, and long distance for campus employees and students living in university-owned housing. On a daily basis, there are between 2,000-3,000 outgoing long distance and nearly 10,000 incoming calls a day when school is in full session. Jensen estimates the volume of outgoing local calls is similar to incoming calls while internal calls has an even higher volume.
