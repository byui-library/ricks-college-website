Cash, guns, meth seized in Rexburg drug bust

by Marie Mackay
MAC00005@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Officer Rick Schmitt and Deputy Mike Courtney conduct a press release at the Rexburg Police Department Friday concerning the arrest of Cristobal Perez for the possession of several ounces of methamphetamine along with firearms and money. Shelly McEuen / Scroll
A 49-year-old man was arrested by the Rexburg Madison Drug Task Force Thursday on two counts of felony complaint for delivery of methamphetamine, the Rexburg Police said.

Catarino Ortiz aka Cristobal Perez was taken into custody and a warrant was obtained for the search of his residence at Thompsons Trailer Court in Madison County, according to the Rexburg Police.
“This is by no means the largest [drug bust], but for the county and the area, it is a very significant seizure,” Deputy Mike Courtney of the Madison County Sheriff’s Office said.

Over $23,000 in cash was seized along with several ounces of methamphetamine worth approximately $5,000. There was also several firearms, vehicles and other items.

A criminal complaint and warrant was issued and a bond was set for Ortiz at $100,000.

The drugs were being transferred from one place to another, so they were not found directly from a meth lab, police said.

“Rexburg has not yet taken down a meth lab, and that’s a good thing,” Lynn Archibald, Rexburg Chief of Police, said.

The Rexburg Police Department and the Madison County Sheriff’s Office with the assistance of agencies from the Upper Valley Drug Task Force have been investigating this drug trafficking for several months, the Rexburg Police said.

“The investigation is continuing, and it just came to a climax,” Archibald said.

The last comparable seizure of this size was in the 1980s when about $1.2 million in cocaine that was brought up from Durango, Colo., was obtained after several months of investigation.

“The drug force has been working on this for a really long time, and it is good to finally get it wrapped up,” Courtney said.

“It’s good to see the fruits of your labor,” Rick Schmitt of the Rexburg Police Department, said.
