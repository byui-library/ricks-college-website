Course Aims

The intent of English 211 is to help you become 1) the best thinker you are capable of, 2) an effective critic of the arguments you encounter in every-day life, and 3) a more competent writer.English 211 aims are based on the assumption that reading and writing are connected and necessary to the development of critical thinking skills. Class readings focus on non-fiction prose drawn from several disciplines. Writing assignments focus on these texts as sources for syntheses, critiques, and arguments. Students of English 211 write papers totaling about twenty pages, typed and double spaced.

Texts
For class you will need 1) Hartvigsen's Thinking about Thinking (Because we will mark Thinking extensively, the Bookstore will not buy back this text); 2) Browne and Keeley's Asking the Right Questions; 3) Lunsford and Connor's Easy Writer; 4) a college dictionary; and 5) several 5" by 8" index cards

Class Procedures
Please 1) Attend class each day-even if you are occasionally unprepared. 2) Plan on "prep" checks at the beginning of most classes. (You'll need to be in class to receive credit for these.)   3) Hand papers in at the beginning of class on due dates. (I read late papers for half credit-if received before the subsequent essay assignment.)    4) Work at improving your academic skills! According to Richard Paul, a university professor, if you want to improve your thinking you "must be willing to learn more about how thinking works and how to improve it." Such practice takes considerable time and effort. This course is built upon the assumption that you are willing to make a two-hour commitment for each hour you spend in class.

Papers
I will ask you to write three papers during the semester, each paper building upon the skills of that unit. The first essay is a synthesis; the second, a critique; the third, an argument. See Appendix A for a detailed description of each assignment. Each paper is due at the beginning of class on designated days.

Grading
Grading is based on the total percentage of points earned (93-100, A; 90-92, A-; 87-89, B+;83-86, B; 80-82, B-, etc.). Assignments are weighted as follows: 1) synthesis essay, 50 points: 2) critique, 50 points; 3) argument, 50 points; 4) final exam, 50 points; and 5) preparation & quizzes, 50 point
