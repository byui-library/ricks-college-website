or 410 when sacked by Visigoths

1. imperialism - Too much money. The spread of Rome's power over the western world created a city mob, excess wealth, and internal corruption..

2. trade deficit - too much $$ leaving the country in trade, so coinage is debased.

3. barter causes a decline in industry, commerce, and a closing of the foreign markets (like today??)

3 Philosophies under Augustus and the Pax Romana

 

1. Epicurians (Lucretius)- follower of Democritus/atoms

·       atoms join at random — Fate controls future

·       everything is either coming together or falling apart (you could fall apart at any minute)

·       since the mind is matter, there is no afterlife

·       excess may lead to a bad tomorrow (drunks have hangovers) so moderation.

·       “Pleasure is the beginning and end of the good life,” and “prudence is the pursuit of pleasure.”

·       so eat little (you'll get indigestion), drink little (hangover), and avoid public office and all other “passionate activities” (including marriage and having children). The only way to be truly happy is to avoid that which causes injury.

·       avoid discussing that which causes fear: religion and death

        NOTE: Epicurianism is NOT hedonism

 

2. Neoplatonism - comes from Plato and the Academy (and later from Plotinus, 204AD)

        - all things emanate from God, (realm of Ideas/Forms)

                - the best is the Good (God, Pure Idea)

- since matter can have no form without an Idea, it is to be despised.

        mysticism of the East.

·       soul of man was once with God

·       became separate when it was joined with matter

·       the soul is constantly trying to reunite with the divine

·       believe in eternal salvation

        asceticism becomes popular

·       try to free the soul from the body

·       be ashamed of the body/subjugate it

        Conclusion: learn what is truth while on earth, so after death you can enter into the city of the Good (this will be changed by St. Augustine to City of God, his book. This, of course, forms the basis of salvation and eternal life, the basis for early Christianity.

 

3. Stoics - founded by Zeno

        Logos (God) guides the world and all that is in it, thus everything is as it should be. (Pope/Essay on Man)

·       your duty is to submit since all is guided by God

accept your station in life

·       be stoic, do your duty, and you'll receive inner happiness

                fight it and you'll be unhappy

                pleasure and pain both are worldly and have no bearing on you—simply bear them and go on.

·       all men form a Brotherhood

                so treat all men the same

                - all are equal under the law

        Cicero - lawyer/one of greatest Romans ever

                - virtue is sufficient for happiness

                - peace of mind is the highest good

                - State is not above the individual; rather govt is a compact among men for their mutual protection (Locke)

                - natural law exists, and is discoverable by reason
