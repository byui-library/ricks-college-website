Ricks College News Release
June 12, 2001

Ricks smokestack coming down with addition 

of new, environmentally-friendly boiler

    
REXBURG, Idaho – No longer will black smoke be seen coming out of the Ricks College smokestack, nor will the smokestack itself be visible.

Ricks College announced today that it will begin extensive renovations on the Heating Plant immediately, which will include installing a larger, more environmentally friendly boiler and replacing the 100-foot tall smokestack with smaller, more earthquake-safe smokestacks for each of the four boilers.

A 35-year-old, 10,000 pound coal-fire boiler, the smallest of four, will be replaced by a 45,000 pound natural gas boiler.

"The boiler has reached the end of its useful life," Chuck Frost, director of the Physical Plant, said. "We want to be environmentally friendly."

With the current system, the four coal-fire boilers sometimes will puff black smoke as the demand for steam changes and rapid adjustments are made. Now the coal-fire boilers can be loaded as needed at a steady rate and load swings can be accommodated by the gas-fire boiler which doesn’t smoke, resulting in cleaner emissions and more efficiency.

"It makes the plant run cleaner, and it has more capacity," Frost said. "We get the best of both worlds, more capacity and a cleaner operation. Natural gas is more expensive, but we feel it is worth the extra cost for a cleaner environment. That is really what pushed us to make this step."

As well as installing the custom-made boiler, Ricks will be adding 1,200 square feet for a new control room to accommodate changes inside the boiler room, which will house new state-of-the-art controls for each of the four boilers.

Seismic upgrades include replacing the roof and the smokestack, which will be torn down in October or November.

"The smokestack is not braced for moderate earthquakes," Frost said. "The cost to upgrade the smokestack to adequate seismic standards wasn’t worth it. It is cheaper and safer to put up new smokestacks and tear the old one down."

Construction on the Heating Plant was approved by the Board of Trustees May 15 and initial stages have already begun. Construction is expected to be completed by the end of the year in time for the heating season. The new boiler will arrive in July. To install a boiler of its size, the north wall of the plant will be removed, and the boiler will be rolled into the plant with only a few inches of clearance.

The project has been under way since 1998, and is not related to the BYU-Idaho transition. The capacity of the plant is currently capable of providing heat to the additional buildings planned, such as the multi-use building and the announced Thomas E. Ricks Building.

"It had to happen whether we were going to four years or not," Frost said. "It does fit in nicely though."

The Heating Plant is one of several construction projects on campus. Renovations have been made on the David O. McKay Library, the Joseph Fielding Smith Building and the Manwaring Center. Additionally, construction of the new Jacob Spori Building and multi-use building are under way.
