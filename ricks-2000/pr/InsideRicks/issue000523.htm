ELDER REX E. PINEGAR TELLS PEOPLE TO BE HAPPY

The best people I know are happy," said Elder Rex D. Pinegar, a member of First Quorum of the Seventy, at Thursday night's Faculty-Staff Dinner. "When people see you, they should be able to smile." Elder Pinegar told the nearly 1,300 people in attendance that they should be happy because "we know the truth and we should just as well enjoy it."

At the same time, he recognized that people face trying times in their lives. "Experiences will come to us that we can't foresee. But it's all right - the Lord has foreseen it."

Elder Pinegar said, "We would encourage you to focus your life around the commitments you have made in the temple." The temple teaches people to be truthful, to be pure and to serve. "We are not takers from the world, but givers."

He told those in attendance that they are all part of the change that is coming to the college. "There are opportunities that you've never imagined that are now yours. Your part in the change is significant. It will take the actions and commitment of everyone here to do what is to be done."

He said the Lord "knows where we are going. Turn to Him and turn to each other."

BYU LIVING LEGENDS

Brigham Young University's Living Legends will kick off the new year in Rexburg with a 90-minute celebration of Latin American, Native American, and Polynesian song and dance. Living Legends (formerly known as the Lamanite Generation) will perform at the Hart Auditorium Saturday, January 8, at 7:30 p.m. Tickets are on sale at the Ricks College Ticket Office, which reopens Tuesday, January 4. Tickets are $8 for chair seats, and a special rate of $2 is offered for bleacher seating. Call (208) 356-2230.

Authentic choreography, intricate costumes, and heart-pounding music bring to life the beauty of traditional cultures often forgotten in today's modern world. From the graceful Hawaiian hula, to Mexican fiesta dances of celebration, to the excitement and beauty of a Native American powwow, this year's Living Legends performance reflects the cycle of civilization. The dances portray the changing seasons experienced by ancient cultures, weaving together legends of the past with the reality of today. All Living Legends members are Native American, Latin American, or Polynesian heritage. Living Legends is directed by Janielle Christensen and originates in the School of Music at BYU.

Living Legends strives to encourage all people to take pride in their individual heritage. Their joyful expression of cultural tradition encourages audiences to better themselves, as illustrated by a statement from the Offices of Culture in Guatemala: "Your performance was wonderful. Your presence here teaches our people something - to have high ideals and to walk proud as a people. We hope this community will not be the same after tonight."

SPORTS REVIEW

Athletics at Ricks College focuses on an identified mission statement. The department strives "to build testimonies; to provide a quality education; to prepare students to further their education, for employment, to be good citizens, and to be responsible parents; and to develop leadership in the kingdom." Additional points within its mission statement are to "provide a broad-based program for men and women that strives for excellence in performance, enhances sportsmanship, and develops character. Provide a venue for campus and community involvement that promotes the mission of the Church, enhances the mission of Ricks College, and meets the needs and expectations of individuals." A review of 1999 sports recognizes great strides of success.

Men's basketball team is undefeated at 15-0 and goes into the year 2000 ranked tenth nationally. Ricks is the only undefeated team in the Scenic West Athletic Conference (SWAC). (Their game at North Idaho Community College in December ended a 27-game losing streak at Coeur d'Alene with a 70-66 win.) Now in his seventh year at the helm of the Viking basketball program, Coach Clyde Nelson has posted a winning percentage of 68.2%

Women's basketball team is 13-1 and ranked fourth nationally. Ricks and Utah Valley are the only teams in the league with only one loss. After three years as sports information director at Ricks College, Lori Woodland returned as head coach of Lady Viking Basketball in 1997-98. Coach Woodland is in her twelfth season as head coach. In eleven seasons, the Lady Vikings have won 224 and lost 102, a winning percentage of 68.7% under Coach Woodland.

Wrestling is ranked third nationally with an 8-4 dual meet record. Coach Bob Christensen, one of the most successful wrestling coaches in the nation, is in his sixteenth year at the helm of the Ricks College wrestling team. Ricks has three returning All-Americans including a defending NJCAA champion. The returning national champion is heavyweight James Huml of Sugar City, Idaho. Huml is duplicating last year's torrid pace with a 21-1 record. The two other returning All-Americans are Shane Wasden of Sugar City and Jeff Ireland of South Dakota.

Women's fast-pitch softball prepares for a spring season currently ranked nationally at seventh with a 39-9 record from the spring of 1999. Coach Larry Stocking enters his fifth season as head softball coach at Ricks College with an amazing record. In four years, Stocking's teams have won 160 games against only 33 losses. His squads have won four straight Region 18 championships.

Cross country teams had a very successful year. Coach Doug Stutz saw both his men's and women's teams win national championships in Lansing, Michigan, in November. Named to All-American second team for men were MikeThatcher and Jason Brown. Honorable mention honors went to Ashley Smith, Dustin Trail, and Ben Wynn. Women recognized on All-American teams were first team, Kristen Ogden; second team, Amber Cellan; and honorable mention to Courtney Allen.

Baseball is coached by Jerry Schlegelmilch who is heading into his ninth season with an accumulated 244-198 record. The team finished the 1999 season at 22-28 and SWAC 15-19. The program under Coach Schlegelmilch uses Ricks College's indoor field house as the off-season training facility. The Vikings moved to a brand-new outdoor baseball facility in the fall of 1998.

Track and field in Region 18 has been dominated by Ricks College for years. Coach Ferron Sonderegger has twelve consecutive Region 18 titles for his women's teams. At the same time, the men have won the Region crown eleven times, slipping to second in the 1989 season. National recognition in 1999 went to Tad Baltzer, discus 153' 09", first place and named to the All-American first team. Second place and second team honors went to Lena Walker, 1,500 meters 4:53.76; Analee Carter, high jump 5' 8.5"; Melody Sheppard, 3,000 meters 10:26.82; Alycia Boyer, 10,000 meters 37:55.31; and Shanae Nixon, heptathlon, 4,671 pts. Third place and honorable mention went to Kelsi Hall, heptathlon 4,640 pts; Melody Sheppard, 1,500 meters 4:56.66; April Jackson, 100 meter HH 14.53; Andrew Levin, decathlon, 6,486 points; Sesha Snell, hammer 150' 11"; Amber Cellan, 10,000 meters 38:29.19; and David Wood, discus 151' 8".

Volleyball Coach JoAnn Reeve entered her 25th season at Ricks College as the second winningness coach in the history of the National Junior College Athletic Association. Her record through the 1999 season stands at 834 wins against 257 losses. That translates into an over 76% win ratio. The 1999 team ended the year ranked twelfth nationally with a 38-13 record. In the SWAC, they were 11-5. Robyn Sonju, a sophomore from St. George, Utah, was named an honorable mentioned All-American.

Football Coach Ron Haun had his 18th season (160-36-2), and the team ended the year ranked fourth in the NJCAA poll. National post season All-American second team honors went to OL-Bart Teuscher, sophomore 6'4" 275#, Clearfield, Utah; and LB-Russell Kay, sophomore, 6'2" 245#, Mesa, Ariz. New Ricks College records were logged for most passes completed in career with 382 by Ryan Harris (old record 368-Darren Wilkinson in 1993-94); most TD passes in career with 51 by Ryan Harris (old record-49-Darren Wilkinson in 1993-94); and highest career completion percentage of 61.7% by Ryan Harris (old record-54.9%-Brian Larsen in 1980-81). Season records broken were most PAT's at 62 by Aaron Edmonds (old record-50-Jesse Neville in 1992); most PAT's attempted at 64 by Aaron Edmonds (old record-53-Jesse Neville in 1991); most consecutive PAT's at 44 by Aaron Edmonds (old record-24-Kevin Doty in 1975); and highest pass completion percentage at 62.3% by Ryan Harris (old record-61.2%-Ryan Harris in 1998). Tied records in a single game were noted for most TD pass receptions with three by Tom Pace in the games with Phoenix and Glendale and most field goals with four by Aaron Edmonds in the game with Eastern Arizona. Tom Pace scored 21 touchdowns to tie the season record set in 1998 by Steve Later.
