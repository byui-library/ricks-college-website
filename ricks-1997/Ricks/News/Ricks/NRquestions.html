Ricks College News Release, Feb. 26, 1998

Questions and answers
from President David Bednar

Ricks College President David A. Bednar recently took some time to sit down and answer a few questions. Here are his responses:

How did you get involved in education?

I always enjoyed teaching, but I think especially on my missionboth in teaching people interested in learning about the church and in working with missionaries in zone conferences and other settingsI initially discovered my love for learning and teaching. Following my mission, I continued my undergraduate work at BYU. When I was a senior, a faculty member asked me to assist him with some instructional responsibilities in a class. That experience built on the teaching opportunities I had in the mission field, and I just loved it. I think those experiences cultivated my passion for teaching.

Could you go over the selection process for being appointed president of Ricks?

I can only tell you about the appointee part of the process. I was contacted by Elder (Henry B.) Eyring, the commissioner of church education. He inquired as to whether I would be interested in being considered as a candidate for the office of president at Ricks. I said, "Certainly." My wife and I traveled to Salt Lake City and were interviewed by Elder (L. Tom) Perry and Elder Eyring. About three weeks after that interview, we received a phone call and were notified that we were being invited to become the fourteenth president of Ricks College. For us, it was very unexpected and sudden. Our lead time was from the middle of April when I received that first call until about a month later when we were notified that we were receiving that invitation. We had no expectation of leaving Arkansas and were quite happy there.

How does your wife figure into being president of Ricks College?

Obviously, she is not engaged in the process of making day-to-day decisions. But in terms of the example that is set for the students and the teaching opportunities that occur, this is a team responsibility. There are a number of teaching opportunities on campus where it takes both of us to be effective. I think, too, that Susan has a very important responsibility to be a role model for the young women on the campus. It is particularly important that she be an example of a righteous LDS woman for the young women and young men at Ricks College.

Could you discuss some of your plans for the college?

The overarching goal is to keep Ricks College the same while it changes. If you consider, for example, the rapid evolution of technology and how it can impact the way students learn and what takes place at a college, it is clear that we will change some of what we now do. But while we make methodological and pedagogical changes, some things at Ricks College should never change. As I interview students and ask, "What do you like about Ricks?" they will answer, "The atmosphere." "I love being around people with similar values." "I love the friendliness of the people." Technology will never replace the friendliness and the spiritual atmosphere, and it is very important to understand that technology is not going to replace people at an institution of higher education like this. But we are going to change how we use people and technology in the process of learning. So we have to keep things the same while we change.

Have you made a pretty easy transition?

I wouldn't say that I have made an easy transition. I would say that the people I work with me have made my transition easy. For example, the four vice presidents have been very helpful in providing background and history about projects that were already "in progress" when I came on board. I compliment them and all of the people here at Ricks with whom I work because they have made the transition very easy.

What do you see in the future for Ricks in the next few years?

The enrollment cap has grown from approximately 7,500 to 8,600.

Do you think it is going to go past that?

No, 8,600 is as large as we will be. Anything beyond a student body of 8,600 would require additions to our physical campus that are not likely. Going from 7,500 to 8,600 is a significant percentage increase, so we will have to work out some bumps in the process of making sure that we are serving those 8,600 students well.

Are there changes that you have already made since you've come here regarding standards? It seems the enforcement is getting stronger and that a great importance is being placed on firesides, devotionalsthe spiritual aspect of campus life.

I have not come to Ricks with a preset agenda and said, "We will do this." I think some of what you perceive grows out of my responsibility as a member of the Quorum of the Seventy. For example, one of the things we have done is make devotionals available to everybody on campus through overflow facilities. One of the things I discovered early on is that many students were discouraged about going to devotionals because they could not get into the Hart Auditorium. To me, there was a simple solution. With the addition of the Taylor Building and use of the Kirkham Auditorium, we have the ability, via technology, to provide every young person on campus the opportunity to attend every devotional. I think the devotional is a time to worship. It is not a social hour. As you are aware, I have articulated that point a number of times and encouraged students to come prepared to worship and to learn. I think those are points of emphasis that have just come quite naturally. Perhaps that spiritual emphasis has brought about a greater desire among the students to abide by the Code of Honor. I hope so.

Another thing I have tried to encourage is that the decision making process, as much as possible, follows the pattern of counseling in councils. There is great value in using effectively the experience and expertise of people in the process of making decisions. Particularly at a Church institution, we can invite the spirit of the Holy Ghost into that process. Counseling in councils is not the spiritual equivalent of participative decision making; rather, it is an opportunity to invite all people to participate in the revelatory process.

Have there been any other changes that have come about since you've come here besides what we talked about?

The answer is yes, but they are not decisions that I individually have made. We receive direction from the Board of Trustees. The board sets direction and establishes policy. As the CEO of the campus, I have the responsibility to implement the policies set by the board. We counsel in meetings with the board and present reports and describe our concerns. They take that information into account in formulating the direction and policies such as the recently announced space-neutral policy.

And that is something you are in the middle of?

At the present time we are engaged in a comprehensive assessment regarding our existing space and facilities on campus. We are identifying the needs of our academic and support programs. We will then align both present and future needs with the space and facilities that we have.

How long in the future might we see some changes in the way our space is allocated? Or will we see any change?

I wouldn't say in the way space is allocated; it may be in how we configure and use space. It is a very holistic assessment. We will have to consider how we might rethink and reconfigure space that we already have. We've been working on this process for two months.

How do you feel everything is going?

Overall, I think we do an excellent job in our classrooms. Our teachers are highly dedicated and committed. They serve students individually in a way that would be difficult to match in other institutions of higher education. They don't just give lip service to serving students; rather, they truly serve and go out of their way to assist and help students. You find the same level of care and concern among staff employees and administrators around the campus. Across this entire college you have a unique collection of people who are both dedicated and committed to serving students. And they do it very effectively.

How does that compare to other institutions you've worked for?

The universities where I have worked before were all comprehensive, graduate-degree-granting institutions. Frankly, at many such institutions, the emphasis is not on serving undergraduate students. It may be on research or other kinds of activities, and spending time with and really meeting the needs of undergraduates would not necessarily be considered a high priority. Constituencies of secular institutions increasingly are saying, "You should spend more time and be more concerned with teaching undergraduates." There is a country song that talks about "being country before country was cool." Well, Ricks was focused upon effective teaching before the politically correct thing to do was demonstrate concern for undergraduates.

Is that the trend right now?

Legislatures and other constituencies are demanding an increased level of performance and accountability. As you look to the future of education, I think that more and more emphasis will be placed on how people learn and not just on how they should be taught. I think the future of how learning will occur suggests there will be tremendous change in this business of education. I think the concept of continuous learning will be more and more important. A student comes to school to learn how to learn. The pace of change is going to accelerate. It's going to move ever more rapidly.

What are your feelings on being here at Ricks and your feelings about the students?

I had never been to Rexburg, Idaho. I had never set foot on the Ricks campus until the day I was introduced as the new president. That is an unusual experience. I would say that it only takes a few minutes to fall in love with this place. I love the challenge of this job. I love the significance of what we do at Ricks College. It is thrilling and humbling to be a part of what is here. I love the people at Ricks. I have already talked about their dedication and commitment, and that is just as sincere as I know how to say it. These are wonderful people to work with.

For the most part, the students here are excited to learn. They are very eager, very fresh. Their vitality is fun to be around. I love to walk across the campus because I get a hundred hellos from smiling students. The people of Rexburg have been equally warm in welcoming our family into the community. I would summarize by saying that I love this place and this job. It is fun!

How has it changed your lifestyle?

I would say that I am a homebody. I enjoy my work and what I do. And I enjoy being able to go home and spend time with Susan and with our son who is still at home. Now there are a great many more demands on my time to represent the institution. There are many more social occasions and banquets that I need to attend. I try to make as many as I can, but I am not going to shortchange my son and my wife. There is a balance I have to achieve. My priorities are the same. There are just more functions that compete for my time and attention.

Do you have to get up earlier and go to bed later?

No. I have always been an early riser. The hours are about the same.

What time do you get up?

About 5:20. I exercise at home, study the scriptures, eat a quick breakfast and then go to my office. I try to work through lunch so I get home at a reasonable time.

Are you getting adjusted to the cold weather?

Everyone here tried to scare us with war stories about how cold it is in Rexburg, but this has not been a bad winter. In fact, this is not terribly different from what we experienced in Arkansas.

When were you called to be an Area Authority Seventy?

Let me back up just a little bit in order to answer your question. I was called as a Regional Representative in 1994.

In what area?

At that time I was the Regional Representative for the Springfield Missouri and Tulsa Oklahoma Regions. At the April conference in 1995, it was announced that Regional Representatives were going to be released and Area Authorities would be called. I was called as an Area Authority in the summer of 1995. In April of 1997 the Area Authorities became members of the Third, Fourth, and Fifth Quorums of the Seventy. Technically, the answer to your question is I was ordained a seventy and sustained as a member of the Fifth Quorum of the Seventy in April of 1997.

Are you still a member of the Fifth Quorum?

Yes. In the last four weekends, for example, I have had three stake conference assignments.

Where?

I was assigned to San Jose, California; Santa Clarita, California; and Brigham City, Utah.

Is that going to be pretty standard?

Yes, as long as I continue to serve in the Quorum of the Seventy, I expect to have one or two stake conference assignments a month.
