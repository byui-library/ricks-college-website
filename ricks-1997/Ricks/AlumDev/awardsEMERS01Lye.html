Distinguished Emeritus Service Award 2001

William and Velda Lye
of Logan, Utah

William and Velda met as missionaries in the Western Canadian Mission. He was her district president. William served a 29-month mission. He was asked to extend due to a shortage of missionaries at the time.

Williams is from Kimberly, British Columbia

Velda is from Ammon, Idaho

William attended Ricks for two quarters in 1953 following his mission.

He had a tuition scholarship and owned a portable typewriter, he says.

"The faculty admitted me as a freshman based on my score on the entrance exam though Canadian universities rejected me because I lacked matriculation."

"The excellent teaching and personal interest of caring faculty at Ricks motivated me to persist and return when finances forced me to drop out and return to Canada."

He came back to Ricks in 1956 for two more quarters.

He graduated from Utah State with a BS in history in 1949 and at the same time had completed work on his MA at U.C. Berkeley in Caifornia.

"When I earned my MA degree, President John L. Clarke (of Ricks) appointed me to the faculty though he had no openings (in 1959). By the time I arrived, he fit me into an open slot in my minor field, Political Science, and assigned me to also teach history, religion, English and geography, appointed me coach of the debate team and moderator of a weekly radio program. As a faculty member I taught 25 different courses, including upper division work at the BYU/Ricks Center--all of it an incredible preparation for my return to graduate school." He left in 1963 but returned for one more year of teaching at Ricks in 1967 to 1968.

Velda took art classes at Ricks while her husband was teaching at Ricks.

William began work on his Ph.D. At Duke University, and completed it in 1969 at UCLA in African Pre-History and Ethnography (study of races).

He taught at Utah State from 1968 until retirement in 1995 and is now an emeritus faculty member there.
