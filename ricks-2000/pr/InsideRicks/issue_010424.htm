NEW POSITION CREATED FOR ASSISTANT ACADEMIC VICE PRESIDENT FOR TECHNOLOGY

An announcement was made Wednesday, April 18, to the employees of Information Technology that after an extensive interview process and discussion by the President's Council, Garth Miller has been selected to be the assistant academic vice president over technology. This new position at Ricks College will oversee the institutional growth in using new advances in technology for education.

Miller transferred from the Engineering & Technology Division two years ago to serve as the project manager for Information Technology. He finished his bachelor's degree at BYU and his master's degree at Georgia Institute of Technology in mechanical engineering. He has worked as an analyst and in other computer-related assignments. He taught at other two- and four-year institutions of higher education prior to coming to Ricks College.

DISTINGUISHED EMERITUS SERVICE AWARDS GIVEN INCONJUNCTION WITH GRADUATION

The semi-annual Ricks College Emeritus Breakfast will be held in conjunction with graduation activities Saturday, April 28, in the Manwaring Center Special Events Room. Alumni who attended Ricks during or before 1951 and retired Ricks employees are invited, says Loren Grover, Emeritus Club president. The cost is $5.25 and tickets are available from the Ricks College Ticket Office at (208) 356-2230.

Distinguished emeritus service awards will be presented by the Ricks College Alumni Association to William F. and Velda Campbell Lye of Logan, Utah, and Richard C. and Barbara Hegsted Rasmussen of Battle Creek, Mich.

The Rasmussens met at Ricks College in the 1935 production of "The Mikado." Barbara Hegsted is from the Salem area. Richard is a native of the Rexburg area and graduated from Ricks in 1937. He received his bachelor's degree and a teaching certificate from the University of Utah and taught at Rigby High School for two years. Richard served for 40 years as a civil servant in the federal government with duties as director in adult education. He is a 30-year veteran of the U.S. Navy and Navy Reserves. Barbara was a homemaker and served in many church positions. They have been married for 63 years, raising three daughters and have eight grandchildren and nine great-grandchildren. The Rasmussens were named Honorary Mayors of Battle Creek, Mich., for one day, March 23 of this year. They have been married for 63 years and are in the process of moving from Battle Creek to the Orem, Utah, area. They have been lifelong supporters of Ricks College.

William Lye, a native of British Columbia, attended Ricks in 1953 and later in 1956. He graduated from Utah State University in history in 1959 and later earned a master's degree at the University of California at Berkeley. "When I earned my M.A. degree, President John L. Clarke (of Ricks) appointed me to the faculty . . . and assigned me to also teach history, religion, English and geography, appointed me coach of the debate team and moderator of a weekly radio program. As a faculty member I taught 25 different courses, including upper division work at the BYU/Ricks Center - all of it an incredible preparation for my return to graduate school." He left in 1963 but returned to Ricks to teach one more year 1967 to 1968. He did graduate studies at Duke University and received his Ph.D. in 1969 at UCLA in African history and ethnography. He taught at Utah State from 1968 until retirement in 1995 and is now an emeritus faculty member there. He provided humanitarian service to the people of Africa in Sudan and Lesotho and served as chairman of the Cache Valley chapter of Habitat for Humanity. Velda Lye, originally from Ammon, has been a homemaker, church worker, had an interest in genealogy and served with the Daughters of Utah Pioneers. They have been married for 47 years and have five living children and 10 grandchildren.

GROUP PRESENT PRE-TOUR PERFORMANCES AND PREPARE TO EXPAND THE INFLUENCE

The real impact of education is measured by performances both on and off the stage of life once one leaves the classroom. Ricks performance groups will be expanding the influence to a wide variety of audiences this spring. Groups also showcase their shows on campus prior to tour. Wherever the stage, Ricks College students are ambassadors of goodwill as they share their talents, enthusiasm, and example.

During the first two weeks of May, hundreds of public school students ranging in age from kindergarten through high school will be visiting the campus in enthusiastic response to an invitation to see the Folklore Revival. The lively show will be presented under the direction of Omar Hansen of the Theatre Department.

The Ricks College American Folk Dancers and Bluegrass Band will perform a graduation show on Friday, April 27, at 8 p.m. in the Kirkham Auditorium. Dances and music depict entertainment enjoyed throughout the history of the United States. Tickets are $6 for the general public and available from the Ricks College Ticket Office at 356-2230. The group has been invited to perform in folk festivals in Poland and Hungary with additional performance in Austria from July 21-August 22. The team is directed by Charles West, chairman of the Dance Department. Eileen Wilcox is the advisor for the Bluegrass Band.

Vocal Union and Sound Alliance will perform a spirited tribute to America, "This Land, the Music and the Promise," Saturday, April 28, in the Snow Drama Theatre at 7:30 p.m. before heading off on a two-week tour through the upper Midwest. Tickets are $3 for the public. Vocal Union, with its 14 performers, is new to the Music Department this year and is headed by Kevin Brower and assisted by his wife Nori. Sound Alliance, directed by Wilson Brown and Mark Watkins, is a 20-piece jazz band. The Vocal Union and Sound Alliance will tour from April 30 to May 13 in Montana, North Dakota, Minnesota, Wisconsin, Iowa, South Dakota and Wyoming.

The Sacred Music Series will give one last presentation of the new work written by composer Daniel E. Gawthrop as part of graduation activities. The Passion and The Promise of Our Lord Jesus Christ was commissioned by the college as part of its biennial Sacred Music Series. The work will be performed in the Hart Auditorium Friday, April 27, at 8 p.m. Tickets are complimentary and available from the Ricks College Ticket Office at 356-2230. The work will feature soloists Kristine Ciesinski and Norman Bailey, both of Victor, along with Michael Belnap of Bloomington, Ind. They will be joined by the Ricks Collegiate Singers, Men's Choir, Women's Choir and Symphony Orchestra.

Extravadance will be dancing their way through the southwestern United States and into Mexico during their annual spring tour April 30 to May 21. The dancers will make stops in various cities in Mexico, Utah, Arizona, Texas and Colorado, says dance director Shawn Fisher. The Extravadance production includes dances ranging from contemporary and hip-hop to a graceful waltz and sizzling cha-cha. The group is made up of dancers from the Contemporary Dance Theatre, directed by instructor Wendy Bone, and Fisher's Ballroom Dance Team.

Specific information is on the web at www.byui.edu/pr/performinggroups.
