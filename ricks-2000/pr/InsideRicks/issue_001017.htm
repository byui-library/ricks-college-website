HOMECOMING SYMBOLIZED BY MANY LIGHTS

"It was a night of lights," wrote Taralyn Trost in Tuesday's campus newspaper, the Scroll. "Light that rekindled past tradition and illuminated the pathway to a new century. Once known as the college on the hill, Ricks College now enhances its vision worldwide."

A highlight of Homecoming and Spori festivities was the placement of a large R and A (standing for Ricks Academy) on the top of the Spori Building last Thursday. The letters were made by welding students and faculty members and put up by college employees. The letters, which are lit at night, are replicas of similar letters that were donated by the academy classes of 1915 and 1916.

There were many other lights as well, including several thousand glow sticks that were passed out to the audience of Friday night's Hallelujah Homecoming concert. The blue and white sticks were symbolic of the individual lights within each of us. And there were the lights of 100 torches that were carried through campus by the Ricks Men's Association. The men formed a circle in front of the Spori Building to sing "Called to Serve." With a full moon shining through the cloudy night, fireworks concluded Friday night's festivities.

Thousands of students and alumni participated in the weekend events that also included a successful football game against Scottsdale Community College (39-14), a parade through Rexburg, a historic photograph in front of the Spori Building with as many as 2,000 people showing up, free potatoes for everyone compliments of the Rexburg Chamber of Commerce, and an alumni concert featuring the nostalgic music of Reunion.


EMERITUS CLUB BESTOWS HONORS

Edna Stowell Taylor of Rexburg and Bill and Verna Egbert of Ogden, Utah, were honored with Distinguished Emeritus Service Awards Saturday at the semi-annual breakfast meeting of the Ricks College Emeritus Club. Mrs. Taylor attended Ricks from 1931 to 1933 majoring in education. She is the granddaughter of school's first principal, Jacob Spori. Bill Egbert, who retired in 1993 as an obstetrician and gynecologist practicing in the Ogden area, attended Ricks in 1946-47 and again in 1949-50 following a mission to the Southern States. He was raised in Lincoln, near Idaho Falls, and married Verna McDonald, a native of Ammon, Idaho.

Emeritus Club membership is open to anyone who attended Ricks College at least 50 years ago. The award is given to individuals or couples who have led exemplary lives in the LDS Church, raised a worthy family, and who have served as good examples to others.
