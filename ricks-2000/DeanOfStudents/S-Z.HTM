Student Employment and Career Placement Center
The Student Employment and Career Placement Center, located in Kimball 244, is open from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., Monday through Friday. Students may search for current job listings online at www.byui.edu/employment.htm.

1. Student Employment
Students who are seeking employment are assisted in locating both on-campus and off-campus positions (normally part-time). Candidates are referred for on-campus jobs on the following basis: 1) first-come, first-served, 2) job skills and qualifications, 3) availability to work, 4) existing job openings. A variety of job openings will occur during the calendar year. In the fall and winter seasons, on-campus and off-campus jobs are extremely limited. Students who are available during a 2-4 hour block of time between 8:00 a.m. and 2:00 p.m. daily, and are willing to accept any type of job, and work the hours required by the job, will enhance their chances of employment both on or off-campus. The best opportunity for student employment on-campus, however, is to begin working during one of the summer terms. Students must be taking at least one credit hour to be eligible for work during the summer terms.

2. Career Placement
Students or alumni who are looking for full-time careers after graduation or are looking for internships, cooperative work, or summer work related to their careers, will be assisted in our office. Students are assisted in the following services by the placement center personnel: effective resume and letter writing, proper interviewing techniques, job searching skills (including networking, Internet, job fairs, employment centers, and personal searches), career researching, and effective follow-through. Critical analysis of resumes and letters, and career counseling are also provided by our trained personnel. Call ext. 2170 to make an appointment. It is highly recommended that students begin their job search in the fall or as early as possible. See www.byui.edu/PlacementCenter/default.htm.

3. Employment for International Students
International students are not allowed to work off campus without a work permit. On campus employment is allowed without such a work permit as long as the campus employer can certify that employing the international student does not displace a U.S. citizen or permanent resident alien student on the job.

Student Review Procedure
Students should assume responsibility for identifying and dealing with the appropriate university office in all matters pertaining to their educational experience. When problems are not satisfactorily resolved at those operational levels, or when the application of regular policies causes an undue hardship because of extraordinary personal circumstances, students may request a review for relief in the following manner:

1. Academic Regulations
(Withdrawals, add-drops, credits, probation, suspension, etc.). Check first with the Registrar's Office and if the problem remains, students may file a formal petition to the Exception to the Rules Committee, stating their personal situation and reasons for an exception. Petition forms may be obtained from the Registrar's Office, Kimball 190.

2. Grading Disputes
Students should first confer with their instructor. If the explanation is not acceptable and the dispute remains, students may request a review by the department chairman. Cases still unresolved should next be referred to the division Dean and finally to the Assistant Academic Vice President, Kimball 290.

3. Behavioral Matters
(Disciplinary sanctions, probations, suspension, etc.). Students should follow the review procedure outlined in the Honor Code procedures listed in the Standards section of this handbook or contact the Dean of Students Office, Kimball 230, for assistance.

4. Policies Concerning Housing Units
Housing (contracts, deposits, refunds, fines, etc.). Students should work directly with the apartment managers and Housing Office, Kimball 196. If not satisfactorily settled, students should appeal to the assistant vice president of Student Life, Manwaring Center 207.

5. Financial Services
(Fees, refunds, loans, dishonored checks, etc.). Contact the director of Financial Services, Kimball 290, for assistance. If, because of extraordinary circumstances, the problem persists, students may appeal to the Dean of Students, Kimball 230.

6. Other
Miscellaneous problems occurring outside the categories listed above may be taken to the Dean of Students for direction and assistance on how to proceed.

Study Skills
The Study Skills Center, located in McKay Library 266, is available to students at no charge. Assistance is available to students in the following study skills areas: concentration and memorization, listening and note taking, thinking skills, textbook study methods, time management, test taking, and studying chemistry. The program offers three options: (1) GS-102--a 1 credit block class. (2) GS-103 A-J: one-half credit self-directed modules offered in each of the areas listed above. Students may select skills of interest, then work through a series of activities including readings, films, audio tapes, and computer exercises. Lab assistants help students verify that they understand the material through open-ended questions and checking their worksheets. (3) Labs--non-credit. Students are encouraged to take advantage of free individual study skills counseling available in the center.

Telephone Service
Telephone service for students residing in on-campus housing is provided by BYU-Idaho's privately owned telephone system. Each dorm unit is provided a telephone with local dial tone access. A BYU-Idaho CALLING CARD gives long distance access from dorm rooms. This Card may be obtained by signing up at the Cashiers Office, and paying a $5 non-refundable application fee. The Calling Card can be picked up in the Accounting Office the next working day. A BYU-Idaho Calling Card is an extension of credit by the university to the card holder. The calling card is to be used ONLY by the person to whom it is issued, all charges made against a card are the legal responsibility of that person. All BYU-Idaho Calling Cards are the property of BYU-Idaho and will be revoked for fraudulent or abusive use. BYU-Idaho Calling Cards are not available to students living off-campus. Students residing in off-campus housing will need to arrange for telephone service through their respective housing unit and through a private long distance carrier.

Other calling cards (i.e., AT&T, Sprint, MCI) can be used at a pay phone. Overseas international calling is not available on the BYU-Idaho system; however, calls can be placed to Canada, Alaska, or Hawaii.

Any questions concerning the BYU-Idaho Telephone system can be directed to: BYU-Idaho Telephone Office, Physical Plant 129, ext. 2011.

1. Phone Mail
Phone Mail is BYU-Idaho's ANSWERING SERVICE. When a person is not available to answer the number you have dialed, Phone Mail will answer it. Students should leave their name, telephone number, and message at the sound of the tone.

2. Call Processing
BYU-Idaho has set up Call Processing to provide, better, more efficient telephone service. To use a Call Processing box: 1) Listen to the message in its entirety. 2) Press the appropriate button on your touch tone pad to transfer to the desired extension.

3. 800/888/ Numbers And Long Distance Dialing
To access 800 and 888 numbers from on-campus students should dial 8 + 1 + 800 or 888 + PREFIX + SUFFIX. (Example: 8 + 1 + 800 + xxx + xxxx).

All Long Distance calls placed from a campus phone are made by dialing 8 + 1 + AREA CODE + PREFIX + SUFFIX, + BYU-Idaho Long Distance Calling Card number. (Example: 8 + 1 + xxx + xxx + xxxx + xxxxxxxx).

Local off campus calls are made by dialing 8 + PREFIX + SUFFIX. (Example: 8 + xxx + xxxx).

Television, Video Tapes, Music, Posters and Internet
In keeping with the ideals of BYU-Idaho and the spirit of the Honor Code, "R," "NC-17" and "X" rated viewing is not allowed. In addition, posters, internet access, music, television, or "any form of entertainment, including inappropriate or suggestive dancing, concerts, movies, and videocassettes, which are vulgar, immoral, inappropriate, suggestive, or pornographic in any way," no matter what the rating, violates the spirit of the Honor Code and is not acceptable (see For the Strength of Youth pamphlet, page 11). Off-Campus apartment units which provide cable or satellite television service to their tenants are expected, as a condition of their BYU-Idaho approval, to exercise their option as subscribers and not provide any channels or services which bring "R" "NC-17", or "X" rated viewing into the homes of BYU-Idaho students. Most premium movie channels are considered inappropriate. Those which often come with basic service and must be filtered out currently include MTV and VH-1. Special events, such as major sports contests, which do not violate the Honor Code, may be procured through pay-per-view channels after the cable or satellite distributor clears that event in advance with the Dean of Students Office.

The expectation of students who subscribe to cable service or internet on their own is the same. Students in all housing units are expected to use filtered internet access services. Students are expected to refrain from subscribing to or viewing any media which compromise their commitment to the BYU-Idaho Honor Code.

Test Week
This is a period of time set apart at the end of each semester for taking final exams. The day and time of the exam are dependent on the day and the time the class meets. This schedule can be found on the back page of your class schedule. Curfew hours are in effect during finals or test week even though some individuals may have completed their exams.

All finals given in the testing center are arranged by the instructor. Students should check with their instructors for exact place, dates and closing time of tests.

Testing Center
The Testing Center, located in the basement of the McKay Library, provides a centralized location where examinations are given. Hours are from 8 a.m. to 10 p.m. on weekdays, except during devotionals, and from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. on Saturdays. Tests will not be given out past 9 p.m. on weekdays and 3 p.m. on Saturdays. The Test Center is closed for holidays and when classes end. For example, if classes end at 12 noon on a particular day then the last test will go out at 11 a.m. and the Center will close at 12 noon. Closing hours for the last day of test week is 6 p.m, with the last test given at 5 p.m.

There is an auxiliary test room in the Taylor Religion building, room 130, for all religion and humanities tests, that opens at 3 p.m. and is open until 9:45 p.m. with the last test given at 8:45 p.m. Monday through Friday.

Closing dates and times are computer controlled and the computer is unforgiving when a student is late. Testing Services student assistants cannot override dates or times scheduled by the faculty and they cannot be held responsible when students come late and the test cannot be given to them. Students must work those problems out with their teacher, not Testing Services.

A student ID and a pencil is required in order to take a test. BYU-Idaho dress standards and Honor Code are enforced.

Ticket Office
The Ticket Office, located on the first floor of the Manwaring Center across from the Viking Place, is open from 9:30 a.m. to 3 p.m. Monday through Friday except on Tuesdays when it closes at 2 p.m. for devotionals. Ticket windows in the building where an event is being held will usually open one hour prior to the event. Tickets can be ordered on-line at www.byui.edu/tickets.

Full time BYU-Idaho students have a variety of athletic and entertainment opportunities available during the school year. Tickets to athletic events are free on a first-come, first-served basis with a valid student body ID card. Some musical events will be free of charge (no tickets needed) throughout the school year. All other events cost $1 to students. Occasionally, there will be an additional popular attraction added to the schedule. These attractions may not be included in the regular entertainment package, and an admission fee will be charged.

It is important to remember that a student ID card alone does not guarantee a ticket to an event. Seating is limited, and tickets need to be acquired early for each event. To ensure that seats are fully utilized, at most events any seat not occupied ten minutes prior to the scheduled start of the event will be made available to those with standby tickets except any event in the Hart Auditorium. Tickets are available to students two days prior to the general public except for athletic events.

Writing Center
Located on the second floor (East Wing) of the Library, the Writing Center is a free service for students who would like extra help with their writing process. Open 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., Monday through Friday. The Center employs peer assistants who teach students a revision process, which helps them write effective papers. Writing Assistants work with students on a one -on-one basis. These personal sessions focus on developing rewriting and critical thinking skills to help students formulate, develop, and refine their own ideas. In this way the Center is committed to helping students learn how to write and think more effectively for college and for life.
