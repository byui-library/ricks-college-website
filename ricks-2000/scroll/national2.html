Eyewitnesses react to attacks

by Chad Phares
pha98001@byui.edu
Religion and Special Sections Editor


Brian Pennington didn't get the job he interviewed for at the World Trade Center 3 months ago.

He did, however, get to keep his life.

"I stopped to think that all those people I met there in my interview and in the building, chances are some of them are dead. It's very strange," Pennington said.

Pennington, a Brooklyn resident, saw the fire and chaos caused by 2 American Airline commercial flights gone awry in the New York City skyline firsthand.

"I was shocked," Pennington said of the terrorist act, "afraid for my friends who might have been somewhere they could be hurt, and my heart went out to those people in the building and in the planes, and the family and friends of those people."

Pennington said that even after the attack, the fear still remains.

"The city could erupt in chaos, looting, etc. I'm afraid for my friends who can't get back to their homes in Brooklyn and every time a plane goes overhead and I can hear it, I get very worried, because you never know what might happen now."

This same fear echoes 237 miles to the south in Washington D.C.

Shannon Zirbel, a freshman at Georgetown University in Washington D.C. watched the fire burn at the Pentagon from a friend's apartment complex.

"It was very surreal that something like that actually happened and it was terrifying," Zirbel said.

"I don't know what's going to happen next," Zirbel said.

"There's a lot of uncertainty."
