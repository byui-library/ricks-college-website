Ricks College News Release, October 19, 1998

Chinese Lily blossoms at Ricks

By Emily Wareburton
Ricks College News Bureau

REXBURG, Idaho -- First introduced to Ricks College faculty members as their tour guide, Chinese native Su Li is now settled in her job as the Rexburg college as this year's visiting faculty member.

Su Li, or Lily as she wishes to be called, arrived from China in August. Today she is teaching Chinese 201-Integrated Chinese thanks to a special Ricks endowment fund set up to support a visiting professor.

Ricks Academic Vice President Don Bird recalls, "A group of us went to China and were visiting the famous Terra Cotta warrior site in the city of Xian. Su Li was our guide and provided the history and cultural background. She could speak English well and really related to us so we invited her to come teach Chinese for a year."

Lily explains, "I love to work here for first, I love the students, and second, I love teaching. All my students work hard, although it is hard. It is especially hard to write Chinese, but in the last month and a half, my students have improved greatly."

She also attends Ricks as a student. She is taking Modern Literature of the Non-Western World and American Heritage. "But it is more important for me to be a good teacher, so sometimes my homework isn't done," she says.
Lily brought her 3-year-old son, Chen Chen, to Rexburg. He attends day care while Lily is at Ricks. "At first he didn't like it, but now that he knows a little of the language, he has a lot of little guys to play together for fun," Lily says. "He also loves American food."

Lily's says her husband, Li Shijie, is trying to visit before Christmas. "It depends on if [the government officials] are in a good mood if the petition will be passed. Otherwise, the visit is in trouble."

She enjoys living in Rexburg. "It is a quiet, safe and peaceful city. People are comparatively more friendly then what I know of the rest of the country. They like to say hello," she says.

In comparing China to Rexburg, Lily says the emphasis on the importance of families is the same. "But there are many more children. I think that is the influence of the Mormon religion," she says.

Lily's goal while in Rexburg is to try her best. "I try my best to attend my classes, and try my best as a teacher. My major goal is to teach my students the general Chinese language, giving them the capacity of writing freely, talking fluently, and reading more then just the Book of Mormon in Chinese," she says.
