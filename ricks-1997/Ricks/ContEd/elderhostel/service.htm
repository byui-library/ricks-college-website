SEPTEMBER 17-23, 2000 - $468
 
ORGANIZATION DESCRIPTION:
Founded in 1888, Ricks College is the nation’s largest private two-year college and lies just northeast of Idaho Falls in the historic Snake River Valley. Rexburg, 85 miles south of West Yellowstone, is surrounded by lush grain fields and acres of potatoes. Hostelers will stay in West Yellowstone which is perfectly situated next to Yellowstone National Park offering the ideal Elderhostel location. Encompassed by the scenic Rocky Mountain wilderness, this small alpine community is nestled serenely in the pines at an elevation of 6,600 feet. Our accommodations are minutes from field study locations for courses and wildlife viewing. As the World’s first national park, millions of people flock to Yellowstone each year. Many spend time visiting the geological wonders of geysers (including Old Faithful), hot springs, mud pots, and fumaroles as Yellowstone is the most densely concentrated area of thermal features in the world. Visitors can also enjoy the abundant wildlife and beautiful scenery including the Grand Canyon of Yellowstone with the famous Upper and Lower Falls. The Washburn Expedition of 1870 camped on the banks of the Madison River in the Park. According to records, that evening it was suggested that “the whole of it (Yellowstone) ought to be set aside as a great National Park.” It is at this historic site that volunteers will help rehabilitate the land and preserve this aspiration of Yellowstone born over 125 years ago. 

NEED:
Millions of tourists visit Yellowstone National Park each year. Each night, Madison campground has approximately 500-600 visitors. The impact on the resources has exceeded its capacity to absorb this use unscathed. The National Park Service is understaffed and cannot perform all the desired rehabilitation itself. The Park Service requests the help of unselfish volunteer groups to assist with preserving and protecting Yellowstone’s fragile natural resources. The need exists as well to educate individuals in resource management concerns and practices associated with National Parks. Currently, the campground is criss-crossed with “social” trails that increase the erosion process. To eliminate the erosion, these trails will need to be rehabilitated and trees and shrubs planted. 

WORK PROJECT:
Under direct guidance of the park rangers, volunteers will work side by side to help rehabilitate the environment of the park. The work pace will be dependent on each Hosteler’s abilities, skills, and interests. Our goal is for each participant to have a meaningful work, as well as learning, experience. Service will include planting trees, shrubs, flowers and so forth. Trees for replanting will be harvested from new growth stands that were reseeded naturally after the 1988 forest fires. Currently, these stands are very dense. The harvesting will occur before the natural die-off of trees that is associated with the dominance struggle inherent in these new growth stands. There may also be the opportunity to build water bars and check dams to help curb erosion. Other tasks may include written documentation that will need to be recorded to aid in interesting history associated with camping. The existing campground is located on an archeological site of early Native Americans. Any artifacts located will be documented according to the park’s archeological survey policy. Only hand tools (such as shovels, rakes, hoes, etc.) will be used. All tools will be provided. 

EDUCATIONAL OPPORTUNITIES:
Volunteers will be working in a learning environment. Each project will have hands-on learning while working directly with the rangers. There will also be educational presentations given by Ranger Naturalists and instructors dealing with the early history of the park, geology, ecology, and other subjects. There will be a daily review as the work progresses to discuss the benefits that are being accomplished. To enhance the learning process, field trips will also be taken to some of the geological and historic features in the Park. 

ACCOMMODATIONS:
Lodging will be in a comfortable motel with double occupancy, private bath, limited singles for extra charge. Meals will be at the motel’s restaurant with sack lunches provided on field days. Transportation will be provided to and from the work site. We will be working at an elevation of 6,800 feet with toilets a short distance from the actual working area. 

FREE TIME OPPORTUNITIES:
West Yellowstone has much to offer any traveler: a museum, art galleries, stores, Imax Theater, and Grizzly Park. Surrounding ranches offer horseback riding and evening cookouts. Guided fishing trips, hiking, canoeing, bird watching, wildlife photography, and visitors centers are located throughout the park area.
