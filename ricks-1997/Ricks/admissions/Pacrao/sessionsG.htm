Session G2

Prerequisite Checking and Cancellation at the University of Washington

Facilitator:
Cathy Lindstrom-Jacobsen, UCLA

Recorder Name:
Frank M. Byrdwell, Jr., University of Washington

Presenters:
Van Johnson, University of Washington
Robert Jansson, University of Washington
Matthew Winslow, University of Washington

In the past years students have been able to register for classes even though they had not completed the prerequisites for a class.  Students would later find out during the quarter that they were unable to complete the required work.  They had filled class spaces and it was too late for other students to register for courses they needed and were more qualified to take.  A mandate came from the Faculty Senate to develop a program for better use of our academic resources.  Thus, development began for prerequisite checking and cancellation program at the University of Washington.  Discussion for this program began June 1997 and was up and running May 7, 1998 for Autumn Quarter 1998.

The program is a "real" time checking system interactive with the database.  Dedicated staff in departments and programs worked very hard.  Information went out to all campus community via e-mail and campus newspaper.  Prerequisites are listed in the general catalog.  They had been listed but were rarely enforced.

Changes had to be made in the University Drop Policy - one annual drop is allowed from autumn through summer quarter for a 2-week period.  Definition of a prerequisite had to be given by departments.  Specific courses had to be set up.  A minimum grade had to be set up by departments.

Prerequisites are not used for post-baccalaureate, non-matriculating, graduate, or professional students.  When canceled from a course - a student receives an e-mail message from the Registrar's Office.  Registration is all processed by touch-tone phone registration.  Prerequisites are given by phone.  WEB registration is in the development stage.  Departments can over-ride prerequisites for registration.  Prerequisites can be viewed on the Web.

There are some positive benefits: much needed review of prerequisites is being done.  Better communication between departments, fewer entry codes are used.  There is now better communication to students.  We are happy with the system thus far.  We want to continue our development.

Session G4

Piloting the Titanic or How to Survive Enrollment Management

Facilitator:
Nora McLaughlin, Reed College

Recorder Name:
Emilie Smith, Reed College

Presenter:
Richard L Backer, University of California, San Diego

This session dealt with the history of the registrar, from its inception to the universities of medieval Europe to it's role and function in present day institutions.

The audience gained a good understanding of how the role and function of the registrar has been modified over the last 400 years to fit the needs of the time.  The session ended with an overview of the skills a modern registrar needs to meet the current adaptation of this position.

Session G6

Registration on the Web

Facilitator:
Barbra Poe, Rio Salado Community College

Recorder Name:
Kathy McBride, Arizona State University

Presenters:
Barbara Balz, Oregon State University
Heather Chermak, Oregon State University

The presenters described how their university, Oregon State University, implemented web registration in 1998 in order to expand and improve their registration service for OSU's students.  The presenters described the process in detail and provided a blueprint for others interested in pursuing web registration.

The first step involved formation of the implementation team with identification of the following members: registrar, assistant registrar, registration/records supervisor, records clerk, and a computing center analyst/web programmer.  A time line was established, beginning in October 1997 and ending in May 1998, with target dates for product review and modifications, design test, registration pilot, training, and campus roll out.

Currently, OSU has all new students register via the Web and has experienced campus-wide utilization resulting in 79% web registration and 21% telephone registration.  The project has been an unqualified success.  The presenters closed the session with a display of all the screens used in their web registration process, followed by a lengthy question and answer period.

Session G7

Do You Need a Bullet Proof Vest?

Recorder Name:
Sunny Burns, Spokane Community College

Presenters:
Robert Smith, Ricks College

Rob opened with a story about on experience in the Vietnam to illustrate the importance of wearing a bulletproof vest.  He had come down a jungle road with a group of soldiers prepared for combat: wearing helmets, bulletproof vests, and carrying weapons.  They turned off the road briefly - and following them as a jeep full of unarmed cooks and workers who were not wearing protective gear.  The jeep was attacked and casualties sustained.  The enemies, Rob felt, were not comfortable in engaging fully prepared soldiers, but would attack persons who appeared to be unprepared.

Rob used various activities, magic tricks, and card tricks to show the importance of PACRAO professionals "weaving" their own bullet -proof vest in our various work places.

  1. Be professional

  • Correctness and precision in the use of English

  • Refined and gentle manners which are the result of fixed thought and actions

  • Sound standards of appreciation of beauty and of worth and character based on those standards

  • Power of habit and reflection

  • Efficiency or the power to "do"

  1. Develop Human Skills

  • Lower level mangers need greater technical skills, small percentage of conceptual or global views.

  • Middle manager needs smaller percentage of technical skills, larger global views.

  • Top management personnel need the largest conceptual view, smallest technical need.  However, all three levels need the largest human relation skills

  1. Develop "service" attitude.  Treat students with respect.  "If you're happy with your lot in life, build a service station on it."  
  2. "Begin with the end in sight."  Understand the whole situation before making decisions.  Be willing to share all information with students so you can be partners in decisionmaking.
  3. Continued growth in:  Physical, social, spiritual, emotional, mental, family, financial and professional.
