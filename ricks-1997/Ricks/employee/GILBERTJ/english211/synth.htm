Synthesis vs. Regurgitation

Blending source material and outside information into your essay is a great deal like cooking. As a cook or writer, you can regurgitate the information you find (not very appetizing at all), or you can synthesize it into your own tasteful, flavorful creation. What is the difference? The need for a cook. Let's look at both.

Regurgitation - The source ingredients (information) are given back to the reader in the same condition they were received. Like a table full of ingredients, the information remains separate. No connections exist between sources. And, like the separate ingredients that go into a single recipe, they are usually not very tasty by themselves. This type of writing does not require a cook.

Synthesis - The source ingredients are gathered and mixed by the cook to create something beyond themselves. No longer separate bits of information, sources become connected and integrated so you can't separate them anymore. They become part of a greater whole, a marvelous new dish that has a unique and inviting flavor all its own. This type of writing requires a cook.

As a writer, it is your responsibility to take your outside sources and find the connections--the significance or point in the information you find and present it to your readers. You are the cook. Sources (ingredients) don't make a satisfying or even edible essay. It's what the cook does with the ingredients. That's the difference--that's the synthesis.
