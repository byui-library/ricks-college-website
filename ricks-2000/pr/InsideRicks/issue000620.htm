RICKS LOOKING FOR STORIES, PHOTOS, ARTIFACTS OF SPORI BUILDING

Ricks College officials are collecting personal stories, photographs and artifacts pertaining to the historic Jacob Spori Building. The stories and photographs will be used in a number of projects that college officials are preparing to commemorate the legacy of the historic building that was built in the early 1900s. The building is scheduled to be replaced with a new structure in the near future. "We are looking for memories that involve the building," says Steve Moser, assistant director of Public Relations. "We would prefer written remembrances. However, if you are reluctant to commit your memories to paper, we'd like to interview you in person." Stories may be mailed to Moser at Ricks College, Rexburg, ID 83460-1660 or e-mailed to him at mosers@ricks.edu.

Moser is also interested in photographs that are at least 20 years old and of good photo quality. While many photographs exist of the exterior of the building, Moser says interior shots of classrooms and people are needed. He is particularly interested in a photograph of the old staircase. Photographs will be copied and returned, or may be donated to the college archives collection. Memorabilia and artifacts pertaining to the building are also being collected for a future museum display. Such items also may be donated or loaned to the college. For more information, contact Moser at (208) 356-1153.

SCHOLARSHIPS GIVE EDUCATIONAL OPPORTUNITIES

Ricks College has awarded 1,650 scholarships as Awards of Excellence to the incoming freshman for fall semester. They are based on a combination of academics, leadership, service, church activity, seminary activity, extra-curricular activities, and essays. These awards range from full tuition plus $500 for two years to quarter tuition for one year.

Additional contributions from private donors greatly help expand the doors of educational opportunity for deserving and needy students. The support is greatly appreciated by the recipients. Erin N. Berrett, a freshman from Salt Lake City, said, "Receiving this scholarship is truly an answer to my prayers. Your money will be put to good use. I think of this as just one more reason to work hard at school. I am so thankful for your willingness to contribute money to those of us who could use a little extra help." (For more students reflections, see http://www.byui.edu/Ricks/AlumDev/development.html.)

SPORTS CAMPS

Sports camps bring a younger generation to the summer Ricks College campus. These popular camps give boys and girls the chance to receive professional instruction by Ricks College coaching staff and team members. The youth strengthen their skills and make new friends during their week-long camp experience. The camps this year feature girls softball, boys junior football, boys basketball, girls volleyball, cross country, boys wrestling, and girls basketball. Over 800 youth have enrolled this summer coming primarily from Intermountain West, but some from as far away as New Jersey. Depending on the sport, the students enrolled range in age from grades 4-12. (For more details, see http://www.byui.edu/Ricks/ContEd/conted.htm)

DINNER, TALK, AND CONCERT SET FOR AN EVENING OF HISTORY

"Exploring the Oregon Trail" is the theme of a dinner, talk and concert set for Thursday, June 29, by the Ricks College Summer in the Snow Entertainment Series. The evening will begin at 6 p.m. with a western- style dinner in the Manwaring Center West Ballroom. At 7 p.m. a talk will be given by Idaho historian Virginia Ricketts of Jerome on the history of the Oregon Trail. She will be followed by Mountain Aire, a folk music duo that performs American and Celtic songs on fiddle, guitar, flute, bodhran and other instruments. Cost is $13 for dinner and show or $3 without the dinner. For more information or to order tickets, call 356-2230 or go online at www.byui.edu/tickets. (For a listing of Summer in the Snow Entertainment Series, see www.byui.edu/events.htm)
