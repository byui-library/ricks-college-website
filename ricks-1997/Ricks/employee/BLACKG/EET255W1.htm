It covers the implementation of finite state machine controllers, microprogrammed controllers, microprocessors, and microcontrollers. Students must complete a system controller project. Laboratory exercises are included.

Tests:

There will be 3 Take-Home Tests. Each one is open-book, open-notes, and open-library, but not open-friend! The Final Test is open-book only, so you may want to transfer important notes to your text book and data book before taking it. The Final Test score may replace your lowest Take-Home Test score.

 

Homework:

Homework is due at the beginning of the class period one week after being assigned. Late homework will not be accepted. Homework must be clearly labeled with your name, class number, chapter number, and problem numbers. It must be neatly printed on green engineering paper. Homework that is not properly labeled or is not on engineering paper will receive a reduced score.

Professional quality work is required. Only write on the front of engineering paper. Any work written on the back will not be graded. Staple multiple pages of the same assignment together. Dog-eared or loose pages will not be accepted. Do not staple different assignments together. Sloppy assignments will receive a reduced score.

 

Quizzes:

Quizzes are worth about 10 points each. They are not announced ahead of time and usually cover material assigned during the previous lecture or lab. There are no makeup quizzes.

Lab Experiments:

Read and follow the ALab Rules@ that are posted at each lab station. If you leave a lab station messy, your grade for that lab will be reduced.

All lab work must be recorded in a Lab Notebook, which has nonremovable pages that are sequentially numbered. All lab work records must be written in ink while the experiment is being performed -- not afterwards! Each page must be signed and dated.

In industry, Lab Notebooks are used for both technical and legal documentation. Four elements are required for your Lab Notebook to be acceptable court evidence during a legal dispute:

1. Notebook has numbered pages that are not removable.

2. Notes are written in ink at the time of the actual experiment.

3. Each page (or revision on a page) is signed.

4. Each page (or revision on a page) is dated.

Lab Notebooks will be turned in for grading at the end of the term and are worth 100 lab points. They will be graded on their completeness and whether they meet the above requirements -- not on neatness. Ten points will be deducted for each missing, or poorly documented lab.

A formal Lab Report, worth 50 points, must be turned in for each lab experiment. Use the information you recorded in your Lab Notebook. Lab Reports must be done on a computer. They are due 1 week after the lab is assigned. Late Lab Reports will not be accepted.

It is almost impossible to over estimate the importance of good technical writing. Unfortunately, there is no list of instructions that can guarantee fluent, lucid, and literate reports. A good format, however, can help organize the presentation and ensure that the most important information is included.

Each Lab Report must have 5 sections, with no more than one section on a page. This is the required format for Lab Reports:

1. Cover Sheet (2 points).

This is the first page and will contain:

Name and number of the lab.

Your name.

Course number.

2. Introduction (10 points).

This will have two parts (label them):

Purpose: What are the objectives of the lab?

Equipment: What equipment, supplies, and materials were used in the lab?

Procedure: What steps were taken to accomplish the stated objectives? Be complete. Give enough detail so that another student in the class could duplicate your effort without referring to the lab handout.

3. Schematics (5 points).

Some labs will require more than one.

4. Experiment Data (10 points).

This may have more than one part (label them):

Expected Results: These are the results you calculated or predicted.

Measured Results: These are the results or values you actually observed.

Code Listing: (If applicable)

5. Conclusion (15 points).

This section is very important. It ties all of the others together. Your responses show how much you have thought about the experiment. Explain the basic principles that were examined and show how your results correlate with the theory. Do not simply say, AEverything went smoothly, and I learned a lot.@

The following points should also be discussed:

How well did you achieve your objectives?

How accurate are the measurements?

What errors were encountered? How were they eliminated or minimized?

If your measurements are different from your calculations, why is that so?

What are some applications for these principles or circuit configurations?

What are the advantages and disadvantages of this circuit implementation compared to others you have used?

 

In addition to the scores listed for each section, 3 points are given for neatness and 5 points are given for professional quality language and spelling.

Project:

Completion of a microcontroller project is required. It is worth 10% of your total grade. You must construct, program, document, and demonstrate the project that uses an 8-bit PIC16C84 microcontroller. Additional instructions and specifications for this project will be given later in the course.

Proto-board construction is not acceptable. Use a permanent or semi-permanent construction method such as soldering on a printed circuit board, point-to-point soldering on a perforated board, or wire-wrapping using the appropriate sockets mounted to a perforated board. (You would be wise to use a socket for the PIC16C84!) Materials may be purchased locally, or through catalogs that will be made available to you. Be sure to order your parts early enough to complete the project. The college will not provide materials (except the PIC16C84 )for your project!

A written functional description must be submitted that describes the design steps, circuit operation, and program in sufficient detail to demonstrate your knowledge. It must be written at a college level and be free from spelling and grammar errors. Include schematic diagrams, code listings, and other appropriate information. The circuit must also be explained and demonstrated in a class presentation. You may work as a team with one other person on this project. It will be graded on the skill level demonstrated by your design, the quality of circuit construction, correct circuit operation, the comprehensiveness of the documentation and your presentation.
