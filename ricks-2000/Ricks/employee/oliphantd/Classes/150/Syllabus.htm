Course Description: A lab course designed to give students practical experience with modeling and analyzing the physical world around them.  Students should have already taken or be currently enrolled in Physics 121 and Calculus 112.

 

Course Objectives:  This is not a lecture course!  Most lecture courses are designed to give students a set of knowledge.  This is a lab course designed to give you some tools so that you can use the knowledge you learn in the lecture courses.  Some of the tools we will begin to develop include data analysis tools, and experimental techniques.  Tools under the category of data analysis include: Curve fitting, error analysis, numerical derivatives and integrals, and other mathematical modeling tools.  Experimental techniques we will look at may include: Equipment limitations, Minimizing contributing variables, Response functions.

 

Course Outline:

Week 0            Introduction

Week 1            Lab 1            Measurement, statistics, error analysis, intro to Excel

Week 2            Lab 2            Numerical Derivatives, intro to Projectile motion

Week 3            Lab 3            SciLab vs Excel

Week 4            Lab 4            Projectile motion with a vengeance!

Week 5            Lab 5            Thrust measurement of Rocket engine

Week 6            Lab 6            Rocket Launch and prediction

Week 7            Lab 7            Paper Due, Numerical integrals

Week 8            Lab 8            Speed of sound in air, by clapping and other

Week 9            Lab 9            response function of thermister

Week 10            Lab 10            Planetary motion, or other

Week 11            Lab 11            Student designed lab

Week 12            Lab 12            Student designed lab

Week 13            Lab 13 Present project

Course Requirements:  For labs 1-5 and 7-10 you will need to write a type report of what you have learned during the lab.  This report should not be longer than one page ,single spaced.  This report will be due at the beginning of the next lab.  In addition you need to keep a lab book.  This book should document what you do in each lab.  As well as insights and ideas that you have as you go through the lab.  This book will be turned in twice during the semester, at the beginning of lab #7 and at the end of the semester. There will also be two group papers one having to do with the rocket launch and prediction (4-5 pages) and the other about the student design lab (7-10 pages).  There will also be a small final.
