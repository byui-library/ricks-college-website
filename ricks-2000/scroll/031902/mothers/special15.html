Jerusalem, Palmyra rich in history

by Kelly Smurthwaite
SMU01001@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Several Orthodox Jews walk towards the Wailing, or Western Wall in Jerusalem. The wall is considered one of the holiest sites to the Jewish religion. It also shares space with the Dome of the Rock. arttoday.com
Jerusalem is one of the oldest civilizations on Earth. Many scholars claim it has one of the most fascinating histories.

Geographically, Jerusalem is between the Mediterranean and Red Sea.

Spiritually, Jerusalem has had many events recorded in the Bible.

For Muslims, Jerusalem is also a land of spiritually history. The Prophet Muhammad’s ascension in to heaven from the rock es-Sakhra is believed to have occurred in Jerusalem.

For Judaism, Jerusalem is a city that first unified their nation under the great ruler, King David.

For all Christians, Jerusalem is the scene of key events in the life of Jesus: his life, death and resurrection.

In 3 Nephi chapter 20, Jesus speaks of the day that the gospel will be restored to the Gentiles:

“I will remember the covenant which I have made with my people; and I have covenanted with them that I would gather them together in mine own due time, that I would give unto them again the land of their fathers for their inheritance, which is the land of Jerusalem, which is the promised land unto them forever, saith the Father.”

Jerusalem is also a place rich in culture.

Today, Jerusalem attracts many tourists and visitors from all over the world. One reason people visit Jerusalem is because of the deep religious background the city has. Another reason is because of the artistic aspect of Jerusalem’s art museums, the city’s architecture and historical collections.

The Dome of the Rock mosque, located on the hill known to Muslims as Haram as-Sharif, is the third most important religious site to Muslims next to Mecca and Madina. Muslims believe this is where Muhammad ascended into heaven. AP Photo Archive
Across the ocean, is another religious place, rich in history: Palmyra, New York.

Palmyra plays a monumental role in the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints’ history, according to Janet Brigham, a former assistant editor for the Ensign.

It was in Palmyra that Joseph Smith had his first vision in the Sacred Grove and the first Book of Mormon was printed.

“Thus, in many ways, Palmyra mirrors the history of the Church’s rejection and acceptance in its wider society; and in many ways, a return to the roots of the Church in Palmyra is, for the members who live and visit there, a reaffirmation of spiritual beginnings and fulfillment,” she said in a 1978 article.

While Joseph Smith was trying to restore the Church, others were trying to defame the LDS Church. Newspaper articles, letters and other anti-Mormon paraphernalia were being circulated by people trying to turn others against the LDS Church.

Those who visit Palmyra often see the Church’s most famous historical sites. The Hill Cumorah, the site said to hold the gold plates of the Book of Mormon is located near Palmyra, and the Sacred Grove and Joseph Smith’s home are two other places people visit.

“It’s special to live around the sites,” Marilyn Dahneke said in the Ensign.
