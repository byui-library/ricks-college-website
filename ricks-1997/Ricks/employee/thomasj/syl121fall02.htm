Welcome to our class! I hope you will enjoy learning and teaching this semester.

 

Aims

1. To deepen your understanding of the text and the teachings of the Book of Mormon.

2. To prepare and motivate you for life-long study and teaching of the scriptures.

Meeting these goals requires “serious study.” President Ezra Taft Benson promised that those who study the Book of Mormon seriously “will find greater power to resist temptation. . . . power to avoid deception. . . . power to stay on the strait and narrow path.”  He also taught that “God uses the power of the word of the Book of Mormon as an instrument to change people's lives” (A Witness and a Warning, 21-22, 32).

I invite you to claim this promise, as a central (though ungraded) aim of this class.

 

Graded Activities

1. Preparatory study: The course calendar specifies a block of scripture to study before each class meeting. Much of your most valuable learning and growth can take place as you study on your own. Please download the two “Study Guides” on my faculty web page to help you search the scriptures each day. You are welcome but not required to purchase the CES Student Manual. You should also take some notes related to your “Highlights Portfolio” each time you study (see below). Please report the quality of your preparation for each study assignment. I may help assess the quality of your preparation as needed. Basic expectation: read and mark the whole block of scripture before coming to class, use the Study Guide questions, and take some notes.

2. Attendance and participation: Class presentations and discussions should complement your personal study of the scriptures. I hope to help highlight important principles, clarify complex issues, and promote understanding consistent with the course objectives. I invite you to participate in that process. Please report attendance and the quality of your participation for each class meeting. I may help assess the quality of your participation as needed. Basic expectation: be in class, on time, with scriptures, notebook, and intent to listen, write, and speak meaningfully.

3. Highlights portfolio: Every time you study, identify and record scriptures you may want to include in your highlights portfolio.  A significant part of the grade depends on how soon you begin and how consistently you compile lists and record thoughts that will be the basis of your final product. The finished portfolio must be your own original work and late submissions are penalized.

The typewritten portfolio is composed of four lists and five essays.

                a. Lists: Identify and record scriptures for four of the following types of list, and then create a numbered top ten list from the first half of the Book of Mormon for each category, using the format outlined:

                  (i) Classic discourses–choose a title that summarizes the content and write the complete reference for each discourse;  (ii) One-line sermons–quote each phrase and write the reference for each memorable saying; (iii) Powerful prayers–for each prayer, identify who prayed, the basic purpose of the prayer, the basic results of the prayer, and write the complete reference; (iv) Classic stories–choose a meaningful title and write the complete reference for each story; (v) Noble and great ones–give the name and identify a few of the best attributes of each noble and great person (or “hero), writing the references that best illustrate each of those attributes.

                  Each top ten list should be ranked, so that number 1 is your top choice.

                b. Essays: After each list write an essay discussing (i) how you identified and selected your top ten selections from a longer list of candidates; and (ii) how you decided to rank those final ten selections.  At the end of the portfolio, write a brief essay discussing three references (taken from any of the lists) that have had the greatest impact on your faith in Christ.

                Please download “Book of Mormon Highlights Portfolio Tips” from my faculty web page to help you do your best on the project. Basic expectation: Assemble material for the lists consistently, select and assemble four complete lists in proper format, write all essays, and submit the finished product on time.

4. Mastery assessments: Two exams will be administered in the testing center on the dates indicated below–late exams are penalized.  The exams use true-false, matching, and multiple choice questions and you can use your scriptures. Study Guides 1 and 2 can help you in all phases of class preparation, including preparation for the exams. If you wish, you may submit 55 typewritten answers to Study Guide questions (30 starred questions first, plus 25 others) instead of taking one exam. If you do so, the answers must be your own individual work. The typewritten answers are due in class on the dates indicated below–no late submissions are accepted.

5. Additional learning activities. Because time constraints are severe in this block class, you must set aside time for additional learning on your own.  For your first additional assignment, please download and study the syllabus and download and review the applicable Study Guide questions. For your second additional assignment, please download and study “Principles and Promises to Guide our Learning,” which is found on my faculty web page. I hope these documents will inspire you to do your best and claim the blessings that follow. As you study each document, identify and record promises and principles that will help you most as a student and teacher of the scriptures.

                I will post additional out-of-class learning activities on my faculty web page as needed.
