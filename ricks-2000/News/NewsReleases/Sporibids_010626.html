Ricks College News Release
June 26, 2001

Bids opened on new Spori Building

    
REXBURG, Idaho – Layton Construction of Sandy, Utah, is the apparent low bidder for construction of the new Jacob Spori Building at Ricks College.

Final approval for the building and the contractor is expected to be made July 17 by officials of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints that operates the college. As is policy at the private school that soon will become known as Brigham Young University–Idaho, the cost of the building was not released.

Excavation work is already under way for the new classroom facility that will be located at same location as the original Spori Building. Construction is expected to begin in late July and be completed in 2003.

The new 44,000 square foot building has a striking resemblance to its predecessor and will feature a similar size and shape, rock exterior, tall windows and pitched roof. Unlike the old structure, the three-story building will feature a 12 by 30 foot skylight that will allow natural light to flow down through the center of the building to the first floor.

The interior design also features efficient use of space for classrooms and offices of the Communications and Art Departments, as well as an art gallery on the main floor. Unlike the old building, it will have a full basement.

The earth-tone color of the building will be slightly different than the original gray rock and feature a veneer of split-faced walnut travertine from the Medicine Lodge area near Dubois.

Some rock and woodwork from the original building were preserved and are incorporated into the design of the new building. A central focus of the interior will be the triangular wooden pediment from the front of the original building that will be mounted on the third floor and visible from the first floor by looking up the skylight. The pediment will be located at approximately the same elevation and location as it was in the old building.

An outdoor terrace in the front of the building will be bordered by a wall of rock from the original building.

The building is designed by FFKR of Salt Lake. The architectural firm also designed the Spencer W. Kimball Student and Administrative Services Building and the John Taylor Religion Building at Ricks.
