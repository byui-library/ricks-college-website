The David O. McKay Library Welcomes You

 
 

The David O. McKay Library is located in the center of campus, just north of the Manwaring Center. The Library maintains a well-balanced collection of traditional materials and new technology to serve the needs of the students, faculty, and staff of Ricks College. The Library also houses a Family History Center.
 

Mission Statement:
The David O. McKay Library will play an active role in supporting the academic needs and mission of Ricks College, including the educational and economic advantages of new technology by acquiring, organizing, processing, and making readily available collections in all media; also by assisting patrons in finding and using information at this College and elsewhere.
 

This virtual tour will acquaint you with the services and technologies that are available for information retrieval. A similar printed walking tour is available when you arrive on campus. Becoming acquainted with these services will help you choose the best sources available in the Library and those available through remote access.

Periodical (magazine & journal) subscriptions to 800 titles are found on the shelves and in the cabinets on the first floor. 
  • Church Periodicals include copies of the French, Spanish, and German magazines are located next to the Ensign, New Era, Friend and Church News. Last row of periodicals
  • Popular magazines and scholarly journals are arranged alphabetically by title on the Periodical Collection shelves near the south emergency exit.  Periodicals in paper format are generally kept for five years. Periodicals and newspapers do not check out but remain in the Library for all to use. Please treat these and all library materials with care. Do not mark in them, mutilate them, or otherwise make them unusable for the next patron.
Older copies of popular periodical titles are available on microforms in cabinets near the reference desk to save space and preserve them. 
  • Microfiche are sheets of film used for some periodicals &  government documents. 
  • Microfilm are rolls of uncut film which are used for newspapers and some older periodicals. 
  • Local newspapers on microfilm are located near the Family History Library on the second floor.
  • Reader / printers for the microforms are located near the cabinets. Paper copies can be made at  ten (10) cents per sheet. Lenses for the reader / printers allow various enlargement ratios.
 The last shelves in the Periodicals section contains paper copies of local and major newspapers. 
  • Our subscriptions to newspapers are generally by mail which results in delays of several days between publication and receipt. 
  • Indexes to the New York Times are located by the newspapers.
  • Telephone directories for nearby states are found at the information desk and on shelves near the newspapers.
  • Index to the Periodicals of the LDS Church in print format can be found near the newspapers. Church publications since 1971 are available using Internet Resources. 
  Index tables near the map case are the location for many indexes to scholarly journals in the fields of nursing, education, arts, humanities, social science, biology, technology, and biographies. A table nearby has the Library of Congress Subject Headings volumes. The subject headings are used by our catalog and many indexes. Like a thesaurus, this list helps identify other terms to use when doing research.

Library Administrative Offices are located in the Interlibrary Loan office. The Library director and other librarians have offices in this area and scattered throughout the library. 
    A Conference Room was near the Administrative Offices. Two conference rooms are available in the Library:  Room 140 on the first floor of the west wing is unavailable and Room 220 on the second floor near the restrooms. Each room and the technology classroom 190 in the east wing are available for use during certain days and hours, will accommodate 15-20 people and needs to be scheduled in the Administrative Offices.
     
    Circulation  maintains patron records and checks Library materials out. The Circulation and Reserve Offices are temporarily located in room 158 in the east wing. Circulation policies follow: 
    • A Ricks College ID card serves as the Library card for students. Off-campus patrons can obtain a Library card at the Circulation Desk for $1.
    • Most Library materials check out for two weeks. Books may be renewed two times. Faculty may keep these items for as long as a semester, if needed.
    • Music CDs check out for one week, with a limit of 2 CDs per patron.
    • Framed pictures circulate for 105 days or one semester.
    • Calculators and headphones can be checked out for a maximum of two hours.
    • Reference books, newspapers, and periodicals do not circulate.
    • Fines for overdue items are assessed at a cost of $0.25/item/day. Fines for Reserve items are $0.25/item/hour.
    Reserve: Teachers may put materials on reserve for students to use. 
    • These items check out for two hours at a time. 
    • Reserve materials include audiocassettes, books, and file folders that Instructors on campus make available to their classes. 
    • Electronic copies of materials can be placed on "Electronic Reserve," which students can then access on or off campus via the Internet.
    Lost & Found is centralized at the Circulation Desk. 
  • Unclaimed items are taken to the Manwaring Center Lost and Found daily. 
    • Exceptions are:
      • lost ID’s, which are taken immediately to the ID Center.
      • copy cards, which are taken to the Copy Center.
      • floppy disks, which are held at desks near the printers on each floor and then taken to the Lost and Found.
    Music recordings and sheet music are shelved at the Circulation desk. 
    • Equipment for listening to music recordings is found in study areas on all three floors. 
    • Headsets are also available at Circulation. 
    • The sheet music collection is coordinated with the Music Department. 
      • Use fees are charged to cover wear and replacement copies. 
      • Campus choral groups have preference on sheet music use.

     
    Photocopy machines can be found on all floors. 
    • A copy card  for use with photocopy machines may be purchased from the dispensing machines on the wall. 
    • One dollar ($1) will buy a card with twenty-five cents ($.25) credit. 
    • Additional credit can be added to the card using these dispensing machines and some photocopy machines. 
    • Copies are ten cents ($.10) each without the card.
    • Copies are five cents ($.05) each with the card. 
    • These copy cards cannot be used to make copies from microforms.

The Government Documents Librarian is Shane Cole. The McKay Library has been a Federal Depository Library for government documents since 1946. This library receives less than a fourth of the documents available to depository libraries. The University of Idaho regional depository receives all documents and serves our state by lending their documents. Other depository libraries with home pages include Brigham Young, Idaho State, and Boise State Univerisites. The documents are located temporarily on the first floor of the east wing near the doors that link with the west wing. Microfiche documents are in cabinets by the reference desk. They are listed in the online Library Catalog and are arranged using the Superintendent of Document call numbers. Many documents are available over the Internet.     East Wing

      The Library has expanded into the former Administration Building. Remodeling of  the West Wing is expected to be completed near the end of 2001. This will expand Library seating from 700 to some 1,200 study seats, of which 400+ will have computer workstations. 

    The Student Copy Center or Copy Spot is a branch of the Campus Press and is located in room 172. 

    • All of the photocopy machines are part of the campus press. 
    • The press can print, collate, and staple materials on a choice of white or colored paper. 
    • Lost copy cards are taken to the Copy Center. 
    • Defective copy cards can be exchanged or replaced in the Copy Center. 
    • Other services include laser color photocopies, T-shirt transfers, and hot glue binding. 
    • Discounts for multiple copies and billing copies to campus departments and groups are also services of the Copy Center. 
    • A computer station in the Copy Center also allows users to print in color or directly to the copy machines.
    • Facsimile or FAX copies may be sent and received  at the Copy Center. The fax number is: (208) 356-2385. Receiving a fax costs $0.25 per page.
  • Interlibrary Loan is located in office 164. If printed material is not available in the McKay Library, it can be obtained by filling out an Interlibrary Loan form on any computer. 
    • All students, faculty, staff, and other Library users may request materials through Interlibrary Loan. The temporary location of Administrative Offices is in room 164
    • On average, it takes 7-10 days or less to receive items. Delivery services and electronic document delivery may shorten the time for delivery. 
    • Materials can be picked up at the Interlibrary Loan office in McKay 164. 
    • Faculty Document Delivery is a service available to faculty.
  • Maps of neighboring states are located by the drinking fountain. Maps are stored in drawers which allow them to be flat. Topographic maps are received from the United States Geological Survey. An index accompanying these regional topographic maps is filed in the first drawer for the state. The index gives the names for quadrangles. Topographic maps are filed in alphabetical order. A topographic map for Idaho in the catalog will have a call number like: Map #.ID 

Maps issued by other groups are arranged by Library of Congress call numbers. An example of a LC call number for a map is: Map G7061.F-1992.U5. The Central Intelligence Agency produces international maps. National Geographic publishes thematic maps.

Technical Services offices are located at the north end of the second floor near the elevator. Librarians,  professional staff, and student workers order and prepare library materials in this area. Technical Services includes Acquisitions and Cataloguing. Acquisitions personnel order materials for the Library collections, handle the invoices and billings, and  process periodicals received. Cataloging personnel prepare the material for use by entering information into the online catalog, by attaching labels and barcodes, by performing minor repairs to damaged materials, and by sending worn materials to a bindery company for more extensive work.
 
 
 
 
Family History Center is located in the glassed in room by the main stairs. Sources for family history and genealogical research, using the latest computer materials, can be found in the Family History Center. Expert genealogists are also available to help patrons. Campus wards and stakes will have orientations to this facility. As a branch of the Family History Library in Salt Lake City, materials can be ordered. A branch family history library has the same computer programs available as stake family history libraries. The Center is open during regular Library hours, as well as 3:00-7:00 p.m. on most Sundays during the regular school year. For more information, visit their home page: http://abish.ricks.edu/fhc/.
Special Collections are located in rooms behind the Family History Center. Special Collections include campus archives, rare books, and local manuscripts. Archival collections include copies of the devotional audiocassettes in a digital format. Microfilming or digitizing some campus publications are ongoing projects to preserve the history of this College. Many Special Collections materials have been moved to Brigham Young University. Special collections are materials which require special handling to preserve them.
 
A reference desk on the second floor has personnel that shelve materials and answers questions.
 
    Comfortable chairs for reading, relaxing, and studying are located on the second and third floor landings. Circular stands contain the paperback book collection. Mormon fiction paperbacks by Orson Scott Card, Anita Stansfield, and others are located in these two paperback book collections.
    Notice the public telephone on the south wall. Each floor landing has a telephone for public use. To reach local off-campus numbers, dial 8 followed by the 7 digit number.   • Posters to accompany elementary school textbooks are located in the wooden shelves and in the metal file cabinet. Portraits of famous people and popular art masterpieces are also included in the poster collection. Along the halls of second and third floor at the start of each semester can also be found framed pictures by famous artists as well as photographs of several LDS temples. Posters and framed pictures can be checked out.
    • A Computer Lab is located on the third floor of the Library. These computers provide access to word processing programs, research databases, multimedia presentation software, and campus email accounts (but not non-campus email accounts). They are available on a first-come, first-served basis.
     The Student Technology Center is located in room 360B on the third floor. Students are able to use PCs, a scanner, ZIP drives, and a CD-ROM burner to prepare PowerPoint presentations or for other class-related projects. Student assistants are available to supervise and help with problems.  
     
    		The Ruth Biddulph Children's Book Collection or Juvenile Literature Collection is located in the open study area. Elementary education majors also have access to textbooks and materials used in elementary schools in this area. Caldecott and Newbery Award winning literature is found in the juvenile book collection.
    Video Viewing Area is in room 324. Students watch classroom presentations, movies, firesides, and other video productions here. The catalog of Library materials also lists these videocassettes. Videos may be viewed free of charge inside the Library by requesting them at the video viewing window. To schedule or rent a video for classroom or other use, ask before 5 p.m. Audiovisual equipment can be scheduled  in room 320 for classroom delivery or use. The equipment includes slide projectors, cassette and CD players, video projectors, digital cameras, computer display panels, camcorders, screens, and similar technology.

The Department of Instructional Technology has offices in room 321. Instructional Technology personnel assist the faculty and other campus departments with presentations and electronic publication. Many campus events and productions are videotaped and copies are available for purchase. Online course CDs are also available in this room. Instructional and promotional videos are produced both on location and in the video studio. Other services include editing, special effects, character generation, duplication, and off-air and satellite taping. A large collection of stock music and sound effects are available for use.  (See their homepage for more information.)
  • The Instructional Technology Center for faculty use is located in room 337. Multimedia presentations are demonstrated and created by faculty in this room.
  • At the end of the hall in room 360 is the Media Lab. Classes and individuals may use this self-serve, hands-on area to produce media materials. You can laminate materials, bind a report, make an overhead transparency, mount a visual, create lettering for a sign, add clipart images to a chart, enlarge a visual, make a banner, cut paper, etc. Student lab assistants are available to help if needed. Contact media specialist Richard Nelson at ext. 1209 for further information.
  • Room 343 is a self-serve audio room. Students can make their own tape recordings or make copies within the copyright law.
  • A self-serve video room is found in room 339. Students can use this room to edit and add music to video productions or create a video from still photographs or slides. A video studio in room 328 can be scheduled for class projects only. Scheduling for the self-serve rooms and studio is done in room 320.
  • The Photo Studio  is in room 342.  A photographer and student assistants in the photo and imaging area shoot portraits, passport photos, group pictures, and promotional photographs. Additional services include copying and duplicating slides, producing copy negatives, making black and white prints, and photo scanning  and restoration. A picture file of campus personnel and other useful material is maintained.
