Ricks College News Release, April 22, 1998

Plans announced for Viking Drive

REXBURG, Idaho — The center of the Ricks College campus is expected to shift southward upon completion of a five-acre construction and landscaping project.
Slated for completion in 1999, it is hoped the project will commence sometime in May upon closure of a portion of Viking Drive that now dissects the campus from east and west.
The road is expected to close in mid-May, or as soon as construction is completed on Seventh South, a new east-west roadway that skirts the southernmost portion of the Ricks campus.
Bids for the project will be opened April 21 and final approval to start construction will be sought from the Appropriations Committee of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, says Charles Frost, director of Physical Plant at the two-year college.
1
The area will encompass the present street between the Manwaring Center on the north and the new John Taylor Building and the Spencer W. Kimball Student and Administrative Services Building on the south.
It is expected that a plaza will replace the present "center of activity" of the campus now located east of the McKay Library and south of the Administration Building, Frost said.
"We'll be creating a new heart for the college," he said of the 20,000 expected crossings of the new plaza to be made by students and others on a daily basis during the regular school year. The plaza, using concrete pavers and accent strips, will measure 145 feet on each side, Frost said.
The construction project will require some 20,000 yards of fill to build up and level the area between the Manwaring Center and the Taylor Building. Steps will bring pedestrians up to the plaza level to the north and west between the two buildings. At each end of the street closure a turn-around area will be provided for vehicular traffic. Some building entrances in the Manwaring Center and Taylor Building will not be in use during the construction project.
During the first phase of the construction project, which will be completed by Nov. 1, the plaza and walkways will be installed. Next year during spring and summer, the underground sprinkler systems will be installed, and trees and shrubs will be planted. At the same time, the Kimball Building is expected to be completed and all offices now located in the Administration Building and some others will move into the new building.
Near the southwest corner of the Manwaring Center a 12-foot-deep underground storm water detention vault will be built to handle water runoff during storms, Frost says. "This entire project is an enormous undertaking."
The project was designed by the Salt Lake City architectural firm of FFKR. The firm also designed the Taylor and Kimball buildings.
