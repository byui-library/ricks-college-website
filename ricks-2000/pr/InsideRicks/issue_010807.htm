COLLEGE OFFICIALLY CHANGES TO BRIGHAM YOUNG UNIVERSITY-IDAHO ON AUGUST 10

After use for nearly 100 years, the name of Ricks College will be retired and changed to Brigham Young University-Idaho on August 10. On Friday, employees will begin answering the phones differently, new stationery will be put into use, college vehicles will have new word marks on the doors, and campus signs will gradually be changed. Even the college radio station KRIC-FM will begin using the new call letters KBYI. This is all in preparation for the beginning of Fall Semester classes on Sept. 5 that will include upper-level classes leading to bachelor's degrees along with associate degrees.

The private school, which was begun by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints in 1888 as Bannock Stake Academy, became known as Ricks Academy in 1902 to honor Thomas E. Ricks, founder of Rexburg and president of the board of education when the academy was founded. Ricks had been invited by Church President John Taylor to settle the Upper Snake River Valley in the 1880s.

Ricks, who died 100 years ago this year, was a contemporary of Brigham Young, second president of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints and American colonizer. Young lived from 1801 to 1877 while Ricks lived from 1828 to 1901.

The decision to change the school's name was announced by Church President Gordon B. Hinckley in June 2000. In making the announcement, he said the name change was designed to give the school immediate national and international recognition. Its larger sister school, BYU in Provo, Utah, has approximately 29,000 students.

President Hinckley added, "The school will have a unique role in and be distinctive from the other institutions of higher education within the Church Educational System. . . . BYU-Idaho will continue to be teaching oriented. Effective teaching and advising will be the primary responsibilities of its faculty, who are committed to academic excellence."

During the past year, college employees have worked long hours to make it possible to start offering 17 bachelor's degree programs in the fall. Approximately 50 baccalaureate programs are expected by 2004, along with 19 associate degrees. (Full story: www.byui.edu/Ricks/News/Ricks/NewsReleases/August10_010802.html)


FIRST IN A SERIES OF PREVIEWS OF NEW FACULTY

As the pages of the calendar turn, we head quickly toward the Fall Semester at BYU-Idaho. Classes will officially begin on Wednesday, September 5. All faculty members are scheduled to be on campus beginning August 16 for course preparation and additional study.

Thirty-seven new teachers will be joining the faculty. Office space has been expanded to include space in William F. Rigby Hall. Several faculty will be shifting office locations to allow new faculty closer proximity to department chairs and mentoring through their first year.
