Radio Programming: Unit 4


Radio is an intensely personal medium that reaches almost all Americans on a daily basis.

From 6 A.M. to 6 P.M., radio reaches more people than TV, cable, newspapers, magazines, and computers and online services.

Modes of Radio Production
• Local, Live Production
• Live-assist Production
• Semiautomation
• Turnkey Automation

Local, Live Production
•Stations employ their own announcers or newscasters locally and play CDs and tapes that they own.

Live-assist Production
•Stations use syndicated programming (reels of prerecorded and/or satellite delivered music) but retain local announcers.

Semiautomatic
•Stations play music from large tape machines or satellite.
• Local announcements from smaller tape machines are triggered by a subaudible cue tone on the master tape or satellite transmission.

Turnkey Automation
•Fully automated stations that rely on a satellite dish and a control board or CD players and tape machines interfaced with a computer console.

Creating A Successful Radio Format
•Identify and serve a predetermined set of listeners (target audience).
• Serve those listeners better than the competition.
• Reward those listeners both on and off the air, so they become consistent customers for the products and services advertised on the station.
