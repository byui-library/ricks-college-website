SECOND SUMMER TERM BEGINS WITH COUNSEL ON RELATIONSHIPS

Monday, June 11, marked the beginning of the second summer term at Ricks College. For many students it is a time filled with new experiences having only recently graduated from high school and moved to Rexburg. At Tuesday's weekly devotional they were instructed to take advantage of internship opportunities as preparation for their future. Specifically, the experience of living with roommates can be an internship in personal relationships.

Initially, identifying goals and what one wants to accomplish is essential. "A walk in the park with blinders on just won't cut it," said Kathleen Gee, faculty member in the Family Science Department. Remember to apply gospel principles to living with roommates. The experience of living with roommates with various personalities and values can help one evaluate the characteristics desired in a future marriage. It can be a time to learn to deal with and resolve differences and identify what really matters. "In large measure, it is through our relationships that we are perfected," she said.

She counseled the students to apply the nine principles and values in the Proclamation on the Family to their relationships in their apartments. Improve habits and become more Christlike by applying principles of faith, prayer, repentance, forgiveness, respect, love, cooperation, work, and wholesome activities. Be patient. Learn to monitor stress levels and to respond with charity. Resolve conflicts by clarifying the issues, finding out what each person wants, identifying various alternatives, evaluating various options, and reviewing and negotiating a solution. Strengthen character and relationships through scripture study, prayer and application of the power of the atonement of Christ. These lessons learned through the roommate internship are directly applicable to future personal and family relationships.

COLLEGE WILL NOT REAPPLY FOR PERMIT ON R MOUNTAIN

The east side of the North Menan Butte that is presently home of the giant block "R" may eventually return to its natural state, say Ricks College officials, who have decided not to reapply this year for a permit with the federal Bureau of Land Management to re-paint the letter. With the college changing its name to become Brigham Young University-Idaho, officials say this may be the most appropriate time to retire the "R" that stands for Ricks.

Student Life Vice President Robert Wilkes says safety of students was the main reason for the decision. The college has serious concerns about potential liability that could result from accidents involving the steep slope, rolling rocks, and lime, which is used in a mixture of white cement and water that burns the skin and eyes.

The "R" was designed and built in 1962 by Mathletes, a college math club, and has been repainted biennially as part of homecoming festivities. Because the "R" is located on Bureau of Land Management ground, the college has been required to reapply for a permit every three years. College officials will work with the BLM to begin the process of restoring the site.

CAMPUS IS BECOMING MORE ENVIRONMENTALLY FRIENDLY

Ricks College has announced that it will begin extensive renovations on the Heating Plant immediately, which will include installing a larger, more environmentally friendly boiler and replacing the 100-foot tall smokestack with smaller, more earthquake-safe smokestacks for each of the four boilers.

A 35-year-old, 10,000 pound coal-fire boiler, the smallest of four, will be replaced by a 45,000 pound natural gas boiler. The results will be cleaner emissions and more efficiency. Ricks will also be adding 1,200 square feet for a new control room to accommodate changes inside the boiler room. Seismic upgrades include replacing the roof and the smokestack, which will be torn down in October or November.

Construction on the Heating Plant was approved by the Board of Trustees May 15 and initial stages have already begun. Construction is expected to be completed by the end of the year in time for the heating season.

The project has been under way since 1998, and is not related to the BYU-Idaho transition. The capacity of the plant is currently capable of providing heat to the additional buildings planned, such as the multi-use building and the announced Thomas E. Ricks Building. "We want to be environmentally friendly. . . . It had to happen whether we were going to four years or not," said Charles Frost, director of physical plant. "It does fit in nicely though."
