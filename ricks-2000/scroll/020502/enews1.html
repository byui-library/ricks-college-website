AWS speaker talks on stress and depression

by Seth Moncur
MON98005@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Kristin Erickson SPELLING is correct spoke to the AWS Thursday about dealing with depression and stress.

Erickson, a counselor at BYU-Idaho, said that things like school, pressures with social life, studying, balancing church callings and school work can cause a lot of stress for students.

“Each person has different levels of stress they can take and the symptoms of stress vary for each person. It can build up until it interferes with your functioning, then you need to seek help,” Erickson said

The symptoms of stress include anxiousness, nervousness, discouragement, crying, cynical, tense muscles, impatient, frustration and lack of clear thinking.

When a person is stressed, it may lead to irrational thoughts and distorted thought patterns; filtering is where you focus on the negative while filtering out the positive, Making mountains out of molehills, and mind reading where

you expect someone can read your mind instead of you communicating with them. (Attribution coming, it was a handout at the speech)

Erickson discussed several ways of coping with stress. First would be identifying specific stressors in all the areas of your life. The more specific you are in the list it will be easier to figure out ways to manage your stress.

Some ideas Erickson gave to cope with your stress would be to change the stressful situation, sometimes that is not always posssible though.

The second step is to change your perception of the situation. Erickson told a story of how she saw some kids snow boarding down the snow piles in the Albertsons parking lot, and how they made the best of the winter.

The third step is to Change your capacity to cope with the situation. Finally creating and implementing a plan in a positive way.

If stress is to much, the counseling office at BYU-Idaho is ready to help. “Some people are uncomfortable with the idea of coming to the counselors office, but most of the people who come in are healthy, and are just looking for additional ideas to cope.”

Brief relaxation exercise

1. Scan body head to toe and notice any tension you might be holding on to. Allow yourself to let go of any tension you might have. Relax the forehead, drop the jaw and drop the shoulders. Continue relaxing each area down to the toes.

2. Take two or three deep breaths. Imagine breathing in peace as you inhale and imagine breathing out tension, stress, worries, or concerns as you exhale.

3. Repeat a relaxing word or phrase such as ‘I feel calm,” “Relax”, or let go.

Repeat that at the first signs of stress, between classes or every hour on the hour. Custom to individual needs.
