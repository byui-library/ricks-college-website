Groom’s responsibilities reach beyond ring purchasing

by Sarah Lunt
LUN99013@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Timothy Robison / Scroll
“First priorities upon becoming engaged
...Purchase the bride’s engagement ring.
...Discuss with fiancee the date and type of wedding.
...Start on your guest list with the help of family.
...Choose best man/groomsmen.
...Start planning and making necessary arrangements for the honeymoon.
...Discuss and plan with fiancee your new home together so you can start making necessary moving arrangements.
...Discuss the amount and financial arrangement for the bridal flowers and luncheon, which are traditionally the groom’s responsibility. Communicate details about the ceremony/reception to your parents and encourage immediate communication with the bride’s parents.
...If you and your fiancee live in different areas and you plan to have a separate open house in your area, your parents should be prepared to plan and pay for this event, as the bride’s family is responsible for the planning and financing of the formal reception.

Second priorities
...Shop with fiancee for wedding rings.
...Complete your guest list, including full names, addresses and ZIP codes. Make it available to the bride.
...Check requirements for blood test and marriage license in your state.
...Select and order men’s wedding attire with your fiancee.
...Finalize all honeymoon plans and send in deposits, if required. Don’t delay — some resorts fill up fast in popular months.
...Assist parents with plans for the rehearsal dinner party/wedding luncheon.

Third priorities
...Meet with officiant to finalize ceremony details.
...Arrange accommodations for out-of-town attendants. (optional)
...See that all attendants have been fitted and wedding attire has been ordered.
...Purchase gifts for the best man and groomsmen.
...Purchase wedding gift for fiancee, if gifts are being exchanged.
...Pick up wedding rings and make sure they fit.
...Together with fiancee, gather necessary documents and get your marriage license. Reconfirm all honeymoon reservations. If flying, make sure you have plane tickets.

One month before
...Take care of business and legal affairs
(add bride’s name to insurance policies
and medical plans, make a new will, add
her name to joint checking account or
joint charge card. If you have both
agreed to a prenuptial agreement, have it
drawn up and signed).
...Discuss all final details with fiancee; offer
to assist if needed.
...Begin packing for the honeymoon.
...Help fiancee with thank you notes.

One to two weeks before
...Arrange for gifts brought to the reception to be taken to your new home.
...Have your hair cut.
...Pick up and try on wedding attire.
...See that attendants get their wedding attire.
...See to it that your attendants are at the rehearsal and that they know their duties.
...Go over special seating or pew cards with ushers.
...Make sure luggage is in the car or to the hotel where you will stay your first night.
...Attend rehearsal dinner.
...Go to bed early the night before the wedding.

The wedding day
...Be sure to eat something in the morning.
...Allow plenty of time to get dressed. Start one hour before ceremony.
...Get to the ceremony location on time.
...Give the best man the bride’s wedding ring.
...Take the marriage license to the ceremony.
...Attend the wedding luncheon. Allow time to rest, if possible, before an evening reception.
...If dancing at the reception, dance first with your bride, then with both mothers. Just before leaving the reception, thank the bride’s parents and say goodbye to your parents.

After the wedding
...Express your appreciation and thank the bride’s parents again for a beautiful wedding reception.
...Take care of your new bride.
...Help your bride write and mail thank you notes.
