HISTORY: GARDEN GROVE AND MT. PISGAH

Garden Grove and it’s sister relief settlement Mt. Pisgah were temporary camps where pioneers stopped to rest and gather needed supplies. These two main stopping points were established for saints on the first leg of their trek from Nauvoo. Many found themselves understandably unprepared for the journey that was compelled by the danger of mobs. But these weigh-stations were also places for the manifestation of simple, selfless love. It was there that a thousand acres of fields were plowed and planted, to be harvested by someone else sharing the struggle and coming after them at the end of the season.

The early saints of these settlements truly worked as Christian brothers and sisters. As instructed by Brother Brigham, they sweated and struggled, not just to get the task done, but to do it well enough that there was hope for germination and harvest. The fields, and the community itself, were maintained by pioneers for unnamed fellow saints who would find food and shelter awaiting them. These inspired temporary communities saved hundreds who would otherwise have died as they often arrived already shoeless and huddled under worn, wet blankets. There are journals filled with heartfelt and simple messages of quiet relief and gratitude.
