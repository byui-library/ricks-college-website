HONORS PROGRAM
Rick Davis / Taylor 240C
Phone 356-1253
The Honors Program is designed for students who do well academically and who have a positive attitude toward learning. Honors classes and activities can be beneficial to students of all majors as well as those who are undecided about their major.

To be admitted to the program, a student must have a minimum cumulative GPA of 3.3 (high school GPA if just entering college; otherwise college GPA). Anyone who meets that requirement will be admitted automatically upon submission of an application to the Director in Smith 294B. Once admitted, a student is not required to take any classes, but should do so in order to benefit from membership. Except as explained below regarding graduation and financial benefits, a student's GPA is not monitored after admission.

Because classes are small in size and taught by excellent teachers, a greater opportunity is available for students to develop their capacities. In addition, honors classes are viewed positively by university admission and evaluation personnel.

Financial assistance is available. A $50 scholarship is awarded each semester to any student who takes an honors class, has a minimum cumulative GPA of 3.5, and pays a fee of $5. When the same requirements are met, a student may also register for more than 21 credits without paying the extra fee that would otherwise be required.
Students who graduate in the Honors Program are named in the graduation program, wear a distinctive yellow tassel at graduation, and have their Honors Program graduation noted on their transcript and graduation certificate. Students who meet additional criteria are guaranteed admission to BYU and receive other benefits. Obtain details from the Honors Program office.

Additional advantages of membership include a lounge located at Smith 294, symposia (informal discussions with significant people), retreats (Saturday, evening, or overnight excursions for intellectual development and fellowship), and other activities.

PROGRAM DESCRIPTION
Students admitted to the Honors Program may take any honors classes, whether or not they intend to graduate in the Honors Program. Those who intend to graduate in the program (which is different than just graduation with honors), must complete the following requirements:
-Have a minimum cumulative GPA of 3.5.
-Complete at least 12 honors credits including at least one of the following:

Art 211H or 212H
Hon 200 or 201
Ltrs. 201H or 202H
Hum 201H or 202H
Phil 201H or 202H

Only two credits in honors religion will count toward graduation, and no more than three credits will be allowed in classes with both honors and general students.

STUDENT EMPLOYMENT
Robert Maxfield
Kimball Bldg. 244
Phone 356-1019
Students are assisted in locating both on and off campus part time jobs. On-campus job referrals are based on first-come first served, job skills qualification, student availability and existing job vacancies. Job vacancies vary a great deal through the calendar year.

During the fall and spring semesters, on and off campus jobs are extremely limited. Any available 2-4 hour block of time between 8 a.m. and 4 p.m. daily and student willingness to accept any type of job and work any hours, will greatly increase the opportunity for on or off-campus work. However, the very best opportunity to get an on-campus job is during the first, second, or third summer terms, in that order.

Student Employment interviews for all new students entering Fall Semester will begin the beginning of Fall Semester. Student interview numbers may be obtained during the Student Employment Call-in held the first Monday in June. Students who miss the call-in deadline in June will be interviewed after all others with interview numbers have been considered.

Summer School and returning students who are here during the week prior to Fall Semester may be interviewed and hired for any vacant job that must be filled during that week or by Labor Day weekend. It is recommended that these students also call in for an interview appointment the same as new students in the event they are not hired by Labor Day weekend.

WRITING CENTER
Sharon Papworth
Mckay E 220 / Phone 356-1158
Located on the second floor of the Admin. building, the Writing Center provides a learning environment for students who want to improve their writing process. Qualified assistants work individually with writers in free, confidential, twenty-minute sessions. Assistants help students analyze drafts and learn writing strategies to help them create and edit college-level papers. Because the Center teaches writing as "re-writing," a student can expect help revising a paper from rough draft form through the final stages of editing. Both assistants and students work together to understand the following writing principles. How to:
1. Discover and generate intriguing ideas
2. Write strong introductions and conclusions
3. Develop focused ideas through detailed, concrete discussions
4. Construct connecting sentences and transitions
Assistants also aid students in organizing and focusing research papers, paraphrases and quotations, and documenting texts, using both the MLA and APA system. Rather than provide a quick stop-fix-it-service, the assistants involve students in critical thinking / writing process. The Writing Center is a great resource for students when they come for help early in the drafting process.
