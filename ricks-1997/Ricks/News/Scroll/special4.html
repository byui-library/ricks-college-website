Men, women give their talents to society differently
By Emily Warburton, Scroll Staff



A DIVINE CALLING--Leaders warn against throwing away motherhood for anything the world can offer.
by Jan Pettersson Scroll Staff



In gaining her education, the Ricks College female questions what she should become to fulfill her role as a woman. How does she balance what the world sees as a women's role and The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saint's view on the role of women. Where does she find success and happiness?

The world feminist's ideals explain that women should acknowledge their "achievements, intelligence, strength, and power," states The College Women's Handbook.

The Church agrees on acknowledging the "achievements, intelligence, strength and power," of women, for as President Gordon B. Hinckley once said to the General Relief Society audience, "You bring a measure of wholeness to us. You have great strength. With dignity and tremendous ability, you carry forward the remarkable programs of the (Church)."

The feminist's ideal is one of "pro-women." A girl can become a stay- at-home mother or leave her kids to pursue a career or even opt not to have children. The world says as long as she is doing her best at what she wants to do, then she is fulfilling her role as a woman.

In an article titled "Feminism and Motherhood," author Alana Wingfoot explains that "many people seem to think that a woman who decides to stay home with kids is copping out from the "far more challenging world of the office."

But "women should not have to be mothers if they don't want to be," Wingfoot said.

Church leaders warn against this idea of throwing away motherhood for anything the world can offer.

"There are ever-increasing demands on women that challenge their traditional role of caregivers. But as women, the roles of wife and mother are in the center of your souls and cry out to be satisfied," said President James E. Faust in his talk "How Near to the Angels."

"With so many exciting possibilities clamoring for our attention, it becomes difficult to separate those which will give only momentary satisfaction from those which offer eternal blessings," said Barbara Smith, former General Relief Society President.

President Faust also admonishes not to be deceived in the quest to find happiness and identity. "You cannot trust the many conflicting voices that clamor about what women should or should not do in today's society."

President Spencer W. Kimball reminds women that "in the world before we came here, faithful women were given certain assignments while faithful men were foreordained to certain priesthood tasks. While we do not remember the particulars, this does not alter the glorious reality to what we once agreed to."

Thus men and women are equal, but give of their talents to society in different ways.

As to how a Ricks College woman can gain happiness and success, President Thomas S. Monson gives three goals: first, gain knowledge through study; second, make home a heaven; and third, find joy in service.

"My dear sisters, this is your day, this is your time," President Monson said.
