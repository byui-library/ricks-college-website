Ricks College News Release
March 20, 2001 

Women's Week Fireside —
 Virginia Pearce

By Jennifer Yates
Ricks College News Service

REXBURG, Idaho — A glimpse into the life of Marjorie P. Hinckley, the wife of the president of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, was given by her daughter, Virginia Pearce, at the Women’s Week fireside Sunday night at Ricks College.

Sister Pearce gave three main points of advice that she said also would be given by her mother.

First, be yourself. The world does not need two of one person, Sister Pearce said. Every person is trying to progress toward the same thing, but cannot do so if they are not themselves.

Second, be excited about learning. Although Sister Hinckley was not able to attend college, she has continued to read and learn throughout her life and always encourages others to do so.

"Is it worthwhile to be an excellent student? Yes it is. God gave you a brain. Use it," Sister Hinckley once said.

"Daily learning will energize you. Look and smell and listen as you walk across campus," Sister Pearce said. "Life will never be boring if you are constantly learning and you won’t be a boring companion. Learn as though your life depended upon it."

Lastly, be kind to everyone. She said Sister Hinckley believes that since people are all in this life together, they should cheer each other on. Sister Pearce said people should be kind not just to those who may fail, but also to those who succeed.

In closing Sister Pearce reminded all sisters that although her mother is married to Church President Gordon B. Hinckley, there are good mothers all around who can strengthen lives.

"Nobody has a mother who has everything," Sister Pearce said.

Take the good things that you find in women all over the Church and learn from them, she said.
