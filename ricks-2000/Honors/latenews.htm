HONORS RETREAT: TERRORISM, THE MUSLIM WORLD, AND AMERICA'S RESPONSE
Answering the question on everyone's mind, "How can anyone do such a thing?" David Peck will address the recent attacks in light of terrorism's agenda and America's response, with emphasis on understanding the problems in the Middle East, how they are of global concern, and what we should do about them.

All honors students are invited to attend; however, seats are limited to 25. Sign up in the main office of the Department of Humanities and Philosophy, TAY 240. Transportation and dinner are provided by the Honors Program. We'll leave from the circular drive north of the Kimball (Administration) Building at 4:30 p.m. and return by 10:30 p.m.

In preparation for the discussion, participants should read the following articles (Adobe Acrobat format):
Who Are They? Who Are We? 
Fertile Fields of Terror

You may wish to look at a handful of other relevant articles at this site. And for a summary of moral principles relevant to America's military response, see the article on "Just War Theory" in the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy.

HONORS PROGRAM RETREAT: EVOLUTION AND THE GOSPEL
 How compelling is the evidence for the evolution of species and, more particularly of the human species? Can one consistently accept the theory of evolution and the creationists' account of the origin of life?
Brother Ed Williams from the Biology Department will be addressing these and other questions on the topic of evolution and the gospel.

FEBRUARY RETREATS  
Two retreats are planned this semester: February 9 and March 23.

National Science Foundation offering fellowships  
Although BYUI students may not be ready for graduate fellowships yet, students in science, math, and engineering may be interested to know that the National Science Foundation (NSF) awarded fellowships to nearly 20% of applicants last year. The NSF is particularly interested in honor students. Students in the targeted fields may want to start planning for their future. For more information, contact Murray Hunt or NSF Fellowship Operations directly at nsfgrfp@orau.gov or at (423) 241-4300.
