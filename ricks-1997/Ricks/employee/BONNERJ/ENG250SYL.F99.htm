THE PURPOSE of reading literature is to benefit from the experience of others. By reading of their experiences, we can avoid certain kinds of mistakes, become acquainted with delightful (and not so delightful) people, and develop awareness of and sensitivity to others and ourselves that we might not otherwise have had. An additional benefit of well written literature is the joy that comes from experiencing the author's beautiful choice and placement of words (J.L.B., 6-93).



from Criteria for Letters Courses:



RECOGNIZING that artistic quality in a formal sense may not necessarily convey quality in a moral sense, each course will assist the student to formulate a process of evaluation based upon discernment of truth and to discover in literary works those personal messages which may lie beyond the surface and to internalize the positive.



EACH COURSE will teach a critical methodology for appraising works of literature and will give students practice in using it. For example:



a. Observe your reaction; withhold judgment.



b. Describe the experience in terms of elements/principles.



c. Analyze. Why has the author done what he or she has done?



d. Evaluate. Make tentative judgment as to whether the author succeeds.

from Course Content Philosophy:



ULTIMATELY, great writing should teach readers to understand and appreciate the dignified, empathetic, and artistically skilled rendering of complex human experience.

7. WRITING PORTFOLIO: Please submit a 2,500 word portfolio by Thursday, December 9. The portfolio will consist of three essays, each with an original title. The first (1,000 words long) is to be based on a book [novel, biography, autobiography, etc.], play or film of your choice. You may want to summarize the story or plot, tell how you identify with one of the characters, analyze the work using literary terminology and critical principles, use a combination of these approaches or some other strategy that you have invented or been taught. May be done in groups of two or three.



The second (about 750 words) should be a response to a short story, essay or another play. In writing your response you may want to use the principles mentioned above or others that would be appropriate.



The third (about 750 words) can be an analysis, appreciation, interpretation, etc. of a poem, song, another short story, or a similar short work (or several short works taken together).



At least one of the three works should be taken from our textbook. Each of the three should have an original title. An original title is a descriptive title that you make up yourself. The title of the work you are discussing (appropriately punctuated) can be part of your title. In other words, don't just call your paper Hamlet. Instead choose a title like "The Justification for Hamlet's Delay." [By the way, you do not need to enclose your own title in quotation marks.] As you mention titles in the body of your paper, remember that titles of longer works such as books, plays and films are underlined (or italicized); titles of shorter works such as short stories, poems and songs are enclosed in quotation marks. If you use another title as part of your own title, be sure to punctuate the other title properly; for example: The Theme of Jealousy in Othello; or, Scratchy's Repentance in "The Bride Comes to Yellow Sky." (An example of a title which includes another title is found on p. 1777 of our text.)



This portfolio is worth 25 possible points. The grading scale is as follows: 23=A, 21=B, 19=C, etc. Grading is based on appropriate length, attractive format, neatness, correctness (spelling, titles, etc.), originality and other considerations.



Each of the three essays should be typed and double-spaced. Please number your pages. [A number on the first page of each essay is, however, optional.] Try for a neat and attractive format.



You may use a title page and/or table of contents for the portfolio, or just title each essay individually and sign your name at the end of the last one. Please do not use plastic or cardboard covers, manila folders or the like. Staple all three essays together.



Please include a one or two paragraph abstract with your papers. Tell how the individual essays relate to each other. Also briefly explain your philosophy of literature (its purposes, possible benefits, etc.). If applicable, you may want to comment on your approach to this project as a group of authors. As mentioned earlier, this assignment can be completed jointly by small groups of two or three students. (Length and other requirements remain the same whether the three writings are completed individually or as a group [about 2,500 words in either case]). Again, if you do write jointly you may wish to include in your abstract comments about your working relationship.
