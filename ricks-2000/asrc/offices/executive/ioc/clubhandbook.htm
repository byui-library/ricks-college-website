Inter-Organization Council Mission Statement

 

 

 

1. To adhere to the mission statements of Ricks College, Student Life, ASRC, and the

    Executive Vice President’s  office.

 

2. To encourage students to participate in campus clubs, organizations and activities.

 

3. To relay information about activities and opportunities for clubs to be involved in the

   ASRC.

 

4. To help students get involved in the planning process of campus activities, and give

    them opportunities to grow in leadership, social and other practical skills.

 

5. To reach out and lift others, that all may benefit from the opportunities to learn and

    improve.

 

6. To aid in the training process of club officers and members.

 

7. To help students understand the importance and purpose of campus clubs in

    Influencing the lives of their members and to create meaningful foundations for the

   development of personalities, talents, and abilities.

The Inter-Organizational Council

 

 

 

We give to  . . . young adults significant and specific responsibilities; we trust them, but do not abandon them in terms of supervision and evaluation. 

                                                                                    Neal A. Maxwell

           

 

 

 

 

            In order to protect its reputation and serve the interest of its students, any college and especially a church college must be careful about what organizations receive official recognition. Our concerns are that any Ricks College activity be consistent with LDS standards, be safe, and be properly advised and chaperoned. In order to insure that the name of Ricks College is not associated with any activity that would be damaging to its reputation and to assure wholesome activities for our students, the Inter-Organizational Council (IOC)  has been organized.

 

            IOC also operates as a vehicle through which clubs and organizations can be recognized as an official organization,  receive assistance, advice and opportunities for participation as they attempt to accomplish their goals. Service opportunities and privileges available to clubs and organizations through ASRC and IOC include but are not limited to:

 

1.         Guidelines and advice.

 

2.         Organization Financial Board (O.F.B.)

            a. Fund raising projects

            b. ASRC funds available for your club

 

3.         Accounting services (Checking accounts, C.P.O., etc.).

 

4.         Travel

 

5.         Use of Ricks College facilities and services.

            a. Publicity (flyers, posters, Campus Calendar, etc.)

            b. Bookstore

            c. Rooms and buildings

            d. Equipment

               e. Ricks College Leadership/Services Institute

 

6.         Participation in Ricks College functions (Christmas Tree Lane, Halloween Carnival, Multicultural Festival, Club Activities, etc.) some of which provide fund raising opportunities.

How to Become a Recognized Club

 

Recognition

            Ricks College recognizes the right of students to form voluntary organizations for the promotion of common interests.  The ASRC responsibility for recognizing new student organizations rests with the ASRC Executive Vice President.    Recognized organizations are required to abide by the applicable local, state, and federal laws and by Ricks College Rules and Regulations, and conform to the

Inter-organizational Council (I.O.C.) policies and procedures.

 

Advantages of Recognition

            Following proper procedures, any recognized organization may:

 

            1.         Schedule college facilities and services for meetings and other approved activities.

            2.         Receive permission by the college to conduct fund-raising projects.

            3.         Participate in all I.O.C. events as well as ASRC sanctioned events or activities.

            4.         Request funds from the Organization Financial Board, (O.F.B.).

            5.         Use the college mailing address.

            6.         Have an account and checking privileges through the accounting office. (Clubs

                        may not set up their own account off-campus.)

            7.         Establish dues according to college policy.

            8.         Invite approved speakers to the campus.

 

Recognition Process

           

            1.         Any group of students interested in forming a new organization should schedule a

conference with the ASRC  Executive Vice President for information and instructions on how to proceed.

2.         Temporary recognition of an organization may be given by the ASRC Executive Vice President to the group for a period of thirty days, which will allow the organization to schedule college facilities and meeting space, with the expectation that the group will apply for I.O.C. recognition during the thirty day period.

3.         New organizations must fill out an “Organization Roster” form and write a constitution following the outline on page 5 of the Organization Manual. Copies of the handbook can be obtained in the ASRC Executive Vice President office, MC 394F.      

4.         The organization will be considered recognized upon approval from the ASRC Executive Vice President and the Director of Student Activities. Any group of students wishing to benefit from the above Advantages of Recognition without the intent of becoming a permanent organization should schedule a conference with the ASRC Executive Vice President to discuss specific objectives and proposed date of termination.

 

Advisors

    Student organizations must have an approved faculty or staff advisor who serves on a voluntary basis and whose major function will be to serve as an informational and administrative resource.  Refer to page 5 for more information on advisors.

 

Registration

   To remain a recognized club, organizations must submit a new roster and current constitution annually by Sept. 30., to the ASRC Executive Vice President office, MC 394F. The purpose of this procedure is to provide an accurate and up-to-date record of clubs and organizations. Registration will be completed upon receipt by the Executive Vice President of the Organization Roster (see insert: Organization Roster) and a copy of the organization’s constitution. This includes any changes in the constitution and/or by-laws that happened during the previous year.  A list of current active clubs will be mailed to Accounting Service shortly after the Sept. 30 deadline.

Constitutions

 

I.          Purpose

            To provide information and guidelines for preparing an approved club constitution.

 

II.         Policy

           

            A.        Every campus organization is required to have an ASRC approved constitution on file in

                             the office of the ASRC Executive Vice President, MC 394E.

 

            B.         In order to renew the organization charter, the constitution must be re-submitted once  

                             annually between April 1 and September 30.

 

C.        The college requires that the following information be contained in every organization’s                                                     constitution (this outline may be used in preparing a club constitution):

                        1.         The name of the organization

 

                        2.         The purpose and objectives of the organization.

a.         An organization may have objectives that include social activities, but may not be limited to social purposes only.

b.         All organization objectives must be consistent with the standards of Ricks College, The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, and the Constitution of the United States.

 

3.         A commitment of the organization to uphold the standards of Ricks College.  Every constitution must specifically state this commitment.

 

4.         The organizational structure including a list of the officers and a statement about the responsibilities of each.

                                    a.         Each organization may elect or appoint officers as needed.

                                    b.         The organization constitution must list all of the officers.

 

NOTE:  The names, addresses, phone number, and student numbers of all officers must

be on file in the office of the ASRC Executive Vice President.  The Organization Roster is the document used for this (See insert—Organization Roster).  Any change of officers should be submitted to the

same offices within seven days after the change has been made.

 

c.         All officers of Ricks College organizations must be full-time students (12 hours) and may not be on academic or disciplinary probation.  They must also maintain a 2.5 G.P.A. while in office.    

d.         Officers are responsible for the programs of their individual organization.  It is their responsibility to insure that all activities are conducted within the standards of the Ricks College policy governing campus organizations.

 

                        5.         An outline of how officers are selected.

                                    a.         How often are elections to be held?

                                    b.         What procedure is to be followed if an office is vacated?

                                    c.         How may an officer be removed?

 

                        6.         The requirements for membership.

a.         The constitution of each organization must specify the requirement for membership.

                                     b.        Membership in organizations at Ricks College is open to

                                                1) Full-time students (12 semester hours or more)

                                                2) Faculty and faculty spouses.

                                                3) Wives or husbands of full-time students.

                                                4) Administrative or staff personnel.

c.         Consideration for membership must be given to any interested student who meets the membership requirements as listed in the organization constitution.  As a general rule, non-students may not have membership in Ricks College Clubs.  The Director of Student Activities must approve exceptions.

 

                        7.         The role of the advisor.

a.         Every organization must have an advisor who is a full-time employee of Ricks College.  It is the responsibility of the club or organization to recommend and have an advisor.  The Vice-President of Student Life must approve advisors who are not full-time employees of Ricks College. The Director of Student Activities should be consulted before an advisor is selected.  The official appointment as advisor to an organization will be made by the Director of Student Activities.

b.         The advisor is responsible for the maintenance of Ricks standards at all functions.

c.         The faculty advisor or an appointed faculty or staff member must be in attendance at all off-campus activities.

                                    d.         If there is more than one advisor, one shall be designated as chairman.

e.                   The faculty advisor should advise the financial affairs of the

 organization.               

                                    g.         The faculty advisor should be aware of all meetings and activities.

                       

8.                  A  statement of the financial procedures and the amount of dues.  No 

organization can assess dues in excess of $15 per semester, unless the OFB and the Director of Student Activities give approval

 

9.                  The method of amending the constitution.  The College will recognize no changes in a constitution until the changes have been submitted in writing to the ASRC Executive Vice President and the Director of Student Activities.

Club Checking Accounts

 

The use of a checkbook by campus clubs is a service and privilege granted by the Accounting Office.  The following guidelines must be followed to retain use of the checkbook:

 

1.         DEPOSITS:  All dues and income should be deposited at the Cashiers Office in the Administration Building.  Do not make any deposit or have an account at other banks or financial institution. A checkbook will be given to those clubs who need it.  Checkbook privileges may be withdrawn by the Accounting Office at any time if used improperly.  The club advisor may make application for a checking account at the Accounting Office.

 

2.         RECONCILE: Record all deposits and written checks in the checkbook when they occur.  Record the date, name, and description of all transactions. Keep a current, accurate running record of the balance in the account.

 

3.         OVERDRAFTS: Never overdraw the account.  Checking privileges may be suspended if an insufficient funds check is written.

 

4.         SIGNATURES: Checks must be signed by either two student officers or the advisor/instructor whose name is on the signature card on file the accounting office

 

5.         WAGES: The checkbook should never be used to pay wages or to pay for a service which must be reported to the IRS.  If there is any question, call the Payroll Office, 356-1063, before the check is written.

 

6.         STATEMENTS: The club advisor will receive a statement each month showing the activity for that month; i.e.,

deposits and money withdrawn and checks used.  The reconciled statement should be kept on file for two school years.

Fund Raising Policy

 

 

I.          Purpose

 

To define the fund raising policy and detail the necessary procedures.  The purpose of this is to appropriately govern fund raising so as to respect student, club, campus, and community needs.

 

 

II.         Philosophy

 

A.      Generally, dues are the primary source of revenue.  These revenues can be supplemented with periodic fund raising efforts.  If the nature of the club is such that significant financial resources are necessary for special projects, equipment, costumes, etc., students should be so informed prior to joining in order that they might know the extent of their financial commitment.  Fund raising projects should be limited in dollars and scope.

 

B.      The academic environment on campus should not be overshadowed by continual pressures placed on students to donate money and time towards a fund raising project. 

 

C.      Performances, rehearsals, and tours should be kept in compatible balance with student’s overall academic responsibilities.  Where feasible, clubs should raise money by sponsoring projects related to their field.

 

III.       Policy

 

A.      Student fund raising projects are limited to officially recognized student organizations on campus. Solicitation by independent or unauthorized groups is prohibited.

 

B.      Personal or individual funds (of students or advisors) are not to be used to initiate any fund raising effort; nor are they to be used to commit an organization to a fund raising program.

 

C.      The Organization Financial Board is the governing board which considers, reviews, and passes or declines             fund raising applications.

 

D.      Clubs are authorized to charge up to $15 per semester for dues (any dues exceeding this amount must have the approval of the OFB and Director of Student Activities

 

E.      No fund raising project or projects should be designed or calculated to raise more than 25 percent of the amount collected in dues for that semester.   (To raise more, the club must  have the written approval of the (O.F.B.).

 

F.       Only ASRC is authorized to sponsor campus wide activities.  This includes activities that could be construed as fund raising in nature.  Clubs and Organizations are not authorized to sponsor any such campus wide activities (such as dances, carnivals or other similar activities).  Any exceptions must  have the support and cosponsorship of ASRC.

 

G.         Off-campus fund raising projects must receive the endorsement of the Campus Development Office (in addition to prescribed procedures outlined below).

 

H.            The Organization Financial Board is organized to disburse a pre determined  portion of the ASRC budget to clubs and organizations. OFB meets twice monthly

 

 

 

IV.       Procedure

 

A.      Complete a  Fund-Raising Request form (enclosed in this booklet or may be obtained from the ASRC office) completed and submit it to the ASRC Executive Vice President.

 

B.      The Organization Financial Board will meet during the ensuing two-week period and consider the request.  Within two weeks after submitting the request the OFB  will notify the requesting organization’s advisor of the board decision.  These notifications will be in writing and signed by the following:  Chairman of OFB, ASRC Executive Vice President, and the ASRC Advisor to OFB.

 

C.      Funds will not be issued to clubs until after the activity and receipts of actual expenses have been submitted to ASRC.

Functions of the Organizational Financial Board (OFB)

 

 

I.          PURPOSE

            To establish duties, responsibilities, and guidelines governing the Organization Financial Board.

 

II.        ORGANIZATION

 

   A. The Organization Financial Board shall be composed of the following members:

1. A Chairman to be appointed by the Executive Vice President and approved by the ASRC Executive Council.

2. Two students to be appointed by the Executive Vice President and approved by the ASRC Executive Council.

            3. The ASRC Executive Vice President

            4. The ASRC Secretary/Treasurer or appointed secretary

            5. An ASRC advisor.

 

III.       RESPONSIBILITIES

 

   A. Approval of club fund raising projects.

1. The Organization Financial Board shall approve or disapprove all fund-raising projects submitted to the  O.F.B.  No recognized club or organization in the Inter-Organization Council may participate in fund-raising projects without O.F.B. approval.

 

2. The Organization Financial Board shall prepare guidelines to be used in determining whether to approve or disapprove fund-raising projects.

 

   B. Special Appeal to raise dues.

1. Any club desiring to assess its membership dues in excess of the $15 per semester rate, must have the written approval of the Organization Financial Board and the Vice President of Student Life.

 

   C. Disbursement of ASRC budget funds.

            1. The Organization Financial Board shall appropriate funds according to the following criteria:          

                        a. The nature of the proposed use and need

                        b. The number of students involved in the proposal

                        c. The number of students affected by the proposal

                        d. Other funding sources available

                        e. The Constitutional recognition given by the ASRC

                        f.  The organization’s effort to utilize other appropriate fund-raising activities

                        g. Past experience with club and suggested proposal

                        h. Prior involvement and support of I.O.C.

 

2. Insofar as it is possible, funds from other sources should meet a percentage of the total coast of the proposal.  The Organization Financial Board shall determine what the percentage shall be for each proposal.

 

            3. No Organization Financial Board funds will be allocated for personal expenses which include:

                        a.  meal costs

                        b. personal spending

                        c.  housing costs

                        d. any strictly social function of the requesting club

 

 

 

IV. PROCEDURE

  A. Fund-Raising Project

1. A Fund-Raising Request form (see insert -Fund Raising Request) must be obtained from the   ASRC office, completed club officers, and submitted to the ASRC Executive Vice President.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 

 

2. The Organization Financial Board will meet during the ensuing two-week period and consider the request. Within two weeks after submitting the request the OFB will notify in the requesting organization’s advisor of the decision of the board.  These notifications will be in writing and signed by the following: Chairman of OFB,     Executive Vice President, ASRC Advisor to OFB.

 

V.  FINANCIAL REPORT

 

   A.     Each club receiving funds from the Organization Financial Board shall summit  a report giving a description and  documentation of actual expenditures of  money received.  This report shall be filed with the Organization            Financial Board on a due date determined by the Organization Financial Board at the time the proposal is approved.

 

B.      If no report is received, the organization in violation will be denied further funding until report of expenditures  is received.

 

C.           Any money not used by the awarded club shall be returned to the Organization Financial Board.

Activity Planning Outline

 

Proper planning well in advance of the activity is the first and most important step in insuring a successful student activity or function.  The outline below may be used as a guide in planning an activity.  This outline must be adapted to meet the needs of the particular activity.  Without prior approval by the ASRC, clubs may not sponsor dances, etc. that include students other than its members.

 

 

NAME AND THEME OF ACTIVITY

 

PURPOSE(S) OF THE ACTIVITY

 

SPONSORING ORGANIZATION, COMMITTEE MEMBERS AND ADVISORS—List committee chairman, members and advisor(s).  Include their responsibilities, addresses and telephone numbers. 

 

CALENDAR & TASK LIST—As you are planning the activity, make a list of all tasks that must be accomplished.  Make a copy of the calendar and enter the tasks on the calendar.  Each member of the committee should have a copy of the calendar.  When follow-up meetings are held you may then refer to the calendar to be sure all tasks have been completed.

 

SCHEDULING—All scheduling should be done well in advance of the activity.  When scheduling an activity, give the scheduling office (2242) the following information:

                                    Date

                                    Time

                                    Location

                                    Number of participants expected

                                    Special instructions

 

DRESS—Indicate what clothing will be appropriate and inappropriate for this activity.

 

BUDGET—Indicate  sources of anticipated revenue and expenses and the revenue and expense account numbers.

Decorations:  A variety of decorations are available through the Dance Office (2280).  Plants, water fountain, etc., call Wayne Leatham (2466).  Go through purchasing for off-campus suppliers.

Guest Speaker Policy

 

 

I.          Purpose

 

 

            To indicate the procedure to follow when inviting a guest speaker to the Ricks College Campus.

 

 

II.         Policy

 

 

A.        All speakers invited to address a Ricks College assembly open to the general student body, must be approved by the Public Relations Office.  Approval must be obtained prior to any contact with the individual being considered.  Approval is not necessary if the speaker is a Ricks College Employee or if the speaker’s audience is limited to a club and its members only and not to the general student body.  If  there is any question about the approval of a particular speaker, the Public Relations office should be contacted.

 

B.         Any invitation to General Authorities and other General Church Officials, will be extended by the President of the College or his designee.  There should be no exception to this procedure.

 

C.        Failure to obtain proper approval prior to invitation of the speaker may result in loss of scheduling  privileges and cancellation of the program.

 

D.        It is a matter of courtesy that a written follow-up of confirmation be sent to the speaker finalizing all necessary details including topic and length of speech,  travel and lodging information, and guest standards.

 

E.         The sponsoring organization is responsible for arranging the program and care of the speaker.  In the event the speaker is a high-ranking official of church, government, etc., hosting will be the responsibility of the college Public Relations Office.

 

            F.         Criteria for selection of speakers.

1.         The speaker must be qualified by education, training, or experience to discuss the topic chosen   for presentation.

2.         The speaker must not advocate the overthrow of the government of the United States or of its constituent units by force, or in any way violate restrictions imposed for public safety. 

3.         During the presentation, the speaker must not advocate ideas contrary to a belief in a Divine Creator, nor take advantage of his forum in any way to demean the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints or its principles.

4.         The speaker must be made aware of Latter-day Saint standards of personal conduct which he is expected to maintain while on campus.

5.         The Public Relations Office is the contact to obtain approval for all speakers.  That office will process the requests and forward those that meet the required criteria to the President or the board of trustees for final approval.

            G.        Sponsorship

1.         Organizations may sponsor lectures or speaker programs for members only.  Speaker programs for the total student body will be sponsored by the Associated Students of Ricks College.

 

 

III.       Implementation

 

            A.        General Authorities

1.         Student committees and organizations are not to present speaker requests for processing  before consulting with the Director of Student  Activities for his/her approval.

            B.         Other Guest Speakers

1.         Each sponsoring group must consult with its advisor and submit a request for approval of guest   speakers to the Public Relations Office before any invitation may be extended.  It is suggested that these requests be submitted at least two months in advance of the date of the proposed presentation.  It is recognized that some speakers may become available unexpectedly without the usual two-month notice.  In these special situations, approval may be obtained, with a request for expeditious action.

                        2.         The following information must be included on the request:

a.         Education, training or experience that qualifies a speaker to address a college audience.

                                    b.         Excerpts of summaries of previous addresses, if available.

                                    c.         Speaker’s present position.

                                    d.         Significant background information or the speaker’s public record.

                                    e.         Signature of the faculty sponsor.

                                    f.          Recommendation of a faculty member.

g.                   Signature, address, & phone number of the responsible officer of the  sponsoring group.

Relationship between Church Organizations and Student Activities

 

 

I.  Purpose

            To define the relationship that should exist between Church organizations and groups that sponsor student activitiy programs.

 

II. Policy

 

A.        All contacts with bishoprics and stake presidents by chairing committee members and clubs or organizations are to be made through the Vice President of Student Life.

 

B.         Ward organizations are encouraged to participate in college and ASRC sponsored activities Homecoming, Women’s and Men’s Week, Christmas Tree Lane, etc.).       

           

            C.        Scheduling activities and meeting times between church and student organizations.

 

1.          Sunday activities and events are the responsibility of the stake and ward organizations.  Sunday events scheduled on the Ricks College campus are to be sponsored through the stake priesthood organization, (This includes all Sunday firesides, except for Men’s Week Fireside and Women’s Week Fireside).  Unless approved by the Vice President of             Student Life, no ASRC sponsored activities or club or organization activities may take place on Sunday.  This includes firesides, meetings, travel to local wards and stakes as a group, etc.

2.         Monday activities:   To avoid conflict with Family Home Evening, college and college organization sponsored activities will not be scheduled after 6 p..m.  Activities or meetings scheduled at an earlier hour may not extend into the 6 p.m. hour.

3.         Tuesday activities To avoid conflict with the young adult, or ward activities, college and college organization sponsored activities will not  be scheduled after 6 p.m.  Activities or meetings scheduled at an earlier hour may not extend into the 6 p.m. hour.

4.         Wednesday and Thursday evenings are normally used for scheduling meetings and activities for student organizations.

5.         Friday and Saturday evenings are normally used for scheduling all campus or major activity events sponsored by ASRC.

6.         Organizations may schedule early morning meetings on any given day, other than Sunday.  Such meetings or activities should not be scheduled prior to 6 a.m.

7.         Unless approval is given by the Stake President who has cleared the function with the Vice President of Student Life, wards and stakes are not to sponsor any activities on Wednesday, Thursday, Friday or Saturday.

8.         Exception to any of the above must be approved by the Vice President of Student Life.

 

D.                 If an organization desires to start a program aimed in any way at missionary work, a written proposal must be submitted to the Vice President of Student Life for approval.

Ricks College Club Travel Policy

 

I.                   Purpose

To document the campus-wide policy and procedures governing all club travel.

 

II.                Policy and Procedure

 

A.     College & Private Vehicles:  To promote proper safety and insurance coverage, all college-supplied transportation must be by Ricks College vehicles, public commercial carrier or other expressly approved modes of transportation.  Travel via private cars is strongly discouraged but may occur if college-supplied transportation is not reasonably available and only after  there has been verification of insurance coverage ($100,000 per person and $300,000 per accident) and approval of the Dean of Students  has been accomplished.  The advisor for the activity must also pre-approve any additional private vehicle transportation.  Students who travel in a private vehicle (driver or passenger) to a student activity, must complete and sign a waiver of liability a sample of which follows this policy.  The approval of the Dean of Students is necessary if private vehicles are the primary means of transportation to an activity.  Compliance with all elements of this student travel policy is expected when using a private vehicle.

 

B.     Advising & Supervision Requirements:  All off-campus student activities, require an advisor (faculty, administrative or staff personnel, or a non-employee that has been officially approved by the Vice President of Student Life) to supervise travel plans, to accompany the students, and to be responsible for the implementation of this policy.  Advisors are also responsible for giving reasonable instructions to be followed if the nature of the activity requires.  For most student activities, their should be at least one advisor for each 30 students or part thereof.  When a trip includes potentially hazardous elements or activities, the degree of supervision should increase proportionally.

 

 

C.     Student Conduct and Appearance:  The conduct and dress of all participants must be in accordance with the Honor Code at all times.  The advisor’s role includes the supervision of students in relation to conduct, appearance, and safety.  Dress standards will be specified for each group according to prescribed standards and the nature of the excursion.  Non-related students, faculty or staff of the opposite sex should not travel together unless trip participants include at least two members of both sexes.  When both sexes are traveling together, all individuals should exercise sensitivity and good judgement to avoid the appearance of impropriety.

 

D.    Request Procedure:  Start this early.  Sometimes it is not easy to get all the signatures.

1.      Complete the “Request for Off-Campus Activity” form including the advisor’s signature.  Make a copy of that form for your records.  Attach a printout of the Vehicle Request Form(s) to the Request for Off-Campus Activity.

2.      Secure the necessary signatures in order as they are listed:

a.  Clubs, organizations, and all other student travel sponsored by the ASRC:  Jim Sessions, Dean of Students.

b.      Club Sports Travel: Preston Haley, Director of Club Sports Activities and Jim Sessions, Dean of Students.

 

E.     Dispatching Ricks College Vehicles:  Ricks College vehicles may be requested using the Ricks College Internal Web.  Click on Vehicle Rental Request under College Services.

 

F.      Drivers:  Advisors are to drive college vehicles.  Should a student group have only one advisor and requires two or more vehicles, students may be authorized to drive, using a caravan arrangement, when the student has met the following conditions:

 

 

A.     Successfully completed the campus defensive driving course.

B.     Obtained a Ricks College student driving permit issued by Vehicle Dispatch. (be sure to check that the student has completed the training for the type of vehicle being used).

C.     Received Student Life approval (Jim Sessions, Dean of Students).

To promote greater safety on longer trips involving more than one participant having a qualified driver’s license and Ricks College Driving Permit, the drivers are encouraged to rotate the driving responsibilities every two to four hours depending upon circumstances.  It is also advisable, when traveling in a caravan, to keep in radio contact with other members in your group.

 

G.    Vehicle Occupancy:  The occupancy of each vehicle should comply with the specified capacity limits.  There must be a seat belt for the driver and each passenger and the drivers of vehicles should encourage vehicle occupants to use their seat belts.

 

H.    Inventory of Participants:  Prior to departure, advisors are responsible for having a complete inventory of participating students on the trip on file, with the Jim Sessions, Dean of Students.

 

I.       Same-Day Travel:  Same-day travel is any trip initiated from Rexburg and completed back to Rexburg during the same calendar day.  Advisors are encouraged, but not required, to accompany students when such travel does not exceed 150 miles.  At least two weeks before leaving, club advisors must submit a signed Request for Off-Campus Activity form to Jim Sessions, Dean of Students, for approval. 

 

J.      Extended Travel:  Extended travel is any trip initiated and completed during two or more calendar days.  Extended travel requires the supervision and accompaniment of full-time faculty or staff. At least two weeks before leaving, club advisors must submit a signed Request for Off-Campus Activity form to the Jim Sessions, Dean of Students.

 

K.    Overnight & Sunday Travel:  To promote safety while traveling, no overnight driving by college or private vehicle is permitted, under any circumstances, between the hours of 12:00 midnight and 5:00 am unless travel distance is not in excess of 50 miles one way.  Night travel in commercial vehicles with professional drivers is permitted.  When Sunday travel is absolutely necessary, the advisor should arrange schedules so the group can attend or hold at least one Sunday meeting service.

 

L.     Student Travel Restrictions:  Students on academic or standards probation may be restricted from participating in college-sponsored travel.  The Dean of Students Office, and not the sponsoring college organization, shall have the responsibility to enforce probationary restrictions.

 

M.  Exceptions:  Exceptions to this policy must be approved in writing by  Jim Sessions, Dean of Students.
