Ricks College News Release
February 23, 2001

Devotional: Elder C. Terry Warner

By TRENT TOONE
Ricks College News Bureau

REXBURG, Idaho — The principles of initiative and diligence were addressed by speaker C. Terry Warner at Tuesday’s Ricks College devotional.

Warner, a professor of philosophy at Brigham Young University in Provo, Utah, said, "Stepping out on our own to do what’s right, to obey the commandments of our own free will, to go beyond the minimum that’s expected – this draws into our lives the delivering and sustaining power of God. I’m going to call it the principle of initiative and diligence."

He addressed a common problem with people and this principle. He called it the "This Is Just the Way I Am" syndrome.

"This moral disease was widespread with I was your age, but it has become an epidemic now," he said. "It afflicts people who stay stuck in their present habits and patterns by telling themselves ‘This is just the way I am.’ They don’t become different because they use the way they are now as an excuse."

Warner said the message of initiative and diligence is simple – one must use agency.

"You must take the initiative to claim your blessings. Work diligently at becoming a Christ-like person, then the help from heaven comes."

Warner said that some claim the ability to change old habits or improve oneself is too hard.

"Examine the statement, it’s not an explanation, but an excuse," he said. "When we take a responsible step to do what is right and sacrifice what is wrong , there flows to us, often immediately, a quiet confirmation from the spirit of the Lord that we are doing right, and encouragement and strength to keep going."

He added, "Recognize from the beginning that it won’t be easy. Recognize that Satan will do everything imaginable to keep you from succeeding. As long as we are slouching around on the battlefield, while people’s souls are being won and lost, he doesn’t have to spend any effort to keep us from doing damage to his battle plan. But as soon as we rouse ourselves, he gets into a real fuss and focuses his big artillery on us."

Warner concluded with the challenge to take initiative in life and pursue the blessings life has to offer.

"If any of us here, during this hour together, has felt a spiritual nudge to take an initiative that will lead to these blessings, my hope is that we will go straightway and do it, and after that keep pressing forward with diligence. The Lord made this promise available to us by sacrificing everything for us. We can bring about its fulfillment by sacrificing everything required if ordered to obey and follow him and bring others souls to partake of his redeeming love."

Next Tuesday’s speaker will be Andrew Skinner, dean of religious education at BYU. He will speak at 2 p.m. in the Hart Auditorium. Devotionals are broadcast live and repeated at 9 p.m. on KRIC-FM.
