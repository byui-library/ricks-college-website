COURSE OBJECTIVES
 
1. To increase your knowledge, love, and testimony of The Book of       
    Mormon   by:
    a. Learning the story line of the first half of The Book of Mormon.
      
    b. Understanding the life-changing doctrines of The Book of Mormon.
    c. Understanding the major theme of The Book of Mormon  and learning 
       to find the sub-themes that relate to it.

    d. Relating your study of The Book of Mormon to your personal, current situation.   
 
    e. Learning scripture study skills that will be valuable to you throughout your whole life.          

COURSE POLICIES AND PROCEDURES

1. QUIZZES- Every day at the beginning of class over reading assignment for that day.
(Five points each.  No make-up quizzes except for school excused or medically excused absences.

2. SCRIPTURE READING ASSIGNMENTS- each day of class you will receive five points just for having 
   read the assignment.

3. ATTENDANCE- each day of class you will receive five points for being in attendance. In a class of
   this nature which only meets twice a week, attendance and participation is crucial. Missing class
   three times for unofficial, non-school excused  or medical-excused absences with a doctor's note 
   will constitute a 10% drop in your grade.  Six unexcused absences will constitute failure in the
   course.

4. ARTICLE SUMMARIES- You will read six articles related to our study of The Book of Mormon that you 
   will down load off my home page.  You will write a one page summary and response to the article.  
   Each summary will be worth 50 points.  To receive the full 50 points,  the summary must be turned
   in on time (the beginning of class on the date outlined in the schedule).  It must be type-written, 
   neatly presented and give evidence of careful consideration and thought.

5. SEMESTER PROJECT- Because you learn so much more from the scriptures when you are searching for and 
   focusing on specifics, I would like you to take one pre-determined topic and study it from a Book of 
   Mormon perspective  the entire semester. On the day of the final exam you will turn in a paper that 
   shows what you have learned from your study.  This project will be worth 250 points, so make it your
   finest work.  Be creative: keep a journal, mark all passages pertaining to your topic in a certain 
   color, use note cards, or put music to scripture. Do whatever is meaningful to you and then report 
   it in a beautiful, lasting way. Make it a significant, growing experience and then record how you 
   accomplished it and what you have learned. The paper itself needs to be from five to ten pages in 
   length with a one page summary explaining the process you used.  Make sure you work on this all along.
   If you procrastinate and throw it together, it will be evident, and your grade will be hurt. You may 
   choose your topic from the following suggestions or choose another that you have me approve.	
 	 a.  an understanding of the character, perfections and attributes of Deity
      (Joseph Smith's Lectures on Faith 3:7)
	 b.  what it means to be "favored" of the Lord and how to attain it
	 c.  the continual theme of "faith, mercy, and deliverance. I Nephi 1:20)
   d.  how to prepare for and have a powerful, successful mission
	 e.  the "teaching moments" of the inspired authors and editors of 
	     The Book of Mormon ("And thus we see," etc.)
	 f.   the creation, fall, atonement
	 g.  hope
	 h.  repentance/Godly sorrow
	 i.   the Abrahamic Covenant
	 j.   being born again/the mighty change of heart
	 k.  pride
	 l.   the natural man
	 m.  obedience
