Women athletes mark century of sports

by Allison King
COI00001@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

After a tumultuous start that didn’t comply with societal decorum, women’s athletics are alive and well — and legendary.

Several hundred years after their first chariot race in 396 B.C., women began making their mark with swimming.

Swimming

Aileen Riggin became the first athlete to win Olympic medals in both swimming and diving. She scored a silver in the springboard and a bronze in the 100-meter backstroke, making her the first athlete, male or female, to medal in both the 1941 Olympic swimming and diving competitions.

Baseball

In 1931, Kenesaw Mountain Landis, the baseball commissioner judge, banned women from professional baseball after 17-year-old pitcher Virne Beatrice “Jackie” Mitchell struck out Babe Ruth and Lou Gehrig in an exhibition game for the Chattanooga Lookouts. Landis voided Mitchell’s contract, claiming baseball to be “too strenuous” for women. The ban lasted until 1992.

Track and field

1946 ushered in the arrival of athletic equality. Alice Coachman became the first woman of color to be a member of the U.S. All-American Track and Field team. By 1948, nine of 12 members of the women’s team would be black.

Willye White, the only American to compete on five Olympic track and field teams, won her second silver medal at the Tokyo Games in the 4x100-meter relay in 1964.

Tennis

Maureen “Little Mo” Connolly, 16, became the first woman to score a Grand Slam in 1953. She won all four major world tennis matches U.S. Open, Wimbledon and the French and Australian Opens in a single season.

The 1970s brought an onslaught of female successes in sports, one of the most memorable being Billie Jean King’s win in the “battle of the sexes” tennis match against Bobby Riggs in 1973. King secured her victory in Houston in front of a 30,000 member audience and a TV audience of an estimated 50 million.

Boxing

Marion Bermudez furthered the progress in women’s athletics when she was named victor over a man in the formerly all-male Golden Gloves boxing tournament in Mexico City in 1975. Bermudez is also a national karate champion, a sport in which she battled and beat men, as well.

Basketball

Women of the ‘80s saw past the big hair and bright pink lipstick to jersies and running shoes and made their mark in sports history with athletes like Lynette Woodward. The Kansas native scored the most career points in the history of women’s college basketball.

In 1985, Woodward, after proving herself as a member of the gold-medal winning Olympic basketball team a year earlier, became the first woman to play for the Harlem Globetrotters. The following year, Jackie Joyner-Kersee became the first woman to break the 7,000 mark in the Olympic heptathalon with a gold medal.

Bobsled

Bobsled driver Jill Brakken and brakewoman Vonetta Flowers set a venerable precedent for women everywhere when they made Olympic history with their gold medal run in the debut of women’s bobsledding with a combined time of 1:37.36 in Salt Lake City in February. Flowers became the first black athlete to grab gold at the Winter Olympics.
