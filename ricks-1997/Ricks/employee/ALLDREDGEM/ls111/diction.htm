Types of dictionaries

Unabridged dictionaries are commonly found in libraries on dictionary stands.
They comprehensively cover all words that are known at the time that they are printed.
Check the date when looking for new words or slang expressions.
Titles of common unabridged dictionaries are:
  • Webster's Third New International Dictionary (Ref PE 1625.W36)
  • Funk & Wagnalls New Standard Dictionary (Ref PE 1625.S7)
  • Random House Dictionary (Ref PE 1625.R3)
An abridged dictionary has been shortened by including only the most common words or the vocabulary of a group.
For example, an abridged collegiate dictionary would have words needed by the average college student.
Titles of common abridged dictionaries are:
 
  • Random House College edition (Ref PE 1625.R34)
  • Webster's New Collegiate Dictionary (Ref PE 1628.W4M4)
  • Funk & Wagnalls Standard College Dictionary (Ref PE 1628.S586)
  • American Heritage Dictionary (Ref PE 1625.A54)
A historical dictionary gives the etymology or derivation of words.
A word at the time of Shakespeare may now have different meanings than common today.
Reading historical materials require using these specialized dictionaries.
  • Oxford English Dictionary (Ref PE 1625.O87)
  • Dictionary of American English on Historical Principles (Ref PE 2835.C7)
  • Dictionary of Americanisms on Historical Principles (Ref PE 2835.D5)
Some dictionaries emphasize certain aspects of the language.
Some dictionaries give just slang words.
Slang includes popular expressions which may not be considered proper in formal use.
Some slang may have vulgar, obscene or profane connotations.
In academic settings formal language usually does not include slang or nonstandard forms of expression.
Usage guides will set the standards for formal writing or expression.
A thesaurus is a dictionary that gives synonyms.
Poets are interested in words that fit the rhythm and rhyme of their work.
Dictionaries exist to give rhyming words.
Government and politics are full of acronyms (NASA), Initialisms (FBI), and abbreviations (v. for versus).
  • A Dictionary of the Underworld (Ref. PE 3726.P3)
  • Dictionary of American Slang (Ref. PE 2846.W4)
  • A Dictionary of Contemporary American Usage (Ref. PE 2835.E84)
  • Roget's II (Ref. PE 1591.R715)

  • Roget's International Thesaurus (Ref. PE 1592.R73)
  • The modern rhyming dictionary (Ref PE 1519.L37)
  • Reverse Acronyms, Initialisms, and Abbreviations Dictionary (Ref. PE 1693.G3)

Using a dictionary

Remember that preliminary pages often explain abbreviations and other special features.
End pages may have tables or charts.
The auxiliary pages may also have information, such as a list of colleges or cities.
A separate appendix may give geographical and biographical entries.

The table of contents or list of illustrations will help locate information on these preliminary or auxiliary pages.

If a dictionary is not found for the language or the subject,
look for reference or nonfiction books with a vocabulary or a glossary.
A "vocabulary" is a list of words from the subject with partial explanation.
Especially school textbooks may have a vocabulary section.

Glossary is a list of technical words or expressions in a subject or book.
As mentioned before, appendix or auxiliary sections of books may contain a glossary section.

The LDS version of the Bible has a dictionary, topical guide, gazetteer, and reference tools.
The LDS InfoBase CD-ROM set also has reference tools including a dictionary.

Dictionary order has become a synonym for alphabetical order.
Therefore, some reference books may be entitled "dictionary" because of their arrangement and not their content.
So if one book called a "dictionary" does not have what you need, try another.

Kinds of information

Elementary students often are tested on their ability to use dictionaries.
Team games can be used to develop skill with
  • alphabetical order,
  • guide words,
  • pronunciation,
  • syllabication and
  • derivation
  • etymology
  • synonyms
  • antonyms
Guide words are placed at the top of a page to help the user find the correct page alphabetically.
Pronunciation involves using various symbols to represent sounds.
Syllabication shows how the word is divided when the word is separated at the end of a line of print.

Derivation symbols tells when or where the word originated.
Derivation traces what root words are used to form the word.
Understanding from which language the word comes helps explain differences in phonic rules.
"Goethe" may not seem to be pronounced the way it was spelled except those who understand German language.
English language is a result of the many cultures from which it is drawn.

Etymology is the study of the word's history including both derivation and how the word's usage has changed.
Usage reflects how different areas use words in unique ways.
For example, "lift" in Britain is used for "elevator".

Meaning or definition is explained or shown through examples.
Synonyms (words with the same meaning) and
antonyms (words with the opposite meaning) also contribute to an accurate definition.
Illustrative quotations, such as those from Shakespeare give insight into meaning and derivation.

Maps and plates of illustration give pictures to provide reference points.
For example, maps can show where Guam is located in relation to other islands.

Geographical facts and history also give the significance of places.
Guam was the site of a major battle in World War II.

Biographical facts are needed because many words have origins in personal names.
A "curie" is one word that is associated with the scientists who discovered it.

A dictionary that contains geographical and biographical information is called an encyclopedic dictionary.
Some dictionaries may have a separate section with geographical and biographical information.

Purchasing a dictionary

Bargains in reference materials should be based on your needs.
Teachers may make recommendations based on their experience.
The American Library Association publishes periodicals which contains reviews:
  • Booklist and
  • School Library Journal .
Encyclopedias, Atlases & Dictionaries (Ref AE 1.E48-1995) discusses the different types of dictionaries, including electronic versions.

Some dictionary features are built into word processing programs.
Spell checking is easier in a word processing program than the traditional dictionary.
Thesaurus and grammar often are included into word processing programs.
Word wrap and alignment does away with the need that existed with typewriters to know where to divide at syllables. Dictionaries are one of the electronic publications that is available now that was not common a number of years ago.
 

History and value

William Caxton (c1483) produced a French-English dictionary.
Then as now, travelers needed a bilingual dictionary when visiting countries with a different language than their own.
Ancient writings were not useful until they could be translated.

The discovery of the Rosetta Stone in 1799 helped unlock many ancient languages.
The stone was written in Greek and two forms of Egyptian.
In 1822 the messages were finally deciphered.

In 1755, Samuel Johnson produced the earliest English language dictionary.
A facsimile reprint of this dictionary is available here at the McKay Library in the Reference collection. (Ref PE 1620.J6 1979)
As a facsimile reprint, letters appear as they were written in 1755.
Alternate spellings are given without preference.
Some word derivation is shown.
For example, gabardine comes from the Italian word "gavardina".
The meaning of the word or definition in Johnson's dictionary is "a coarse frock; any mean dress."
Examples of the word in literary works are shown.
This quote from Shakespeare's Merchant of Venice is given:

  • "You call me misbeliever, cut-throat dog, And spit upon my Jewish gabardine."
Noah Webster printed the first modern dictionary in 1828 which set the standard for spelling and pronunciation.
His dictionaries also distinguished American word usage from usage common in England.

Sir James Murray gathered historical background on words.
The Oxford English Dictionary traces the origin of words and their usage through time with examples.
While the British usage is emphasized, recent editions include English usage in other parts of the world.

Summary

Dictionaries are important reference tools for college students to use.
Different kinds of dictionaries are available for various needs.
