Women’s history celebrated in March

by Mikie K. Hayhurst
HAY01001@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

March is National Women’s History Month, and from east to west, women are being honored for their contributions to society past and present.

Idaho Senator Mike Crapo has dedicated a portion of his Web site to women who have enriched Idaho through their leadership and dedication. The site includes a timeline tribute to women of Idaho’s history, past and present. Women were chosen to represent a broad group of ladies whose contributions range from education to farmers to legislators.

The 2002 theme for NWHM is “Women Sustaining the American Spirit.” This theme was developed to recognize women’s courage and vision, to “set a standard for ourselves and provide a beacon of inspiration for future generations,” according to www.nwhp.org.

“It is clear that the strength of character that has always prevailed among Idaho women, and continues in abundance today, is one of our state’s greatest blessings,” Crapo said. “Idaho is the great state it is today because of those women who have been and continue to be dedicated to the welfare of Idaho’s natural resources, economy, political community and various industries.”

This year, six women have been chosen by the NWHM board. Alice Coachman, Dorothy Height, Dolores Huerta, Gerda Lerner, Patsy T. Mink and Mary Louise Defender Wilson are being showcased because of achievements that have inspired a larger sense of possibility for women in 21st Century America.
