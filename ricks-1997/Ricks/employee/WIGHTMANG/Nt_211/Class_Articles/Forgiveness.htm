FORGIVENESS


" . . . the sin of offense is not limited to those who give offense. Some of the greatest wrongs come because someone takes offense. Taking offense is such an easy sin and can be committed over a mere trifle. But when once we have become offended, we hug our grievances to our hearts with all our might. We usually will not give them up even to save our souls.

I have talked with a great many people who have become inactive in the Church. I have never yet talked with one who dropped out of activity because he did not believe the doctrine of the Church. I am sure there are some, but I have never talked with one. Everyone that I have talked with has become inactive because his feelings were hurt or he was not properly recognized, or didn't like someone, or he didn't like what someone had done. These obstructions in our way can be so small as to be barely visible, and yet we stumble and lose our blessings because of them." (Sterling W. Sill, Leadership, 2:39-40)


"Why is our salvation at stake when we fail to forgive others? Because such an act is '. . .the greater sin.' (D&C 64:9) Why is it 'the greater sin'? When we take the position of withholding forgiveness from our fellow men, we are attempting to block his progress towards salvation. This position is satanical and our motive is not Christlike. We are endeavoring to impede the progress of a living soul and deny him the forgiving blessings of the atonement. This philosophy is saturated with impure motives that are designed to destroy the soul. What greater sin is there than this? Perhaps many Latter-day Saints have failed to realize the seriousness of refusing to forgive others. But the Lord called it evil and for such we do stand condemned before Him and the greater sin remaineth." (Otten & Caldwell, Sacred Truths, p.314)

"I once heard of a man who became angry at the water master. He said, 'I will never use another drop of water out of this canal as long as you are water master.' With the same kind of logic we say, 'I will never go to church as long as you are bishop.' And thus we waste some of our most valuable resources trying to even up the score or outdo someone in giving offense." (Sterling W. Sill, Leadership, 2;42)

"Carrying a grudge is a loser's game. It is the ultimate frustration because it leaves you with more pain than you had in the first place. Recall the pain of being wronged, the hurt of being stung, cheated, demeaned. Doesn't the memory of it fuel the fire of fury again? Do you feel that hurt each time your memory lights on the people who did you wrong? Your own memory becomes a videotape within your soul that plays unending reruns of your old rendezvous with pain. Is this fair to yourself - this wretched justice of not forgiving?

"The only way to heal the pain that will not heal itself is to forgive the person who hurt you. Forgiving heals your memory as you change your memory's vision. When you release the wrongdoer from the wrong, you cut a malignant tumor out of your inner life. You set a prisoner free - yourself." (Lewis B. Smedes, Forgive and Forget, Harper & Row)


"Forgiveness heals terrible, tragic wounds, for it allows the love of God to purge your heart and mind of the poison of hate. It cleanses your consciousness of the desire for revenge. It makes place for the purifying, healing, restoring love of the Lord." (Elder Richard G. Scott, Ensign, May 1992, p. 33)

"Let us also learn to be forgiving of our parents, who, perhaps having made mistakes as they reared us, almost always did the best they knew how. May we ever forgive them as we would likewise wish to be forgiven by our own children for mistakes we make." (Pres. Ezra Taft Benson, Ensign, Nov.89, pp.6-7)

"Benedict Arnold betrayed his country because his feelings had been hurt and he wanted to get even. Cain was offended and became angry because the Lord did not accept his offering. The Lord tried to reason with Cain, saying, 'If thou doest well, thou shalt be accepted, and if thou doest not well, sin lieth at thy door.' (Moses 5:23) But logic doesn't have much power when our feelings have been hurt. Cain couldn't reason very well while he was upset. Finally he killed his brother Abel, whose offering the Lord had accepted. It is so easy to do perfectly stupid things when some offense is rankling in our hearts." (Sterling W. Sill, Leadership, p. 42)
