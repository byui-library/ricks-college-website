Teaching Philosophy: Do you think you can experience the power of the Book of Mormon as taught by President Benson because you were obligated to take the class for college credit or some graduation requirement ? Is it possible that taking the class for credit could actually get in the way of coming to understand and love this wonderful book? Is it also possible this class could be a tremendous blessing in your life to help you experience exactly what President Benson has suggested? Please, Please, Please understand there are two very important processes at work in this class. You will be graded on how well you are doing with both processes. The first, and most important process you'll be graded on is how has my study of the book Of Mormon increased my capacity to be a better latter-day saint. This grade will be computed by the power of the Holy Ghost and I most likely will never know your final grade. The Lord, and only the Lord, can judge how well you do in this area. However, I can promise you that He will grade you for your sincere or insincere effort. The other process we will measure is the effort you put forth to obtain an "A" grade with the Lord, or the first process. You are taking this class for college credit. My desire is to help you grow spiritually, to become a lifelong learner in the scriptures, while earning college credit, but not losing sight of the primary goal. The credit, the grade, attendance, while important, are of lesser importance when compared with the greater goal of receiving revelation and having the book speak to you as Mormon intended. I want you to come into this class prepared to be taught by the power of the Holy Ghost. It is foolish to think the Lord is going to unfold the mysteries of the scriptures because you studied them for college credit. The Lord is not going to open the book to your understanding without significant effort on our part. Therefore each class should move you a little closer to discovery of what the Lord has in store for you, not just here at Ricks, but for life-each class then becomes a revelatory session which moves you to deeper covenant making and keeping. Fulfilling the basic requirement will earn you somewhere between a "C" or a "B". An "A" grade is the result of effort put forth above and beyond what I would require of you. Whatever you produce to earn an "A" will be discovered by you, through the power of the Holy Ghost, and will have greater importance and require greater effort for you accomplish than any assignment or exam I could ever make.
 

Grading: Students are expected to read assigned scripture blocks and talks, come to class spiritually prepared to invite the Holy Ghost, ask questions, participate in class discussions, and learn a process for and develop the discipline of recording effectively the "thoughts" and "feelings" (D&C 8:2) that come as a result of studying the scriptures. Class attendance is expected, as is your participation in and contributions to class. Your grade will be computed as follows:
 

Preparation points: 80% of grade is made of preparation for class, participation in class, quality of assigned work, and personal progress.)

Serious Study points: 20% of grade.
 

Preparation points: These points are earned by coming to class each day having done the following:

1) Discovering ideas particularly important to you from reading.

2) Marking important verses of scripture quoted in the talks.

3) Writing summary questions: formulate two very good summary question from the reading. Note: the best questions (level three questions) often have multiple answers or even sometimes cannot be answered at all. You will keep a cumulative list of your questions and answers.

4) Answering questions which I pose to you. These are designed to push you even harder in your preparation.

5) Class attendance-attendance points will not awarded after the opening song has begun.

6) Writing summary papers-these must be typed and kept as part of your overall portfolio.
 

Serious study points: As you have experienced a serious study of the Book Of Mormon in what ways have you begun to experience life in greater abundance? What invitation have you accepted from the Holy Ghost as a result of your study of the Book of Mormon? As you reflect on what you came into this class knowing, how are you different now as the class ends because of your pondering, insights, and efforts to improve? In what way can you demonstrate you have grown significantly this past few months? Some ideas:

    • Study the book with a nonmember in mind, read with the idea of how would I teach this to them? Then put these ideas together in lessons or letters to this individual.
    • Keep a personal set of small plates wherein you record the impressions or will of the Lord as it has been revealed to you throughout the semester.
    • Develop a new way of marking the scriptures that has deepened value to you and those you'll teach in the mission field or perhaps your own posterity.
    • Develop a study guide that shows your path of study and learning.
    • Identify significant scriptures for mastery and memorization.
    • A portfolio of your study of the Book of Mormon.
    • Other ideas
