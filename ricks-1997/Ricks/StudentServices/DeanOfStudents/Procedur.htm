HONOR CODE PROCEDURES

         I. INTRODUCTION

A.  Purpose: The BYU-Idaho Honor Code specifies personal standards of behavior         expected of all members of the university community. Students who violate a provision of the Honor Code should be treated fairly and disciplined appropriately with a view to helping them develop into men and women who personify the ideals and principles contained in the Honor Code.

B.  Administrative Responsibility: BYU-Idaho is committed to providing procedures which assist in administering individual compliance and commitment to the Honor Code. The Dean of Students is responsible for overall administration under the direction of the vice president of Student Life. The Dean of Students Office has the responsibility to safeguard the interests of both the student and the university by application of the Honor Code procedures.

C.  Violations of the Law: Students in the university community have the rights and obligations of citizenship. They are responsible for their personal conduct under federal, state, and local law, being neither excused nor protected from civil and criminal sanctions by their status as students. Violations of the law neither require nor preclude university disciplinary action under the Honor Code. University discipline is intended to protect the integrity and purposes of the university and maintain a fair and equitable balance between the interests of the student and the university.

D.  Scope: This procedure does not address the application of standards of the Honor Code to university employees, nor does it address academic probation or disqualification.


II.   ORGANIZATION OF THE HONOR CODE COUNCIL

A.  Composition of the Council

 The Honor Code Council shall consist of seven voting members: four members of the university faculty or administration, three students, and the Dean of Students or his/her representative as an ex-officio member of the council. The ex-officio member will serve as a nonvoting advisor to the council.

B.  Selection and Tenure of Council Members

1.  The President of the university shall, upon the recommendation of the Student Life Vice President, appoint four members of the faculty or administration (one of whom shall be designated as chair) to be members of the council. The President shall also appoint two or three faculty members or administrators as alternate council members. These council members and alternates shall serve until released by the President.

2.  The Student Life Vice President shall select a panel of student council members from a slate of nominees submitted to him/her by the president of the Associated Students of BYU-Idaho. Only three students will be asked to serve in any meeting of the council. Student council members remain on the council until the end of the academic year in which they are appointed.

C.  Secretary

1.  There shall be a secretary present at every meeting of the council. The Student Life Vice President shall select one or more secretaries from among those employed by the university to serve as secretary to the council. In the event that an appointed secretary is unable to attend a meeting of the council, the council chair may select another secretary employed by the university to serve on an ad hoc basis.

2.  The duties of the secretary shall include taking minutes of all council business, preparing a summary of the proceedings for the council chair to review and approve, and submitting these documents along with all supporting notes to the Dean of Students.

III.  JURISDICTION

A.  Academic Standards: Questions of academic probation or disqualification are not within the council's jurisdiction, although academic dishonesty or cheating may constitute grounds for council action.

B.  Nonstudents: Allegations or reports of misconduct on the part of nonstudents residing in approved student housing may be reviewed by the council, but any sanction imposed on a nonstudent is limited to permanent or temporary denial of the privilege to register as a student and removal from living in approved housing. Such information relative to nonstudents may be given to the Director of Admissions and Records.

IV.  PROCEDURES

A.  Initiation of Allegation

 Any person having knowledge of an infraction of the Honor Code may provide the information to the Dean of Students Office. Reports of infractions may be initiated by oral or written statement.

B.  Dean of Students Action

1.  The Dean of Students Office will conduct an investigation into any reported allegation(s) to determine whether or not further action is merited.

2.  The Dean assigned to the case will visit with the student and discuss the nature of the infraction and the evidence against the student. The student may present the Dean information and the names of individuals having firsthand knowledge in response to the allegations. This review may result in the Dean imposing upon the student any of the disciplinary sanctions listed in this document.

3.  In the event a student refuses or chooses not to meet with the Dean, action may be taken against the student based on the investigation. Some actions which may be taken are: blocking the student from taking tests or from attending class, placing a hold on the student's records at the end of a semester, suspending the student immediately, or blocking the student from registering in a future semester, block, or term.

4.  If it is determined after review by the Dean that the student's infraction merits suspension, the student may choose to withdraw, understanding that a needs clearance hold will be placed on their record, or they may request that a review of the case be made by the Honor Code Council. The Dean will then give the student a copy of the "BYU-Idaho Honor Code Procedures" and the Notification of Student Rights.

5.  The student may exercise his/her option to a meeting before the Honor Code Council by signing the Notification of Student Rights on the appropriate line. This must be done within 24 hours (not counting nonschool days) after receiving the Notification of Student Rights. Any request for extension of the 24-hour period due to extenuating circumstances requires approval of the Dean of Students or an appointed designee and requests must be made in writing in a timely manner.

6.  A student may elect to withdraw from school any time prior to a determination being made by the council. If the student chooses to withdraw, the matter will immediately be withdrawn from the jurisdiction of the council.

7.  If it is determined by the Dean of Students that it would be dangerous, extremely inappropriate, or disruptive to allow the student to continue in school pending the council meeting, the Dean of Students may direct that the student be asked to leave immediately, so long as the student is accorded a meeting before the council not later than three school days after the removal.

8.  Prior to meeting before the council, the student's disciplinary file will be given to the chair of the council, who thereafter becomes responsible for processing the case under the council's direction.

9.  Prior to the council meeting, the Dean handling the case will inform the student of the nature of the evidence which will be placed before the council. Unless the student is independent or circumstances dictate that parents should not be notified, the Dean will assist or encourage the student to notify his/her parents of the facts of the case before the meeting. The Dean may, for good cause, choose not to notify the student's parents.

C.  Honor Code Council Meeting

1.  If a council member's association with a student appearing before the council is such that he/she is unable to render a fair and impartial decision, the member has a duty to disqualify himself/herself. The student may request that the chair disqualify any member of the council when the student shows that a bias exists on the part of that member towards the student.

2.  Both the Dean handling the case and the student shall be present in the meeting. It is the responsibility of the Dean to present the facts of the case to the council. No information shall be presented except in the presence of the student. Facts presented should be relevant, material, and reliable. The council shall assume no conclusions, but reach its decisions solely on the basis of information presented.

3.  The council shall invite and encourage the student to comment upon conclusions which might reasonably be drawn from the facts and to offer alternative interpretations in whole or in part. The student may question any person making a statement before the council. Furthermore, the student may present facts in his/her own behalf, call and question persons having first-hand knowledge, and offer his/her own statement or details. The council shall request persons having first-hand knowledge to appear or offer written statements if the student is, through his/her own efforts, unable to secure their cooperation.

4.  The student shall not be required to make any statement. If he/she should choose to make a statement, he/she may refuse at any point to answer any question.

5.  The student may be accompanied to the meeting by an advisor of their choice. The advisor may be a faculty or staff member, fellow student, parent, or other person so long as the availability of the advisor does not hamper the timeliness of the meeting. The advisor does not represent the student during the meeting and will not be permitted to address the council. The student will be expected to speak for himself/herself at all times.

6.  Honor Code Council meetings are closed-door meetings in the interest of the student and the university. Individuals providing information or statements shall be utilized at the discretion of the council chair and will be invited into the meeting at the appropriate time. In no event shall tape or other electronic recordings be made of any meetings, although any person present may take notes on the proceedings.

7.  Witnesses or persons giving character statements may be invited to address the council during the meeting. The chair may limit the number of persons providing character statements to two unless the student demonstrates good cause why more than two should speak. Witnesses or those giving character statements will be invited to make their statements as directed by the chair of the council.

8.  The student may at any time request changes of schedule in the interest of a more adequate presentation of his/her case. Such requests will be considered with the council chair, the student, Assistant Dean of Students handling the case, and the Dean of Students present. If there appears to be a compelling reason to postpone the review, the Dean of Students may cancel or reschedule the review to a more appropriate. 

9.  The chair of the council has the responsibility to insure that procedural fairness is observed during the review process. The chair or his/her designee should (a) make an initial response to any procedural question that arises, (b) alert the council to the procedural implications of any action they may wish to take, (c) identify any inconsistencies between the demands of fair procedure and the actions of the council or its individual members at any point in the proceedings, and (d) in situations where he/she feels it is desirable or the council instructs him/her to seek further guidance, seek out and present to the council whatever relevant information may be available in the experience of previous councils or from other sources. The council should nevertheless retain its ultimate authority to determine its own procedures within the framework of these principles and the dictates of fairness.

10.  A student who does not appear may still be subject to council action so long as the council is satisfied that the student has been given adequate notice of the charges and has had sufficient opportunity to respond.

D.  Council Decisions

1.  All deliberations of the council shall be in private. The council may determine to use any disciplinary sanction listed in this document. They may choose to uphold the recommendation of the Dean of Students Office or use any other sanction or stipulation which would be in the best interest of the student and the school.

2.  If a student is placed on probation, the council may recommend probation stipulations and the dean assigned to the case will implement appropriate probation stipulations which would serve to strengthen the student's commitment to the Honor Code, dress and grooming standards, or housing rules and regulations.

3.  Five members of the council constitute a quorum. Decisions shall be made by a simple majority of the voting members. The secretary is not a voting member of the council.

4.  The council chair will deliver two copies of the decision of the council in writing to the Dean of Students. The Dean of Students or his designee will review the decision of the council with the student and the Dean handling the case.

5.  The council chair will initiate appropriate steps to notify the student in a letter setting forth the council's decision in the case. Copies of the letter will be sent to the Student Life Vice President, to the Dean of Students, and, unless the student either is married or independent, to the student's parents. The council chair may, for good cause, direct that no copy be sent to the student's parents.

6.  The chair does not vote except in the case of a tie.

E.  Sexual Assault Victim's Rights

1.  The alleged victim of a sexual assault has specific rights under "the Student Right to Know and Campus Security Act." All rights enumerated in the Honor Code procedures apply to the alleged victim as well as the accuse

2.  The victim of an alleged sexual offense may choose to report the accused to the   Dean of Students Office for assessment of appropriate disciplinary sanctions provided under the Honor Code Procedures.

3.  The alleged victim may be present at the disciplinary meeting of the accused and has the right to make a statement, present witnesses, and be accompanied by an advisor of his/her choice.

4.  The alleged victim has the right to not have irrelevant past sexual history discussed during any council meeting.

5.  The alleged victim has the right to know the outcome of any disciplinary meeting held in regards to an accused individual and to be informed of any options available at the point of the council decision.

6.  An alleged victim is entitled to have the proceedings of the meeting kept confidential.

V.  ADMINISTRATIVE REVIEW BY THE DEAN OF STUDENTS AND/OR STUDENT LIFE VICE PRESIDENT

A.  Request for Mitigation of Sanction

1.  A student who has received a major sanction by the Honor Code Council may, within two school days of receiving notification as outlined above, petition the Dean of Students, in writing, to review the procedure of the meeting to determine if there should be a mitigation of the sanction imposed. If the Dean determines that there are grounds for the Student Life Vice President to review the case, the Dean will review the case with the Vice President or an assistant. Consideration for mitigation will be based on the following: (a) the student establishes to the satisfaction of the Dean and/or the Vice President that he/she was denied a fair council meeting; or (b) the student establishes to the satisfaction of the Dean and/or the Vice President that since the council meeting new and material evidence has been discovered which, had it been introduced at the council meeting, would probably have changed the decision. The petition for mitigation and review shall be in writing and shall contain a summary of the case, a statement of the sanction imposed, and reasons why the decision should be modified.

2.  The Vice President and/or Dean may, at his/her discretion, either grant or deny the student's petition. In either case, he/she shall inform the student of his decision, in writing, within two school days after receiving the student's petition.

3.  If the Vice President and/or Dean agree to review the case, they may permit the student to make an oral presentation in addition to any written statements or explanations he/she desires.

4.  The Vice President's and/or Dean's review shall determine whether or not a different outcome should be implemented in the student's behalf.

5.  The Vice President and/or Dean shall notify the student verbally of his/her decision and submit written notification setting forth his decision in the case. Copies of the letter shall be sent to the Honor Code Council chair, the Dean of Students, and to the student's parents if the parents had previously been notified.

6.  The decision of the Vice President and/or Dean is final.

B.  Request for Second Meeting

Within 60 days after notice of a decision has been delivered to him/her, any student who has received a major sanction as outlined in Section VII D may, in writing, petition the Student Life Vice President to require the Honor Code Council to hold a second meeting. The Vice President may order a second meeting if, and only if, (a) the student establishes to the satisfaction of the Vice President that he/she was denied a fair and impartial council meeting; or (b) the student establishes to the satisfaction of the Vice President that since the council's meeting new and material evidence has been discovered which, had it been introduced at the council meeting, would probably have changed the decision. If a second meeting is ordered, the council will follow the same procedures as in the original council meeting.

VI.  RECORDS AND READMISSION

A.  Records and Reports

1.  Any student who is placed on a major disciplinary sanction shall have the right to place in his/her disciplinary file a statement of his/her own regarding the case.

2.  Five years after a student graduates, without further enrollment, or five years after the end of the last period of enrollment by a student who has received no degree, the contents of all Dean of Students Office files pertaining to that student shall be disposed of as follows:

a.  Records necessary to explain an expulsion, suspension, or needs clearance of any student will be transferred to the BYU-Idaho archival vault.

b.  All other records will be destroyed.

3.  All personal notes kept by the Dean of Students or assistant deans are private and not subject to disclosure.

B.  Readmission: The conditions by which a student who is suspended may be readmitted shall be specified by those making the decision--the Dean of Students Office or the Honor Code Council. Readmission to BYU-Idaho or to any other unit of the Church Educational System (CES) is subject to the fulfillment of those conditions. Unless otherwise stated in writing to the student at the time he/she is suspended, and assuming that a student otherwise qualifies for admission to the CES unit, the following conditions shall apply:

1.  An expelled student will ordinarily not be readmitted.

2.  A student who is suspended, except in the case of expulsion, may petition the Dean of Students for clearance to apply for admission upon the expiration of the specific period of suspension (or if no period is specified, one year from the date of suspension). This does not mean that the suspended student will automatically be readmitted to BYU-Idaho; it only means that the petition will be considered. If the petition for clearance is granted, the student's application will be considered along with all other students who are applying to BYU-Idaho. The petition for clearance to seek admission must be in writing and include the following: (a) a letter from the student requesting readmission and indicating his/her present activities and goals as well as what he/she has done to overcome the circumstances which gave rise to suspension, (b) a letter from the student's current bishop (or ecclesiastical officer) verifying the student's personal commitment and worthiness in the same manner and to the same degree as required of new applicants for admission to the university, and (c) in the case of a dependent, unmarried student, a letter from the student's parent or guardian (or, in unusual circumstances, some other reliable person) affirming the student's good character. Additionally, the student will have to provide documentation that any conditions required at the time of suspension have been completed.

VII.  DISCIPLINARY SANCTIONS

Where appropriate, disciplinary authorities may impose one or more of the following sanctions for violations of the Honor Code:

A.  Sanctions

 Disciplinary sanctions may vary from case to case or even among those involved in the same case based on circumstances and facts of the case. Each sanction should reflect each person's involvement.

1.  Counsel and Education

 Counsel and education is used when a student has committed an Honor Code violation which is one of immaturity, thoughtlessness, or lack of judgment rather than disregard for and intent to violate the principles and behaviors associated with the Honor Code. The student is counseled regarding the nature and significance of the violation. The behavioral requirements of the Honor Code are clarified. There is no further contact with the student unless there are subsequent reported Honor Code violations.

2.  Honor Code Warning

 Honor Code warning is used when the student may not have been aware of the seriousness of the violation and the impact on self and/or others was limited. The student is given a warning letter explaining the behavioral requirements of the Honor Code. The letter may also outline any conditions required to be satisfactorily completed in order for the student to return to good Honor Code standing. This option is not to be used for such violations as alcohol use and/or theft.

3.  Honor Code Probation

 Honor Code probation is used when a student has committed an Honor Code violation which has serious implications for self and/or others. In order to be returned to good Honor Code standing, the student is required to satisfactorily complete specified conditions.

4.  Honor Code Suspension Withheld

 Honor Code suspension withheld is used when a student has committed serious violations of the Honor Code and facts or circumstances dictate that the student be permitted to continue with the understanding that any further violation(s) may result in suspension.

5.  Honor Code Short-term Suspension

 Honor Code short-term suspension may be used in lieu of immediate suspension when the circumstances of the individual case indicate that the violation is serious, but there is not a threat to the safety or well-being of the campus community. Suspension may be for not more than five (5) days.

6.  Referral to Mentor

 Referral to mentor is used alone or in conjunction with other actions when a student's insight regarding his/her Honor Code violation can be most effectively enhanced by working with someone who can serve as a role model. Individuals who most frequently fall into the category of mentors are bishops, professional counselors, family members, and faculty members. If there are no additional violations, no further contact is maintained with the student.

7.  Honor Code Needs Clearance

 Honor Code needs clearance is used when a student would otherwise be suspended from the university for a violation of the Honor Code and chooses, in light of the information presented, to voluntarily discontinue. The student discontinues from the university (including current classes) and is prevented from subsequent enrollment until satisfactory completion of the specified conditions is verified and the Honor Code registration hold has been removed.

8.   Honor Code Suspension

 Honor Code suspension is used when the student's violation is egregious or repetitive. The student may be required to discontinue immediately from current classes or at the end of the term or semester as determined by individual circumstances. The student is prevented from subsequent enrollment until satisfactory completion of the specified conditions is verified and the Honor Code registration hold has been removed.

9.  Honor Code Expulsion

 Honor Code expulsion is used (a) when a student, after having been suspended once, returns to the university and again seriously violates the Honor Code or (b) when a student is involved in egregious violations, particularly if a serious threat to others is present. The student may be required to discontinue immediately from current classes or at the end of the term or semester as determined by individual circumstances and is permanently prevented from subsequent enrollment.

10.  Right of Access--Banning

 BYU-Idaho is a private institution; and all grounds, walkways, roads, parking areas, and buildings owned by the university are private property. As such, the university retains the right and legal authority to limit or otherwise restrict access to the campus for any reason it may deem appropriate. Individuals determined to fall within the purview of this policy will be banned from campus; and, if banned, will be subject to arrest and prosecution for Criminal Trespass in violation of Idaho Code 18-7008(8) if they return to campus without authorization.

11.  Withholding the Posting of a Degree or Denying Graduation Participation

 Withholding the posting of a degree or denying graduation participation is used in all cases of probation and on a case-by-case basis, when criteria such as the following exist for withholding the posting or denying graduation for violation of the Honor Code when all other requirements for graduation have been met. Graduation may be delayed or denied for students who violate the BYU-Idaho Honor Code, whose ecclesiastical endorsement has been withdrawn, or who have unmet financial obligation with the university. The criteria include:

a. Excommunication, disfellowshipment, or disaffiliation from the Church.

b. Serious violation of position of trust or responsibility within the university.

c. Serious criminal activity or offense, including predatory behavior or crimes against another person, or the discovery of such a prior offense.

d. Egregious academic dishonesty.

e. Discovery of a violation that occurred prior to graduation and, if it had been detected earlier, would have resulted in dismissal from the university.

f. If a student is not in good Honor Code standing, the university will not award a degree.

Any Dean of Students Office hold or probation must have been properly cleared before graduation. All decisions to delay or deny posting of a degree or participating in graduation ceremonies due to misconduct are made in consultation with the university Office of General Counsel. If the diploma is to be held, the student may request a university review through the Dean of Students.

12. Honor Code Transcript Notation

 Honor Code transcript notation is used only when a student's behavior is determined to be of sufficient risk to others, or when there is repeated academic dishonesty of probable interest to other institutions, that the university may have a responsibility to caution those with a legitimate need to know that a problem exists by placing a transcript notation. A notation would not be placed for violations particular to the LDS Church and of no civil or criminal interest to others. All decisions to place a transcript notation due to misconduct will be made in consultation with the university Office of General Counsel. If the notation is to be made, the student may request a university review through the Dean of Students.

B.  Suspension

Students who have a disciplinary sanction that results in suspension will not be allowed to participate in any curricular or extracurricular activities and may be denied campus access and asked to move from his/her approved housing unit. In addition, he/she will not be allowed to return without obtaining clearance through the Dean of Students Office.

                C.  Disciplinary Conditions

Disciplinary conditions may be varied in scope and nature and should be appropriate at all times. Some conditions are:

1. Monetary fine

2.  Community service hours

3. Loss of privileges, for example:

a.  Loss of parking privileges
b. Removal from housing
c. Relocation of residence
d. Loss of scholarship
e. Denial of campus student employment
f. Loss of privilege to participate in extra-curricular or curricular activities
g. Restitution for damages or losses as a result of misbehavior
D. Major Disciplinary Sanctions

A major disciplinary sanction would be considered any sanction that results in a loss of at least $300 to the student or his/her financial sponsor or results in the student being suspended from school.

E. Implementation of Sanctions

Although violations of any standard of the code may result in suspension, a reasonable relationship should exist between the seriousness and repetitive nature of a violation and the severity of the sanction imposed. The disciplining authority may withhold the implementation of any sanction it imposes at any time and on whatever conditions it stipulates.
