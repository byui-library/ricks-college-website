 MASKING,

CHANNELS, FILTERS, GRADIENTS

AND SELECTING

IN PHOTOSHOP

 

1. First, a few definitions:

· Selecting: This is basically cutting out a portion. You do it by shape or by color using the marquee tool, the quick mask tool and the magic wand selection tool -- not to mention the eraser tool with quick mask on. You can then paste the selection on another image or manipulate it separately from the rest of the image.

· Masking: This is covering one area with another, so only the portions you want to see are visible. You do this by pasting one layer over another or one channel over another.

· Channels: These show colors or elements of the image from a light perspective. With an RGB image, for example, you'll have one channel for Red, one for Green and one for Blue -- and one that shows them all together. You may add additional channels by adding a mask or cutting out a selection.

- Filters: These are changes applied to the entire image or portions of the image. These filters might make the image or portions look like a watercolor painting or a mosaic or similar things.

- Gradient: This is a selection that applies colors across an image. It may be complete or a translucent effect. The colors fade from one color to another.

2. Open a picture.

If you wish to follow along, open iceberg2.gif -- convert it to RGB under your mode submenu of the image menu before doing anything else to it. You may have to save it as a different format when you finish. That's OK for this exercise. The reason for doing the conversion to RGB will become clear later. (We've saved it as indexed color to make it into a Compuserve gif, so it will take up less space on your disk.) Make sure the colors in your tool pallette are default white and black -- click on the little box that looks like two squares and are black and white to return to default colors.

With this exercise, you'll first select an area and then apply a filter to one portion of the image, then you'll apply a mask to the image as a whole. The mask you will be doing is a gradient mask. Basically you will be making it so your image fades out from one side of it to another. This will help you complete one of your in class assignments. At the end of this assignment will come two other techniques.

For the class assignment, make sure you read all of these instructions. Some of the instructions will be useful on one assignment and others on another.

3. Make a selection of the image using one of the tools.

In the example, please note that I used the ellipitical marquee tool to select one nice portion. If you were selecting just portion of the iceberg or, you might have used the lasso tool. You might also have used the magic wand tool and selected similar or grow. You might apply the mask mode -- the small button at the bottom of your tools pallette -- and red will apply over everything selected (or not depending on your preferences) and use an eraser to get only what you want selected.)

4. Under the Select menu, select inverse, after making the selection

The only difference you will notice is that the dotted line will also be around the outside of the area you have selected.

5. Apply a filter to the inverse area, that is everything that you want to focus on.

Any filter that suits your tastes will do. (I have applied sponge under artistic in the filter menu.) You must be in RGB, not indexed color to make this work. Later, for web publishing, you may need to shift it to indexed color to save it as a CompuserveGif format. Here's how the filter looks with the selected area still active -- that's what the dotted lines are for:

6. Select inverse again.

This will give you the original selection again. We won't give you a photo here because it will look exactly like the photo in number six except the selected area is only the middle.

7. Under the select menu, go to Save selection as.

This dialog box will appear. Hit OK.

8. Your channels pallette should change from having four elements -- in an RGB file, to having five and even more.

Here is a sample channels pallette for this image:

 

9. Under the select menu, select none.

The selection lines will disappear.

10. Make a new channel by hitting the new channel button.

The new channel button is at the bottom of the window. It is left of the one shaped like a trash can and sort of looks like a folded over piece of paper. (Or new channel from the menu on the channels pallette-- the menu emerges when you click on the small, right-facing arrow at the top of this pallette.) If you wish, name the new channel as gradient. You will be seeing only that channel in your window -- the picture will go white -- meaning nothing is in that new channel.

11. Next, double click the gradient tool.

That's the black and white rainbow, third from the bottom on the right side of your tools pallette and the gradient tool pallette will appear:

Select black, white and make the opacity 100 percent, not the 39 percent shown here. To set it at 100, merely move the arrow beneath opacity to the right. To get new gradient options, just click on the down-facing arrow to the right of where it says "Black, White" in this demonstration.

12. Next, hold down the shift key and drag the gradient across the image you have open.

(It is important to hold down the shift key.) You can go any direction you wish. I go from left to right. Your white window now looks black, gray and white.

13. Next, you'll load that gradient as a selection and fill it with a color.

(This is a gradient acting as a mask.) So, click on the RGB channel in your channels pallette -- the top one -- to display the complete image instead of the black, gray and white gradient. The gradient has disappeared from your view. Its effects will emerge later.

14. Next, you will load a selection.

You can do that by clicking and holding on the gradient and dragging it to the load selection icon -- the small one two over from the small new icon we talked about in step 10 and three over from the trash can -- at the bottom of your channels pallette.

15. A selected area will appear over the image -- a dotted line -- that shows about half the image.

This is how it will look:

16. Hit delete, and the the background color will emerge as a gradient

In this case the background should be white -- the default, but you can do any color you wish.

17. The result is a slight fade.

18. Notes.

1. You might copy a small portion out of the background layer in your layers pallette and then, after you've completed step 17, switch from channels to layers. Then paste in a new layer -- an overlay -- to make a certain portion appear without the masked gradient.

2. You can also apply gradients -- not as masks really -- to layers -- such as words. You might try one that goes to transparent to get the words to fade. In the example, we've merely applied a rainbow.

21. One more tip.

When using the magic wand tool, if you double click the tool, its pallette will appear. With that pallette, you can adjust tolerances. The lower the tolerance number, the more precise the color you will select. The higher the tolerance, the more breadth of color you will select -- the wider area. A higher tolerance, put another way, selects similar colors instead of exact matches. This might help you on some unusual selections.
