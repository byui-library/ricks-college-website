Snowman wars receive community attention

by Jenna-Leigh Tracy
TRA99010@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Once there were 40 snowmen, tall, tall, tall; in the sun they melted, small, small, small.

Half-melted snowmen line three neighbors’ yards along Highway 191. These snowmen tell the story of good-hearted neighborly fun.

On a January day Donna and Kyle Nelson found the snow outside to be perfect building material.

“We discovered wet snow so we went outside and built snowmen,” Donna said.

The Nelson’s neighbors, the Schmidts, were clearing out their driveway when Donna and Kyle volunteered to help.

Before either party knew it, they were having a snowball fight. Then the Nelson’s decided to continue the neighborly ‘fight’ by making snowmen in the Schmidt’s yard.

“We built snowmen in [Schmidt’s] yard and rang their doorbell so they could be seen,” Donna said.

The Schmidt’s decided to build snowmen on their neighbor’s yard.

“Our neighbor’s played a joke on us, so at 11 p.m., my husband and I put more snowmen in their yard,” Tara Schmidt said.

To the Nelsons’ surprise, they awoke to new snowmen in their yard.

“We looked outside to see eight new snowmen, with one huge one in our driveway,” Donna said.

The Nelsons decided to put a snowman in the Schmidt’s backyard.

“We found one in our backyard, which looks into our window,” Tara said.

The other neighbors, the Jacobsons, were included in the fun.

“We went down to the Jacobson’s house and we built more snowmen and had some snowball fights,” Donna said. “We thought it would be funny to put up an ‘adopt-a-snowman’ sign.”

“We really like our neighborhood — it’s a fun place,” Tracy Jacobson said. “To be included in the snowman ‘wars’ is exciting.”

Tracy said she enjoys living in the neighborhood and being able to participate in friendly activities between families.

“It’s been great living in the neighborhood,” Tracy said. “I think it’s great that we care for one another and can do fun stuff together.”

Tara said she is still thinking of ways to continue their neighborly war.

“We can’t figure out how to get them back,” Tara said. “We have to think of something bigger — we’re in the process of figuring that one out.”

Tracy agrees that there will be even more antics among their families.

“It’s been fun to see what will happen,” Tracy said. “We’re just waiting for more snow to come so we can find ways to get back.”

With this neighborly bonding going on, the Idaho community has received recognition.

“I can’t believe how many people are interested in this,” Tara said. “We’re pretty famous for having all these snowmen. … I even heard we were on the news.”
