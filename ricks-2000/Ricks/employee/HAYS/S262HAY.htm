COURSE SYLLABUS



Texts:   Chess, W.A. & Norlin J. M. (1997).  Human behavior and the social environment, social system theory (3rd ed.).  Massachusetts: Allyn and Bacon.

             Crain, W. (2000).  Theories of development, concepts and applications (4th ed.).  New Jersey:  Prentice Hall.

I. PURPOSE:
 

This course uses a social systems approach to focus on the bio-psycho-social context of human development. Relationships between life-span issues and the understanding of person-environment transactions with individuals, families, groups, organizations, communities, culture and society are all areas of study.
 

II. OBJECTIVES:
 

1. To introduce Social Systems Theory as a back drop for understanding organizational functioning and human behavior within the context of community and social institutions.
 

2. To study the reciprocal nature of person-environment transactions.
 

3. To develop an appreciation for socio-cultural, institutional, environmental influences in relation to human culture and diversity.
 

4. The study of life-span issues, to learn and appreciate how developmental theories and/or constructs influence, guide, and direct peoples lives.
 

5. To provide a focus on human behavior that takes into account the interaction between the individual and his or her environment.
 

6. To develop a social work perspective on human behavior and the social environment. To appreciate social institutions established to support and enhance human growth and development.
 

7. To introduce methods for implementing interventions based on an understanding of person-problem-situation cluster.
 
  
 
 
 

III. This HBSE course maintains a generalist social work flavor by looking at human behavior across the span of life by focusing on the person-in-the-situation. Social systems thinking is a big part of the exploration of person-in-the-environment focus. Human diversity, biological, psychological, cultural, social structure, metaphysical and spiritual aspects of human behavior and the social environment are studied.
 

IV. Methods of instruction include but are not limited to; faculty lecture, small learning groups, audio-visual presentations, assigned readings, structured experiences, life cycle topics and/or papers, writing and exams.
 

V. Examinations will be on material covered in readings, and lecture discussions. There will be four  exams including the final. Exams will be graded on 100 point scale and assigned a letter grade using the BYU-I College grading system. Quizzes will also be given over reading and discussion materials. Quizzes will be averaged to count the same as one exam at the end of the semester.
 

VI. Small group learning experiences will consist of students discussing various life span issues within the context of social systems thinking. Groups may report their findings to the class.
 

VII. Two  writing assignments will be required on life-span issues and/or social systems person-in-the-environment triplex.
 

VIII. GRADES
 

Final grades will be computed using major exams, quizzes, writing assignments, group presentations, attendance and participation.

Quizzes or tests that are missed may be taken within a one week time frame, if a valid excuse exists for their not being taken at the regular time. This window of opportunity is in place only for valid excuses, e. g. severe sickness etc. Exams or quizzes not taken within the one week time frame are forfeited.
