Sligo
Sligo Co., Eire

Sligo is so rich in ancient monuments that to visit them all would constitute a tour in itself. Listed here are the most significant monument types to help you appreciate the evolution of man, his society, his wars and his rituals over the last 6,000 years.

Court Cairns or Court Tombs are the earliest megalithic tombs in Ireland, dating from 3,500 - 2,500 B.C. Examples in Sligo include Creevykeel near Cliffoney and Deerpark (Magheeraghan rush) in a glade overlooking Lough Gill.

Passage Graves or Passage Tombs came next, with the oldest ones currently excavated dating form 3,000 - 2,500 B.C. Excellent examples of these tombs are to be found at Carrowmore to the west of Sligo, which is the largest Megalithic cemetery in Ireland. Other examples at Heapstown and Carrowkeel near Lough Arrow and on Knocknarea Mountain west of Sligo Town.

Portal Dolmens Although concentrated mainly on the east coast and dating from about the third millennium B.C., there are some dolmens at Carrowmore Megalithic Cemetery, west of Sligo Town. Wedge-shaped Tombs followed shortly after - approx. 2,000 B.C. Examples at arricknagat, south of Dromahair. Hills forts were built in the Late Bronze Age/Early Iron Age (around 1,100 - 100 B.C.) and consist of a large stone wall with a ditch either inside or outside. Cashel Bir near Dromahair and Clogher, overlooking Lough Gara, exemplify this type of structure.
 

Early Christian Period The establishment of monasteries occurred in Ireland in the 6th and 7th centuries. These were important centres of learning and missionary activity. Most of their structures were made of wood and have long since disappeared, but Inishmurray in Sligo is a fine example preserved in stone showing how these early monasteries were organised. Drumcliffe with its Round Tower and High Cross is another important representation of early Christian monastic structures.
 

Medieval Churches The 12th century saw a spate of church building which continued right through the medieval period. The church at Ballysadare is an example, as is Sligo Abbey, a Dominican foundation dating from 1252 and Ballindoon Dominican Friary (1507) near Lough Arrow. Creevelea Friary (1508) was the last such Franciscan foundation built in Ireland before the suppression of the monasteries in the 16th century.
 

Castles When the Normans came to Ireland in the 12th century they first built mottes - earthen mounds with a flat top accommodating a wooden tower. By the early 13th century they needed to consolidate their power and more permanent structures were built. Examples in County Sligo of Norman castles can be found at Ballymote (1300) and Ballinafad (1590). Parkes Castle on the shores of Lough Gill, Ardtermon Castle near Lissadell, and Castlebaldwin Castle are 17th century fortified houses.
