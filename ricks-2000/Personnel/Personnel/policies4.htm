Eligibility Requirements for School Activities (4-1)

I. PURPOSE

To establish eligibility requirements of students to participate in university events or activities.

II. DEFINITION

Full-time Student - for the purpose of this policy will mean at least 12 semester hours of credit.

III. POLICY

A. Eligibility

Eligibility to participate in BYU-Idaho events and activities will be based upon cumulative grade point average. Students will be considered making satisfactory academic progress if they maintain a cumulative grade point average, including transfer credit of 2.0 or greater.

Freshmen students with no previous university record are eligible for participation in school events and activities. Part-time students and non-students must be cleared by the dean of students prior to any participation.  Every effort will be made to use full or part-time students before involving non-students.

B. Eligibility Checks

The dean of students will make grade checks of all students involved in activities or events at the beginning, mid-term, and the close of each semester. Directors, coordinators, coaches, instructors, and any sponsor of an activity, event, or club will submit a list of all participants including managers, stage hands, prop people, and any other people who participate in any way with the event or activity.

C. Probation - Academic

Should a student’s cumulative GPA fall below 2.0 he/she will be placed on academic probation and the dean of students will notify him/her and the program advisor or sponsor of their ineligibility to participate in the program. A student will be removed from probation when their cumulative GPA is 2.0 or greater.

D. Probation - Behavioral

A student may not participate in a university sponsored even,t program, or activity wherein they represent the institution in a public appearance or performance if he/she is currently or is placed on behavioral probation.  Eligibility may be rescinded for a violation of the behavioral standards of the university by the dean of students.  (e.g., on the basis of excessive absences from class, the violation of civil or criminal law, breaking the Honor Code, housing regulations, or other university standards, or the breaking of Church standards.

E. Participation Requirements

A student must meet the above eligibility requirements if he/she wishes to participate in any university-sponsored program or extra-curricular activity.

F. Department/Organization Standards

A university club, organization, or department may set a higher eligibility standard than that established by this policy. The club, organization, or department should refer eligibility cases to the Dean of Students for clearance. When a different standard is set by virtue of the university’s membership in a national organization, that standard will be adhered to as the minimum requirement for participation in that particular activity or event.

G. Absence from Class

Students should procure the authorized absence from class form from the sponsor of the event and present it to the class instructors so that he will not receive a grade cut due to the absence. However, the student is still responsible for all work missed due to the absence.

H. Standards

Students who represent the university in any manner are expected to abide by the Honor Code and the dress and grooming standard as well as the standards expected of those who represent the university while participating in any activity, performing, or representing the university.

Off-Campus Performing Tours (4-2)

I. PURPOSE

To establish the institutional controls and policy for the financial arrangements, booking, scheduling, and implementation of off-campus performances or performance tours by official BYU–Idaho performing groups and/or individuals.

II. REFERENCE

Policy and Procedure 4-1, "Eligibility Requirement for University Activities."  CES Performing Group Travel Policy.

III. DEFINITION

A. Off-campus Performing Groups

Off-campus performing groups include all music, drama, speech, and dance groups as well as ensembles scheduled by the Performance Tours Office and any other individual, club, or organization which in performing would be looked upon as officially representing BYU–Idaho.

B. Classification of Extended Tours

The BYU–Idaho Board of Trustees has defined three classifications of extended tours by performing groups: (1) International tours (tours going out of the United States or Canada), (2) major national tours (tours of two or more weeks duration), and (3) short, mid-semester tours (six school days or less).

IV. POLICY

A. All international and major national tours by performance groups are to be coordinated within the Church Educational System through the Commissioner’s Office. Requests to receive proper clearance must be made by (1) using the official forms provided, (2) being cleared by department chair, college dean, and the president, and (3) forwarding the forms to the CES office. These requests should be made during the fall semester two years in advance of the suggested tour.

B. Performance Tours specialists in the Public Relations Office are responsible for booking and scheduling all off-campus performances for official BYU–Idaho performing groups or individuals. All requests and correspondence relative to these programs must be channeled through this office. Additionally, a list of university employees or individuals who are to accompany the tour must be submitted six months prior to the departure date.

The president will review and approve all arrangements for international tours and major national tours and the list of individuals who will accompany the tour prior to the departure date.

C. All one-day, short, or mid-semester performance travel must be approved and scheduled by the Performance Tours Office. Requests for entertainment from area businesses and church or civic groups should be referred to this office for scheduling at least two weeks before the intended departure.

D. No mission or stake personnel are to be approached in regard to booking, scheduling, or conducting business pertaining to a performance except through the Performance Tours Office. The scheduling of a tour among Church sponsoring units may not in any way disrupt the normal function of a stake or mission.

E. Only those programs that are booked through Performance Tours are officially endorsed by the university.

F. All performances must reflect positively upon BYU–Idaho and the Church. Faculty and administrative personnel directly in charge of the various groups are responsible for the quality of the performance.

G. The same standards and deportment that exist on campus will apply to all personnel participating on any BYU–Idaho performance tour for the duration of the tour. Dresses for the women and dress shirts and ties for the men may be required at the discretion of the tour leaders.

H. All students participating in off-campus performances must meet eligibility requirements as established by the university. In addition, students must be in good Honor Code standing with the university or have clearance for each tour from the Dean of Students Office.

I. Eligible students are permitted six school days of authorized absences during each semester to officially represent BYU–Idaho before the public.

J. Spouses of artistic directors and tour managers are encouraged to travel and to act as chaperons and record keepers. Their travel expenses will be assumed in the budget.

K. The tour manager is required to file an overall tour report with the Performance Tours Office within two weeks of the tour’s completion.

L. The Performance Tours Office is responsible for conducting a pretour orientation for all chaperons and students participating in any off-campus performance.

M. The Performance Tours Office is responsible for the business management of all touring groups and for the development of funds to help defray tour expenses. Any requests for special arrangements of any type, including special travel arrangements, relatives accompanying the tour other than the artistic director and tour manager’s spouses, etc., must be approved by the Performance Tour Office prior to the departure date. The student’s financial contribution must not be used for special arrangements which benefit a few people at the expense of others.

1. All student funds to be used in travel activities are to be deposited directly by the student with the Cashier’s Office.

2. No funds shall be collected or disbursed for tour activities without following procedures outlined by the Accounting Office or in this policy.

3. All disbursements shall be made by campus purchase orders, requisitions, or check requests which have been signed by an authorized person.

4. Cash received must be accounted for in the same manner as for a petty cash account. Tour expense procedures are furnished by the Performance Tour Office.

NOTE: Details of the procedures and proposals for tours are available in the Performance Tours Office, Public Relations, Kimball (ext. 496-1151).

Student Non-Discrimination Statement (4-3)

I. PURPOSE

The purpose of this statement is to provide a non-discrimination policy to assist University employees, administrative department officers, and student organization officers in providing for equitable treatment of applicants and students with regard to race, color, creed, sex, and national or ethnic origin.

II. POLICY

"BYU-Idaho admits students of any race, color, creed, sex, nationality or ethnic origin to all rights, privileges, programs, and activities generally made available by the university. It does not discriminate on the basis of race, color, creed, sex, national or ethnic origin in the administration of its educational policies, admissions policies, scholarship and loan programs, student employment program, and other school-administered programs. Students are admitted, provided that they meet the university’s academic requirements and that their personal behavior conforms to the standards of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. High standards of honor, integrity, and morality; graciousness in personal behavior; application of Christian ideals in everyday living; and abstinence from tobacco, alcohol, and harmful substances are required of every student."

Access to Student Records (4-4)

I. GENERAL PROVISIONS

A. Family Education Rights and Privacy Act 

The Family Education Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA) imposes specific requirements concerning student educational records upon colleges and universities receiving federal financial assistance. This Act is applicable to BYU–Idaho as a recipient of federal funds under the Pell Grant and Federal Family Loan Program.

This policy is the official University statement concerning a student’s right to access his or her student educational records and the procedures to be followed by University personnel in response to requests for access to a student’s educational records. This document is not intended, however, to fully implement the University access to student records policy. Rather, individual academic departments and administrative areas are to prepare their own policies and procedures consistent with this University policy.

The Assistant Academic Vice President for Curriculum shall be responsible for University compliance with this policy. This policy applies to students attending the University and the students who have previously attended the University. It does not apply to persons who have applied for admission but have not been admitted, nor does it apply to others who may have corresponded with the University concerning admission or for other purposes. After a student has registered, this policy governs access to his/her official application file as well as to his/her other student records.

B. Annual Notification

Students will be notified annually of their right to access their student education records by publication in the BYU–Idaho Bulletin/General Catalog.

This notice will include, but not be limited to the following:

1. The right of a student to inspect and review his or her education record.

2. The intent of BYU–Idaho to limit the disclosure of information contained in the student’s education records to the following circumstances:

a. with a student’s prior written consent;

b. as an item of the directory information which the student has not refused to permit the University to disclose; or

c. circumstances under FERPA which allow the University to disclose information without the student’s prior written consent.

3. The right of a student to petition BYU–Idaho to amend or correct any part of his or her education record which he or she believes is inaccurate, misleading, or in violation of the privacy or other rights of students. When the University decides it will not amend or correct the student’s record, the student has a right to a hearing to present evidence that the record is inaccurate, misleading, or in violation of the privacy or other rights of students.

4. The right of any person to file a complaint with the Family Policy Compliance Office, U.S. Department of Education, 400 Maryland Avenue SW, Washington, D.C. 20202-4605. The Family Policy Compliance Office’s website is at: www.ed.gov/offices/OM/fpco.

C. Role of Registrar's Office

The Registrar’s Office is the official guardian of all permanent records of all academic work done at the University. This office is also responsible to issue official transcripts of credit upon request of an eligible student.

II. EDUCATIONAL RECORDS

This policy applies to any record (in handwriting, print, tapes, film, electronic, or other medium maintained by BYU–Idaho which is directly related to a student, except:

A. A personal record kept by faculty, staff, administrative, or auxiliary personnel if it is kept in the personal possession of the individual who made the record and the information contained in the record has not been made available to any other person except the maker’s temporary substitute. These personal notes are to be referred to in departmental and administrative implementing policies as "sole possession" records. Records containing information obtained directly from a student or that are used to make decisions about the student are not "sole possession" records.

B. An employment record of an individual whose employment does not result from the fact of his or her status as a student (example—work study or teaching assistant employment records are also education records, but the employment records of students hired for general grounds maintenance would not be education records) provided the record is used only in relation to the individual’s employment.

C. For law enforcement records that are created by a law enforcement unit for a law enforcement purpose, and are maintained by a law enforcement unit. Law enforcement records do not include records that are maintained by another component of BYU–Idaho, records relating to a disciplinary proceeding conducted by BYU–Idaho, or other internal matters relating to BYU–Idaho.

D. Parents’ confidential financial statements, income tax records, and reports received by the University.

E. Records maintained by either the BYU–Idaho Health Center or the BYU–Idaho Counseling Center, if the records are used only for treatment of an individual and made available only to those individuals providing the treatment.

F. Alumni records which contain information about a student after he or she is no longer in attendance at the University and the records do not relate to the person as a student.

III. STUDENT INSPECTION OF EDUCATIONAL RECORDS

A. Statement of Rights

BYU–Idaho encourages students to exercise all of their rights under FERPA and this policy. Since the student’s education record will be repeatedly used by University officials and others to make important decisions affecting the students academic program and future career, the student should assume a personal responsibility to make certain that his or her education record is complete and accurate.

This policy is intended to inform each student about BYU–Idaho’s procedures to provide students the rights to:

1. Inspect and review their education records;

2. Exercise control, with some limitations, over disclosures of information contained in their education records;

3. Seek to correct their education records and to a hearing if necessary when they believe that their records are inaccurate, misleading, or in violation of the privacy or other rights of students;

4. Be informed about the FERPA rights; and

5. Report violations of FERPA to the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act Office, Department of Education.

C. Procedure to Inspect Education Records

Upon presentation of appropriate identification and under circumstances that prevent alteration or mutilation of records, a student with proper identification will be permitted to inspect all education records not restricted by a pledge of confidentiality or considered to be private records of University personnel.

Students should submit to the record custodian or an appropriate University staff person a written request which identifies as precisely as possible the record or records he or she wishes to inspect. However, oral requests may be honored at the sole discretion of the University upon proper presentation of identification and in circumstances where a written request would be burdensome or impractical.

The record custodian or an appropriate University staff person will make the needed arrangements for access as promptly as possible and notify the students of the time and place where the records may be inspected. The University will facilitate access in 45 days or less from the receipt of the request.

When a record contains information about more than one student, the student may inspect and review only the records which relate to him or her.

D. Right of the University to Refuse Access

BYU–Idaho reserves the right to refuse to permit a student to inspect the following records:

1. The parents’ tax returns.

2. Letters and statements of recommendation for which the student has waived his or her right of access, or which were placed in the student’s file before January 1, 1975.

3. Records connected with an application to attend BYU–Idaho or a component unit of BYU–Idaho if that application was denied.

4. Any records which are maintained at the University, but which are not education records as defined by FERPA or this policy and which are not otherwise accessible pursuant to law.

E. Refusal to Provide Copies

BYU–Idaho reserves the right to deny transcript or copies of educational records if:

1. The student has an unpaid financial obligation to the University;

2. There is an unresolved disciplinary action against the student;

3. There is unresolved litigation between the student and the University;

4. The student has failed to comply with the decision of the BYU–Idaho Housing Arbitration Board; or

5. Other cases as determined by the University Policy on Registration and Academic Holds or as determined appropriate by the University.

BYU–Idaho will generally NOT provide copies of those education records related to disciplinary action taken against a student even at that student’s request unless refusal of such a request absolutely limits the student’s right to inspect and review those records.

F. Fees for Copies of Records

For any valid reason such as work hours, distance from a student’s place of residence to a record location, distance between record location sites, or health, a student cannot personally inspect and review his or her education record, BYU–Idaho will arrange for the student to obtain copies of the record.

Those having a legitimate access to education records will be charged a reasonable fee for copies.

There is no charge for search or retrieval of education records nor for personal inspection of education records.

IV. DISCLOSURE OF EDUCATION RECORDS

A. BYU–Idaho will disclose information from a student’s education records only with the written consent of the student, except:

1. To school officials and to specified agents of the University who have a legitimate educational interest in the records.

A school official or specified agent of the University is:

a. a member of the board of trustees;

b. a person employed by the University in an administrative, housing, supervisory, academic, research, or support staff position;

c. a person employed by, under contract to or designated by the University to perform a specific task, such as an attorney, auditor, or in some limited circumstances, a student’s ecclesiastical leader for endorsement purposes.

2. A school official or specified agent has a legitimate educational interest if the official is:

a. performing a task that is specified in his or her position description or by contract agreement;

b. performing a task related to a student’s education;

c. performing a task related to the discipline or continuing ecclesiastical endorsement of a student; or

d. performing a service or benefit relating to the student or student’s family, such as health care, counseling, job placement, or financial aid.

3. To officials of another school, upon request, in which a student seeks or intends to enroll.

4. To certain officials of the U.S. Department of Education, the Comptroller General, the U.S. Attorney General, representatives of the Veterans Administration, and state and local educational authorities, in connection with certain state or federally supported education programs.

5. In connection with a student’s request for or receipt of financial aid, as necessary to determine financial aid, or to enforce the terms and conditions of the aid.

6. As required by state law requiring disclosure that was adopted before November 19, 1974.

7. To organizations conducting certain studies for or on behalf of the University. They cannot permit personal identification of students by anyone other than the organization’s representatives. All information will be destroyed when it is no longer needed for the study’s purposes.

8. To accrediting organizations to carry out their functions.

9. To either parent of an eligible student if the student is claimed as a dependent for income tax purposes regardless of which parent claims the student as a dependent. Parents requesting information from a student’s file shall be responsible to demonstrate that the student in question is a dependent pursuant to Section 152 of the Internal Revenue Code. In addition, BYU–Idaho may disclose to parents of an eligible student information regarding violations of local, state, or federal law or of the Honor Code regarding the use or possession of controlled substances for student violators under the age of 21.

10. To comply with a judicial order or a lawfully issued subpoena in which case the order or subpoena shall be directed to the Office of General Counsel for review prior to dissemination of the education record. The University will make a reasonable attempt to notify the student in advance of disclosure when non-directory information is released in response to subpoenas or court orders.

11. To appropriate parties in a health or safety emergency.

12. To the victim of a crime of violence or nonforcible sex offense, the results of any disciplinary proceedings conducted by BYU–Idaho against the alleged student perpetrator regardless of the outcome of the proceedings.

13. To disclose to the public the final results of a disciplinary proceeding only if BYU–Idaho has determined that:

a. The student is an alleged perpetrator of a crime of violence or nonforcible sex offense, and

b. The student has committed a violation of the BYU–Idaho’s rules or policies with respect to the allegation.

c. BYU–Idaho may not disclose the name of any other student, including the victim or witness without their prior written consent.

V. RECORD OF REQUEST FOR DISCLOSURE

BYU–Idaho will maintain a record of all off campus requests for and/or disclosures of information from a student’s education records. The record will indicate the name and address of the party making the request, an additional party to whom it may be redisclosed, and the legitimate interest the party had in requesting or obtaining the information. The record of the request for disclosure may be reviewed by an eligible student or qualifying parents.

VI. DIRECTORY INFORMATION

A. BYU–Idaho designates, but is not restricted to, the following items as directory information:

- student name
- home and local address
- telephone numbers
- e-mail addresses
- date and place of birth
- dates of attendance (current and past)
- major field of study
- degrees and awards received
- participation in officially recognized activities and sports including height and weight
- previous educational institutions attended
- religious affiliation to local church or congregation
- current class schedule
- pictures
- number of months/semesters enrolled
- class standing (freshman, sophomore, etc.)
- total hours earned
- enrollment status (full-time, part-time, less than half-time)
- anticipated future enrollments
- course registrations prior to the beginning of a semester/term
- expected date of graduation
- deferred registration eligibility

B. The University may disclose any of these items and any other information contained in the educational record of a student which would not be harmful or an invasion of privacy if disclosed without the prior consent of the student, unless notified otherwise in writing by the eligible student.

Any student who does not wish to have the designated directory information disclosed may file a written notification of his or her objection with the Registrar’s Office on or before the tenth day of a semester or the sixth day of a term. Forms for this purpose will be made available at that office upon request.

Except as otherwise authorized by the Registrar, directory information is not provided to third parties in the form of mailing lists or labels.

C. University departments requesting mailing list information may do so by contacting the Registrar’s Office. Departments should provide the following information when requesting mailing lists, labels or other directory information.

1. Explanation of the need for the information and how it will be used.

2. Requests should be submitted in writing with the signature of the Dean, Department Chair, or Director.

VII. CORRECTION OF EDUCATION RECORDS

A. Students have the right to ask to have records corrected that they believe are inaccurate, misleading, or in violation of privacy rights. The following are procedures for correction of records:

A student must ask the custodian of the applicable BYU–Idaho education record to amend a record. In so doing, the student should identify the part of the record he or she wants changed and specify why he or she believes it to be inaccurate, misleading, or in violation of the student’s privacy or other rights. Any such request shall be in writing to the director of the area in which the records are maintained. The director shall promptly review the facts and seek to resolve the complaint by informal discussions with the student.

1. Discussions. In the course of discussions, BYU–Idaho education record to amend a record. In so doing, the student should identify the part of the record he or she wants changed and specify why he or she believes it to be inaccurate, misleading, or in violation of the student’s privacy or other rights.

2. Hearing. Upon request, BYU–Idaho will arrange for a hearing and provide the student with reasonable advance notice of the date, place, and time of the hearing. The hearing will be conducted by a hearing officer appointed by the Exception to the Rule Policy Committee which individual shall be a disinterested party. However, the hearing officer may be an official of BYU–Idaho. The student shall be afforded a full and fair opportunity to present evidence relevant to the issues raised in the original request to amend the student’s education records. The student may be assisted by one or more individuals including an attorney, at the expense of the student. BYU–Idaho will prepare a written decision based solely on the evidence presented at the hearing. The decision will include a summary of the evidence presented and the reasons for the decision.

3. Statement of Disagreement. The statement will be maintained as part of the student’s education records as long as the contested portion is maintained. If BYU–Idaho discloses the contested portion of the record, it will also disclose the statement.

4. Amendment of Record. If BYU–Idaho decides that the information is inaccurate, misleading, or in violation of the student’s right of privacy, it will amend the record and notify the student, in writing, that the record has been amended.

VIII. MILITARY RECRUITERS AND THE SOLOMON AMENDMENT

Requests for student recruiting information from military recruiters are made to the Registrar’s Office. Student recruiting information will not be supplied with respect to students who have not reached the age of 17. If a student has formally requested BYU–Idaho to withhold FERPA directory information from third parties, BYU–Idaho will withhold this information from military recruiters as well.

Upon request, a file containing the name, address, telephone number, age, level of education (e.g. freshman, sophomore, or degree awarded for a recent graduate), and major of all students enrolled for the term in which the request is made will be provided.

Recruiters will be charged a reasonable fee for the requested information. Military recruiters will be given an explanation of the information to be supplied, the associated cost and rationale for the cost within 15 days of receiving the request. The requested information will be provided within a reasonable period of time, normally 30 days.

Student Admittance to Academic Buildings during Evening Hours (4-5)

I. PURPOSE

To outline the policy regarding students in academic buildings after lockup time.

II. REFERENCES

Policy and Procedure 4-1, "Eligibility Requirements for School Activities."

III. POLICY

A. Students needing to be in academic buildings after the time they are normally locked for the evening, are required to have an authorization card signed and dated by the appropriate faculty member.

B. Generally, authorization to be in academic buildings will be for one evening only; however, authorization maybe granted for a longer period if the authorization is so dated.

C. Students may be required to show their authorization cards to employees charged with maintaining the security of the building.

D. The Student Life Office will maintain on a weekly basis a list of students receiving the authorization cards and the buildings the students intend to use.

IV. PROCEDURE

A. Students should pick up an academic late pass from the department and have their instructor date and sign it.

B. Students must then have the pass signed by the Student Life Office.

Student Authorized Absence Policy (4-6)

I. PURPOSE

To outline what will constitute an authorized absence and the procedure of obtaining an authorized absence.

II. REFERENCES

A. Policy and Procedure 4-1, "Eligibility Requirements for School Activities."
B. Policy and Procedure 4-3, "Student Non-discrimination Statement."

III. PHILOSOPHY

All field trips, activities, tours, or events approved by a department chairman or tour director as a part of the regular program of the university are official university activities. These activities should be planned in advance of each semester so students may know what absences will be required, notify their teachers in advance, and plan their work accordingly.

IV. POLICY

A. Students on official university activities who have signed authorized absence forms are not to have their grade reduced for absence resulting from the official school activity and are to be allowed to make up any work they may have missed.

B. Students are required to show to their teachers signed Authorized Absence forms as far in advance of the official university activity as possible.

V. PROCEDURES

A. At least one week before the official university activity, the sponsor must provide each student involved in the activity with a signed copy of the Authorized Absence form.

B. At least one week before the official university activity, the sponsor is to submit to the Dean of Students Office a list of the involved students along with the dates of the activity and a travel itinerary. This list must be signed by the sponsor.

Children in the Classroom (4-7)

Children are not allowed to be present in BYU-Idaho classes due to space restrictions, fire code compliance, and the need to limit distractions. Students have the responsibility to arrange for childcare away from classrooms and study areas so as to preserve and enhance learning opportunities for all students.

In one-time emergency situations, the teacher of a class may allow a student to bring a child to class based on terms set by the teacher. Teachers are neither required nor encouraged to consent to a student's request to bring a child to class, but should use discretion based on the best interest of all students in the class.
