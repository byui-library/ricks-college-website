CHAPTER 18  

RENAISSANCE MUSIC  1450-1650

 

Beginnings

          - in North with Duke of Burgundy in Dijon, 1363

                   - money from textile industry

                   - he sets the style

                             -dress, manners, and music

                   - first to split instruments for his dances

                   - haut—shawms (oboe), slide trumpet  (balcony)

                   - bas—harp, lute, flute             (main floor)

                             - this sets pattern of loud/quiet instruments

The North

          - rules from 1350-1450

                   - courts of Burgundian kings were most powerful in Europe

                   - ladies in waiting/conical hats/lavish dress

                   - celebrated court of love, not of heaven

          - power center gradually shifts from France to Pays Bas

                   - most imp. writer is Josquin

                   - his music will unite Holland and Italy, and

                             create the “international” style

Machaut (1300-60)

          - first to use popular songs as a basis for church music

(This is the big difference between Ren and Gothic music, in that the subject determines whether music is sacred or secular. Before, it was held that the “style” determined everything.)

 

Josquin Des Pres 1450-1521

- the “Prince of Music,” the Michelangelo of music

          - creates the “international” style

          - introduces the motet

          - sacred music with non-scriptural text

          - usually a cappella (inst. are no longer in fashion in the church)

          - he uses ceaseless, intertwined melodies (to replace the accompaniment) are mark of High Ren.

                   - his style leads to extremely complex music

                   - some works have 16 separate voices

The South

          - for more than a century it imports its musicians from North

          - whole age of Michelangelo/Leonardo is sung to

                   Northern directors

Decline

          - by late 1500's Spain has taken over Low Countries, and

                   music wanes

                   - Counter Reformation is very strong—no “worldly”

                             music allowed

          - composers move to other countries

 

The Italian Style

          - Counter Reformation is strong

          - many musicians come in from all over Europe

          - music is still too complex/many complain

          Erasmus wrote: “We have introduced an artificial and theatrical  music into the church, a bawling and agitation of various voices, such as I believe had never been heard in the theatres of the Greeks and Romans. Horns, trumpets, pipes vie and sound along contantly with the voices. Amorous and lascivious melodies are heard such as elsewhere accompany only the dances of courtesans and clowns.”

 

Council of Trent (Counter Reformation)

          - 1543-64 banned all seductive and impure melodies

          - tried to ban all polyphonic music

          - Palestrina protested and wrote a Mass

                   - Church finally accepts music/says it's OK, BUT MUST

                   - music must become clearer, less complex

                   - he uses plainsong/plainchant/Greg. chant as basis

                             for all his melodies

 

Instrumental music

          - is always used as accompaniment, never as solo

          - dancing becomes popular after introduced by Flagellants

                   of 1300's (would dance/chant/whip)

          - chief instruments were lute, and later harpsichord

                   (called “virginal” in England after queen)

Late developments/England

          - Late Ren music reaches its height in England

          - madrigals are the most popular

                   - secular melody

                   - a cappella

                   - 4 voices

                   - polyphonic and homophonic

                   - either sad or happy
