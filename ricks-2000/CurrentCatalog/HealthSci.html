During the last decade there has been a strong shift in emphasis toward well trained technical support for professionals at the Baccalaureate and Graduate level in the Health Care Sciences. To address this need, in addition to providing undergraduate courses designed for those desiring to transfer to a four year school, Ricks College has developed a variety of courses designed to focus on the preparation of specialists and pre-specialists in a variety of Health Care Services.

Those students interested in four year transfer programs in
1) Health Promotions,
2) Community Health
3) Occupational Health and Safety and
4) Environmental Health, contact your assigned adviser.

Associate in Arts and Sciences with a major in Pre-Occupational Therapy (130)

This major is designed for students planning to enter one of the Occupational Therapy schools located in the United States. The first two years of course work may be completed at Ricks. Check with the Department Chair for information about transfer institutions and majors in related areas of Pre-Occupational Therapy.

Courses for Majors

Chemistry: Substitute Chem 105 and 106 General Chem for Chem 101 and 103 if desired.

Math: Math 101 Intermediate College Algebra, may be taken in place of Math 110 College Algebra, and Math 111 Trigonometry, if you desire.

Associate in Arts and Sciences with a major in Pre-Physical Therapy (072)

A Health Science Major with emphasis in Pre-Physical Therapy is offered to students planning to enter one of the Physical Therapy schools located in the United States. The Pre-Physical Therapy major is offered mainly for advising purposes. Students are encouraged to graduate with a major in a related field and fill the prerequisites for physical therapy school at the same time. This major should be one they would like if they don't go to physical therapy school.

It is important to check with the bulletins (available in the Department of Health Science) of the Physical Therapy schools in order to properly prepare your course outline.

Most physical therapy schools require the following classes that can be taken during the freshman and sophomore years at Ricks:

*College Algebra (Math 110) AND Trigonometry (Math 111). These are math prerequisites for chemistry and physics.
Psychology 111, 201
Sociology 111 (one course)
Biological Science 100 or 200, 264, 265, 266, plus labs.
Chemistry 105, 106 plus lab (some students may need Chem 101 as preparatory class)
Physics 105, 106 plus lab
Math 221 Statistics
HS 204 Intro to Physical Therapy
Eng 111 College Writing and one of the following: 211, 215, 216, or 252.
HS 280 Medical Terminology
HS 298A First Year Clinical
HS 298B Second Year Clinical

*Generally speaking, it is better to start at a lower level of math or science classes and get A grades than to start at a more advanced class and end up with a lower grade. For example, some students may need to start with Math 101 Intermediate Algebra rather than Math 110 College Algebra.

Remember all Science courses require labs and some physical therapy schools require some additional courses.

General Education Requirements

For a complete explanation of the General Education requirement. In addition to the courses outlined, you must take six credit hours of Arts & Letters.

Specialized Associate Degree in Paramedicine (225)

This program is designed for those who desire to become Paramedics and who desire to take the examinations necessary to certify through the National Registry of Emergency Medical Technicians. Course work is approved for those wanting Idaho certification and who can satisfy the necessary requirements.

Prerequisites and General Education for this program can be accomplished in two semesters previous to entering the program. Applications must be handed in to the program director by February 15 of the Winter semester preceding the paramedic classes. Prerequisites must be completed or in the process when the candidate makes application. If the prerequisite year is completed at Ricks College the following course work is suggested.

COURSE DESCRIPTIONS

Credits

Health Science

HS 100 Introduction Allied Health Careers: Hospital Orientation 2
Two hours lecture per week, some lab -- T.B.A.

An introduction to several occupations in health careers including education, personal, financial requirements and career opportunities. A relationship will be established between the health careers and the world of hospital work.

HS 122 Advanced First Aid and Emergency Care 3
Three class hours per week

Qualifies student for National Safety Council certification. Covers first aid and medical emergencies.

HS 131* Personal Health and Wellness 2
Two class hours per week

This Personal Health and Wellness course is designed to provide scientific health information which promotes desirable attitudes and behavior toward health and wellness. This course includes lecture and outside of class activities. This class satisfies the Basic Skills Option requirement.

HS 131W* Woman's Health Issues 2
Two hours classroom lecture

Introduction to Woman's Issues: A multidisciplinary study of Woman's emotional and physical issues in today's society, including health care, identifying groups at risk, and learning to maintain a healthy life style.

HS 203 Introduction to Occupational Therapy 3
Two hours classroom lecture
One hour clinical lecture per week

This course explores the roles, activities, and expectations of occupational therapists.

HS 204 Introduction to Physical Therapy 3
Two hours lecture per week
One hour clinical lecture per week

This course explores the roles, activities and expectations of physical therapists.

HS 248 Athletic Training Practicum 1-3
May be repeated for a maximum of six credits
Hours per week to be arranged with instructor

Practical application of athletic training skill, and use of modalities in the training room.

Need to be registered with Idaho Athletic Trainers Assn. as a student member.

HS 249 Introduction to Sports Medicine 4
Four class hours per week plus one hour of lab application (HS 248)
Lab Fee: $35

Designed for athletic trainers, coaches, health and physical educators, physical therapists, EMT's and premedical students for recognition, evaluation, and care of athletic injuries. Techniques in taping, prevention, and rehabilitation of injuries will be presented.

HS 260 The Use & Abuse of Drugs 2
Two class hours per week

The nature and effects of alcohol and other drugs on human beings, considered from physiological, psychological, and sociological viewpoints as well as the educational and legal aspects.

HS 280 Medical Terminology 3
This class is an introductive course into the language of medicine. The student will learn the meaning of over 20,000 medical terms.

HS 298A Independent Clinical Experience - Observation 1-3
Prerequisite: Instructor Permission

Independent clinical experience with observing licensed professionals at a local Health Care Institution: hospital, nursing home, clinic, etc.

HS 298B Independent Clinical Experience - Practicum 1-3
Prerequisite: Instructor Permission

Independent hands-on clinical practicum with a licensed professional at a local health care institution: hospital, nursing home, clinic, etc.

HS 299 Professional Experience Seminar 1-3
Prerequisite: Consent of supervising faculty members
May be repeated for credit
Hours per week to be arranged

Designed to offer credit in a specialty area where teaching, administrative or organizational experiences are providing for individual students in the area of Health Science.

Paramedicine

PARA 181 Emergency Medical Technician - Ambulance 6
Prerequisite: Concurrent enrollment in PARA 182
Consent of instructor
Six class hours per week
Fee: $275 for disposable supplies, uniform, equipment, MOCK accident, and national exam

Emergency Medical Technician training designed to qualify student to become ambulance attendants at the Basic Level upon passing the National Registry of Emergency Medical Technicians - Basic written and practical exams.

PARA 182 Emergency Medical Technician Lab Level 1 (required) 0
Prerequisite: Concurrent enrollment in PARA 181
Four class hours per week -- plus one Saturday lab

Successful completion of this lab along with PARA 181 will prepare participants at the National Registry Emergency Medical Technicians - Ambulance Exam.

PARA 184 EMT Review 2
Exam Fee: $50 for National Registry written and practical exam

National refresher and review of basic skills and didactic work. National EMT-Ambulance exams given upon completion.

PARA 200 Paramedicine Orientation 0
Fee: $275 for disposable supplies

Orientation to the Paramedic Program at Ricks College. Required of all students planning to enter the Paramedic Course work in the fall semester. Includes: Basic EMT evaluation, IV therapy, rescue techniques, and ACLS.

PARA 250 Assessment Based Management I 1
Prerequisite: Concurrent enrollment in PARA 260, Permission only
Four lab hours per week

Ambulance scenarios with assessment based management for trauma and medical patients. Situations move from field to ambulance to Emergency Department. Emphasis is on working with a partner and aggressive treatment and transport.

PARA 251 Assessment Based Management II 1
Prerequisite: Concurrent enrollment in PARA 278, Permission only
Four lab hours per week

Ambulance scenarios with assessment based management for trauma and medical patients, adults, pediatric, and geriatric. Situations move from field to ambulance to Emergency Department. Emphasis is on team leader experience, scene control, use of personnel, aggressive treatment and transport.

PARA 252 Advanced EMT & Paramedic Review 2
Prerequisite: Current certification as a Basic EMT and course completion or certification for EMT-I or EMT-P
Permission only, 48 hours-time arranged
Fee: $85 for National Registry Written and Practical Exams

Refresher and preparation for National Registry Paramedic or Intermediate Exam.

PARA 260 Paramedic Preparatory 6
Permission only

Preparatory fundamentals to include: EMS Systems, Roles & Responsibilities, Well Being, Injury & Illness Prevention, Medical & Legal Issues, Ethics, General Principles of Pathophysiology, Pharmacology, Venous Access, Medication Administration, Therapeutic Communications, Documentation, Life Span Development, Patient Assessment, Clinical Decision Making.
Fee: $385. For disposable supplies and Competency Exam fees.

PARA 264 Airway Management 1
Prerequisite: PARA 260
Permission only

Airway anatomy, Physiology assessment and management to include: endotrachaeal intubation, combitube, nasal intubation, digital intubation, transilluminated intubation, EOA, EGTA, tracheal suctioning, extubation.

PARA 270 Special Considerations 3
Prerequisite: PARA 260
Permission only
Three lecture hours per week
Two lab hours per week

Assessment and management of neonate, pediatric, geriatric, special patients, challenged patients, and abused and assault patients. Includes: Pediatric Advanced Life Support provider certification preparation and critical care and acute interventions in the Home Care Patient.

PARA 274 Trauma 2
Prerequisite: PARA 260
Permission only
Three lecture hours per week

Prehospital Trauma Life Support to include: mechanism of injury, assessment, pathophysiology and management of patients exhibiting shock, soft tissue injury, burns, head, face, spinal injuries, chest and abdominal injuries, and musculoskeletal trauma.

PARA 278 Medical Emergencies 6
Prerequisite: Para 260
Permission only
Six lecture hours per week
Pathophysiology, assessment & management of patients with medical disorders & emergencies. Areas include: Neurology, Endocrinology, Allergies & Anaphylaxis, Gastroenterology, Renal/Urology, Toxicology, Hematology, Environmental Conditions, Infectious & Communicable Diseases, Behavioral/ Psychiatric Disorders, Gynecology & Obstetrics. Lab practice in MEGA-CODES & patient care in Trauma, Cardiac Medical Emergencies, Pediatric, Geriatric and Street Sense.
Fee: $444. For disposable supplies, competency and national exams, and graduation fees.

PARA 280 Cardiology/Pulmonary I 3
Prerequisite: PARA 260
Permission only

Cardiovascular & Respiratory Anatomy, Pathophysiology, assessment and management of patients with cardiac and respiratory related emergencies, ECG monitoring 1-3 lead, Advanced Cardiac Life Support provider preparation.

PARA 286 EMS Operations 1
Prerequisites: PARA 260
Permission only

Mass Casualty Incident Command, Rescue Awareness, HAZMAT Awareness, Street Sense and Crime Scene Awareness.

PARA 287 Cardiology/Pulmonary II 1
Prerequisites: PARA 280
Permission only

Pathophysiology, assessment including 12 lead ECG evaluation and management of cardiac and respiratory emergencies. Includes etiology, signs and symptoms of compromise, clinical implications, treatment protocols, pharmacological & electrical interventions and termination of resuscitation, MEGA-CODE practice & patient assessment & management.

PARA 290 Paramedic Hospital Clinical 1
Prerequisite: Concurrent enrollment in either PARA 260 or 278
Permission only
Eight clinical hours per week

Hospital experience: Emergency Department, operating room- intubations, IV therapy, labor and delivery, pediatrics, ICU/CCU, cardiac catheterization lab, ECG, post anesthesia recovery, respiratory therapy, medical floor, geriatrics, central supply/pharmacy, rehabilitation and behavioral health.

Must be taken for a minimum of 420 hospital hours. (May be taken more than one semester.)

PARA 292 Paramedic Ambulance Internship 1-12
Prerequisites: PARA 250, 251, 260, 264, 270, 274, 278, 280, 286, 287, 290,
Permission only
40 ambulance hours per week

PARA 299 Professional Experience Seminar 1-3
Prerequisite: Consent of supervising faculty members
May be repeated for credit
