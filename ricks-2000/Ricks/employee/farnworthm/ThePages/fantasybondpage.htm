When some one is first confronted with the realities of the fantasy bond there is a natural tendency to be somewhat defensive about it.  It is hard to see reality at times- when we are so close to it.  The fantasy bond is a very normal and common occurrence in family and social relations.  We are very familiar with it in the social settings of polite but often strained relationships and exchanges.  It is expected there and often promoted and nurtured.  The political realities of  superficial behavior in social settings is something to be aware of and the resulting behaviors are important but not necessarily critical to one's happiness and peace.  On the other hand, superficial and disingenuous exchanges in the personal- family realm carry a different charge.  We expect family and loved ones to be honest and intimate (intimacy is defined as the sharing of energies, positive and negative) with us.  When we learn that role appropriate-superficial behavior is part of the home, family and marriage scene it can be initially disconcerting.  If viewed on a continuum then the issue becomes how much of a cherished relationship are we going to sacrifice to the fantasy bond.  Honesty and intimacy are difficult to maintain over time and most of us fall into the comfort of emotional distancing to help maintain our zone of safety and familiarity.

The work on fantasy bonds come from Robert Firestone and his published works:

The Fantasy Bond: Effects of Psychological Defenses on Interpersonal Relations.  Human Sciences Press 1985.

Fear of Intimacy.  American Psychological Association 1999.

It is also written about in his works:

Compassionate Childrearing.  Insight Books 1990.

Psychological Defenses of Everyday Life.  Human Sciences Press 1989.

A fantasy bond is a relationship that is built on the shoulds and expectations of what is culturally approved of and considered normal.  It is a relationship that becomes a mutual role play with the real sharing of feelings and energies missing... as that would detract from and betray the created relationship.

In dating, it happens when one or the other loses interest but doesn't tell the other because of the professed reason of "not wanting to hurt their feelings".  The person whose feelings have changed, continues in the relationship acting as if everything was the same but it becomes a role play to protect themselves and not the other person.  This play acting is the essence of the fantasy bond. Deception for the good of the other while being dishonest with your self.

In marriage, it happens slowly over the years as the intimacy is replaced by pretensions, obligations and role appropriate behavior for the situation.  It is a well know phenomenon that many marriages are dead on the inside... but the people in them keep up the "appearances" for their own reasons.  The act is often times so well maintained that if divorce is sought by one of the parties in the marriage it comes as a complete surprise to everyone around them.

In parenting, it occurs because parents are suppose to love their children.  Even if the parents behavior is hostile and mean spirited- it can always be defended as for the 'child's own good'.  Good parents love their children and as no one is willing to acknowledge they are lousy parents the role play continues often at the expense of the children.  Much abuse occurs in the name of feigned love.

The following poem illustrates the dynamics of the fantasy bond over time...

"Walls"

Their wedding picture mocked them from the table, 

these two whose minds no longer touched each other.

They lived with such a heavy barricade between them 

neither battering ram of words nor

artilleries of touch could break it down.

Somewhere, between the oldest child’s first tooth 

and the youngest daughter’s graduation, 

they lost each other.

Throughout the years, each slowly unraveled that tangled ball of string called self, 

and as they tugged at stubborn knots each hid his searching from the other.

Sometimes she cried at night and begged the whispering darkness 

to tell her who she was.

He lay beside her, snoring like a hibernating bear, unaware of her winter.

Once, after they had made love he wanted to tell her how afraid he was of dying, 

but fearing to show is naked soul, he spoke instead 

about the beauty of her breasts.

She took a course in modern art, trying to find herself in colors 

splashed upon a canvas, 

and complaining to other women about men who were insensitive.

He climbed a tomb called "the office," wrapped his mind 

in a shroud of paper figures

and buried himself in customers.

Slowly, the wall between them rose, cemented by the mortar of indifference.

One day, reaching out to touch each other, they found a barrier 

they could not penetrate, 

and recoiling from the coldness of the stone 

each retreated from the stranger on the other side.

For when love dies, it is not in a moment of angry battle, nor when fiery bodies lose their heat.

It lies panting, exhausted, expiring at the bottom of a wall it could not scale.

Relationships are suppose to be based on love but often times they are more a function of  emotional hunger, desperation and selfish romantic fantasy.  The relationship becomes an illusion, fantasy or mirage that is suppose to bring a healing wholeness... and when it fails (as illusions always will) the couple retreat into a mutual wounded tolerance of each other and establish safety through emotional distance and pretense.  The idealized notions people have about love and marriage are legion.  

"In this culture, marriage may be the most popular form of psychotherapy.  We all seem to believe that marriage will change our lives, will make us feel better about ourselves.  this special person will make us strong when we feel weak, whole when we feel empty, comforted when we feel lonely.  this is the magic union, the one that has the power to transform reality.  we need only listen to the lyrics of the popular songs to be aware of how widespread these expectations are."                          Augustus Napier, The Fragile Bond.

I believe the scriptures label the dynamics of the fantasy bond as feigned love.  In the Doctrine and Covenants 121:41 we read about the Godly characteristics which can only occur in a virtuous setting with gentleness, meekness, long suffering and love unfeigned.  Unfeigned means un-pretended.  It is genuine, without hypocrisy (the act of pretending) and guile (which is insidious cunning).  I believe God is not pleased with dishonesty of any kind, but especially between people who are to love each other and relate to each other as fellow and equal beings in the Household of God.

I believe we can have a fantasy bond with ourselves, with God, with institutions and ideas.  The fantasy bond becomes manipulative and dishonest and keeps us hidden from ourselves and others we say we love and care about.  The issue then, is how ready are we to surrender our role plays and embrace ourselves and others in love, affection, honesty and truth?
