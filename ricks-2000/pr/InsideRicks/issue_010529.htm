GROUND BROKEN FOR NEW SPORI BUILDING

Ground was broken May 29 for a new building to replace the former Jacob Spori Building at Ricks College. Prior to the symbolic turning of soil, Elder Henry B. Eyring, a member of the Quorum of the Twelve and Commissioner of Church Education, paid tribute to faithful, pioneer Church members who built the original building and he challenged the younger generation to follow in their path.

The former Ricks College president (1971-1977) said Church members sacrificed to build the original academy building at the turn of the 20th century that later became known as the Spori Building. He said these Saints were poor and living on the frontier, but they sacrificed because they valued education. In looking at the new Spori Building, he said, "When you see the (new) building go up, I hope you will see Latter-day Saints in Albania and Peru who have built it out of sacrifice," referring to the use of tithing funds from Church members.

Elder Eyring paid tribute to Jacob Spori, a Swiss convert who served as the first principal of Bannock Stake Academy, which grew into Ricks College. He said Spori symbolizes many people who are seekers and willing to sacrifice everything for the gospel of Jesus Christ. "Real seekers seek the kingdom of God and build it up," he told the students. He hopes that seekers like Spori will always continue to come to the college.

President David A. Bednar said the building is intended "to look to the past with fondness and gratitude and to future with hope and anticipation." He said there are many parallels between the old and new Spori buildings that will be built almost exactly 100 years apart. "There's a deep sense of history as we retrace these steps."

The new building is designed to meet the needs of a growing campus as the college becomes Brigham Young University-Idaho. The new 44,000 square foot building, which will sit at the exact location of the original building, has a striking resemblance to its predecessor and will feature a similar size and shape, rock exterior, tall windows and pitched roof. The building will house both the Art and Communications Departments at the university. The building has been designed by FFKR of Salt Lake City. The bid opening will be held in June and construction is expected to start in early July and be completed in 2003. Excavation of the foundation already is under way by Edstrom Construction Inc. of Rexburg to expedite the construction schedule.

RICKS COLLEGE PHASES OUT NON-ACADEMIC STUDENT TRAVEL PROGRAM

Ricks College is phasing out its non-academic student travel program effective at the end of the third summer term. "The student travel program has served our students well for a number of years when the options for travel were either nonexistent or very limited," said LaNae Poulter who managed the student travel program for 13 years through the college's public relations office. "Recently there has been expanded service and interest by local carriers to assist our students with their travel needs. We feel confident in their ability to handle the service needed especially if our students have the foresight to plan ahead."

In addition to shuttles to the Idaho Falls and Salt Lake airports, several companies expressed a desire to have special runs to various destinations throughout the United States exclusively for students at the beginning and end of semesters and for holidays. Students interested in using bus transportation for the holidays are advised to plan early and to contact the carriers directly so routes can be arranged to meet their individual travel needs.

For convenience and to help students and their parents weigh the options and make decisions based on their own special needs and preferences, a list of carriers has been added to college's web site www.byui.edu/pr/studenttravel/options.htm.

RICKS IS IN MIDST OF MIGRATING TO NEW EMAIL SYSTEM

Ricks College is in the midst of migrating all of the employee email accounts from Novell GroupWise to Microsoft Exchange. The student accounts were part of a third email system that has been shut down. All of the student accounts are on now on the Microsoft Exchange system. All student emails should be addressed to the student @byui.edu. Students can no longer receive email addressed to @ricks.edu.

Employees are being gradually migrated to the new email system. Because it is impossible for the public to determine who has been migrated and who has not, all employees will be able to receive email addressed to them @ricks.edu until early next summer. If a message is sent to an employee @byui.edu before their account has been created, the message will be returned undeliverable. Until further notice is given, it would be safest to continue to address all employee email to the employee @ricks.edu.

SUMMER IN THE SNOW SERIES OFFERS VARIETY OF ENTERTAINMENT

This year's Summer in the Snow entertainment series will include a variety of concerts, several dinners, folk dancers and a theatrical production. Tickets for all events are now on sale at the Ticket Office which is open weekdays while school is in session from 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. Call (208) 356-2230 or go online at www.byui.edu/tickets. Costs range from free to $15, depending on the show. http://www.byui.edu/Ricks/News/Cultural/Centerstage.html

The Ricks College Choir will perform three times during the summer at the end of each term. The free concerts will be on Saturday, June 2; Wednesday, July 11; and Wednesday, Aug. 15 at 7:30 p.m. in the Barrus Concert Hall.

Three free organ recitals will also be presented. They include Darwin Wolford Thursday, June 28; Marsha Bjornn, Friday, July 27; and Rebecca Parkinson, Friday, Aug. 10. The recitals are in the Taylor Chapel at 7:30 p.m.

Songwriter Michael McLean will return to the Hart Auditorium Thursday, June 7, at 7:30 p.m. to entertain during Education Week. The concert is open to the public and concert-goers do not need to be registered for Education Week to attend. Tickets are $8 and $6.

The Vigilante Theatre Company of Bozeman, Mont., will present their ever popular dinner theater show "Mystery Manor" Thursday, June 14, at 7 p.m. Tickets for this dinner show are $15.

The Ricks College faculty jazz ensemble Swingset will present an evening of summertime jazz Friday, June 22, at 7:30 p.m. in the Kirkham Auditorium. Cost is $3.

The annual concert featuring Sunbird Dick Clark from Sun City West and his musical friends will be held Friday, June 29, at 7:30 p.m. in the Barrus Concert Hall. The concert has been a tradition in Rexburg for several years. Cost is $3.

The Ricks College Theater Department will present "This Castle Needs a Good Scouring" from July 5-7 and 12-14 in the Snow Drama Theater at 7:30 p.m. Written and directed by Ricks faculty member Omar Hansen, this summer's theatrical production is a new twist on the Cinderella story. Cost is $6.

Last summer's biggest hit of the season is returning Thursday, July 12, at 7:30 p.m. to the Barrus Concert Hall. Consisting of four talented singers and a pianist from Montana, Opus IV combines the best of classical music and musical comedy into one program. Cost is $3.

The Ricks College American Folk Dancers and Bluegrass Band will present their annual tour show Friday, July 20, at a 2 p.m. matinee in the Kirkham Auditorium. The group will be leaving on a month-long summer tour of Austria, Poland and Hungary. Cost is $6.

The South family of Idaho and Utah will perform in the Barrus Concert Hall Saturday, July 21, at 7:30 p.m. "Legacy of Music" will be presented by the South family featuring everything from cello and piano to old time fiddling and Broadway tunes. Music has played an important role in the South family from the log cabin days at the sawmill in Island Park to the present. Cost is $3. A pre-show dinner is also offered for an extra $10.

InsideOut, a six-man, all-vocal group from Provo, Utah, will return to Ricks with a show Saturday, Aug. 11, in the Kirkham Auditorium. With their musical capability, vocal percussion and invigorating performance, InsideOut has produced show that entertains audiences of all ages. Cost is $5.
