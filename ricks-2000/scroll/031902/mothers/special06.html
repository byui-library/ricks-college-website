Women measure standards of modesty

by Katie McMurray
MCM00003@BYUI.EDU
Arts editor
When women freely choose to flaunt themselves using fashion, they can become victims of lust.

“The most precious thing that a girl has is her modesty and if she preserves this in dress, in speech, in action, it will arm and protect her as nothing else will. But let her lose her modesty and she becomes a victim of those who pursue her,” Melvin J. Ballard said in an April 1929 conference report.

The word modesty stems from the Latin term modus, meaning measure. Are women measuring up?

BYU-Idaho students and members of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints are taught principles of modesty throughout their lives. The Code of Honor, the Strength For Youth Pamphlet, teachers, apostles and prophets all provide standards of modesty for women and men to follow.

“[Modesty is] a quality of mind, heart and body ... An attitude of humility, decency and propriety that may be evidenced in thought, language, dress and behavior,” according to The Encyclopedia of Mormonism.

Modesty is a protection and a way to help women live clean and wholesome lives, Peggy Hawkins said in the October 1971 New Era.

“Modesty in dress is one of the identifying characteristics of Saints,” Bruce R. McConkie, a former member of the First Presidency, said. “Immodesty in dress is worldly, [and] excites passions and lusts.”

Being modest is more than wearing appropriate attire, like long skirts and shirts with sleeves; it is an attitude of respect towards the body.

“[Modesty] is a line drawn in the heart; it’s the result of truly believing that the body is the temple of the spirit,” John S. Tanner said in the August 1992 Ensign.

Modesty is not a trend; it is a style, Caroline H. Benzley said in the January 2002 New Era. The way women dress can influence other people’s thoughts and actions. Women can choose what they wear but they cannot choose the way other people perceive them, Benzley said.

Woman is God’s supreme creation, Richard G. Scott, a member of the Quorum of the Twelve Apostles, said. Consequently, women seek appraisal and respect from those surrounding them. What many women do not realize is that their lack of modesty can result in the wrong kind of attention.

“There is none more beautiful, none more inspiring than a lovely daughter of God who walks in virtue ... and respects her body as a thing sacred and divine,” Elder Scott said.

There are, however, many people in the world that seem to find joy in robbing women of their esteem. Negative and vulgar comments from the opposite sex can be degrading to a woman’s self-esteem.

“Because it is their nature to please others, many women do not realize their intrinsic worth. That loss makes them vulnerable to those who would convince them that their major role is to be physically appealing,” Elder Scott said. “Many dress and act immodestly because they are told that is what [men] want.”

What women must realize is that sacrificing their modesty can, in turn, lead them down a path of immorality. Many agree that maintaining standards of modesty will help women hold a sense of self-worth and self-respect and they will gain respect from others.

“Though dress lengths have changed over the years, the spirit of what the prophets have said remains the same. Our bodies are the temples of God. They are sacred and beautiful and not to be exposed to all the world to become common and ordinary,” Hawkins said.

Each age group of women experiences different challenges with modesty. Fashions are different between a teenager in high school and a working mother, but the principles of modesty remain unchanged.
Latter-day Saint women have a clear standard of modesty. Modesty is defined by the temple garment. However, once a woman is appropriately covered, she can still be immodest in her actions. Modesty includes the way women present themselves in their dress and actions.

Immodesty contributes to broken families, marriages and other relationships. Immodesty in women can cause men to think vulgar thoughts about them.

“Young women, you have a responsibility not to invite unwanted thoughts into the mind of a young man by the way you dress; and, obviously, a young man has the responsibility not to dwell or act on those thoughts,” Sister Susan Bednar said.

“Modesty in dress is one of the identifying characteristics of saints. It is an aid in preserving chastity,” Elder McConkie said.

Women must not be accommodating to an immodest environment before they become spoiled by seductive evils of the world.

“Being ladies, you must constantly wear the hallmark of modesty. No man admires an immodest woman,” Hugh B. Brown said in his article, “The Abundant Life.”

Women cannot underestimate the power and influence of their appearance. Choosing to measure up to modesty results in the companionship of the Spirit of God and wholesome respect from the world.
