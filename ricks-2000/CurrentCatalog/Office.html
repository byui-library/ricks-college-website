The Department of Office Systems Management addresses the needs of students who wish to work on computers and to manage the various systems in the office environment. This department also provides courses for career exploration for General Studies students with an interest in business, computers, word processing, desktop publishing, and records management.

Students who include Office Systems Management courses while pursuing other majors have an advantage when seeking employment. A background in word processing and business writing is a definite asset to almost every other major.

The Office Systems Management Department offers the following degrees and certificates:

- Associate Degree in Office Systems Management (Major 175)
- Associate Degree in Arts and Sciences (Major 297)
OR
- Combination of above two Associate Degrees (one additional semester or summer) (Major 297 and 175)
- Associate Degree in Arts and Sciences (Major 314)
- One-year Certificate (Major 174)

An Associate Degree in Office Systems Management (175) prepares the student for a position of executive secretary, administrative assistant, medical or legal secretary, data typist, or receptionist.

An Associate Degree in Arts and Sciences (297, 314) is the perfect major for the students who know they want to earn a bachelor's degree and wisely use elective credits to gain sufficient skills to work part-time while they attend a college or university.

A One-year Certificate (174) prepares the student who wishes to gain the skills for an entry-level position in an office.

COURSE DESCRIPTIONS
Credits
OSM 101 Fundamentals of
Keyboarding 3
Five class hours per week

Semester class with emphasis on developing speed and accuracy in basic keyboarding. Designed for beginning typist or student who has not exceeded 25 net words a minute in keyboarding. Taught on computers.

OSM 105 Brush-up Keyboarding 1

Block class for students who are typing from straight-copy material between 25-40 words a minute on a 5-minute timed writing. Class will emphasize building accuracy and speed on straight copy and numbers. Introduction to correspondence, tabulation, and manuscripts. Class may be repeated for a maximum of two credits.

OSM 106 Business English* 3
Three class hours per week

The study of basic grammar; parts of speech; number, abbreviation, spelling, capitalization, punctuation, and proofreading rules.

OSM 131 Beginning Word
Processing 1
Prerequisite: 20 wpm required or have consent of instructor. Students not meeting this requirement, take
OSM 101 Fundamentals of Keyboarding.

This block class meets two days a week and is designed to provide students with a working knowledge and application of the basic capabilities of the current release of Word Processing software.

OSM 203 Production Keyboarding* 3
Prerequisite: 40 wpm typing speed on 5-minute timed writing with an average of one error or less a minute. Five class hours per week

Emphasis on accuracy, format and speed in production work, letters, manuscripts, tables, and business forms using computers.

*Students may test out of this class. For further information contact teacher first day of class.

OSM 220** Business Communication 3
Prerequisite: Majors 167, 242, 244 and 297 must have completed Eng 111 and 22 credit hours.

This course includes analyzing, composing, and dictating business letters, job application documents and reports. Students will also give oral presentations.

Requirement for 167, 242, 244, 297 and 314 majors. Some transfer institutions may still require that you take their Advanced Writing English class.

OSM 231 Word Processing* 3
Prerequisite: Students must be able to type at least 35 words a minute

Designed to provide students with a working knowledge and application of the basic and advanced capabilities of the current release of word processing software.

OSM 271 Desktop Publishing 3
Prerequisite: OSM 231 or concurrent registration

Documents and graphics applications of design and layout principles. Word processing software and HP Laserjet technology emphasized.

OSM 272 Office Equipment 3
Prerequisite: OSM 106, OSM 203 and OSM 231

The development of speed and accuracy in the transcription of documents in the fields of legal, medical, travel, small business, etc. The development of speed and accuracy using the ten-key calculator and other office machines.

OSM 273 Records Management 3

An overview of records systems and
basic files maintenance.

**Gives GE credit in the Reading & Writing area of the Basic Skills

OSM 274 Advanced Office
Systems 3
Prerequisite: OSM 231 & CIS 140

Provides advanced software training for the computerized office environment. Emphasizes Netscape Navigator/Composer; Microsoft Internet Explorer, Word, Excel, Access, and PowerPoint; and Intuit QuickBooks Pro. Students develop, manage, and maintain a personal web site. Students are also required to pass the Microsoft Office Skill Assessment Management (SAM) examinations at the expert level. Microsoft Office User Certification (MOUS) is available at the proficiency and expert levels.

OSM 275 Advanced Software Applications 3
Prerequisite: OSM 231

Advanced projects and applications utilizing personal computer software used in the modern office environment.

OSM 276 Executive Office Procedures and Systems 3
Prerequisite: OSM 231
Three class hours per week

Designed to prepare the student for job responsibilities requiring specific skills, human relations skills, understanding and knowledge, motivation and initiative. How to achieve successful public relations is covered extensively. Other areas covered are making travel, meeting, and conference arrangements; mail handling; financial and legal responsibilities; administrative support; office etiquette; and career planning.

OSM 278 Introduction to Legal
Assistantship 3

Class will familiarize student with basic vocabulary and substantive knowledge useful in working as a legal assistant. Will provide opportunity to develop skills in law office communication, legal research, people skills, and professionalism.

OSM 295 Practicum .5
Required of majors 174, 175 and 314 if not registered for OSM 298 or 299
Activity/Lecture Fee: $15

An advising/communication class to allow students opportunities to develop professionalism by participating in symposiums, business lecture series, study skills, career vision activities, job seeking exercises, field trips, competitive events, and leadership activities.

A "summer only" program for OSM majors who have completed at least one semester in their program. Jobs are secured by student with help of program director in any part of the world. Each credit requires 90 hours of work and two written reports.
