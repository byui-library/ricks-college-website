Prerequisites: Math 110 College Algebra, or concurrent.

Objectives: The student will learn the syntax of the C++ language and to use the design process in the development of computer programs. This knowledge will be useful in developing solutions to engineering problems and in understanding the limitations of commercial applications programs and libraries.

Contents: This course is an introduction to computer programming using the C++ language, with applications in engineering and technology. The course will cover general computing concepts, problem analysis, and program development. Emphasis is placed on the concepts and processes used in structured programming, as well as learning the syntax of the C++ language.

Computer programming is a learn-by-doing subject. The student is required to design a solution to a problem, build the result of that design, and test its effectiveness in meeting the problem specifications. This entire process is what is defined as programming. It is impossible to learn programming without doing it. There will be many assignments to provide the needed practice. Most of the assignments will be done as homework exercises, but some will be done as lab exercises during class time in the lab. Do not limit yourself only to those exercises assigned. Practice with the examples and "Try it" exercises in the textbook.

Lab assistance: There is an optional scheduled lab time each week at which the course instructor will be in the lab for assistance with questions and assignments. There will also be a lab assistant available during much of the time that the lab is open. Consult the schedule near the door for the times when an assistant with capabilities specific to this class will be available.

Program grading: Each homework assignment must be turned in by its due date to receive full credit. No credit will be given for assignments more than one week late. The exercises will be graded on programming and writing style, and must run correctly. Do not get behind in your work. Each new concept or skill builds upon those learned earlier. Getting behind causes great frustration and creates difficult work in trying to catch up. There will be no make-up or extra credit work.

Tests: There will be two tests given during the term along with a final test. These tests will be announced well in advance along with the material to be covered on them. The final test will be given in the classroom at the time published in the Ricks College class schedule. There will also be quizzes, announced and unannounced, given throughout the semester.
