Ricks basketball outduels Miles Community College

by Nathan Hathaway
hat97013@byui.edu
Scroll Staff

They say defense wins ball games.

The Ricks men’s basketball team proved it Saturday night, beating Miles Community College of Miles City, Mont. 88-66 behind a superb defensive effort.

Ricks held the Pioneers to 38 percent shooting from the floor while shooting 45 percent.

“Defensively I thought we played really well. They’re a good shooting team and we took their inside game away from them,” Viking Coach Clyde Nelson said. “That’s what we need to do. That’s our most important job is to go out and play defense. Everything else will take care of itself, but you’ve got to play defense. I thought we did a good job.”

Defense may win games, but a little offense certainly can’t hurt.

The Vikings were led by guards Adam Hiatt and Aaron Bowman, each of whom had 21 points. It was the third straight game in which Bowman was the high scorer for Ricks.

Ricks established the tempo early. Down by two after a Miles 3-pointer, Bowman and Hiatt took Ricks on a 10-0 run sparked by a Bowman dunk to tie the game and capped off with a Hiatt trey to make it 18-10. After the run Ricks never fell behind again. The Pioneers scored six more points before the Vikings went on an 11-0 spurt over nearly four minutes to double up MCC 32 16.

But that was just the beginning. The Vikes went on another 10-0 run before halftime to go up by as much as 22, but Miles scored the last eight points of the half to cut the Ricks lead to 46-28.

The second half was much of the same as Ricks piled run upon run to go up by as much as 25.

Reserve forward Todd Amberry, who played on the 1997-98 Region 18 Championship team, opened some eyes with his 10 points off the bench, including 2-for-3 from 3-point range.

Aaron McKee also scored 10 to join Hiatt, Bowman and Amberry as the only Vikings in double figures.

Ricks hit 12 3-pointers in the game, shooting 43 percent from beyond the arc, and made 89 percent of their free throws compared to 66 percent for the Pioneers. Of Ricks’ 30 baskets, 23 were assisted. MCC had only eight assists on its 17 buckets in the game.
