COURSE DESCRIPTION

Basic Reading is a 2.0 credit course that meets three times a week and provides instruction and practice to improve basic reading skills through individual tutoring.  The course emphasizes the essentials of reading, i.e. word attack skills, vocabulary, comprehension, and fluency in oral reading. 

 

REQUIRED TEXTS 

            Langan, J. (1999).  Ten Steps to Advancing College Reading Skills.  West Berlin, NJ: Townsend.

Fulghum, R. (1988).  All I Really Need to Know I Learned in Kindergarten: Uncommon Thoughts on Common Things.  New York: Ivy.

            Fleischman, P.  (1997).  Seedfolks.  New York: Harper Trophy.

You may also read at least one novel.  Materials needed for the class include a 3-ring binder and divider tabs for your Reading Portfolio, computer disk for your Journal, 3x5 cards, and a pocket dictionary.

 

CONTENT AND METHOD

The class is conducted as an individual tutorial.  Students will be assigned a tutor and appropriate meeting hours.  Working with a tutor involves a student in oral interaction.  Students in college reading courses need to talk about ideas before they read about them, they need to stop and talk during reading, and they need to react through talking and writing after reading.  Additionally, they need to listen as classmates talk about how they formed ideas while reading.  In a tutoring session, the tutor orally models the kind of thinking they do as they read.

 

The assignments in E-100 will involve students in writing.  Today, reading specialists recognize the interrelatedness of reading and writing.  Through writing, students can think in greater depth about what they have read; they can begin to look at reading from the point of view of the writer. The need to write as part of reading is why students will be asked to keep a journal, and create and write answers, in addition to objective quizzes and tests for each chapter in their workbook.

20%        ATTENDANCE: Required tutoring sessions, preparation points, participation points

20%        HOMEWORK: Daily homework, Frontier Cards, Oral Reading Assignment, Seedfolks Map

20%        PROJECTS: Booktalk, Booktalk Closure Paper, read Fulghum, Novel Summary Assignment

20%        QUIZZES: Ten Steps Mastery Tests, Latin Roots Quiz, Main Idea Matrix Exam, Woodcock Reading Mastery Test-Revised improvement from based on pre and post testing (overall improvement is not something you can cram for at the end of the course--it takes care of itself when you prepare on a daily basis)
