Welding Links
By Greg Palmer

"Big Joe"Joel Arqueta, of Mexico City, had recently married the love of his life in the temple and looked to his future with optimism. He had served well as a missionary. He was sure the Lord would provide a way. But as a newlywed, particularly as he learned his wife was expecting, his optimism turned to frustration, which turned to discouragement. He and his wife worked as hard as they could at a number of jobs, and still were not making ends meet. How could he support his family, especially with a baby on the way, and still have time to actually spend at home, and serve the Lord?

Since coming into the Church seven years earlier, Rodolfo Uribe’s life had been one of continual growth. Through his conversion and missionary service, he had found joy he hadn’t imagined possible. But now he felt stagnated. Rodolfo had always worked, and worked hard, but employment opportunities were limited. And now there was Reyna, a beautiful sister from his Empalme, Sonora Branch. He wanted to ask her to marry him. But what could he offer her? What sort of future could he provide her, and then a family?

All that was about to change in the summer of 1998.

As Dr. Donald C. Bird, Ricks College academic vice president explains, “Each year hundreds of young men return from the mission field to their homes in Mexico. They have served exemplary missions, they have gained a strong core of values. But many will return to limited futures. It is especially frustrating to see what a benefit the skills, maturity and values they have gained (on their missions) could be to industry, yet they don’t have a marketable skill to allow them entrance into the work force.”

Addressing these concerns, a group gathered on the Ricks campus, including returned mission presidents from Latin America, with an understanding of Mexican culture and conditions, and arrived at a trade that could provide a good living for the young men: welding.

Ricks College, the Church Education System’s Benemerito High School in Mexico City, Church Welfare Services, and Mexican industry partnered to bless the lives of these returned missionaries. Brother Bird explains, “The concept was that although one entity might not be able to do much, together we could each provide unique resources that could bless these deserving lives. Ricks determined what we could offer, and we went to work.”

A notice announcing the program was disseminated through the institutes of religion and stakes throughout Mexico. From the beaches of Cabo San Lucas to the bustle of Mexico City, the Spirit was brooding over and encouraging the young men who would be chosen to participate. Over 180 applications were received for 20 available spots.

Chuck Cartmill, dean of the Division of Engineering and Technology at the time, was given the responsibility to oversee the project, and perhaps it’s most important aspect: the selection of the students. He is moved as he tells of the selection process: “We went about it just as you would selecting any applicants for jobs. We reviewed them and then just chose those who looked like they were the most qualified. 

“But then I was troubled, couldn’t sleep, and knew we hadn’t gone about it right. My wife and I fasted and prayed and I asked the rest of the selection committee to do the same. We then came up with a completely different group, and each individual member of the selection committee came up with nearly the same list. In the end, the list couldn’t be narrowed to any fewer than twenty-two.” 

Leo Castagno, a welding teacher at Ricks, was chosen as class instructor. He agreed with the selection process. “I will tell you one of the main elements of the success of the program was the mix of participants, just the right guys,” Castagno said.

Ricks College provided equipment, instructors, and other support, and the group began training in May of 1998. Castagno remembers fondly the day the welders received their tools, “The look in their eyes was just like kids at Christmas. As a matter of fact, I couldn’t get them to throw away the boxes the equipment came in. They held on to them like a treasure.”

Castagno would lecture in English as Lane Jacques and Romero Galvan, Ricks students sent to assist in the program, translated into Spanish. “The language just wasn’t a barrier,” says Castagno. “These guys were so bright and attentive. I have the best students in the world at Ricks, but they could learn a lesson in hard work and attentiveness from my welders (in Mexico).”

One visitor to the training facility, a North American who had served his mission in Mexico, observed, “I have been in holy places. But the intense power I saw as I looked into the faces of these young men and saw the hope this program provided them, it just smote my heart. There was a power in the welding shop, a spirit to what was going on, that was overwhelming. You could instantly, and tearfully, feel the hand of the Lord in what was taking place.”
The hand of the Lord was evident in myriad details, and the first class graduated in August after a ten-week, 400-hour intensive course.

The young men stood out, and most were very quickly moved into management positions. The response by industry to this caliber of welder has been overwhelming. Benemerito employee Emilio Juarez, who played a key role in finding jobs, says, “At every contact we were received with great excitement. Without hesitation the business owners and managers not only said they needed welders, but raved about the program, saying it was the finest training in Mexico, and offered jobs on the spot.”

During an open house for businesses at the training site, one manager, not a member of the Church, commented, “I don’t know what there is about these young men. There’s a light in their faces. They are just too good to be true. To know there’s a pool of young men out there who are honest and hard working, who know how to relate to people and who have strong values! These young men will be foremen and leaders.”

The results have been impressive: the graduates that first year have, on average, quadrupled their incomes. And that group of pioneers set a standard for what was to come.

Another group would follow in the summer of 1999 with very similar results.

During the school year of 1999-2000, two graduates of the 1998 course, Josafat Torres and Rodolfo Uribe, were chosen to come to Ricks College for additional intense training.

Torres began as instructor of a year-round welding course at the Benemerito campus in Mexico City in 2000, assisted by Edgar Cervantes, a 1999 graduate. They have successfully completed four courses, with an average of 16 welders graduating per course, all of whom have certified in the American Welding Society exam.

Uribe, assisted by Ismael Rosalino from the 1999 course, instructed a unique course for returned-missionaries in Monterrey, Nuevo Leon, Mexico. A major plant in Mexico generously opened the doors of their facility for the holding of the course.

But the real story is in the difference it has made in the lives of the participants.

These young men are moving into management positions. Their earning capacity greatly increased, they are able to better provide for their families, to spend more time with them, and to be more diligent in their Church callings.

On the day Josafat Torres was sealed he ran into one of the graduates from his course at the Mexico City Temple. They embraced, and Torres asked what he was doing. He grinned a reply, “The same as you. I have had a girlfriend for many years and for many years have wanted to marry her. But her parents wouldn’t allow it until I had a good job.” He had finished the welding course during the summer, obtained employment with a major automotive company, and proclaimed, “I’m now here to be sealed to her!”
Briscoe Roberts, a former Ricks College employee and service volunteer who, with his wife Brenda, are in Mexico to support the program was told by a participant of the Monterrey course, “This is the first time I have felt peace. I’m a poor boy, and I have never known what I would do to provide for myself or for a family. I loved my mission, but even then I didn’t feel the peace I do now, knowing the Lord cares for me and that now I have a future.”
Back to “Big Joe,” Joel Arqueta: “After the program I quickly was given work, but after a month felt a prompting to go out on my own. My wife and I worked hard and I was able to buy a very old welding machine. The happiest day of all was when we were able to comply with the words of the prophet and my wife could quit her job. Our daughter soon came, and every day we are grateful my work allows my wife to stay home to raise her. Soon I was able to buy a better welding machine, then a cutting torch. Now I have been given contracts to build exercise machines. They require very exact welding, very clean and strong, but I have the training. We thank the Lord every day for this opportunity and for the difference it has made in our lives.”

When “Big Joe” was introduced to some Benemerito campus visitors during the summer of 1999, he was asked, “What do you do for a living?” His smile filled the room as he explained with pride, “I am a professional welder.” That smile tells the whole story.

Rodolfo and Reyna Uribe were sealed in the Mexico City Temple in December of 1998 and their son was born in 2000. Uribe expresses, “What a great thing it is to know the Lord is mindful of us and answers our prayers. This program will bless not just me but my family, the Church, and Mexican industry.”

Many miracles and blessings have sprung up in unexpected places as a result of the training program, among them strong feelings in the participants for Ricks.

Says Arqueta, “I thank the Lord every day for Ricks College. Ricks has made such a difference in my life; I want everyone I know to know what a great place Ricks is. At first people almost don’t want to believe. Why would Ricks come down here? What’s in this for them? But I’m able to explain there are people who care, and there really is such a place as Ricks.”

Greg Palmer (78) is a religion instructor and Director of Ricks College Outreach. He oversees the Mexico welding program. He served in the Mexico Hermosillo Mission. 
He is a weekly columnist for the Rexburg Standard Journal newspaper. Photos from Mexico are by the author.
