DATA SHEETS

USING THE STUDENT ADVISEE DATA SHEETS

This fall semester each advisor will have access to the new student advisee data sheet. It has been redesigned with both advisee and advisor in mind. Its purpose is threefold:

A It pulls together, into one resource, much of the advising information you will use. The five sections of this report are:
1.Biographical Data-preferred name, local and home address and phone numbers, date of birth, LDS mission served.

2.High School Data and Test Scores-name of high school, high school GPA and class rank. ACT standard scores and national percentiles.

3.Educational Plans-first; whether the student plans to
graduate, second; the number of semesters they plan to attend, and third; where they plan to transfer after Ricks. Also, information regarding any special needs the student might have and vocational interests are available.

4.Ricks Data-this section includes the date the student first registered and last attended, whether or not they are a transfer student, their GPA and credits carried.

5.Class Registration Data-the three most current class schedules will be available for review.
B.This report enables you as an advisor to be proactive- the data is action oriented. You no longer need to wait until your advisee makes mistakes before you help them. You can now troubleshoot your advisee' schedules and determine what they need to be on target. With the three most recent class schedules and the intended transfer schools you can make viable suggestions regarding courses needed and potential trouble areas.

C.Each semester you meet with your advisee and update their educational plans, those plans will be entered into the computer and you will be provided an updated version of your advising session. Hence, the report reflects your most recent conversations with your advisee and should serve as a summary for your advising sessions.

What Kinds of Questions will the Data Sheet Answer?

SECTION ONE
Where is your home town? Are you a return missionary? If yes, where did you serve?

SECTION TWO
What were your high school grades?
How do you rate your high school experience? Has high school adequately prepared you for your particular discipline?

SECTION THREE
Where are you planning to transfer after Ricks? What do you plan to major in? What are your vocational aspirations? Do you have special needs? How long do you plan to be at Ricks? What is your ultimate degree goal (A.S., B.S., Ph.D.)?

SECTION FOUR
Are you returning after having
previously stopped college? Are you a transfer student? Are you on probation? How many credits are you carrying? What is your GPA? What college ward are you in?

SECTION FIVE
Are you on track to accomplish your educational plan? What classes have you taken? What courses have you signed up for next term?

GRAD REPORT

GRAD REPORT is the name of the Ricks College advising report and graduation degree audit program. It is designed to help both students and faculty with academic advising. These reports are available at the Registrar's Office upon request. One very popular feature of the GRAD REPORT for the students is that they can "shop" for a potential major by requesting a GRAD REPORT based on several different major codes.

Features of the GRAD REPORT are explained below. The numbers below correspond with that particular feature on the attached sample of the GRAD REPORT.

1.Year. This year can be either the date of first enroll ment or a subsequent catalog year-whichever works to the student's advantage.

2.Major. This can either be the student's major record or a
potential major.

3.Status. "Complete" would
indicate that the student had completed all requirements and was eligible for graduation.

4.General Education Groups. Each of the G.E. groups is separated by a line of dashes, with the heading being the first line in the box. If there is no "GROUP NOT COMPLETE" message at the bottom of the box, that particular group has been filled. (Refer to paragraphs 7 and 8 below.)

5.Religion Requirement. Each student must earn a passing grade in four religion courses; and these courses should be taken one each semester. If two courses are taken in one semester, only one counts toward the Religion requirement and the other is simply elective credit. If a student is attending school part-time, one Religion course must be taken for each 16 credit hours earned toward graduation. If a student is a transfer student, he/she is not required to make up Religion courses at Ricks for those semesters not actually at Ricks. All exceptions to the Religion requirement policy must be approved by the Chairman of the Religion Department.

6.Major Requirements. Boxes containing these requirements appear following the G.E. program boxes.

7.Sub-Group Not Complete. This notation appears when the requirement is in two parts; one portion of the requirement may have been filled, but the other is not.

8.Main Group Not Complete. Some portion of there requirement has not been completed.

9.Substitutions. A General Edu-cation requirement may be filled by a class not on the approved list only by the Division Chairman who has stewardship over that particular requirement. Major requirement substitutions must be approved by the faculty advisor and Department Chair.

10.Waivers. Essentially the same as "Substitutions". However, this involves the actual waiver of a requirement and may not give credit toward graduation. This involves major requirements only-not G.E.

11.Special Exams. These may include CLEP, AP, foreign language, departmental special exams, etc. Grades are usually posted as "P" grades (which give credit toward graduation, but are not calculated into the student's GPA).

Please Note: "P" grades are not converted to "C" grades at other colleges and universities. They are handled in exactly the same manner as here at Ricks.

12.Transfer Credit. Courses which have been evaluated as being a rough equivalent of a course offered at Ricks. Quarter credit hours are devalued in the conversion to semester credits, often resulting in an irregular credit value (e.g., 3.3 credits).

13.Currently Enrolled. Even though these appear on the GRAD REPORT, the requirement is not filled until a final passing grade is posted in the student's academic record.
