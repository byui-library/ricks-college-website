PREREQUISITE SKILLS: E-211 assumes: 1) that students will be familiar with the writing process (pre-writing, drafting, revising, and editing) and the conventions of writing (grammar, punctuation, mechanics, and spelling); 2) that students will have reading skills appropriate to the college level; and 3) that students will have been introduced to the library and forms of documentation.

COURSE DESCRIPTION: E-211 emphasizes the application of critical thinking skills to college reading and writing. Readings focus on expository and argumentative discourse drawn from various disciplines. Writing assignments spring from class readings and research. (Unlike the prerequisite E-111, which may in part employ the personal experience essay, E-211 focuses on written texts as sources for summary, analysis, critique, synthesis, and argument.)

COURSE AIMS: E-211 aims are based on the assumption that reading and writing are connected and necessary to the development of critical thinking skills.

CLASS PROCEDURES. To receive credit for quizzes, prep checks, and writing exercises, you must be in class. Attendance is taken daily. You cannot miss more than two weeks of class and still pass the course. Papers are due by 5 p.m. in Clarke 329A, and late papers can earn no higher than a C grade if received within one week of the due date. Every class session there will be a quiz and/or homework due. Discussion, student interests, and student needs will direct the daily work and corresponding homework. If you are uncomfortable with the topic or language of any of the assigned reading, please let me know immediately.

ESSAY GUIDELINES. All essays should be typed (computer generated), double-spaced, with one-inch margins. Submit papers according to the APA format. Your title page should look like the sample student essay in The Little Brown Handbook, p. 321. You are required to visit the Writing Center once each block, to assure you know where to receive assistance with your papers.
