The Lighter Side
School board, officials question in-school spa

PITTSBURGH (AP) — Makeovers, basketball, scuba diving, lowfat cooking lessons and yoga — that’s how teachers at a high school spent their day Tuesday. And they got paid for it.

Now school board members, district administrators and parents are asking why teachers at Erie’s Central High School used an in-service day, usually used by teachers to get organized and discuss school business, for an in-house spa.

Teachers started the day with medical checkups, moved on to massages, classes in cooking, yoga, scuba, tax tips and then played pickup basketball games before ending the day at an Erie tavern.

Principal Jerry Mifsud said it was a wellness day a way to “get rid of winter blues.”

Meanwhile, teachers at other schools spent the day in administrative meetings and discussed curriculum.

Both sets of teachers received 90 percent of their usual daily wages. Central’s teachers and administrators earned $17,000. A hospital and other local businesses donated their time and the activities did not cost the district any money, school officials said.

Central High teachers and officials said district officials didn’t complain about a similar day last year. They say people often ignore the stress associated with teaching and argue teachers and students will benefit because teachers will be healthier and more relaxed.

“Our staff does a tremendous job on a day to day basis. People don’t realize the stress — in the past six months we have had three teachers have heart attacks,” Mifsud said.

Jim Herdzik, president of Erie’s school board, said he had never heard of teachers using an in-service day for “a stress-free day” in 10 years with the district.
