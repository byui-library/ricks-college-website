5.    Locate #1 cylinder, then connect a timing light to your engine. Most lights have leads that hook to the terminals on the
         battery, and an inductive pickup that should go around the #1 spark plug wire near the spark plug. Some have an arrow
         on the pickup that indicates polarity, and the arrow should point toward the plug. If not, it doesn't matter.

NOTE:  Make sure the timing light leads and wires are out of the way of moving parts, belts, pulleys, hot manifolds, etc.  Make sure you set the timing light somewhere secure so it doesn't fall on the floor and break.  They are Fragile!
 
6.    With the engine turned off, clean the timing marks on the crankshaft pulley or the flywheel, and mark them with chalk
        or white paint so you can see them more easily.

7.    Start the engine and point the light toward the timing marks and pull the trigger. The light should flash steadily and stop the
        marks so you can see them.
Caution:  Make sure the cooling fan doesn't hit the timing light or your hands.  It looks like it's stopped when you shine the light on it, but it will bite you!

8.    Adjust the timing so it is at the setting specified in the book.

9.    What was the actual timing set at when you started?_______________ degrees

10.    What is it now that you have adjusted it? _________degrees.

11.    If your distributor has a vacuum advance, start the engine and apply a vacuum of 15" or more with a vacuum pump, or
         suck on it hard through a hose with your mouth.

    • Does it hold a vacuum? ______ Yes ______ No
    • Does the engine change r.p.m. when you apply the vacuum? _____ Yes ____ No

12.    With the vacuum advance disconnected, raise engine speed to about 2500 rpm and record the timing advance at that
         speed:_______________ degrees. (Note: This will tell you if the mechanical advance mechanism is operating properly.)

13.    Connect the vacuum line to the advance mechanism. Increase engine speed to about 2500 again and record total timing
         advance:_______________ (Note: This will tell you if your vacuum advance and mechanical advance are working
         togther to give you proper ignition timing at cruise speeds.)

14.    Disconnect the timing light and remove it. Reconnect connectors, hoses, etc. that you had to disconnect to set the timing.
         Make sure distributor is tightened down so it won't move by itself.
