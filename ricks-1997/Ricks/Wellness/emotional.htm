How to Feel Like a Winner Every Day

The key to controlling stress and boosting your energy level is finding the time in your hectic schedule to do more of both.

 Stress-busting essentials

Sleep soundly. You can cope better with stress when refreshed.

Talk about what’s bothering you. Your family, friends and co-workers can help just by listening.

Relax at least once a day. Try to reserve at least 20 minutes to put your feet up, listen to music or daydream.

Exercise regularly. It helps release the physical tension caused by stress.

Snicker, giggle, laugh out loud. You’ll feel better and put your stress in perspective.

Set limits on extra demands. Learn to say "no" to the nonessential.

STEP 1 Try to cut back by just 10% the time you spend on daily activities you have to do, like taking a shower, getting dressed or going to the store.

STEP 2 Instead of eliminating other routines, like watching TV or reading the paper, simply shave the time you spend doing them by the same amount.

STEP 3 Once you’re in the habit of trimming time, you’ll begin to feel not as rushed, more satisfied and less stressed out.

STEP 4 Use the time you’ve "made" for pastimes that can further energize you. Examples: a new hobby, playing with the kids or a half hour workout every other day.

Know your peaks. Schedule your most difficult work for when you’re rested and alert.

Breathe deeply. Every 1-2 hours, spend five minutes inhaling and exhaling deeply, hands on ribs. Benefit: You’ll promote calmness, mental focus and energy.

Take steps to success. For a quick burst of energy, bypass the elevator and use the stairs.

Get an instant charge. Splash cold water on your face, snap a rubber band worn on the wrist or pop a breath mint - mint flavors are stimulating.

Smile! The shift in facial muscles and changes in blood flow can make you feel more alert.

Prioritize what you need to do in order of importance and timeliness. Separate tasks into three lists: "Must do now," "Must do soon" and "Must do."

Manage time-wasters. Idea: Remain standing when someone drops by (it minimizes chit-chat)...keep a clock nearby (it will make you more aware of time).

Set boundaries. Let people know when you’re available and when not to interrupt. Try: Partially closing your door or just telling coworkers you’re busy.

Bundle activities. Set aside blocks of time each day to return calls, process paperwork or run errands.

Recommended: Try these proven ideas in your home routine, too.

Pile on the praise. Offer genuine compliments to others. Reason: Someday they’ll return the favor, making you feel like a winner.

Minimize "homework." Plan tasks so you finish as many as possible before you go home. You’ll be in better shape at the start of each new day.

Make fitness fun. Advantage: You’ll stick with it and stay energized. Suggestions: Stroll briskly with a friend...dance wildly to your favorite music...walk the dog.

Tap the power of optimism. Before going to bed, jot down all the good things that happened during the day. You’ll energize yourself for peak performance tomorrow.

 

Eat Right

Consume a variety of foods so you’re getting energy-boosters like magnesium, zinc, iron, B vitamins and the antioxidants beta carotene and vitamins A and E. For quick lifts: Keep lunch light - and snack on raisins, low-fat granola or fig bars later in the afternoon to get fast-fuel carbohydrates.

 

Sleep Tight

Wake up and go to bed at the same time each day to help your biological clock run smoothly. For a restful night: Avoid caffeine and alcohol, which can disturb sleep...drink a glass of warm milk or listen to relaxing music before bed...keep bedtime reading light.

 

See Friends

Numerous studies suggest friendships boost your health and happiness. Example: People with close relationships live longer and handle personal setbacks better.

 

Keep Fit

Exercise strengthens your heart, lungs and muscles to help you do more physically. But it also increases the supply of oxygen to your brain, boosting alertness so you can absorb information quicker and perform other mental tasks faster.

U Always allow extra time for everything you do to avoid the needless stress of being rushed.

U Set aside one day a week for rest and renewal to recharge your physical, mental and emotional batteries.

U Keep moving - garden, jog or find a sport you enjoy. Benefit: You’ll boost natural energy-building hormones.

U Drink at least six glasses of pure water daily. Why: You’ll fight dehydration, which can trigger fatigue.

U Read more. Learn something new every day to stimulate your brain and keep it active.

U At your next check up, ask your doctor whether any medications you’re taking can sap your energy or cause drowsiness or trouble sleeping.
