FROM POLITICS TO PERFORMANCES

Sen. Hatch, Janice Kapp Perry inspire patriotic spirit at Veteran's Day concert

Paul Engemann performs a song by Sen. Orrin Hatch and Janice Kapp Perry at the Veterans Day concert Saturday night. TIMOTHY ROBISON / Scroll

by Andrea Scott
scroll staff

It would be hard to have walked away from the Hart Auditorium Saturday night without feeling proud to be an American.

"Freedom's Light," hosted by Sen. Orrin Hatch of Utah and Janice Kapp Perry, featured Pastor Wintley Phipps, Jenny Jordan, Paul Engemann, Marvin Goldstein and the Ricks College combined choirs in a Veteran's Day concert.

Along with the festive decor of red, white and blue, the concert included both patriotic and gospel songs, most of which were written by Sen. Hatch and Perry.

The night began with Engemann, along with the combined choirs, singing "Freedom's Light," the title song. After a few more numbers, Goldstein graced the ivories with a patriotic medley.

"I put three songs together that all mean freedom. 'America the Beautiful' - freedom. 'Battle Hymn of the Republic' - freedom. 'Exodus' - freedom of the Jews," Goldstein said.

Perry later addressed the audience and shared some light-hearted songs she had written about Goldstein celebrating his 50th birthday this year and Hatch's ambition in writing song lyrics. Then Hatch and Perry sang "I'll Pray for You."

Sen. Hatch recognized all veterans attending the concert and shared the story of his brother, Jess, who was pronounced missing in action when Sen. Hatch was 10 years old. The remains of his brother were found two years later.

Toward the end of the evening, Phipps, an African American pastor originally from Trinidad, recounted the origin of "Amazing Grace." The lyrics were written by John Newton who, before he became a Christian, was the captain of a slave ship. The melody is believed to have been heard by Newton from the slaves beneath the deck of his ship, Phipps said.

The sound of waves crashing and rolling accompanied Phipps' bass voice as he sang the song.

To cap off the night, all participants stood and sang together for the finale.

"God bless America. God bless our country," Hatch said.
