Robot rescue responds to WTC cleanup

by carl neil ekman III
ekm98001@byui.edu
Scroll Staff

The Packbot, used to search through the rubble of the fallen World Trade Center, has been able to locate several victims because of its ability to fit where a human cannot.
Searching through rubble and wreckage, a force of 12 robots is going places men cannot. These shoebox-sized robots wound their way inside the World Trade Center and two other buildings searching for survivors of the Sept. 11 attack. They were guided by a crew of specialized rescuers from Center for Robot-Assisted Search and Rescue (CRASAR).

Although iRobots found no survivors, several bodies were located, Lt. Col. John Blitch, coordinator for the robotic rescue, said.

The robots have now been pulled back due to repairs and are being prepared for the possibility of further need.

The iRobot Packbot, were originally designed to aid military mobile strike forces.

“The iRobot’s Packbot project was instituted to aid reconnaissance operations in urban terrain. Currently, a prototype under development with funding from the Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DAPRA), the robot is designed for durability and versatility, featuring robust systems and onboard data-processing capabilities that will enable rapid response to a dynamic, urban environment. Its portable size and ability to endure adverse conditions will make it ideal for venturing into areas that are hazardous or inaccessible,” according to the iRobot Web site.

These robots were used because of their effectiveness in going to unsafe or inaccessible places to human or canine searchers. The Packbot can be dropped from three meters above the ground onto concrete, according to iRobot, and other robots “can flatten themselves to squeeze through tight spaces or position themselves upright to look over objects,” according to the New York Times.

“Using the robots can not only save trapped victims, it can protect the lives of rescuers confronted by unstable rubble,” Sylvia Pag n Westphal of New Scientist said of the expendability of the robots.

These robots’ cost ranges from $10,000 - $40,000, depending on the design and the sensors that are attached, such as microphones, infrared cameras, sonar and much more.

The robots are “very effective for finding bodies in confined spaces,” Blitch said.
