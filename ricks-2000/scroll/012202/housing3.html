Establishing residency requires meeting conditions

by Sarah Lunt
LUN99013@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Establishing residency can be a painstaking task, but under a few conditions, students can become a resident and avoid paying the high costs of out-of-state tuition.

State schools have different policies than private universities like BYU-I. Tuition costs can jump when a student is not a legal resident of the state.

Many students, when finished with BYU-Idaho, want to stay in Idaho and go to one of the many universities in the state or transfer to a college in Utah, but they are not from Utah or Idaho.

Certain qualifications are required to be a legal resident of Idaho, and thus be able to claim in-state tuition. These conditions are provided in the Idaho State Code 33-3717, found on the Web site http://www3.state.id.us/cgi-bin/newidst?sctid=330370017.K.

Nine conditions qualify a person to be a legal resident of Idaho. These are:

1) One or more of the person’s parents must claim Idaho as their permanent home and prove that they live there most of the time. The parent or guardian must have lived in Idaho at least one year before the person enters school.

2) Less than 50 percent of the person’s support must come from parents or guardians who are not residents of Idaho. The person must also have lived in Idaho for at least one year and be living there for other reasons besides just schooling (his or her home must be established there permanently).

3) A person who went to high school in Idaho and then entered college the following school year is legal.

4) The person may be married to a legal resident and be legal.

5) The person is a member of the U.S. Armed forces stationed in Idaho on orders.

6) A parent or guardian is a member of the Armed Forces, is stationed in Idaho and the person receives 50 percent or more of his or her support from that guardian.

7) The person is honorably separated after two years of service from the Armed Forces and says Idaho is his or her home at the time of separation.

8) The person is a resident and has been away for less than a year without legally establishing residency anywhere else.

9) The person is a member of one of the Idaho Native American tribes.

There are many students at BYU-I who are residents of states further east that may want to stay in areas with higher LDS populations. Other states that students may wish to transfer to, in which they would have to qualify as residents to receive lower tuition rates, are Utah, Arizona and California.

These three states may be a little harder to gain residency in. Many students may choose to move to the state of their choice and work until obtaining residency. Young men who wish to return to a certain state after missions may obtain drivers’ licenses in those states in hopes to obtain residency.

Regardless of situations in life, whether newly married or just wishing to transfer to a new school, students should call the admissions offices of the schools to which they wish to transfer and obtain a list of residency requirements.
