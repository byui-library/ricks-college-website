Federal documents

During the early days of the United States government printing contracts were issued by Congress.
The poor quality of some of these publications led Congress to start reforms in printing practice:
to appoint a superintendent to supervise contracts, and finally
to establish its own printing facilities.
The pendulum has swung back to privatizing many governmental publications.
Journal of National Cancer Institute was made available only through subscription with a non government contractor recently.

The Government Printing Office is an independent part of the legislative branch of government which provides printing services to all branches of the government.
The Public Printer is appointed to manage the GPO, the Government Printing Office.
The Superintendent of Documents is responsible for distribution of publications.
Part of the superintendent's duties is to assign classification numbers to documents.

In 1859, depository libraries were established in each congressional district.
State libraries and land grant college libraries were also designated as depositories.
Land grant colleges are often those with the agriculture and cooperative extension service programs.

Through time the number of depository libraries has increased to over a thousand.
The McKay library is a selective depository library which means only selected documents are sent to our library.
We became a depository library in 1946.
We are obligated to keep any document that we receive as a depository for five years unless it is replaced by a revised copy.
If the document does not have lasting value to our collection, after five years we offer to give it to other depositories. Universities because of graduate programs keep documents for longer periods of time and select more publications.
Regional depository libraries keep everything that the government produces.
University of Idaho in Moscow and Utah State University in Logan are regional libraries.
Regional libraries supply other depositories with materials that we do not select.

Our library catalog includes everything that we have received since 1993.
Soon all of our documents will be listed in our catalog.
The paper collection has "GDF" as the abbreviated collection code.
Microfiche materials have "GDM" as the collection code.
Look for [microform] next to the title or (mf) in serial holdings summary.

The Monthly Catalog of U.S. Government Publications will show the variety of government documents.
The printed catalog had a number of indexes at one time.
We have microfilm copies of older catalogs in the newspaper area.
Examples of the older (GDF 3.8:) and new condensed catalog (GDF 3.8/7:) are available.
An electronic version of this index is available on CD and on Internet.
Monthly Catalog or MOCAT  http://www.gpo.gov/su_docs/dpos/adpos400.html

A CD-ROM version from a company is available on the computer network.
Use the menus and help screens to use this program.
The best results come from understanding Boolean mathematics ideas.
Boolean operators are "and," "or," and "not" which create groups.
"And" creates a group that has both words.
"Or" expands the group to include groups that either of the words.
"Not" excludes from the group any that also have the word.
To combine the results of search 1 with search 2, type "#1 and #2".

Many publications are available for purchase.
If you find a publication that you feel would have lasting value to our collection let someone know.
Addresses of bookstores where documents may be purchased are found in the printed Monthly Catalog and subject bibliographies.

Pamphlets are available free of charge or at inexpensive prices from the Consumer Information Center in Pueblo, Colorado.
Use the catalog to these publications,

Consumer Information Catalog (GDF GS 11.9:) or  http://www.pueblo.gsa.gov/
Topographical maps issued by the U.S. Geological Survey are often sold in sporting goods or book stores.
Indexes to these maps often have addresses to these stores on the back of the index.
Our collection of topographical maps is found in map cabinets on the second floor.
We only collect Idaho and neighboring state maps.

Some government periodicals are indexed in periodical indexes, such as Reader's Guide and Education Index.
An index for just government periodicals, Index to Government Periodicals, gives SuDoc numbers and subscription information but is not current.
Full text periodical CD-ROM products often include government periodicals.
Copyright for government publications allows reproduction without as many restrictions as is available to private publications.

Public Affairs Information Service: PAIS Bulletin or PAIS International  indexes books, pamphlets, periodicals, Internet sites and government publications published throughout the world. This is a valuable source for locating international, national, state, and local documents.

Arrangement

Libraries arrange government documents in a variety of ways.
Some libraries classify, catalog, and shelve the books with the rest of their collection.
Our catalog is the clue to our arrangement.

Some libraries place documents in a closed stack area requiring a call slip be given to the attendant.
Here electronic publications are the only documents that require a call slip or having a library worker assist you.
Materials that have [computer file] next to the title may require assistance in identifying which computers are prepared to access the computer database.

Documents are arranged by the Superintendent of Document numbers.
SuDoc numbers keep materials by the same government agencies together.
The bulk of our collection is arranged in two collections the first floor.
"GDF" is the collection assigned to paper formats.
"GDM" is the collection for microfiche.

An Explanation of the Superintendent of Documents Classification System (GDF GP3.2:C 56/8/990) gives a more detailed discussion.
Briefly slashes, dashes, dots, and colons are used to separate parts of the number.
The agency is represented by letters.
"GP" are letters assigned to publications by the Government Printing Office.
The next number identifies the office within the agency or department.
"GP 1." is used for materials by the parent agency.
"GP 3." represents Superintendent of Documents publications.
"GP 3.2:" is assigned to the series of general publications.
Cutter or book numbers are assigned to pamphlets or materials.
Numbers separated by (/) slashes may represent parts of a series.
The final number in this instance stands for the year that the publication was produced, 1990.
Using three digits to identify the year is already causing some debate among librarians as the year 2000 approaches.
Using a four digit number would solve the problem.

The arrangement is roughly how a computer would file the call numbers.
List of Classes of US Government Publications Available for Selection by Depository Libraries (GDF GP 3.24:) is used as the authority on which publications come first.
The word "classes" means groups with similar classification.
A class stem is the number that all issues of a periodical or series share.
Class stems will be used with serials.
"GP 3.24:" is the stem assigned to the List of Classes.
This is a publication that we only keep until a new one is produced.
"GP 3.24:997/1" is the number that was assigned to the March 1997 issue.
This publication comes out quarterly so the one stands for the first quarter.
Other issues will be published in June, September, and December.
