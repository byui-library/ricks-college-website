BYU-Jerusalem art on display at BYU-Idaho
Piece by Ricks College alumnus displayed in Taylor Building this week

by Sarah Lunt
LUN99013@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

This painting of Orson Hyde dedicating the land of Israel is on display in the John Taylor Building. It will be on display until Thursday on the second floor near the religion department office. The painting was commissioned to Ricks College alumnus Clark Kelley Price by the BYU Jerusalem Center. James Ricks / Scroll
A 24-by-30-inch painting of Orson Hyde dedicating the land of Israel is on display in the John Taylor Building through Thursday. It is located on the second floor in the balcony area facing the elevators near the religion department office.

The painting was commissioned to Ricks College alumnus Clark Kelley Price by the BYU Jerusalem Center.

Orson Hyde was born January 8, 1805, in Oxford, Conn. After becoming an orphan, he desired an education and set out on his own to gain one, according to Orson Hyde’s 1841 Mission to the Holy Land by David B. Galbraith.

Hyde heard of Joseph Smith’s “golden Bible” while living in Kirtland, Ohio. He began to gain a testimony of the truth of the Book of Mormon even before he saw it, asking, “Who knows but that this golden bible may break up all our religion, and change its whole features and bearing?”

On October 30, 1831, Hyde was baptized. He was set apart by the Prophet Joseph Smith to be a missionary, especially to the Jews, Galbraith said in his book.

After being ordained an Apostle, Elder Hyde was commissioned by Joseph Smith to go to Palestine and dedicate the land for the return of the Jews.

On October 24, 1841, Elder Hyde climbed the Mount of Olives and recorded a dedicatory prayer. His prayer included requests to God that Judah be gathered, Jerusalem be built up again and a temple be reared there.
