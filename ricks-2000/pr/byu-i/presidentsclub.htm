Church leaders foresee great future for BYU-Idaho

It was a night to look to the future as supporters of Ricks College listened to LDS Church leaders and college President David A. Bednar describe the transition of the junior college to Brigham Young University-Idaho.

"The Lord knows the future," said Elder Henry B. Eyring to members of the President’s Club on Wednesday night. "The Lord has known this opportunity was going to come here."

The former Ricks president, who now serves as commissioner of education and an apostle for the LDS Church, was in Rexburg for meetings with the Executive Committee of the Board of Trustees.

During a banquet in the Manwaring Center, he said some people might think that Rexburg is an unlikely place for an institution like BYU-Idaho. But he said BYU-Idaho will have a great influence on the world and that people already have been led here to help with the changes that will come. "The Lord not only leads the church, but he love the institutions of the Church Educational System," he said.

He compared BYU-Idaho to a beautiful new temple that he recently visited in the Dominican Republic. He did not quite understand why such a large temple had been built there, but he knows there is a reason that will become very evident in the future.

Elder Richard G. Scott, also an LDS Church apostle, added, "This is clearly going to be a blessing that will resound around the world." After sitting in meetings at Ricks during the day, he was encouraged that "all the things I’ve treasured about Ricks are going to preserved."

Elder Scott added that when LDS Church President Gordon B. Hinckley told of his vision of converting Ricks to BYU-Idaho that there was enthusiastic support among the church leaders. "There was a feeling in the room that we were participating in a very important moment and the president was acting under inspiration."

Elder M. Russell Ballard, also a church apostle, stated, "What is happening at Ricks is a result of revelation." He said the Lord has handpicked a team of educators that includes having the Bednars at Ricks at this time, Elder Merrill J. Bateman as president of BYU in Provo, Utah, and Eyring as commissioner of church education.

"Whatever occurs in the next few years, I hope all of you will sustain and support it," Elder Ballard said. "It will be a tremendous thing for this university for graduates to go into the world and say we graduated from Brigham Young University-Idaho."

General Relief Society President Mary Ellen Smoot, who has had five children attend Ricks, shared her enthusiasm for the changes that will come. "You’re going to see something exciting in the future and you need to lend your support."

President Bednar stated, "The challenge is to keep things the same while many things change." He explained, "The spirit of Ricks is not found in a building, it’s not found in a place, it’s found in the people, the students, the teachers."

He is surprised when he sometimes hears a person say the spirit of Ricks was changed on the day of the announcement. "The spirit of Ricks will not be diminished. In fact, if we do this transition properly, it will be greatly enhanced."

President Bednar recalled a conversation with President Hinckley when the church president said the time was at hand for Ricks College to become a university. "That’s all I needed to know. I have no doubts," he said of the prophet’s vision. "This is truly a spiritual event."

He added, "We truly are going places in higher education where no one has ever gone before."
