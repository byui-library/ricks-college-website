CELEBRATING THE SPORI

With progress often comes an increased awareness of history. Such is the case as the college acknowledges the forthcoming replacement of the Jacob Spori Building. As the aging structure is scheduled to be removed this fall, the college will be "Celebrating the Spori" in association with homecoming. A committee chaired by Bruce Hobbs, director of Public Relations, has been selected to plan ways to honor the heritage which the Spori Building symbolizes. The committee's ideas illustrate the scope of change during the past nearly 100 years of existence of the building: from archival photos to virtual tours on CD and Internet; from oil paintings to digital camera history of construction; from incorporating existing features into the new structure to preserving pieces of the stone and hardwood as collector items.

The Spori Building has been an important part of the lives of many students, employees, and citizens of the Upper Snake River Valley. The Celebrating the Spori Committee is actively seeking insights as plans develop. Faculty members have submitted proposals for commissioned artwork that will reflect the heritage and character of the building. Alumni and friends are asked to submit their personal Spori Building stories which can help keep the memories alive. Submissions of stories may be sent to Devin Shaum, director of the Alumni Association and a member of the Celebrating the Spori Committee, by e-mail to alum@ricks.edu or mailed to Alumni Office, 220 Kimball, Rexburg, ID 83460-1656.

CONSTRUCTION BEGINS ON NEW RADIO/GRAPHICS BUILDING

Ground has been broken on a new building that will house the studios of two college-operated radio stations and the graphic services office. The 4,225 square foot building will be located between the Kimball Student and Administrative Services Building and the tennis courts along East Viking Drive. The one-story, steel-frame building will have a brick veneer with stone quoins, says campus architect Rulon Nielsen. It was designed by Nielsen and is being constructed by Vern Clark and Sons Construction of Idaho Falls. The building will house KRIC-FM, a public radio station owned and operated by Ricks; KWBH-FM, a low-power, student-operated station; and Graphic Services. The building is scheduled to be completed Sept. 1 in time for the radio stations and graphic services to move out of the aging Jacob Spori Building that will torn down later this year. Although a new classroom building will be constructed on the location of the Spori Building starting in 2001, college officials decided to relocate these services in a separate building for several reasons. A higher location on the hill will give KRIC-FM a direct signal to its transmitter on the South Menan Butte, the radio stations will have a facility built specifically to their needs and the offices will be close to the administration building.

CONSTRUCTION PROJECTS

The reduction in the number of students' vehicles on campus at the end of winter semester brings an increase in other vehicles - notably dump trucks, back hoes, and construction equipment. It is obviously once again construction season on the Ricks College campus. Projects in the works this summer are: renovation of the old Administration Building to become part of the McKay Library; Phase 1 of the upgrade of the Smith Building HVAC controls, lighting, windows, and asbestos abatement; Manwaring Center HVAC controls, lighting, and ceiling replacement; Hart Building fire alarm system, pool wall vapor-seal, and temperature humidity controls; KRIC-FM /Graphic Arts Studio construction; various high voltage upgrades campus wide; various roof replacement contracts on campus buildings; and preparations to facilitate the Spori Building replacement.

PUBLIC FORUM ON HISTORICAL RECORDS

The Idaho State Historical Records Advisory Board will hold a public forum at Ricks College in Rexburg Thursday, May 18. The state board is looking for input into access and preservation of historical records in Idaho. The meeting will run from noon to 2 p.m. in the McKay Library and the public is invited. Blaine Bake, archivist at Ricks College and a member of the state Historical Records Advisory Board, says the group is seeking input on a proposed 10-year strategic plan to improve the access and preservation of historical records.

The mission of the board is to be an advocate for the creation, preservation, dissemination and use of information that accurately chronicles the people and institutions of Idaho, both public and private. The board has 14 members from across Idaho, including archivists, librarians, historians, records managers, court records administrators, and current and former elected officials.
