ASAP (AdaptiveService Adventure Program) is a program with a difference. We get people, disabled and non-disabled, to do things they never thought they would or could. We started out as an appendage to the Outdoor Club, which was run by Dave Wescott. Both Dave and the Outdoor Club are gone now. But when we go rappelling, you'll meet Dave. He’s one of the top men in outdoor recreation Nationally. And a good friend to ASAP. We’ve been a lot of places since 1986.

The ASAP agenda is simple. We encourage everyone to have fun. We believe people learn more, adapt better to change, and gain interesting insights, if they are having fun. We will challenge you, and you will creatively get through the difficulties that keep coming up. That is our agenda. We will be there to help you any time, except for 5:30 in the morning. That’s a bit early for us non morning people.

We encourage the disabled students of the University to join us. ASAP was created for them. As a volunteer, you will be expected to search these people out, befriend them, and encourage them to come out to our activities and meetings.

It has been our experience that if a disability is faced and accepted, most of the challenges and difficulties that come with it become secondary, and very minimal. This is your prime objective as a ASAP volunteer. Get these handicapped students out. President Joe J. Christensen gave it to me, and I’m giving to you. He said, “Part of ASAP’s mission to Ricks is to bring the disabled students out and bring them into the mainstream of the College’s life.” And as President Kimball taught, “Just do it!” Your secondary objective is to have fun, and be creative.

The program part of ASAP consists of those off campus activities which are considered 'High Adventure.' These activities The ASAP StaffI maintains complete control over for safety's sake. At this point, we have three months worth of skiing planned for winter semester. April is left open. In February we'll have an over nighter at Badger Creek. This should be a fun start at Y2K and Y2K+1. And we'll start ski training in January.

It should be understood that most of our events depend on Idaho's unsteady weather conditions, which can change, and very often does, every five minutes. If we do not have good weather during the activity's planned time frame, we will either reschedule it, or drop it all together. There have been times, and still will be, when the weather has been rotten down here on the flats, and we've gambled and gone anyway, and the day has turned out to be perfectly beautiful in the high country.

There will be a sign up sheet for each of these activities. We have to know how many people will be going for logistical reasons. These sheets will be closed one day and a half prior to the scheduled activity. Usually Thursday at noon. If you are not on the list, you don't go. And if we feel like we don't have enough warm bodies to make the event worth while, we won't go. So please sign up as soon as you can.

If there is an activity that you would like to try, and think our group would enjoy, just come to us. We are pretty open about these things. But there are two things the Administration frowns on. They are sky-diving and bungie-jumping. The college's insurance won't cover them...so just forget about those two activities. We can be very narrow minded, too. And its not that we wouldn't like to do them, but we’d rather keep our employment.

As for the club, you guys have complete control over it. You can have one on-campus based activity a month. We've gone to the game-room, played indoor baseball, had fun going to the Planetarium...and then there's always bowling. Steve loves bowling.

There will be a slight minimal fee for these activities. The highest charge will be for our Badger Creek retreat in February. We charge $15.00 for it. That's about the highest charge we'll give you. Shouldwe go Snowmobiling in april...that too will be $15.

If we could, we'd make everything for free. But if we did that, we'd get a lot of 'Free-Bees.' We want only quality and responsible people in AAG... We want only the best!

We do not want money to be an obstacle, however. If you want to be an active AAG volunteer, or have been in the past, and have run short on funds, just grab Bruce or myself...we'll work something out.
