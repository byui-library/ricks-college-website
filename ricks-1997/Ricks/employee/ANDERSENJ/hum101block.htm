Course Objectives:

            The study of humans’ attempts to find meaning and fulfillment in life is called the humanities.  Our course focuses specifically on the arts as an avenue of human expression.  Like most everything in life, appreciation of the arts is enhanced by education and exposure. It is the goal of this course to introduce you to the basic elements of the arts and to acquaint you with some of the greatest human achievements in art, music, architecture, and literature.  This is an introductory course, designed for those who have little knowledge or experience with the arts.

            As a result of this course, I hope you will not only be better educated, but will also embark on a journey of arts appreciation and enjoyment that will last a lifetime.

Expectations:

            People sometimes compare the relationship between student and teacher in the learning process to an empty glass and a pitcher of water.   This analogy assumes students have no knowledge of the subject.  Their job is to bring their empty minds to class, and the teacher’s job is to fill it up.  The weakness of this analogy is that it places the entire responsibility for learning on the teacher; when in reality learning is a joint-venture.

            I see my relationship to you as similar to a jar of Tang to a glass of water.  You already have some knowledge; my responsibility is to build on your experience and add flavor to it.  Your job is to make use of this new knowledge by stirring it in and making it a part of you.  If you don’t take an active part in this process, then try as I might, my efforts will sink to the bottom of the glass, and make very little impact in your life.

My Job:  I commit to do my very best to present our course material in a professional and interesting manner.  I promise to respect your views and opinions, and make any effort to help you succeed.

Your Job:  I expect you to be a scholar, not just a student, as J. Bonner Ritchie explains:

In the student-scholar model, the individual students not only accept responsibility for their own learning, but for contributing to the quality of the class.  They read and communicate to learn and to grow, not just to fulfill an assignment or to get a grade.  They define learning objectives and activities beyond the course requirements.  They do not continually ask if a reading assignment or discussion point “is going to be on the exam.”  ... Scholar students can make almost any class a worthwhile learning experience for themselves and class members.  (Brigham Young Magazine August 1996)

Grading:

            Grades will be determined by your performance on four exams (100 points each), three major quizzes, several unannounced quizzes designed to encourage regular attendance and preparation (10 points each), and the completion of four value assessment modules (25 points each).  Grades will be assigned according to the following percentages:

                                                A      95-100              C       74-76

                                                A-     90-94                C-     70-73

                                                B+     87-89                D+    67-69

                                                B       84-86                D      64-66

                                                B-     80-83                D-     60-63

                                                C+     77-79                F       0-59

            In grading, I try to follow as closely as possible a gospel model.  I don't grade on a curve because it suggests that standards are relative (Will Jesus judge according to a curve?).  Moreover, I'd be glad to assign everyone an A, if everyone earns 90% or above on exams and assignments.  I don't believe in extra credit (Would the Lord allow us to pay 15% tithing in exchange for our not living the Word of Wisdom?) but I do believe in repentance in keeping with the spirit of Alma 34:33 (see me for details).

Exams:

            The exams are objective in style and are to be taken in the testing center (dates are listed in the lecture schedule).  You are allowed two days in which to take the exams.  Should you not take the exams when they are scheduled, you will receive a zero, unless you miss the exam due to official school business, weddings, etc.  In this case, I expect you to notify me in advance so that arrangements can be made for you to take the exam without a penalty.

Cultural Events Attendance:

            Experiencing the arts is as important as talking about them.  For this reason, one of the General Education requirements mandates attendance at several cultural events through a non-credit course known as Fine Arts 100.  If you are not familiar with this GE requirement, see p. 28 of the Catalog or contact the secretary of the Division of Performing and Fine Arts in Sporri 270 (ext. 1248). There is a myriad of on-campus events which you can attend, and many are free of charge to full-time students. For more information and schedules of events, call the Arts hotline at 356-1251 or visit the Fine Arts homepage.

Value Assessment Modules:

     One of my primary objectives of this course is for you to make a connection between the course content and your own values.  The 13th Article of Faith states, "If there is anything virtuous, lovely, or of good report or praiseworthy, we seek after these things."  The Value Assessment Modules are designed to help you incorporate that which is of value in the arts in your daily living, and to eliminate from your life that which is of little value.  There are four modules, each corresponding to a major section of the course.  Modules one, three, and four are based on articles included in the back of this booklet.  To complete a module you will read each article, then complete a form which will ask you to think about the ideas presented in the modules and apply them to your own life.  Module two requires you to listen to a cassette tape on reserve in the libraryand complete the form in the back of this booklet.  If you respond thoughtfully and completely and submit the forms on the appropriate date, you will earn 25 points per module.

Classroom Conduct:

            Most Ricks students choose to study here primarily because they want to learn in an environment free from worldly excesses in dress and conduct.  This is also one of the main reasons I chose to work here.  We all have committed to abide by the Ricks College Code of Honor and Dress and Grooming Standards.  I expect each of you to fulfill your commitment as you attend class, just as I intend to fulfill mine.  Students who do not conform to the established standards will be asked not to attend class.  Moreover, the consumption of food and beverages and the wearing of hats in class are considered inappropriate.
