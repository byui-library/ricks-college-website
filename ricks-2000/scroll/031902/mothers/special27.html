Artist spends majority of time in the desert to paint

by Lindsie Taylor
TAY01027@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Artist Georgia O'Keeffe is shown in this undated photo. AP Photo Archive
Regarded as one of the best woman artists in the 20th century, Georgia O’Keeffe is most noted for her art work of flowers up close, southwestern motifs and various abstract designs.

O’Keeffe, born in 1887, began art lessons at age 11. She attended and taught at seven different colleges throughout her life.

She worked briefly as a commercial artist in Chicago in 1908, but later displayed her works at fellow artist Alfred Stieglitz’s gallery in New York City, starting in 1917.

Stieglitz acted as Keeffe’s agent, selling her paintings and arranging her shows. They later married in 1924 and moved to the top floor of the Shelton Hotel in New York City, where many of her city skyline art work originated.

In 1929, O’Keeffe left her home, her husband and her job to move to New Mexico, for a change of scenery and new artistic references.

She spent the summers backpacking around the desert, visiting friends and monuments while painting. She drove her Model A Ford to New Mexico each summer until her husband died in 1946, when she moved there permanently.

Ghost Ranch, her home in the desert, was the place of much of her southwestern artwork, including bleached animal bones, the mesas, rugged mountains and various desert plants.

During the 1950s, O’Keeffe artwork fell out of the limelight, and she spent 20 years in New Mexico painting with only three shows to sell her artwork.

Her popularity picked up again as she was elected to the American Academy of Arts and Letters, the nation’s highest honor society for people in the arts, in 1962.

By the time O’Keeffe lost her eyesight in 1971, her art work had been shown in the Art Institute of Chicago and the San Fransisco Museum of Art.

She spent the rest of her time at Ghost Ranch with a close friend making pottery and painting occasionally. After her death in 1986, O’Keeffe’s ashes were scattered to the wind from a nearby mountainside.
