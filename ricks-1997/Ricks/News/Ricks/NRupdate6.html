Ricks College News Release, May 26, 1998

Ricks Update - A weekly newsletter for friends of Ricks College

May 26, 1998

In addition to 2,874 April graduates, approximately 500 more students will receive their degrees from Ricks Wednesday, June 3, at 10 a.m. in the Hart Auditorium. The traditional summer commencement follows the first five-week summer term that ends June 2 and includes students who will be completing graduation requirements during one of the three summer terms. An academic march will begin at 9:50 a.m., followed by commencement at 10 a.m. A graduation breakfast for graduates and their parents and friends will be served at 8 a.m. in the Manwaring Center prior to graduation.

Our women's softball team just returned home from the national championship in Hutchinson, Kansas, where they finished second. They lost to Illinois Central in the championship games on Saturday. Women's fast-pitch softball is in its third year in existence at Ricks, yet in that time Ricks Coach Larry Stocking's teams have won three Region 18 titles and finished third and now second at the National Junior College Athletic Association tournaments.

The men's and women's track teams also recently returned from Edwardsville, Ill., with four individual national champions, 15 All-Americans, one runner-up trophy and one Coach of the Year award. Sophomore Jason Williams (decathlon), sophomore Geraldine George (javelin), freshman Alycia Boyer (10,000 meters) and freshman Sesha Snell (discus) were national champions and the women's team finished second behind Central Arizona. The men's team placed eighth in the national competition. Ferron Sonderegger was named Coach of the Year for his work with the men's team.

It's been quite a year for our athletic teams at Ricks. Here is a summary:

The women's basketball team won the region and placed fifth nationally at the finals. Sophomore Cady Williams was named a first-team All-American and a member of the all- tourney team. The men's basketball team also won the regional championship for the first time since 1984 and placed eighth nationally at the finals. Ty Church was named the tourney's outstanding small player and freshman Ricky Bower was named to the all-tourney.

The wrestling team placed seventh nationally with three wrestlers named All-Americans, headed by Dave Anderton who placed second. Ricks was second in the region.

The men's football team was ranked first nationally among junior college teams for most of October and early November until its single loss to Air Force JV. It won the fifth league title in the 13-year history of the Western States Football League and was ranked third nationally at the end of the season by the NCAA with an 11-1 record.

The women's volleyball team placed second in the region to a team that would win its fifth unprecedented national title. Ricks was the only team to defeat the College of Southern Idaho during the season and was the team that snapped CSI's 189 consecutive match wins after three years of competition. Mari Burningham was a first team All-American.

The women's cross-country team won the national championship for its third consecutive year. Of the seven women on the team, five are considered All-Americans because of their placement in the finals. The men's cross-country team placed sixth nationally. Coach Doug Stutz of both the men's and women's teams was named Coach of the Year at the national meet.

The baseball team finished No. 2 in the northern division of Scenic West Athletic Conference, finishing the year with a 27-27 record.
