Course Description & Objectives: Managerial Accounting is structured to build on the foundation of financial accounting concepts which were covered in Accounting 201. The central objective of the course is to introduce basic concepts and procedures of managerial accounting to the student through a combination of reading assignments, classroom discussion-lecture sessions, and outside problem assignments. The course emphasizes the use of accounting data in the decision-making process, and seeks to sharpen the student's analytical and interpretive skills through exposure to many business situations in which accounting data are needed and used by the manager. The course covers product costing, cost behavior, segmented reporting for management, profit planning, standard costing, alternate-choice decision making, capital budgeting, statement of cash flows, and statement analysis. Students will also work with a partner in doing a project that will require analyzing a corporation's financial statements. The course provides the skills necessary to work as a junior cost accountant in a manufacturing firm or as a junior accountant in the controller's office of a large organization. Individual entrepreneurs will also gain knowledge of costing and management concepts.

Course Routine: Prior to each class meeting, students are expected to study the chapter material assigned for the day and complete the given assignment. The instructor will normally devote a part of each class meeting to lecture and demonstration of selected items from the assigned materials, the objective being to clarify and elaborate on the selected items because of their special relevance or difficulty. Students are encouraged to ask questions and otherwise enter fully into class discussions.

B. Written work will normally be corrected by the students in class. Written assignments more than one meeting late will not be accepted. Each individual problem or exercise will be graded and given a rating. The grading system will be:
2 points: Satisfactory. The written assignment is complete and accurate and is clearly and properly presented in an acceptable form.
1 point: Acceptable work, but deficient in completeness, in accuracy, in clarity of presentation, or in form of presentation. You should do all parts of every problem if you expect to receive full credit for your homework. Assignments turned in one class meeting late will also receive 1 point. Homework problems are assigned to help students learn and experience the principles covered in the course. Accounting is something that usually has to be experienced to gain the most from it, although some memorization is essential, too. One cannot learn to play the piano by merely reading and memorizing from a book. Accounting works the same way. Exercises are generally easier than the problems. A variety of each is usually given for each chapter. All homework will be averaged together in calculating a semester homework score.

Exams: Four exams are given in the form of multiple choice questions and work problems. The questions are both subjective and objective in nature. The final is 1/2 comprehensive in order to test the student's retention of the full course material. The exams test student memory, understanding, and ability to analyze and apply concepts covered in the course. Students should read each question carefully and should attempt to answer all problems on the exam. With the exception of the final, all exams will be administered at the testing center. It is the student's responsibility to be aware of the testing center hours and to make sure that adequate time is allowed for completing the exams.

Project: Students will work with a partner in preparing a financial statement analysis on a publicly traded corporation. This project will help students better understand the financial statement relationships. Working with a partner will provide students a chance to improve their human interaction skills.

Quizzes: Quizzes help prepare the student for the exams. With the exception of Chapter 1, a quiz will be given for each chapter. Each quiz is worth 10 points and all quizzes will be averaged together in calculating a semester quiz score.

Instructor Expectations: Students are expected to come to class prepared each time. Chapters should be read, homework done, questions formulated, etc. for each appropriate class period.

Mortimer Adler said, "Why is marking up a book indispensable to reading? First, it keeps you awake. (And I don't mean merely conscious; I mean wide awake.) In the second place reading, if it is active, is thinking, and thinking tends to express itself in words, spoken or written. The marked book is usually the thought-through book. Finally, writing helps you remember the thoughts you had, or the thoughts the author expressed." He further stated, "You buy a steak..., but you do not own the steak in the most important sense until you consume it and get it into your bloodstream. I am arguing that books, too, must be absorbed in your bloodstream to do you any good."
