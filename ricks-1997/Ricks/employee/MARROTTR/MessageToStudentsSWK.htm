Spencer W. Kimball

"A Message to Students"

20 November 1977 in the Tabernacle in Salt Lake City

IN THE WORLD, BUT NOT OF THE WORLD

The constant pounding and pummeling of the world upon you wonderful young people would be difficult to cope with if you didn’t have the regular reinforcement of religious education, the same reason you mothers need to go to Relief Society, your fathers in priesthood meeting, and the need of all the family to participate in the Church programs so that each can be reinforced, reassured, and if necessary redirected. We must be fed and fortified regularly in order to live in the world but not be of the world.

As a keenly observant generation of Latter-day Saints, it must be clear to you by now that you live in times filled with perplexity and difficulty, but these are times which also are filled with great opportunity.

WE LIVE IN CHALLENGING TIMES

I am grateful that you and all of us have the gospel of Jesus Christ as a guide, so that you have a framework of understanding into which you can fit the events and circumstances which you will live to see. I feel a great sadness for those who will see all the tragedy and difficulty in the world but who have no understanding of the purpose of life. I fear for those who do not accept the warnings and follow the signposts that the Savior and His prophets have placed along the pathway of life. It is clear from the scriptures that we in this part of our dispensation cannot be promised by our political leaders that there will be "peace in our time," but we, as Church members, are given the means of having a personal peace and of coming to know serenity in our soul–even when there is no peace without!

JUDGING RIGHTEOUSLY

Thus, do not be surprised if, as you cling to the commandments and as you are guided by the gospel, you find yourselves putting more and more distance between yourself and the world. Don’t be surprised if others who are not believers act condescendingly toward you because of your righteousness and because of your determination to do what is right. Don’t be surprised either if others are puzzled at times by what seems to motivate you. Remember you can disapprove of the deeds of others without lessening your love for them as individuals. Remember that you can exercise righteous judgment concerning others and what they do without being judgmental of them. The Prophet Joseph Smith in the Inspired Translation of the Bible [Joseph Smith Translation–JST] helped to give us a more clear understanding of our relations with each other when he rendered the words of the Savior as follows: "Judge not unrighteously, that ye be not judged; but judge righteous judgment." (Mt 7:2, JST.)

This was inspired of the Lord and was underlined as the Prophet took those terms and added to them.

There are times when we must judge and evaluate, but we should do so righteously. This is different, however, from merely assuming that we must never judge.

THE NEED FOR REPETITION

By now you are perhaps accustomed to having those of us who are further along the pathway of life describe to you the importance of staying on the straight and narrow path. So often we say many of the same things to you again and again, but if you reflect upon why that is so, you will soon discover that the precipices that lie on each side of that straight and narrow path don’t change or become less dangerous; the steepness of that path does not change.

Church leaders are not able, each time we speak with you, to offer a new or more glamorous route that will lead back to the presence of our Heavenly Father. The route remains the same. Hence, encouragement must often be given concerning the same things and warnings must be repeated. Just because a truth is repeated does not make that truth any less important or true.

There is another reason for repetition that is more subtle, and it has to do with the readiness of individuals to receive the truth. Most of you have had the experience of reading in the scriptures a passage you have read before, when it seemed to hold no significance for you. Later on when you read it again, it seemed almost to come off the page. The particular truth was always there, but now you are ready to receive it! Fortunately for us, God through His Church, through prayer, and through wise parents continues to make available to us those precious truths we have yet to discover, to accept, and to apply in our lives. Therefore, we need repetition in order to match our readiness to receive. Be patient, therefore, with repetition because something that may bore you today, may lift your soul tomorrow. Something that may puzzle you today, may penetrate into the very depths of your soul next year.

A FAITHFUL GENERATION

I see in you a generation of young Latter-day Saints rising up who will be much more familiar with the scriptures than previous generations of Latter-day Saints were at the same age. You can be lifelong students of the scriptures. I see in you a rising generation of young Latter-day Saints who will be more willing to do missionary work (both before and after your formal full-time missions) than previous generations. Speaking collectively, your generation will see, even more clearly than you predecessors, how important it is to take the gospel to your fellowmen.

Your generation will be unashamed of the gospel of Jesus Christ and equally unashamed of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.

I see in you a generation of young Latter-day Saints whose hearts will be turned to your forefathers as has never before happened on such a scale. You will develop a natural interest in research and temple going, surpassing the interest levels of your parents and grandparents in this regard when they were your age.

I see in you a generation of young Latter-day Saints who will make effective use of your leadership experiences gained in the Church in the Young Men’s and Young Women’s programs, in Sunday School, in Relief Society, in Primary, and in especially the priesthood quorums–who will then be sought after by the thoughtful people of the world who will want young men and women of integrity and competency to serve in various ways. Such young Latter-day Saints will carry their beliefs with them as well as carrying with them their skills, their competency, and their integrity.

I see in you young Latter-day Saints testimonies much more advanced for your age than preceding generations.

Much of this happy trend in your generation will be due to our effective seminary and institute programs, for we are producing a generation of young gospel scholars such as we have never had before. Hopefully the gospel will also have been drawn into your souls in sacrament meetings, around the dinner table, in Family Home Evenings, and in personal study.

BUILD A LATTER-DAY SAINT HOME

Now a word of encouragement to you young Latter-day Saints who come from homes where the gospel is not taught and may not be lived. There will be special blessings for you as you attend seminary, as well as in all your efforts in Church activities and experiences. Drawing upon the qualities of your parents, you can then one day build a Latter-day Saint home of your own as pioneer parents. Do not be discouraged–respect and love your parents.

AVOID SELFISHNESS

To do the special thins given to this generation, you will need to guard against selfishness. One of the tendencies most individuals have which simply must be overcome is the tendency to be selfish. All that you can do now while you are young and are more pliant to become less selfish and more selfless will be an important and lasting contribution to the quality of your life in the years, indeed in the eternity, to come. You will be a much better wife or a much better husband, a better mother or a better father, if you can curb the tendency to be selfish. Your children whom you will not know for a few years yet have a stake in your conquest of selfishness.

As in all things, we have the example of the Savior on the cross at Calvary. He did something that He was not forced to do–something which would benefit others with the gift of immortality which Jesus already had. He was the supreme act of selflessness.

You may recall reading in 3 Nephi about the visit of the resurrected Jesus to this continent and how after blessing the children He wept twice and He also said, "And now behold, my joy if sull." (3 Ne 17:20.)

True joy can only come from giving ourselves to correct causes such as the building up of the Kingdom, causes which are in a sense larger than we are. Pleasure tends to be self-centered. True joy always includes others. Empathy extends outward to others whereas pity tends to be turned inward upon ourselves. It is simply not possible to feel compassion for others if our own problems are made to seem so large that they get in the way. When we are selfless, we are freed to see others with more genuine concern and thus can generate compassion for them. But if we are constantly concerned only with ourselves, there is no room left for caring for others.

INVITE YOUR PARENTS TO GIVE YOU COUNSEL

Another matter of counsel for you: Have you ever considered asking your parents to take a few minutes, from time to time, to talk to you about some of the most important lessons they have ever learned in their lives? Your request might startle them at first and then they may even become anxious about responding to your request. What a wonderful thing for sons and daughters to invite at times teaching from their parents! I think you will be surprised at what serious thought and reflection your parents will engage in if they know you really would like to have them share their wisdom and insights. Of course, your parents are teaching all the time–hopefully in Family Home Evening, over the dinner table, and so on; but there could be some such special teaching moments, too.

MAKING DECISIONS–JUST ONCE!

May I mention two more recommendations? Develop discipline of self so that, more and more, you do not have to decide and redecide what you will do when you are confronted with the same temptation time and time again. You only need to decide some things JUST ONCE!

How great a blessing it is to be free of agonizing over and over again regarding a temptation. To do such is time consuming and very risky.

Likewise, my dear young friends, the positive things you will want to accomplish need only be decided upon once–like going on a mission and living worthily in order to get married in the temple–and then all other decisions related to these goals can fall into line. Otherwise, each reconsideration is risky, and each equivocation may result in error. There are some things Latter-day Saints do, and other things we just don’t do. The sooner you take such stands, the taller you will be!

A DECISION TO READ THE SCRIPTURES

I have made mention of this before but would like to share the experience with you. Once I heard a forceful appeal by a woman from the Mutual at Salt Lake City. Perhaps it was the approach she made or perhaps it may have been the mood I was in. She gave a rousing talk on the reading of the scriptures and making them our own; then she stopped her dissertation to ask this mixed congregation, about 1,000 of us, "How many of you have read the Bible through?"

I think I was about 14 years old at the time. An accusing guilt complex spread over me. I had ready many books by that time, the funny papers, and light books, but my accusing heart said to me, "You, Spencer Kimball, you have never read that holy book. Why?" I looked behind me and at the people in front and on both sides of the hall to see if I was alone in my failure to read the sacred book.

Of the 1,000 people, there were perhaps a half dozen who proudly raised their hands. I slumped down in my seat. I had no thought for the 1,000 people who had also failed, but only a deep accusing thought for myself. In my slumped posture, I condemned no man, only my little insignificant self. I don’t know what other people were doing and thinking. I heard no more of the sermon. It had accomplished its work. The meeting closed. I sought the large double exit door and rushed to my home only a block east of the chapel, and I was gritting my teeth and saying to myself, "I will. I will. I will."

Entering the back door of our family home, I went to the kitchen shelf where we kept the coal oil lamps, selected one that was full of oil with a newly trimmed wick and climbed the stairs to my attic room and I opened my Bible and began on Genesis, first chapter, and the first verse, and I sat well into the night with Cain and Abel, and Adam and Eve and Enoch and Noah and through he flood even to Abraham.

Many times my older brothers twitted me about it. "You will never get it read," they said. "Why don’t you read something more easy and entertaining?" But I had made up my mind not to be dissuaded. In January I read the first chapter of Genesis. In December that year I read the last chapter of Revelation. I have always been delighted.

A DECISION TO KEEP THE WORD OF WISDOM

Again from my infancy I had heard the Word of Wisdom stories about tea and coffee and tobacco, etc. Nearly every Sunday School day and Primary day we sang lustily, I with the other boys,

"That the children may live long,
And be beautiful and strong,
Tea and coffee and tobacco they despise,
Drink no liquor, and they eat
But a very little meat;
They are seeking to be great and good and wise."
("In Our Lovely Deseret")

We sang it time and time again until it became an established part of my vocabulary and my song themes, but more especially my life’s plan. Frequently fellows I knew sneaked out some tobacco or rolled their own on times like a 4th of July celebration. Some might every have had a sip from some man’s liquor bottle.

These facts were drilled into me. Occasionally some respected speaker said he had never tasted the forbidden things we sang against and then I made up my mind. Never would I use these forbidden things the prophets preached against. That decision was and unalterable. I would not and did not partake.

In 1937 my wife and I were touring Europe. In France I sat at a banquet table of the Rotary International Convention in a fashionable hotel. The large spacious banquet room held hundreds of people. The many waiters moved about the tables, and at every place besides plenteous silver, linen, and fancy serving dishes were seven wine glasses; at every plate seven different kinds of champagne, wine and every kind of liquor.

No one was watching me. Shall I drink it or at least sip it, the temptation nudged me. No one who cares will know. Here was quite a temptation. Shall I or shall I not?

Then the thought came: But I made a firm resolution when a boy that I would never touch the forbidden things. I had already lived a third of a century firm and resolute. I would not break my record now.

"WICKEDNESS NEVER WAS HAPPINESS"

Remember, O youth of a noble birthright, that "wickedness never was happiness." The unrighteous may pretend to be happy and may seek to entice others into such a way of life because misery loves company, as you know, but you will never see a happy sinner. Even the discontent of good people is traceable to such shortcomings as they have.

As a casual observer may feel that an unrighteous person "has it made," and for a fleeting moment it may even seem so. But gross sin produces a deep emptiness. Thus the wicked seem to do more of the same in order to reassure themselves and to try to fill the void. When you see a life filled with desperation, there is transgression in it. We may pity such people, but it is wrong and naïve to envy them.

STRENGTH FROM THE PAST

To know the patriarchs and prophets of the ages past and their faithfulness under stress and temptation and persecution strengthens the resolves of youth. All through the scriptures almost every weakness and every strength of man has been portrayed, and rewards and punishments have been recorded. One would surely be blind who could not learn to live life properly by such reading what the Lord has said, "Search the scriptures; for in them ye think ye have eternal life: and they are they which testify of men." (Jn 5:39.)

He was the same Lord and Master in whose life we find every quality of goodness, every quality we should develop in our own lives.

Can you find in all the holy scriptures where the Lord Jesus Christ ever failed his church? Can you find any scripture that says he was untrue to his people, to his neighbors, friends, or associates? Was he faithful? Was he true? Is there anything good and worthy that he did not give? Then that is what we ask–what he asks of a husband, every husband–of a wife, every wife–the girl, every girl–the boy, every boy.

EVERY GIRL HAS SPECIAL RESPONSIBILITIES

Every girl, and I say every girl, should prepare herself throughout her life for marriage and for domestic responsibilities. You are not hearing or seeing these things on the radio and television today. You are not reading that in the magazines and newspapers today, but it’s true nevertheless. She should be encouraged to be proud to prepare for true womanly service. She should become educated and skilled in things that are useful and enriching to her family. She should develop her talents, strengthen her knowledge and testimony of the gospel, and be eager to serve others. Some girls may be called on full-time missions, and all will have the opportunity to be highly useful in the kingdom of God if they prepare themselves. It has been said that "when you educate a man, you educate an individual; but when you educate a woman, you educate a whole family." (Dr. Charles D. McIver.) We want our women to be well educated, for children may not recover from the ignorance of their mothers.

After marriage young wives should be happily occupied in bearing and rearing children. Again, you sometimes read and hear things quite different from that. But it is still the truth, and it will still be true when the last trumpet is sounded. I know of no scriptures nor authorities which authorize young wives to forego families purposely. Young married couples can make their way and reach their educational heights if they are truly determined.

AVOID WEDGES OF SIN

This generation of young people should strive to avoid the hidden wedges of sin that so often are inserted into lives, and though hidden, with time divide them from God and each other and deplete their moral strength. Above everything else, the Lord needs a pure and holy generation through which to accomplish his purposes. He needs servants who are clean and worthy of the endowment which they can receive in the Lord’s holy temples. "Be ye clean that bear the vessels of the Lord." (D&C 38:42.)

THE MIRACLE OF FORGIVENESS

Where mistakes occur, our youth need to understand the route to repentance. They need to have a personal testimony of this miracle that brings forgiveness. Each one needs to understand that repentance can be properly applied in his life as well as in the lives of others. The mission of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints is to save souls and call people everywhere to repentance so that they might know the joys of gospel living. If this generation of youth is to fulfill the great missionary opportunity that will be given them, then they must learn and live the principles of repentance. If you have a problem, or have made a mistake, go at once to your parents and to your bishop and get their counsel and make your peace with the Lord. "Behold, he who has repented of his sins, the same is forgiven, and I, the Lord, remember them no more. By this ye may know if a man repenteth of his sins–behold, he will confess them and forsake them." (D&C 58:42-43.)

A SPECIAL GENERATION

Yours is a special generation with a special message to deliver to the world at this special time.

The coming generation must also be nurtured in their faith in Jesus Christ to the point that it will empower them to rise above the selfishness of society. Selfishness strikes a deadly blow at the root of true character. Failure in the eternal sense of the word is almost always associated with selfishness.

Now is the time to set your life’s goals. Now is the time to firmly set your standards and then hold to them throughout your life.

Ernest Renon gave us this, "Everything favors those who have a special destiny; they become glorious by a sort of invincible impulse and command of fate. (The Life of Jesus.)

The life of Jesus Christ is before us, always in our scriptures before us. That you are learning as you go to the seminary and the institute.

DIAMONDS–IN A GREAT SETTING

The Lord bless you, my young friends, that you may always follow the teachings that you are receiving; and may you, as diamonds in this great setting, always have an influence for good upon your fellows, the members of your families and others. May you always be certain and sure that Jesus lives and Jesus is the Christ, and don’t be ashamed or hesitant about proclaiming it. May God bless you. You have our great love and our faith and our prayers for you. In the name of Jesus Christ. Amen.
