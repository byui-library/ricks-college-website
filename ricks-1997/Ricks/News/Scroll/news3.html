Crime shoots up when organized
By Jay Mathew Alley
scroll staff


 

By Jan Pettersson

 

The days of mobsters, prohibition and tommy guns are gone, but organized crime is still alive with the Italian Mafia, drug cartels and other groups.
They are thriving off the black market with hands in prostitution, illegal alien smuggling, human organ trade, money laundering, extortion and traditional drug smuggling, according to the Organized Crime Registry. Law Street Journal said alliances are even being formed between Russian gangs and South American groups.
"The international exporters of crime and terrorism, who capitalize on the vulnerable free society include South American drug cartels, terrorists from the Middle East and an array of organized crime groups from Europe, the former Soviet Republics and Asia," Louis J. Freech, FBI director, said in a 1997 statement. "Regardless of origin, these and other international crimes daily impact directly on our citizens and our economy."
Organized crime is defined by the International Police (INTERPOL) as "any enterprise or group of persons engaged in a continuing illegal activity which has as its primary purpose, the generation of profits irrespective of national boundaries."
Just one example of organized crime activity's that affects the United States and even Rexburg is that of drug trade.
Four organizations dominate the international drug trade in northern Mexico. The Tijuana, Sonora, Juarez and Gulf cartels, along with smaller groups, have been dubbed the Mexican Federation by the United States Drug Enforcement Administration. The federation rakes in $10 billion to $30 billion a year in narcotics sales according to The Washington Post.
Alcohol thrived in the '20's, and it has not gone away, but new drugs have been added to it.
Beer is the form of alcohol that is the most abused drug in Rexburg, Captain Randy Lewis of the Rexburg Police Department said.
He also said marijuana and cocaine are used and have been traced to Mexico at times while methamphetamine is manufactured here in Idaho.
To be organized crime, a group doesn't have to be the Mafia. Street gangs like the Bloods and Crips, especially prevalent in Los Angeles, are considered organized crime. The 18th Street Gang in Los Angeles is estimated to have 8,000 to 20,000 members according to streetgangs.com.
Although Rexburg does not have a rampant gang problem, Blackfoot and St. Anthony do have trouble, and there is some gang activity in Rexburg as well as "Wannabe's." Captain Lewis said, "to my knowledge we have no 'organized' gangs."
When a known gang member comes to Rexburg "we pay extra, extra, extra special attention to them. We'll stop them and shake them down - let them know we know they're here," Captain Lewis said.
Weapons have been seized in some instances, including two .380 semi-automatics when a driver was pulled over for speeding.
