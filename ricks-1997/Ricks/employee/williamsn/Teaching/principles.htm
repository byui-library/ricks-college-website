Principles of Teaching
by Wayne Brickey, Robert Mouritzer, Jerry Lund

1. The ways of God in teaching, as in all other things, are not the ways of man.
2. The student must accept or reject the message by his own agency on the basis of inward commitment and feelings that may be independent of intellectual, social or emotional considerations.
3. Even the least Saint may know all things as fast as he is aable to bear them.
4. Divine inspiriation is given without respect to any standards, evaluations or prerequisites devised by man.
5. Divine inspiration is given to all those who will meet the Lord's requirements.
6. The important place of one who teaches the things of God requires that he be appointed by authority from God.
7. The single most important factor governing preparedness to teach is a worthy, circumspect life.
8. It is an absolute imperative for the teacher to get and retain the Holy Ghost.   The teacher will receive the Holy Ghost through ordinances and worthiness.
9. The Holy Spirit will grant inspiration in all matters which may contribute to faith and righteousness.
10. Revelaion is not given to the faculties of the physical body but rather to the spirit entirely as if there were no physical body at all.
11. Effects and impressions of the Holy Spirit are not generally perceived by the natural faculties of man.
12. The power of the Holy Spirit may operate through one who is not immediately conscious of its operation.
13. The Holy Spirit knows all things but is not obliged to impart all things.
14. The Holy Ghost will operate only in the soul of a man who has sufficient freedom to reject his message.
15. The Holy Spirit always carries a witness of the truth it is conveying.
16. The Holy Spirit does not confuse or discourage.
17. The Holy Ghost has power to convince far beyond any means devised by man.
18. The Spirit testifies when he convinces or proves; the teacher testifies when he proclaims that which the Holy Ghost has witnessed to him.
19. Any words, actions or methods that offend the Spirit are inappropriate.
20. Sacred truths are conveyed independent of unnatural and dramatic decorations or other devices designed to attract  attention.
21. The teacher grieves the Spirit when he attempts to convey sacred truths by unnatural or dramatic decorations.
22. External influences whether intellectual, emotional or social, which do not intrude upon the agency of the mind, may be administered under the direction of the Spirit.
23. External pressures, whether intellectual, emotional or social, which do intrude upon the agency of the mind will offend the Spirit.
24. The teacher who conscientiously seeks to create contention, offend his listeners, or inspires anger or persecution towards himself, grieves the Spirit.
25. A teacher who gives way to lust shall not have the Spirit.
26. Any method or manner of delivery contrived to inspire faith in the teacher rather than faith inthe Lord offends the Spirit.
27. The teacher who conscientiously attempts to please, or to inspire love and admiration towards himself offends the Spirit.
28. The teacher must never presume as his own any power or brilliance produced in him through the gifts and operations of the Holy Spirit.
29. There is a broad and fundamental distinction between preparing a lesson and preparing to deliver what the Spirit directs.
30. Proper preparation can be accomplished only under the influence of the Holy Spirit.
31. Proper preparation may be had only in deep pondering, appropriate reading, devout, humble prayer, and in a recognition of need and dependence upon God.
32. The guideline of the Spirit will come more completely to those whose abiding motivation is to labor for the glory of God.
33. The teacher must have a continuous, substantive program for treasuring up in his mind the words of life.
34. Without the prayer of faith, there is no promise of inspiration.
35. To study, teach, or prepare as if the promised inspiration may not come is a patent demonstration of unbelief.
36. The teacher must learn the operatios of the Holy ghost in order to understand his suggestions.
37. The Holy Spirit is one of only a few resources God has approved for those who teach.
38. Preaching, as defined in the prohpet sources, goes far beyond its usage in our present society.  properly defined,  preaching means to proclaim, declare, announce and exhort and embraces a variety of methods.
39. The Lord expects his servants to use boldness and courage, but not overbearance in declaring his gospel.
40. Every man appointed to teach sacred things has the right to seek and to receive the gift of preaching.
41. The teacher may properly seek the gifts of the Spirit in his sacred stewardship.
42. The teacher should be prepared to respond to the dictates of the Holy spirit whenever and wherever they may be delivered.
43. All are under a strict command that they shall not impart beyond that which God grants unto men according to the degree of heed and diligence which they give to Him.
44. The teacher may be restrained from teaching all that has been made known to him.
45. A teacher succeeds when the message is carried into the hearts of his hearers by the power of the Holy Spirit.
46. A teacher fails when the Holy Ghost refuses to attend him in the delivery of his message.
47. The teacher does not fail if his hearers reject the message he delivers to them by the power of the Holy Ghost.
48. Some who hear the word of God as it is preached by the Spirit may respond with anger, belligerence, rebellion or rejection of the proclaimed message.
49. It is the teacher's role and obligation to prepare himself so that he teaches clearly and without misunderstanding whatever the Spirit directs him to teach.
50. It is the student's role and obligation to prepare himself so that he may understand the message, know whether or not it is of God, and respond to it properly.
51. Tangible or outwardly perceived attitudes, expressions or responses may not necessarily reflect success or failure in teaching.
52. The teacher may appropriately seek to know if his message has been proclaimed in a manner that it cannot be misunderstood.
53. The teacher must never probe into or require the disclosure of inner feelings and attitudes beyond that which the student may voluntarily with to share. (Honor the agency of others)
54. The proper measure of methodology is how it enhances the teaching of truth.
55. Any appropriate love the student may have for the teacher will result from the student having been brought closer to the Lord.
56. In order that the teacher may be free to respond to the impressions of the Spirit, the curriculum design should not encumber. (Iron rod v. Liahona)
57. The foremost prerogatives for lessons planning rest with the teacher.
58. Granting the teacher his rightful prerogatives for lesson planning reserves for him hi God-given agency to fail.
