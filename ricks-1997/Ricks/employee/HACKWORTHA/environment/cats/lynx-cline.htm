The Canadian Lynx
in the High Mountains
By Doug Cline
 
In the high mountains you can encounter some of the most beautiful country in the world as well as see fascinating and majestic animals.  However, as you spend time in the mountains, you probably know how hard it is to see some of them. Often they are elusive and nocturnal.

To be able to see some animals, one must spend a lot of time in the wild and know what to look for.  Although I have seen elk, deer, moose, and bear, the most exciting for me was seeing a high mountain cat.

There are basically three types of high mountain cats: the mountain lion (cougar), the bobcat, and the lynx.  The cat I am most interested in is the lynx. This elusive creature hides from people including those who try to study the it.

When I go into the wild country, I am always alert to see animals not only for their beauty but also for their well being, that is, I like to see if they are doing well.   Controversy shaddows the lynx just as it does the cougars and bobcats.  Some people don't want these predators too close to their  homes and ranches.

The Canadian lynx is the species native to my area. The lynx have many features which make them stand out from the other mountain cats.   First is their facial features.  Most have probably seen a lynx in a zoo or picture book.  Remember the look?  On the face are tufts of hair that hang  along the cheeks and extend under the lower jaw.

This gives the appearance of a beard. It also gives the lynx a rugged look, like a mountain men of the old west.   Also the lunx has tufts of hair that stick up of the tops of their ears. The combination of these two facial features gives the lynx a distinguished look.

The Canadian lynx stands about twenty-four to twenty-six inches tall and is about thirty-four to forty-six inches long. It weighs eighteen to thirty pounds and is smaller than a mountain lion or bobcat.   Actually, the lynx is about the same size as a labrador.

But interestingly enough, this animal lives in more harsh conditions than the other two cats. The lynx searches out the most rugged and remote country to live in. They can only be found in the tip-top of the high mountains. They are mostly found in Alaska and Canada, but have also been reported in the northern United States.

Although Northern Idaho is about as far south as the Canadian lynx has been seen naturally, the Canadian lynx was reintroduced in Colorado a few years ago.

The grayish-brown color of the Canadian lynx  allows them to blend with the surroundings. This type of camouflage is very important because it allows the lynx to hunt for its food without being seen. Since the Lynx lives in the high mountains of Canada, Alaska, and Northern United States, one must realize that for a large part of the year there is abundant snow.  Their coloration allows them to stay camouflaged easily even in the winter.

Now the lynx has some very interesting characteristic that allows them to walk on top of the snow very easily. The Lynx has a paw that is very unique. Their paws are very large in comparison to their bodies. If one were to come upon the tracks of a lynx the thought that would probably cross their mind was that this animal is very large. But the looks are deceiving, the reason that that Lynx has such a large paw is so that they can walk and run on the snow easily. This combination of its large paw and its relatively low weight allows this cat to live and hunt in an area that there is very low competition because the mountain lion and bobcat can’t travel as high into the mountains because of their weights and their body structure. These other cats tend to stay at the lower elevations.

The Lynx is a carnivore, so it must hunt to survive. Its primary food source is the snowshoe rabbit. It also eats small rodents interestingly enough it will eat insects like grasshoppers. The word lynx comes from the Greek word ‘to shine’. This may refer to the reflective cells that assist cats to see at night by re-using light that has already passed through the eye chamber by reflecting it back a second time. They are mainly terrestrial and nocturnal, although they may also hunt during the day if prey is scarce. Lynx are thought to hunt mainly by sight and hearing, relying on smell to a lesser extent. They usually stalk their prey to within a few bounds before pouncing, but they are also known to wait in ambush for hours. Because of its size the Lynx tends to stay away from the larger animals such as deer and, elk. Lynx’ very rarely will attack a human being. They will usually go out of their way just to avoid contact with anyone. If a Lynx attacks anyone it will be because they felt threatened and will just be defending itself.

So does the Lynx pose a threat to man? Many people say yes, many people say no. There has been debate whether or not the lynx should be put on the endangered species list. Trappers who were lured to various places by the Lynx’ brownish-gray coat cut the lynx population drastically, and in some areas such as Colorado the Lynx hasn’t been seen since 1973. But there is good news. Just last year two Canadian Lynx were released into the Colorado back country by Fish and Game officials. Its an interesting story: The two females released by wildlife officials were trapped in British Columbia and trucked 1,700 miles to southern Colorado. Officials chose a broad, open space on private land at 9,200 feet near the Rio Grande National Forest.

Wildlife officer Bill Andree, flanked by about two-dozen journalists and biologists, set a metal cage containing the wildcat with tufted ears on a 6-inch layer of snow. Then he unlocked the door.

"You're free," Andree whispered to the 3-year-old cat nicknamed No. 7. He stood back and waited in the bright, mid-morning sunshine. No. 7, which arrived in Colorado last week, remained crouched on a grass bed in the cage for a little more than five minutes.

Andree and biologist Gene Byrne tilted the cage forward gently and she stepped out on the snow, looked about for another minute or so and raced into a canopy of trees.

When Andree and Byrne opened the cage for the second lynx, nicknamed No. 9, she darted quickly toward the trees and the 13,000-foot snowy peaks towering in the background as the crowd applauded.

The reason the reintroduction of the Lynx was opposed wasn’t because people feared for their own safety, which is an issue with the reintroduction of wolves and grizzly bears, but it was because they were worried that their might be new land-use restrictions, which could restrict logging and deny access to public land by hunters and skiers.

Many people in Washington, where there has been talk of placing the animal on the threatened species list, argue that if the Lynx really isn’t part of the habitat anymore. One of the arguments was; ``We're talking about listing an animal that has not been seen (in Oregon) in over 30 years,'' said Sen. Gordon Smith, R-Ore. ``If we're going to do
that, we might as well list Sasquatch,''

Through all this controversy I feel that there should be measures taken to help protect this extraordinary animal. The mountains are a place where we can go to relax and have an adventure. Part of that adventure is the wildlife. To be out where nature still rules and there is freedom for the animals to roam is very enjoyable. The Lynx truly helps make the outdoors more enjoyable.
