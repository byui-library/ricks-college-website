Keyboard


Three full-time instructors and nine adjunct instructors bring a wealth of background in professional and concert performance to the piano faculty at Brigham Young University-Idaho.  Their top students have an enviable record of success in local, regional, and national competitions .  

The Piano Program


For the piano majors, the Associate in Music degree and the Associate in Arts and Letters with a Major in Music lay a strong foundation for degrees in piano performances and related areas.  The Associate in Piano Pedagogy degree prepares the student to teach piano in the studio or home.  The pedagogy program develops a knowledge of piano teaching skills and literature and features "hands-on" teaching experiences, group and private teaching techniques, and the teaching of music theory through piano lessons.  Many who wish to supplement their family income and still stay in the home find this program attractive.  Successful graduates of this program have established their own businesses and become respected musical leaders in their communities.

Performance Opportunities


Brigham Young University-Idaho is rich in opportunities for pianists.  The Student Afternoon Recital (STAR) program and the Piano Master Class provide music students many opportunities to perform.  Accompanying skills are developed in private music lessons, the Piano Ensemble class, and through the many performing opportunities that are associated with a very active music program.  Students may also participate in the annual concerto festival/competition where they may win the privilege of performing a major work with the Symphony Orchestra.

The modern Eliza R. Snow Center for the Performing Arts has 43 practice studios  with well maintained grand pianos for piano majors or uprights for non-piano majors.  Both major performance areas, the Ruth Barrus Concert Hall and the Recital Hall, are noted for their magnificent acoustics.

Popular Styles


If popular music is your thing, you will find an active program of jazz ensembles and combos under the direction of Wilson Brown, former music director and keyboard artist for "The Lettermen."  Classes in jazz improvisation add another interesting dimension to this program.

Organ Studies


The magnificent 80-rank Ruffatti pipe organ in the Barrus Concert Hall is one of the reasons Brigham Young University-Idaho has become a significant center for organ studies.  The new John Taylor Religion building houses a three manual Rodgers pipe and electronic combination organ.  A 26-rank Wicks pipe organ which was rebuilt in 1998 and enlarged by the Rodgers Organ Company, five electronic practice organs, and a regular recital program add depth to the organ program.  A Bigelow tracker action pipe organ and a choice of two harpsichords turn the Recital Hall into an authentic lab for Baroque keyboard music.

Director of Organ Studies at Brigham Young University-Idaho is Dr. Darwin Wolford, whose enthusiasm for the organ extends to an active recital program across the country and over 350 compositions for organ, piano, and voice.
