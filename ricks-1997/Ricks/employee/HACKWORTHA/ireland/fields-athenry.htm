 Athenry Castle

This Norman castle was erected by Meiler de Bermingham, who was granted much of the lands of Connaught in 1235. After an attack on the castle in 1316, town walls were erected. These remain partially intact. The castle consists of a three story tower surrounded by an outer wall. Athenry Castle is one of the most notable medieval walled towns surviving in Ireland. Recently re-roofed the interior contains an audio visual room and exhibition. The average length of a visit is one hour.

The Fields of Athenry Thatched Heritage Cottage

A recreated traditional Irish cottage, bringing to life a time when people gathered together in such homes. Friends and neighbors came to listen to a seanachai (story-teller) or to the music from a travelling fiddler or piper. The flagstones were ideal for tap dancing. The cottage has a kitchen where artifacts from the early years of this century are displayed and farmyard machinery from the early 1900s can be seen in the yard. Coffee, tea and scones are served daily. Bar facilities, toilets, telephone and bureau de change are all available. Traditional music and dance on Wednesday and Saturday nights until 23.45.

The Athenry Medieval Festival

This is a unique medieval festival which is centered around Lady Day. Entirely family orientated with one day devoted to children. A spectacular pageant on the final night culminates in fireworks. Indulge in street theatre, sporting events and the best in popular music for free. Enjoy open air entertainment every night in one of Irelands finest heritage towns. Fringe events include concerts in Athenry's recently restored castle.

Athenry Town

Renowned internationally through the song, the medieval town of Athenry represents the ideal opportunity to sample the atmospheric hustle and bustle of 13th century life. Its strong links with the medieval past are immediately evident through the towering presence of the town castle, built by founder Meyler de Bermingham in 1240. The town's history is chronicled in the Athenry Heritage Center, located in the ruins of a medieval Collegiate church in the Market Square.
