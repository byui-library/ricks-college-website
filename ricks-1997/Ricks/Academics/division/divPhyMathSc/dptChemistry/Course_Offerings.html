Ricks College Chemistry Department

Course Offerings

The Department of Chemistry offers all of the chemistry courses needed by students in their first two years of college. Chemistry majors take the traditional two-year sequence of Principles of Chemistry and Organic Chemistry. Students with other majors that require chemistry (such as engineering, biology, agriculture, and others), will find all of the supporting chemistry courses needed for their majors. In addition, several of the chemistry courses satisfy the Physical Science component of the Natural Science General Education requirement.

 

Transferring students find that chemistry courses taken at Ricks College are accepted well by other institutions. Students who have done well in their chemistry courses at Ricks find themselves well prepared for further coursework in chemistry and in other areas at their transfer institutions. An experienced faculty, small classrooms and a commitment to students provide a superior learning environment in chemistry. The Department offers two degree programs in Chemistry.

 

Chemistry majors find that many doors are open to them. At the Associate degree level, lab technician jobs are available. A Bachelor's degree in Chemistry can lead to a productive career in industry or government laboratories, and can also lead to graduate study in chemistry or in other sciences. In addition, a Bachelor's degree in Chemistry is an excellent preparation for medical school, law school, and other professional schools.

Course Descriptions

Chem 100* Chemistry in the Modern World    3

Three lectures per week

A basic nonmathematical, nonlaboratory course with emphasis on the applications of chemistry in areas of food, health, con-sumer products, natural and synthetic resources, energy, and the environment. Designed for those majors requiring only a one-semester introduction to the modern applications of chemistry and those desiring a general education course in the physical sciences. This course is not designed to prepare students for other chemistry courses.

Chem 100L* Chemistry in the Modern World Lab    1

Two lab hours per week

Prerequisite: Previous or concurrent registration in Chem 100

This course is a common laboratory course for Chemistry 100 that is designed for students who wish to satisfy graduation requirements by completing a laboratory course in the physical sciences. The course places emphasis on the everyday applications of chemistry. Experiments are performed in such areas as foods and food additives, health, consumer products, natural and synthetic resources, energy, and the environment.

Chem 101* Introductory General Chemistry    3

Three lectures per week

Prerequisite: Concurrent registration in Math 101, or completion of the math requirement

An introductory course covering basic concepts in general chemistry. The course is designed for students in home economics, nursing, agriculture, biology, and other areas that require a broad introduction to general and inorganic chemistry. It serves as preparation for Chem 152. Along with Chem 103, fills the GE requirement in Physical Science for students not planning to attend BYU.

* Gives GE credit in the Physical Science area of the

Natural Science requirement.

Chem 103* Introductory Chemistry Laboratory    l

Three lab hours per week

Prerequisite: Completion of, or concurrent registration in Chem 101

This lab consists of classical chemistry experiments in general chemistry.

Chem 105* General Chemistry Chem 105H* General Chemistry      4

Four lectures per week, one hour lab lecture, and one 2-hour lab period per week

Prerequisite: Concurrent registration in or completion of Math 110 or equivalent. High school chemistry or Chem 101 recommended

A course designed to meet the general chemistry requirements in technical fields such as engineering, life sciences. agriculture, pre-professional, etc.

Chem 106* General Chemistry Chem 106H*     4

Four lectures per week, one hour lab lecture, and one 2-hour lab period per week

Prerequisite: Chem 105 and completion of Math 110

A course designed to meet the general chemistry requirements in technical fields such as engineering, life sciences. agriculture, pre-professional, etc.

Chem 111* Principles of Chemistry     5

Five lectures and four hours of lab per week

Prerequisite: High school chemistry, completion of, or concurrent enrollment in Math 162, or permission of the instructor

A course similar in content to Chem 105 but provides a stronger background for those who will continue the study of chemistry beyond the freshman year. The course is intended for students who plan to major in chemistry or whose majors will suggest a minor in chemistry (chemical engineering, pharmacy, biology, and pre-professional programs). Students who have taken a second year of chemistry in high school should register for this course instead of Chem 105. The topics covered are similar to those in Chem 105 but with slightly more emphasis placed on theoretical and mathematical aspects. Topics include stoichiometry, types of reactions, periodic table, atomic structure, bonding theories, states of matter, and properties of solutions. The laboratory places emphasis on quantitative and instrumental analysis. Completion of Chem 111 and 112 will transfer to BYU as Chem 111, 112, and 213 and will satisfy any major that requires Chem 105 and 106.

Chem 112* Principles of Chemistry     5

Five lecture and four hours of lab per week

Prerequisite: Chem 111, completion of Math 162, or permission of the instructor

A continuation of Chemistry 111 for students who desire a stronger background than provided by Chem 106. The completion of Chem 112 will help students transferring to other colleges and universities to more easily complete the requirements for a major or a minor in chemistry. Topics include thermodynamics, kinetics, equilibrium, acid/base theory, nuclear chemistry, and coordination chemistry. The laboratory emphasizes both qualitative inorganic chemistry and quantitative analysis techniques. Completion of Chem 111 and 112 will meet the department requirements for Chem 111, 112, and 213 at BYU and will satisfy any major that requires Chem 105 and 106.

Chem 150 Introductory Organic and Biochemistry     5

Five lectures per week

Prerequisite: Chem 101 or equivalent

A second semester course in a sequence with Chem 101. This course is designed for students in home economics, nursing, dental hygiene and other areas that require an introduction to organic and biochemistry. Students who need an organic/biochemistry course with a lab should take Chem 153 also.

Chem 153 Introductory Organic/Biochemistry Laboratory     1

Three lab hours per week

Prerequisite: Concurrent registration in Chem 150

This course is an optional laboratory for Chem 150. It consists of experiments on organic techniques, properties, and syntheses and biochemical principles.

Chem 251 Organic Chemistry    4

Four lectures, one lab lecture, and one 3-hour lab per week

Prerequisite: Completion of or concurrent enrollment in Chem 106

A study of the principles and theories of organic chemistry including the properties, preparation, and reactions of organic compounds. The course is designed for students in Chemistry, Chemical Engineering, Pre-medicine, Pre-dentistry, Pre-pharmacy, Pre-veterinary and Biology.

Four lectures, one lab lecture, and one 3-hour lab per week

Prerequisite: Completion of or concurrent enrollment in Chem 106

A study of the principles and theories of organic chemistry including the properties, preparation, and reactions of organic compounds. The course is designed for students in Chemistry, Chemical Engineering, Pre-medicine, Pre-dentistry, Pre-pharmacy, Pre-veterinary and Biology.
