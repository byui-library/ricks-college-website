Are there any risks in exercise?

Muscles and joints.

The most common risk in exercise is injury to the muscles and joints. This usually happens from exercising to hard or for to long- particularly if a person has been inactive for some time. (See effective ways to avoid injury.)



Heat exhaustion and heat stroke.

If precautions are not taken during warm, humid days, heat exhaustion or heat stroke can occur- although relatively rarely. The symptoms of heat exhaustion include a body temperature below normal, dizziness, headache, nausea, and sometimes confusion. The other and more serious occurrence is heat stroke. The symptoms are dizziness, headaches, thirst, nausea, and muscle cramps. But there are two important symptoms that occur with heat stroke: (1) sweating stops and (2) body temperature may become dangerously high- which can be a very serious problem.

Both heat exhaustion and heat stroke can be avoided by drinking enough liquids to replace those lost in exercising and by taking other important precautions.

Heart problems.

Occasionally, individuals have died while exercising. Most of these deaths are caused by overexertion in people who already had heart conditions. Many of these deaths have been preceded by warning signs such as sudden dizziness, chest pains, fainting, and extreme breathlessness. These symptoms should be brought to the attention of a doctor.

Exercising too hard is not beneficial for anyone and is particularly strenuous for out-of-shape, middle-aged, and older individuals. It is important for these individuals to follow a gradual and sound exercise program.

If you consider the amount of time your body has been out of shape, it is only natural that it will take time to get it back into shape. A gradual approach will help you maximize your risks.
