Congratulations on your acceptance to

Outdoor Youth Adventures Session I, June 17-21, 2002

 

The Outdoor Youth Adventures program is a learning-by-doing program. Not only do those 

attending learn something about the outdoors, but they should also learn something about 

themselves. Participants may take home many values which they discover while participating in the 

week's activities including goal setting, leadership development, trust, spiritual growth, an increased 

awareness of each other and nature, self-confidence, and a better appreciation of home and 

parents. Each participant is an individual and, as such, will be affected differently by this program.

 

We changed the name from Outdoor Especially for Youth to Outdoor Youth Adventures to help 

remove confusion with the Especially for Youth program offered by BYU (the two programs are 

not connected) and to help define the program more clearly with its name.

 

Please read through this information packet so you can prepare well for your stay at the 

BYU-Idaho Outdoor Learning Center (OLC) more commonly known as Badger Creek Ranch.

4.    ACTIVITIES - The week will be filled with many activities including hiking, canoeing, ropes 

course activities (climbing, rappelling, negotiating natural and pre-built obstacles), camping, 

participating in physical-type initiative games and activities, and a hand cart trek. The facilities at 

Badger Creek have been updated to include a new canoeing pond and all new or improved ropes

courses.

 

5.    RELIGIOUS INSTRUCTION - The past year has been full of changes. Foremost, Ricks 

College became Brigham Young University-Idaho. This change required a rethinking of most 

everything that occurs on the campus. As part of the changes, we have reevaluated OYA and will 

have some changes in the program. The major change will be in the spiritual instruction given during 

the week. We are putting a renewed emphasis on teaching from the scriptures. Each week will have 

a scriptural theme we will build upon throughout the week. We are encouraging participants to 

bring their scriptures to the firesides and actively participate. We will also have nightly counselor 

devotionals and daily group scripture study & prayer.

 

6.    STANDARDS -- Participants accepted into the program MUST agree to maintain the 

standards of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints and the BYU-Idaho code of dress 

and conduct. The code includes neat and modest dress NO SHORTS, TANK TOPS, CAPRIS, 

SANDLES, OR FLIP FLOPS, -no extreme appearances (including nose/body piercing, 

multi-colored or spiked/shaved hair), abstinence from tobacco, alcohol, coffee, tea, and harmful 

drugs, high principles of integrity and decency, and respect for the personal rights of others. 

students who do not plan to participate in workshop activities during the program and who are not 

willing to uphold the high standards expected of them are discouraged from coming to OYA.

 

7.    FOOD & HOUSING - Meals start with lunch on Monday through lunch on Friday.  

Participants will camp out each night in tents/tepees provided by BYU-Idaho. Roommate 

preferences will be honored where practical. Participants will need to bring a sleeping bag and pad 

for use at the OLC. If you contact us by June 3, 2002, we can reserve a sleeping bag and/or pad 

for you to rent. The cost to rent a sleeping bag is $12 and a pad is $3. Showers will be available on 

a limited basis because of the time restraints and limited facilities. Toilet facilities will consist of 

regular restrooms and some outhouses. Remember, part of the fun of attending Outdoor Youth 

Adventures is in meeting new people from all over the country -- so don't be upset if you're 

not together with everyone from your hometown.

 

8.    ROOMMATE PREFERENCE -- To eliminate problems we have experienced in years 

past, this year we will limit the roommate request to one person. If you are part of a larger group, 

please choose just one person as a roommate. Larger groups may not be together for some limited 

activities and housing assignments, but there are several opportunities throughout the week for being 

together as a group of friends, cousins, etc. If per chance your choice of person to be with in a 

group is of a different gender (i.e. your brother or sister) simply list them as roommate preference 

and e-mail the address below to ensure you are not mistakenly housed together.

 

On the online registration form in the housing section where it designates; female or male housing, 

there is a place where you can enter the ID # for who you are requesting, or you can send an e-mail 

with both names and the program you are enrolled in to wyliec@byui.edu.

11.     ASSUMPTION OF RISK FORM -- Please understand that when you participate in 

adventurous activities, there is always an element of risk to your physical being. Safety precautions

return the attached Assumption of Risk form, filled out, and signed by a parent or guardian. Return 

to BYU-Idaho by April 19, 2002.  Without a form, you will not be allowed to participate in the 

activities, and will be dropped from the program.
