Calling and Organizing a Dance Committee

In order to set the proper "bounds and conditions" of a L.D.S. dance, simultaneous training of the mind and body is essential. This training instills the goals and the importance of music and dance in all youth and adults. If you have a Stake Dance Committee they are the vehicle which initiates these training programs. This committee, under the direction of the Stake Activity Chairman, consists of a young man, a young lady, and an adult couple (Ward Dance Specialists) from each ward in the stake. From among these committee members a Priesthood holder and his wife are set apart to be the The Stake Dance Specialists. Training Programs

In order for a committee to successfully train, they need to be trained. The best way to crystallize this instruction is to sponsor a RETREAT. A retreat is an overnight get together away from family and friends, uniting all committee members, and teaching them their responsibilities through a series of workshops. These workshops fall into two areas: educating the mind and training the body. All committee members should be instructed in all areas of training responsibilities. In the retreat, instruction should be focused on how to effectively teach a lesson, plan a dance workshop, organize a party, etc.
Two Manuals along with accompanying videos tell how to put on the RETREAT. These are available. Write or call Bob OLiphant, Ricks Collage, Rexburg, ID. 83460-0300. Phone # 208-356-2080

Educating the mind
The Stake Dance Specialist has the responsibility of overseeing the education of the attitude of music and dance in the minds of stake members. This can be done through a variety of ways. Here are some examples:
1. Lessons: Lessons involve specific training in Sunday School, Priesthood, Young Men/Young Women and other meetings regarding how music and dance effects our spiritual growth. There are various talks by church leaders on the effects of music and dance on the body that can be used as subject material.
2. Workshops: These workshops could focus on how to plan, organize and carry out a successful dance or activity. Other workshop topics include dance etiquette, equipment training, or environmental effects on social behavior within a dance.
3. Firesides: Firesides involving guest speakers on the subject of music and dance can provide a strong spiritual unity among the members of each ward/stake.

Training the Body
The Stake Dance Specialist has the responsibility of overseeing the dance training in the stake. Here are some ways to initiate this training.
1. Formal dance classes: These classes should be the responsibility of the Ward Dance Specialist. They should be opened to all ward members, and consist of basic instruction of dance, both partner and non partner. The Stake "Dance Team" can be a great support in these formal dance classes. See "Dance Team" on next page.
2. Inservice dance class: Inservice dance classes are a series of dance classes for the thirteen year old to prepare them for the stake dances. To emphasize the importance of dance to these youth, an interview by the Bishop before the inservice, and award of a dance card upon completion of the class should be given. This activity could include more than one ward, but should be kept small. Parents and older youth or the Dance Team members are used as partners to alleviate peer pressure problems.
3. Dance Cotillion: This is an organized dance for the 12 and 13 year old youth of the stake. This dance needs to be held as often as twice a month or no skill can be gained. This is a time for training the skills of dance, with no so called free time.


The Dance Committee must realize that the training process does not produce immediate results. Patience is the byword! Be prepared to spend at least a year establishing the proper atmosphere at your dances. Just as an athletic team spends years working together, developing skills, you need to continually nurture your dance and social skills in order to train the youth to fully enjoy their dances. Never forget the goal of why dances and dance training is needed, Interpersonal life skills are being practiced at the dance. Proper uplifting dances are places where youth learn the social skills that they are not getting out in the world. Never forget that Satan knows this. He has changed the way we view dances to fit his view (read the Comparison Chart). Unity and cooperation are critical to the Dance Committee's success formula. The youth of the stake will mirror the unity and enthusiasm of the Dance Committee.
The major focus of the Dance Committee is the training programs. Only through proper training can the five goals of dance be reached.

There are two areas that aid in the training process. The Dance Team and Resource Library are equal to the training programs.

The Dance Team
The Dance Team is a catalyst for the training programs. They show the dance skills they have learned, through floor shows, to the youth. Members of the team can attend dance instruction workshops and help teach. They could be the partners for the thirteen year olds in the inservice programs. They also help control the youth (at dances or at training sessions) through their example. When selecting youth members of the Dance Committee ( who can also be the Dance Team members ) look for qualities of rhythm, desire to help others, commitment, and personality. The Stake Dance Specialist or one adult couple is chosen to be advisors and trainers for the Dance Team.

The Resource Library
The Resource Library is a wealth of information established within each stake that anyone in the stake can use. It consists of books, church talks, magazines, films, videos and any other media resources that pertain to music and dance. The Stake Dance Specialist is responsibile to organize and compile these materials. In addition to media resources, the stake needs to purchase and have on hand dance equipment that would be used by the Dance Committee as well as any organization within the stake that wants to sponsor a dance. The Stake Dance Specialist is responsible for choosing, with input from the rest of the Dance Committee, the music that becomes a part of the dance music library. This would include all types of dance music, such as Folk, Round, Square, etc. No unapproved music is appropriate at a dance.
