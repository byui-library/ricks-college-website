THE MATHEMATICS PROGRAM

 

We provide students the opportunity to explore and use the beautiful and powerful language of mathematics.

 

What do Mathematicians do?

 

Students who major in mathematics and go on to complete a Bachelors degree or higher can choose from a wide variety of careers -- from forecasting next month’s weather to predicting the winner of an upcoming election, from designing insurance policies to planning large scale irrigation projects, from teaching students the Pythagorean Theorem to creating new theorems. Mathematicians discover and explore the properties of abstract structures and apply them to almost every area of human endeavor.

 

Need Help?

 

If you need to acquire or renew mathematical skills, we offer courses in beginning levels of algebra. The Department takes primary responsibility for the administration of the mathematics graduation requirement.

Please study carefully the program and course descriptions that follow and become as excited as we are about offering them.

 

THE COMPUTER SCIENCE PROGRAM

 

Are you interested in becoming involved in one of the greatest revolutions our society has ever seen? Do you wish to solve complex and challenging problems that will shape how our world thinks and functions? Then Computer Science is the major for you. We provide a program that is intended to start a student on the path toward a Bachelors degree in Computer Science and give them the opportunity to be on the leading edge of technology. Computer Science majors get jobs in every area of computer use.

Associate in Arts and Sciences with a major in Computer Science (237)

 

The purpose of the Computer Science major is to provide a solid background in Computer Science that facilitates transfer to a four-year university or college. To accomplish this objective, the student is given experience in algorithmic development, programming, data structures, computer architecture and the theoretical foundations of Computer Science. This, coupled with science and mathematics courses, prepares students to transfer to many accredited universities and colleges.

 

Computer Science majors should be prepared to enter the program at the Math 162 (Calculus) level. Those who are deficient in math may have to take courses to prepare them for Math 162. This may require a student to take longer than four semesters to complete the program.

 

Students who have had no programming experience should enroll in CS 170. Those who have a strong background in C are encouraged to sign up for CS 165. Please see an advisor if you have questions.

 

In addition to the General Education requirements, any student graduating with a major in Computer Science is required to complete with a grade of “C” or better:

1. Math: Math 162, Math 243

2. Science: Chem 105, Ph 121, BS 200

3. Computer Science: CS 165, or CS 170, 171, 233, 235, 236.

COURSE DESCRIPTIONS

 

Mathematics

 

 

Math 100A Arithmetic    1

Three contact hours per week

 

A study of arithmetic and applications using arithmetic. This course is only for those needing a review of elementary school arithmetic including signed numbers, fractions, decimals and percents.

 

Math 100B Beginning Algebra Part I     1

Three contact hours per week

 

The arithmetic of integers and rational numbers as well as an introduction to algebra. Success in the next math class requires a grade of “B” or better. This course is recommended for those needing basic algebra before starting into progressively higher math courses.

 

Math l00C Beginning Algebra Part II     1

Prerequisite Math 100B and consent of instructor

Three contact hours per week

 

This is a continuation of beginning algebra introduced in Math 100B.

Roughly equivalent to the second half of the first year of high school algebra. Linear equations and their graphs, systems of linear equations, quadratics. Success in the next math class requires a grade of “B” or better.

 

Math 100S Agricultural Mathematics     3

Prerequisite: Admission to the technical agricultural programs

Three lecture hours per week or offered in a lab setting

Fee $15 for supplies

 

Mathematics applied to agricultural problems.

 

Math 101* Intermediate Algebra    3

Prerequisite: Ability to demonstrate proficiency in first year algebra or Math 100B and C with a grade of “B” or higher

Three contact hours per week

 

Fundamental operations of algebra, properties of exponents, solving linear, fractional and quadratic equations, graphing linear and quadratic functions. Math 101 may not be taken for credit if Math 110 has been completed with a grade of "B" or higher. Success in the next math class requires a grade of “B” or better.

 

Math 108* Quantitative Literacy     3

Prerequisite: Math 101 or two years of high school algebra

Three contact hours per week

 

A course designed to meet the mathematical needs of the liberal arts student. Topics may include mathematical modeling, regression, finance mathematics, probability, statistics, logic, and mathematical patterns and aesthetics. This course will satisfy both the Ricks College and Idaho Core math requirements but will not serve as a prerequisite to other mathematics courses. A graphing calculator may be required.

(See class schedule.)

 

Math 110* College Algebra    3

Prerequisite: Two years of high school algebra or Math 101.

May be taken daily for 10 weeks or MWF for the full semester

 

Theory and applications of polynomial, rational, exponential, and logarithmic functions, and systems of linear equations.

(This can be followed in the same semester with Math 111.)

Graphing calculator may be required.

(See class schedule.)

 

Math 111* Trigonometry    2

Prerequisite: Math 101 or equivalent

 

Circular functions, triangle relationships, identities, inverse trig functions, complex numbers, and applications.

Graphing calculator may be required. (See class schedule.)

 

Math 162* Plane Analytic Geometry and Calculus    4

Prerequisite: Math 110 and Math 111

Five hours of lecture per week

 

One-dimensional differential and integral calculus with applications.

Graphing calculator may be required. (See class schedule.)

 

Math 205* Basic Concepts for Elementary Education Teachers    3

Prerequisite: Math 110

Three hours of lecture per week

 

This course is primarily for education majors. The content focuses on important mathematical ideas and their interrelationships. Problem solving, sets, relations, functions, systems of numeration, operations on the real numbers and number theory are included.

 

Math 206* Math Concepts for Elementary Education Teachers     3

Prerequisite: Math 205

Three hours of lecture per week

 

Enrichment or extended study of the concepts covered in Math 205 plus the study of geometry, probability, statistics and logic for elementary education teachers. (Winter Only)

 

 

 

*GE Credit in Math

 

Math 209* Elementary Calculus    4

Prerequisite: Math 110

Five hours of lecture per week

 

A one-semester terminal course of single and multi-variable calculus designed primarily for students in biology, agriculture, and business. Topics include derivatives, integrals, and their applications.

Graphing calculator may be required.

(See class schedule.)

 

Math 221* Principles of Statistics    3-4

Prerequisite: Math 110

Four hours of lecture per week

 

Frequency distributions; measures of central tendency and dispersion; elementary probability; regression and correlation; sampling; statistical inference and estimation including the normal, t-test, chi-square, and analysis of variance tests; multiple contrasts and multiple regression.

Graphing calculator may be required.

(See class schedule.)

 

Math 243* Linear Algebra    3

Prerequisite: Math 162

 

Linear systems, matrices, vector spaces, linear transformations, determinants, Eigenvalues, Eigenvectors.

 

Math 263* Solid Analytical Geometry and Calculus    4

Prerequisite: Math 162

Four hours of lecture per week

 

Applications and methods of integration, hyperbolic functions, infinite series, polar coordinates, parametric forms and vectors.

Graphing calculator may be required. (See class schedule.)

 

Math 264* Multidimensional Calculus     3

Prerequisite: Math 263

Four lecture of hours per week

 

Partial derivatives, n-dimensional vectors, multiple integrals, integral theorems of vector calculus including Green’s and Stokes’ theorems.

Graphing calculator may be required. (See class schedule.)

 

Math 274* Introduction to Ordinary Differential Equations     3

Prerequisite: Math 243 or concurrent registration

Four hours of lecture per week

 

An introduction to the theory of ordinary differential equations with applications, linear systems, series solutions, the Laplace Transform, and numerical methods.

 

Math 280* Topics in Pure Math     3

Prerequisite: Math 263 or consent of instructor

Three hours of lecture per week

 

Reading, understanding and creating rigorous mathematical proofs. Fundamentals of set theory, relations, functions and cardinality will be covered. Instructors may choose to cover additional topics from Algebra, Analysis, or Topology.

CS 100* Computer Basics    0-2

 

This self tutorial option prepares the individual for challenging the knowledge and competency exam's to meet the General Education Computer basics requirement. Areas covered by this option include: hardware and operating systems, word processing, spreadsheet, database applications and ethical use of computers.

 

CS 100L* Computer Basics Lab    2

 

This computer lab prepares the individual for challenging the knowledge and competency exams to meet the General Education computer basics requirement while applying these skills to a specific discipline. Areas covered by this option include: hardware and operating systems, word processing, spreadsheet, database applications and ethical use of computers.

 

CS 144 Introduction to Programming     3

 

Introduction to programming using the C programming language. Subjects include algorithm development, problem solving, testing and debugging, all done in the C language. All programming will be done in the UNIX environment This course is highly recommended for non-Computer Science majors and those that need a course in the C language.

 

 

*GE Credit in Basic Skills

 

CS 152 Discrete Structures    2

Prerequisite: Math 162

Algebraic structures applied to computers, including sets, graphs, logic and functions. (Fall only)

 

CS 165 Objected-Oriented Programming with C++     3

Prerequisites: CS 144 or a solid background in the C programming language

A course on Object-Oriented Programming (OOP) using the C++ programming language in the UNIX environment. Includes classes, operator overload, object-oriented I/O, and templates. Class will emphasize the four areas of OOP: encapsulation, abstraction, inheritance and polymorphism.

 

Note: Students are expected to have had the C language prior to taking this class. Those deficient in this area are encouraged to instead take CS 170.

 

CS 170 Programming I     4

 

Introduction to object oriented programming using C++ lanurage in the UNIX environment. Topics include introductrion to software engineering and structured programming, basic UNIX shell commands, the C++ language, procedural programming using C++ and C++ object oriented programming emphasizing abstraction, encapsulation, hierarchy, and polymorphism.

 

CS 171 Programming II     4

Prerequisite: CS 170 or equivalent in procedural and object oriented languages

 

Introduction to Web programming in a client-server environment. Topics include Web site design, HTML, JavaScript, CGI programming, Perl, and Java.

 

 

CS 233 Assembly Language Programming    3

Prerequisite: CS 170

 

Fundamentals of computer architecture and organization. Students will be introduced to number systems, memory structures, registers, instruction formats, addressing modes, assembly language, and CPU structure and function. Classes will do projects in assembly language that illustrate the various architectural features of the computer.

 

 

CS 235 Foundations of Computer Science I     4

Prerequisite: CS 170, Math 162

 

Iteration, induction, recursion, lists, trees, sets, relations, functions; mathematical analysis of algorithms and data models, object-oriented implementation of abstract data types. All programming will be in the C++ language using the UNIX environment.

 

CS 236 Foundations of Computer Science II     4

Prerequisite: CS 235

 

Continuation of CS 235; relations, graphs, automata, grammars, propositional and predicate logic. Implementation of object-oriented algorithms using C++ in the UNIX environment.

 

CS 290 Special Topics    1-3

Prerequisite: Consent of Instructor

 

Faculty/student consultation will determine an area of study/research that will give an advanced student greater appreciation and experience in this field. Terms of enrollment, credit, etc., will be determined by the instructor.
