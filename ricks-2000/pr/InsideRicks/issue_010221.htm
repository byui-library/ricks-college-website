ELDER ZWICK INSTRUCTS ON NAVIGATION SYSTEMS  FOR THE ROADS OF LIFE

Elder W. Craig Zwick, a member of the LDS Church's First Quorum of the Seventy, taught about navigating the roads of life at the Ricks College devotional Feb. 13. "We each have our own onboard navigation system to give us turn-by-turn instructions," Elder Zwick said. "However, it has nothing to do with finding a certain street or a specific location. It has everything to do with our eternal destination. Our destination is clearly defined and a good navigation system will help us achieve exaltation and eternal life."

He said that for our navigation system to work effectively, we must have an understanding of where we are and where we are going. He also warned of distractions along the way. "Often you can be on the right road, but will be assaulted from all sides with offers to take other roads or more attractive travel itineraries. The adversary will be doing his best to lead you off your desired course. He'll stops at nothing to catch your attention, and then, ever so craftily, he will lead you into blind canyons and dead ends. Satan's desire is for us to not know where we are and to not check in to confirm our location," he said.

Elder Zwick told a story of a young man named Cody Parmer from Idaho Falls, who was involved in a serious accident and suffered many health problems on his long road to recovery. Zwick invited Parmer to share some words about overcoming his challenges and his feelings on the Savior. As the young man finished telling how he overcame his adversity, Elder Zwick pointed out this principle: "Don't allow yourselves to be discouraged by events which you cannot control. Overcome discouragement by losing yourselves in the service of others. This will also qualify you to receive more responsibility and to have added light and understanding."

He said that the navigation system has a detailed set of instructions with specific directions that must be followed in order for it to function properly. These instructions are the holy scriptures. Another navigational tool Elder Zwick mentioned was hearkening to the voice of the living prophets.

He concluded, "You are powerful and will make a positive difference in the lives of thousands while here on this beautiful campus and well after you leave. You must always know precisely where you are, even when all that surrounds you is a background of darkness and confusion. You are on a well-marked path that leads to our eternal home."

TWO WRESTLERS EARN NATIONAL TITLES

Ashton Buswell, a freshman from Ogden, Utah, and Ryan Hansen, a sophomore from Idaho Falls, brought home national wrestling championship trophies following the junior college finals at Rochester, Minn., last weekend. It was only the second time the Vikings had more than one national champion at the end of a season. As a team the Vikings placed third behind North Idaho and Iowa Central despite amassing the most number of points ever at the national finals.

The third-place finish equals last year's national finish. The Vikings have now placed in the top ten nationally the past six years. North Idaho dominated with 152 points to Iowa Central's 113 1/2 and Ricks' 108 1/2. Last year Ricks placed third with only 93 points. In all, Ricks had seven wrestlers earn All-American designation. The seven tie the Ricks record for the most wrestlers to place nationally which was set in 1992.

In addition to Buswell, a 157-pounder, and Hansen, a heavyweight, others placing were Nate Ploehn and Travis Marx, both third, and Ty Rollins (fifth), Jeremy Lewis (sixth), and Andy Messersmith (seventh).

MEN'S WEEK ENCOURAGES 'MEN LIKE UNTO MORONI'

"Men like unto Moroni" is the theme of Men's Week taking place Feb. 20-25. "We are trying to encourage the young men of Ricks College to set a standard for themselves - to be valiant, to encourage good activities and to be honorable," says Marci Clay, Associated Students of Ricks College programs vice president.

The week involves several events that honor men, according to Clay. Kicking off the week, there are photographs, paintings and poetry on display in the Manwaring Center through Friday. The winner of the contest will be announced during the Men's Week Talent Show which will be held Thursday from 7-10 p.m. in the Hart Auditorium. No tickets are required and the event is free.

The Man of the Year will also be announced at the Talent Show. Finalists are Kris Beldin of Richland, Wash.; Garv Gianchetta of Idaho Falls; Mike Hammond of Rexburg; Jeremy Jackson of Logan; Hojin Kang of Kwangju, South Korea; Medilir Mema of Durres, Albania; Zabron Mwaipopo of Dar es Salaam,Tanzania; Spencer Shimanda of Boise; and Hutch Swan of Twin Falls.

The finals for slam dunk basketball competition will be Friday in the Hart Gym at 7 p.m. The competition will be followed by a dance. Men's Week will conclude Sunday with a fireside in the Hart Auditorium at 7 p.m. The speaker will be Max Craner, president of the Logan Temple and former Area Authority from Rexburg.
