Tree fall paralyzes student

By Jennifer Jones
scroll staff

Emily Young, a freshman from Tygard, Ore., fell from a tree near Edna I. Ricks Residence Hall (Dorm 4), leaving her paralyzed from her naval down.
Young and her friend, Skip Stellhorn, a sophomore from Manteca, Calif., climbed a tree after swing dancing Wednesday. Young fell, breaking her back when she landed perpendicular on a jagged limb that broke in her fall.
Stellhorn heard her scream and watched her fall. He told friends that he knew it was bad when he saw how she was lying on the ground. He hurried to the ground where he talked with Young until paramedics arrived.
Young was flown from Madison Memorial Hospital to Eastern Idaho Regional Medical Center where she underwent six hours of surgery to re-align her spine. A four-inch disparity marked the break in her spine, but doctors aligned her spine straight. Clamps, rods and screws are holding it in place.
Doctors will not be able to fix any nerve damage.
Her back was broken at the 10th vertebrae. If the break were any higher, she would not be breathing on her own or have the use of her arms.
Infection, severe headaches and leaking spinal fluid are still a concern for family and doctors. "We'll rely on faith to sustain her and hold
complications at bay so she'll be able to recover and head down a new path," her mom Brenda Young said. "
Young worked the past several years on voice, piano and art. "She'll still be able to have all those capabilities," Brenda Young said.
Young's friends describe her as strong and amazing. "She won't have a pity party," friend Kristi Hawks, a sophomore from Clearfield, Utah, said.
"Life isn't over for Emily," Amy Hayes, a friend from church and sophomore from Colville, Wash., said.
She is a little frustrated about her future - a little anxious. But that's normal, her mom said. There is hope, acceptance and gratefulness in the family's attitude, not anger or resentment.
"Emily is on a different road. She had a slight detour," Brenda Young said.
Young remembers falling for a long time, and wondering when she was going to hit the ground. She remembers every detail. She wasn't careless. She didn't slip. She wasn't distracted.
"There was no cause for her fall," her mom struggled to say. She was careful and aware of every limb. She didn't lose her footing. It was just a twist of fate. The family won't use the term "freak accident" because it makes it sound dangerous or scary.
"It was a blissful twist of fate," her mom said.
Young has felt the community's love, strength, and support. "I'm the luckiest girl in the world," she said.
Concerned friends and teachers are wearing blue "Emily ribbons" to show their concern for the Young family. Young's insurance won't cover the medical expenses. Friends are organizing an ASRC-sponsored charity dance. All proceeds from the dance will go to help the family with medical expenses. Friends are asking for donations from students and faculty as well as local businesses. Tables will be set up around campus for donations. Students can also pick up "Emily ribbons" at these tables.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

Boone Barnes sings "Perhaps" at Friday night's Best of Guitars Unplugged in the Hart Auditorium.
by CHRIS PARKINSON, Photo Editor

Brian Schenk caught one pass all night, but it's the only one people will remember.
With his team down by one in overtime, Schenk, a sophomore from Bellingham, Wash., blocked his man on the line, ran to the end zone and pulled down the two-point conversion to make the Crushers ward intramural football champions over the Screaming Weasels 26-25.
"I knew that there was a shirt on the line, so I just kept trying to keep everyone pumped up," Schenk said.
With the entire season resting on one play, Ben Wall, a sophomore from Lyden, Wash., decided to throw for two instead of one.
"Every one on the team was saying 'let's go for two' so I just went with it," Wall said.
The Screaming Weasels had dominated the league and been the No. 1 ranked ward team until the week before tournaments when they were dethroned by the Crushers. The championship game was the ultimate rematch.
"This is where it's at, baby. We're either going to crash and burn or win big time," Weasels' Mike Lewis, a sophomore from St. Maries, Idaho, said before the game.
The Weasels had been behind for most of the game but with 11 seconds left in regulation, Lewis jumped four feet in the air and caught a desperation hail-mary pass to tie the game at 19-19.
With two seconds left, Weasels' Jay Singleton, a sophomore from Woodinville, Wash., intercepted the ball to send the game to overtime.
In overtime, each team had four downs to move the ball 10 yards.
The Screaming Weasels scored first on a quarterback keeper from Dominic Bonadio, a freshman from Ada, Mich., who threw for the extra point to Bob Perkes, a freshman from Greenacres, Wash., making the score 25-19.
Wall bounced his touchdown pass off a Weasel defensive man to his teammate Tim Cronin, a freshman from Salem, Ore., to set up the two-point pass that won the game.
Bonadio was 15 for 26 and threw for three touchdowns with one interception. Singleton had four receptions and Matt Munson, a freshman from Arlee, Mont., had four tackles and batted away three passes.
Wall completed 14 of 26 for three touchdowns with two interceptions. Cronin had four receptions and Chris Beuhler, a freshman from Mount Airy, Md., had three while Mike Williams, a freshman from Jerome, Idaho, and Jack Anderson, a sophomore from Federal Way, Wash., combined for seven tackles and four broken-up passes.
