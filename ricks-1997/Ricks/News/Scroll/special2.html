Women should prayfully consider decision to serve a mission
By Krista Hansen, Scroll Staff



ON YOUR KNEES-Women should prayfully consider a decision to serve a mission
by Ryan JonesScroll Staff


President Gordon B. Hinckley announced at the priesthood session of general conference in October 1997 that women are not required to serve missions and "missionary work is essentially a priesthood responsibility." However, this statement was not a revolution in the Church.

As early as 1992, the Church News published a statement saying, "There is increasing evidence that some young women are being strongly encouraged to serve full-time missions. Though capable and effective, young women do not have the same responsibility to serve full-time missions as do young men who hold the priesthood. We are grateful that some desire to serve ... but they should not feel obligated to do so. A young woman should not be recommended for a mission if it would interfere with a specific marriage proposal."

Angela Marrott, wife of campus bishop Robert Marrott and a returned missionary from the North Central States Mission, said she felt strongly she should serve a mission when she received her patriarchal blessing. "It said I would serve as a missionary to other parts of the vineyard," she said. "I always had a feeling I would need to serve."

While she was living in Salt Lake City, Marrott went to see her bishop because she was feeling depressed. She mentioned she had always thought about serving a full-time mission and the bishop was excited with the idea "got excited." The bishop fasted about it with her, and they both knew it was the right thing for her to do.

Marrott said her mission was a "wonderful experience" and would advise sisters thinking about serving a mission to "really pray" about the decision. She said sisters should think about why they are going on a mission and be sure they really want to do it.

President Ezra Taft Benson said he supports sisters who choose to serve a mission. In Teachings of Ezra Taft Benson he said, "Remember, young women, you may also have the opportunity to serve a full-time mission. I am grateful my own eternal companion served a mission in Hawaii before we were married in the Salt Lake Temple, and I am pleased that I have had three grand daughters serve full-time missions. Some of our finest missionaries are young sisters."

Sisters deciding whether to serve should "be sincere in their feelings and pray for guidance," Marrott said.

President Hinckley said in his priesthood conference address that the sisters "are under no obligation" to serve a mission and that sisters who choose not to go "will be as highly respected, you will be considered as being as much in the line of duty, your efforts will be as acceptable to the Lord and to the Church whether you go on a mission or do not go on a mission."
