TI-86 Help Sheet
Key Ideas / Features
  • Order of Operations
    (PEMDAS = Parentheses, Exponents, Multiplication, Division, Addition,     Subtraction)
    Note:  The TI-86 evaluates "implied" multiplication prior to "explicit" multiplication or division.  For example, 1/3x is evaluated as 1 divided by 3x as opposed to 1/3 multiplied by x.
  • Insert vs. Overstrike Cursor
    A blinking rectangle will overwrite characters on the screen but pressing [2nd] [INS] will allow for typing between characters instead of on top of them.  This is denoted by an underscore.
  • MORE
    To help promote a user-friendly interface, anytime there are more options to choose from you will see a bolded right arrow on the right-hand side of the screen.   Use MORE to see "more" options.
  • [2nd] [MODE]
    If your calculator is not responding the way you want, consider checking your MODE.   The defaults are easy to     remember—they are all on the left-hand side of the screen—use [ENTER] to highlight words.
  • [2nd] [CATALOG-VARS] [CATLG]
    If you ever forget where a feature is located, use the TI-86's built-in     alphabetical catalog.  To find a feature quickly, just press the first letter.  If you would like to store it permanently, press [F3] to access your CUSTOM menu and store it on any of the fifteen slots available.
  • [2nd] [:]
    You can do several things at the same time on the home screen by separating commands with a colon.  For example 5 [STO] [x-VAR] [2nd] : y1  will store 5 in for x and then display the y value for the equation y1 from the grapher.
  • [2nd] [MEM]
    Memory management is as important for a     calculator as it is for a computer.  You have five options here.
    RAM tells you the bytes that are free and how memory is allocated.
    DELET gives you the chance to     remove variables, lists, equations, programs, etc. by pressing [ENTER]  Be careful! 
    RESET will wipe out anything in memory and reset your defaults.  Only use if you want to start all over.

 
Helpful Hints / Shortcuts
  • To darken the contrast of your screen, press [2nd] [down-arrow] and to lighten, press [2nd] [up-arrow].  The number in the upper right-hand corner represents your level of contrast from 0 to 9.
  • When transferring data/programs between two TI-86's, make sure the cable is pressed firmly into the port.
  • When your graph doesn't look like you thought it would, you usually have one of four problems:
    1)  bad equation:  check for accuracy of your     syntax and make sure the equals sign is bolded (turned on)
    2)  bad window:  check     the validity of your x- and y- min/max settings using the range menu
    3)  bad formatting:  check the FORMAT menu [GRAPH] [MORE] [F3] to reset your defaults
    4)  bade modes:  check your mode screen by pressing [2nd] [MODE]
  • When you change your batteries, pull them out and replace one at a time just in case the backup battery fails.
  • If you want a previous entry just entered, try pressing [2nd] [ENTRY].  The calculator retains the last 128 bytes of data you have entered in the home screen.
  • If you want to use the previous answer in a calculation, press [2nd] [ANS].
  • Right-handed parenthesis are not always necessary if they are only needed at the end of an expression.
  • To get to the end/beginning of an expression quickly, press [2nd] [right-arrow] or [2nd] [left-arrow].
  • If you want to pause in the middle of graphing a function, press [ENTER].   If you want to cancel, press [ON].
  • In the [2nd] [SOLVER] you should use [2nd] [RCL] prior to pressing an equation from your F keys.
  • To type words more efficiently, press [ALPHA] twice to lock the calculator in ALPHA mode.  (This is similar to caps lock on a keyboard.)
  • If you want to produce quick y values from any given x values, consider doing one of the following:
    1)  graph the function and then use EVAL ([GRAPH] [MORE] [MORE] [F1])
    2)  stay in the home screen and try entering your x value then pressing STO [x-VAR] [2nd] y1
    3)  use the features eval or evalF (eval needs one x parameter and evalF needs three parameters:  f(x), x, and x-value)
