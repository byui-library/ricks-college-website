The Transition: Press Conference- Rexburg
Excerpts are from a press conference held in Rexburg with President David A. Bednar at noon at June 21, 2000.
Ladies and gentlemen, good afternoon. We appreciate your attendance here this afternoon. Our primary purpose is to simply respond to questions that you may have about the announcement today in Salt Lake City made by President Gordon B. Hinckley. I hope you won't be totally frustrated if you find a lot of "Well, we are not totally sure right now" responses to some of your questions, but we will be happy to answer whatever you want to ask. What questions do you have? Please.

How will this affect the school's accreditation?
We recently went through our ten-year formal accreditation review and passed with flying colors. Now, as a four-year institution, it will be necessary to revisit that accreditation for a four-year status. We have been in contact with the accrediting bodies this morning and are working with them to outline the guidelines necessary as we move forward.

What certain time lines do you perceive in implementing the change?
It will be phased in. The most specific answer I can give you is that at 12 o'clock today, programmatically we are the same institution that we were at 8 o'clock this morning. And frankly, that will be the same in the fall as well. We have a lot of evaluation and assessment to do and that, in large measure, will determine the pace in which we implement four-year programs. So we need to consult with our academic leaders. We have a great deal to do, and it would be premature to say precisely how long it will take.

What does this mean to the athletic department and athletic program? I have heard that possibly it may be stopping.
No "possibly." President Hinckley indicated that it will be phased out. We will honor all of our commitments to student athletes in terms of scholarships and other commitments that we have. But, over time, those programs will be phased out. That is the implication.

Is this for the next year?
No. Athletics will operate this fall and spring as planned because that is part of honoring those commitments to our student athletes and to our coaching staff.

President Bednar, a lot of people will be disappointed by that. What is the reasoning behind that?
I guess I would rely on the statement made by President Hinckley this morning in terms of it [this change] reflecting a shift of emphasis and that there will be - not that we haven=t had an academic emphasis - but a renewed and increased emphasis on the academic excellence of BYU-Idaho.

Will this affect enrollment?
The likelihood is that over time, we will see increases in enrollment. That is part of the innovation we have been working on so that with the campus facility we have at the present time we can serve more students.

How long has this been in the works? How long have you been developing the change?
This was a Board-initiated action; and though we participate in Board meetings, we do not participate in their private deliberations. So I cannot give you an exact answer. We received official notification yesterday afternoon.

How long has the idea been thrown around?
Again, we have not been a participant in those discussions. You will recall there was a time when Ricks was a four-year school back in the 1950s. Whether or not that has been an ongoing topic of discussion or whether that should come back, I frankly don't know.

How do you think the faculty and students will respond to this decision?
We have had selected responses thus far today. In the meeting at 8 o'clock in the Hart Auditorium, there was tremendous excitement and applause. The faculty with whom I have visited thus far are very enthusiastic and excited. I am sure there are some people who have questions and people who are not as thrilled. But, at this point, it seems to be very well received.

Can you describe the process now that will be needed to develop some of these bachelor programs?
Certainly. I can describe a series of steps that will be necessary. It is important to recognize that this is not an expansionist step. In other words, if we think that now we will follow the same precise course that Provo has taken in terms of its physical plant and the number of programs, I think we would be in serious error. So we will begin by asking the questions: What must we have at BYU-Idaho to be the finest teaching institution we can possibly be? What must we have in terms of our academic offerings? We will then have to make some adjustments. We now have approximately 140 degree programs. We are not immediately going to go to 140 baccalaureate degree programs. So there must, of necessity, be an evaluation and adjustment of resources internally - space, FTE [full-time employees], faculty, and so forth. And then as we develop that plan, we will make an assessment of what additional resources may be necessary to be able to offer those high quality programs. So those are really the steps.

Will you be introducing any new curriculum under the four-year college plan?
At this point we have to consult with our academic leaders. Please don't interpret this as I am trying to duck your question, but we really will consult with them and ask the question: What do we need to have to be that kind of an institution? As we answer that question, it may be that we would add some new courses. We will have to see at that time.

I know this is at the preliminary phase, but do you have sort of a visionary plan of how you are going to accommodate increasing enrollment and how the city of Rexburg is going to work with the college growth?
I think it is crucial to recall that this will be phased in. We will not have a significant increase this fall. And so we will work with the city in a very cooperative way. I met with the mayor this morning immediately following the announcement. We talked about how we will move forward. There are impacts in housing and in other areas. We will be very forthcoming with information so the community will be able to adjust.

Is there currently a restriction on enlarging the campus here and something [a policy] that the Church has in place?
All of the institutions in the Church Educational System are operating under what is called a "space neutral policy." That simply means that, given existing space and an inventory of how much that is, you cannot have net new additional square footage. So yes, we are operating under that policy.

Is this an exception?
After we go through the process of defining what we need and of allocating and reallocating resources internally, if there is a need based on clear evidence, then we will be able to take that request to the Board for their consideration.

What about enrollment caps? Could you expand that with this new enrollment?
As we continue with the innovation - for example, the things that we have been working on with year-round calendaring, different points of entry for the academic year - that facilitates more students having the experience. The answer is yes. That is a possibility.

A change like this is going to take a considerable amount of funds. Is there a capital campaign planned, or what is the financial backing to help with the growth?
That is a good question. Again, keep in mind that President Hinckley indicated today that the decision came because of the existing facility and some of the innovations that already are underway. Undoubtedly, there will be an increase in the resource requirement; but we have an ongoing development program. We have a series of five-year priorities. We put those in place for the years 2000-2005 when we were Ricks College. We will probably need to revisit those a little bit, but they will not need drastic alteration. That helps. But the major sources of our funding are appropriations from the Church and tuition.

What admission changes are we going to see?
Likely none. In the announcement today, it was indicated that BYU-Idaho will retain both its autonomy and identity. Though we coordinate with BYU in terms of admission practices, they are not identical. Our plan at the present moment is to continue with the admission policies we have.

Why are you changing to a four-year university? What advance or what situations have come up that have made you decide that you need to be a four-year university rather than a two-year junior college? Why should the public here be excited for that?
I guess I would back up and just reiterate that this is a Board-directed initiative, and so the members of the Board came to that conclusion. President Hinckley simply indicated that this is a resource - this campus, this faculty, this student body - that will be greatly benefited to the move to four-year status.

In terms of the community, it will bring an additional element of diversity to what we have done historically on this campus. The possibility - very strong possibility - is that we will have a significantly greater number of married students. And we will also have to consider the role of having [more] international students as well.

Does this decision have anything to do with the over population at BYU? I understand there is an increase there. There a lot of students there. Is that part of the hope that this will help out also Idaho students who end up going down to Brigham Young University?
I would not view this as a spillover decision. I think there are some distinctive features here at BYU-Idaho that the Board really wants to capitalize on - especially the innovation and the opportunity to learn ways whereby we can touch and bless many more students. I see that as a greater impetus than just simply the spillover from Provo.

You see this as an impetus for local students or students already who are coming here for a two-year degree and want to stick around and get a four. Is that what you are saying?
We are not really just a local school. We have students from all 50 states and more than 40 foreign countries. That is very uncommon for a two-year school. In that sense, we already fill the profile of a four-year school.

What will happen to the people who work here - athletic staff, coaches, facilities? Any idea what will become of those?
We are going to take every measure possible to assist in the transition. Again, please keep in mind that given the announcement this morning, that does not do away with our athletic schedule for the forthcoming year. There is a period of time - and the Board has been sensitive to recognizing the need - for transition. So we are going to evaluate the skills of those people - where they might contribute to where we are going to be going in the future. We will just have to evaluate and make those decision as we move forward.

President Bednar, I apologize; but I will pursue this. You have now, I guess, about 9,000 students give or take. What can Rexburg expect? Will it grow to 11,000?
This is where I said at the beginning, "I may frustrate you"; but I am just not at a point now where I can give you a number that I really want to bank on. There are some things that we have to evaluate as this moves forward. I hope people will catch a sense of both the magnitude of this change and the innovation that is involved. Some very distinctive features were announced by President Hinckley today. And all of the impacts of those are not perfectly known at the present time, so we want to be cautious. We want to do our planning as effectively as we can. When we have those estimates, we will make sure that you are some of the first to know. But I am not willing to go there right now.

I assume that would apply towards faculty numbers as well.
Sure.

Will this be different than BYU-Hawaii, or is it basically the same concept?
President Hinckley was very clear today indicating that only baccalaureate degrees will be offered here at BYU-Idaho.

Will the degrees say BYU-Idaho or BYU in general? Will it be interchangeable with the regular BYU degree?
This may sound like a really weak answer, but we have not even thought about that yet at this point. Again, that is something that we will need to coordinate. This is new for the Church as well with BYU-Hawaii, now BYU-Idaho, and Provo. So there are a number of those things that we will have to coordinate with all of the institutions as well.

Are there other schools that are thinking of gearing toward this and coming under the BYU umbrella?
To my knowledge the Board does not have that in mind for any other schools. One of the questions asked this morning was, "Will there be any additional two-year schools in the Church Educational System." President Hinckley's answer was no.

What will be the program emphasis of the bachelor degrees you are going to offer here? I know BYU has a strong law program. Is there going to be any kind of focus here as opposed to BYU - something alternate for students to look at?
I think as a principle it makes sense. Again, looking at the worldwide and Church view, we will have to very carefully consider whether duplicate points of emphasis make sense. In some academic areas, it may. In others, it may not. That is part of what we have to evaluate. And the other thing is - I go back to my same answer - I can't tell you now what those points of emphasis will be. It is premature until we do that work with our academic and other leaders on campus.

As far as student enrollment, do you feel that there is a draw, that there are enough students out there that are interested in coming; or do you think you are going to have to take a very aggressive approach to recruiting for admissions?
Our history in the 90s has been that we always have more students attempting to get in than we have space for. One of the most challenging parts of what we do at Ricks College is telling academically qualified and spiritually prepared young people that, "Yes, you could get in; but we have no space." That has been the impetus for some of our recent innovation. My projection would be that will continue to be the case.

Can you give me an outlook of what kind of student it is you are looking for and if that is going to change at all under the four-year plan?
Given no anticipated change in our admissions policy, we are looking for an energetic, academically active young person. They do not have to be the star of stars, but they need to be committed to very hard work and achieving excellence. Then BYU-Idaho is a place where they ought to be.

Is there a profile you can give of a Ricks College or BYU-Idaho student as far as test scores or GPA?
At the present time, for our students coming in for the fall it is about a 3.4 GPA and 24 on the ACT. That would be an average for the folks in our freshman class for the fall.

Do you have fear of what expansion to a four-year university might do to the culture on campus? You work very hard to create a certain image for the Ricks College campus. Now that it is expanding to a four-year university, you are going to have more culture, more diversity, stuff like that. Are you afraid of what it is going to do to the feeling, I guess, on campus among students and among faculty?
I am not afraid of that. But I am committed, as are these gentlemen and everybody else on this campus, to working very hard to make sure that does not get lost. That is the challenge of this change, and we are confident that we will be able to achieve that.

When does the name change take effect?
President Hinckley was asked that question this morning and he said, "Well, it was announced today, and we will worry about that in a day or two." So I will just use his answer. The practical answer is: It is beginning to take effect right now, but we are in a period of transition. As I made brief remarks to the assembled group in the Hart this morning, I said we should view this announcement as the initiation of a transition, not the announcement of a final product. That is why I give you so many, "Well, that's in process; we will have to evaluate." We are initiating that transition. We are certainly not done with it. [See update]

You also said this was one of the most significant events in the last 50 years. Could you expand on that? Why?
I will just speak from my own experience and then some of these gentlemen might want to participate as well. I have been the president for three years. I was coached pretty strenuously by Henry B. Eying, the commissioner of Church Education, that Ricks College would not become a four-year school. I have become pretty good in the last three years in explaining all the reasons that would not happen. So to say that this is stunning would be quite an understatement. I think if you try to imagine sitting in the position of the Presidency of the Church and look at the Church in the world and then you take a look at BYU-Hawaii, and BYU-Provo, and BYU-Idaho, what we begin to see is something very significant as we look out to the future. So I think today the historic significance of what occurred is not just what we see here and now but what it portends for there and then as we look to the future.

With the athletic program being phased out, are there other things that you will introduce as extracurricular options for the students coming here?
Yes. I need to be careful because I do not want people to think of the word "intramural" in the traditional sense. But envision a wide-ranging and extensive activity program, including athletics. It is not that athletics will go away. Intercollegiate athletics will be phased out, but there is still a need for athletic participation. That, in an innovative way, will occur. There will also be that same type of activity and involvement in performing and fine arts and in academics. A part of what happens is that we cannot have an academic program restricted to just the fall and winter semesters. As we equalize that program across the summer as well, there is a tremendous need for a balanced activity program throughout the year. That is where we are going - that level of involvement of students - not just in physical education and athletics but in a wide number of areas.

Are you worried that maybe that will deter some scholar athletes that might have considered BYU-Idaho?
Again, if what they want to do is come and compete at the intercollegiate level, this will not be the place for them.

As an educator, generally, do you see the need for a junior college to be developed in this part of the state?
For the State of Idaho? I think CSI does a very good job. As a non-native Idahoan - [regarding] the need for a two-year school in this immediate area, I honestly do not know. Any of you? I just don't know.

This appears to be in the very early stages, but do you have any preliminary goals? Do you have a ten-year plan, somewhere you want to be ten years from now?
Again, I would reiterate what I heard President Hinckley say this morning, which I resonate to and would align with perfectly. "We want to make this the finest undergraduate teaching institution it can possibly be." Again, if you would consider that some of the steps we are taking today are student focused and teaching oriented, that is who we have been. And as we move to the future, we want to become even better in achieving that objective.

The press release talks about eliminating some long-standing beneficial programs. Does that refer to athletics, or are there other areas that are less academic?
That refers to the process whereby we're going to have to ask the question, "What will it take to be the kind of high quality, four-year institution that I just described?" As we make that evaluation, we may need to make some adjustments as we also look at the offerings of all of the BYU campuses.

On a personal level for President Bednar himself, what are your feelings about this?
Well, I've gone through a series of stages from being stunned and shocked to incredibly overwhelmed and humbled. That continues. That stage has not ceased. It is one of the most exciting things I can imagine in a lifetime. To have been given this assignment and this trust from our Board of Trustees, to move forward with this task is the experience of a lifetime.

As you emerge into this four-year college, whom will you consider your peers in the academic world?
Our peers would be other teaching-oriented schools. I keep coming back to that. But many institutions, in fact most, will have a diversified mission in terms of teaching and research and service. Now, when you use the word research, that does not mean that people here would not be intellectually engaged and very active. But it has a different focus at a teaching institution. That is what we are going to be. So as you look down the road, that is the consistent focus. That part of our mission is not changing as we move from two to four years.

When students are applying to BYU-Idaho, what are the schools they are going to be looking at. What else is going to be on their checklist of schools that they want to apply to?
All I can say is other schools that would have that kind of parallel caring, teaching focus.

Can you name any?
It is a very different kind of an experience than going to a very large university where you have very large classes. Our teacher-student ratio of 25 to 1, we anticipate keeping the same.

I just want to know who you think . . .
What you want is a list.

Well, not a list. But just, who do you think your competitors are? There are tons of small, good colleges with a very clear-cut mission. Who do you want to have as your peers? Who are you going to turn to, sort of, to learn from?
I guess the reason I balk a little bit at the question and hesitate is because it's not just a matter of looking to the side or even to what we have been in the past. The real majesty of this announcement and this change is what it will mean in the future. I am so focused on that, that I have a hard time looking to the side to respond to your question.

How much of this move has to do so much with academics versus the Church at large. Is it more about the individual school or is there a larger mission?
I think it really is elements of both. I think it relates to what has matured and developed at Ricks College over a period of many years. President Hinckley said: "You need to go to this campus. You need to see what it's like. You need to look at the caliber of the faculty." The institution itself has matured and progressed in a significant way. I think that dovetails into the growth and expansion of the Church and that they are tied together through that entire CES system with all of the BYU campuses. So I think they are linked.

How does the trend work? Right now you have to have pretty high academics to get in here. Was it a 3.2?
3.4 on average.

Making it a four-year, for the students what does this mean? Does it mean harder to get in? Or, maybe, will that change? Do you have any idea?
In its origins, Ricks College was a second-chance kind of a school. In the 1960s and 1970s when you were rejected everywhere else, Ricks College was the place to come. There are scores of stories - hundreds of stories - of people who given that opportunity seized it to the fullest and now have very, very productive lives because of that educational experience. We don't want this place to become so exclusive that you have to have a 3.9 and a 34 ACT to be able to get in. We presently have policies in place to ensure that, and we will continue with those same policies.

Is there any interest in expanding the student body beyond the spiritual students who are part of the Latter-day Saints? Holy Cross, for example, is a Catholic school that has a lot of kids from all sorts of faiths. Is that a mission that Ricks or BYU-Idaho - do you want to diversify the classroom as far as faith at all?
We have never discriminated against nor discouraged such applicants. I think they rather self-select out given the mission and the nature of the school. But, certainly, we welcome those students to apply.

Would you recruit students of other faiths to come here? I just want to know if that would be a good addition? Is that a mission that you would want to have - a larger population of different students coming from all different angles.
We would welcome them.

You mentioned that you want to honor your commitments to the athletes. Does that mean anyone under scholarship here now will keep that scholarship?
It does. We are not going to pull that rug out from under them. As a part of the transition, we will be very sensitive to those who have made commitments to come. We will honor those. And if I can repeat again, in my mind for all intents and purposes, the athletic schedule that we have in place for 2000-2001 would be unaltered. The other schools in our leagues - football, basketball, baseball - should know that our anticipation is that we will be participating in those as planned.

For 2000 and 2001?
The next academic year - not the entire year - the academic year of 2000-2001.

Is there a plan on how to phase it out?
There is. Meaning that what we will have to do is assess as we go forward. Frankly, a young man or a young woman hearing this news may participate this year, have a year left on a scholarship, and say, "I'd much rather go someplace else." So the behavior and decisions of our student athletes may have an impact on the pace at which the programs are phased out.

Is there a target? ISU has been the only game in town in this area for a long time. Now you are going to be competing with them also, whether they think so or not. I am wondering if there is any kind of segment of students that currently goes to ISU that you are going to be focusing on trying to bring up here? And how do you think it is going to effect Idaho State University currently, especially with their expansion in the Idaho Falls area and things like that.
We do not have a particular profile that we target. We're just looking for students who are balanced - academically, spiritually - in a wide range of other kinds of extracurricular activities. That balance is what we are looking for. I spoke with President Bowen this morning, and we talked about the need to coordinate this action as we move forward. Frankly, the major impact will be perhaps in the number of juniors that may or may not be transferring to ISU. There is a significant number at ISU now. However, depending on how we configure our student body, that may make available to ISU more freshmen students. The net impact, at this point, is not clear and may not be as negative as one might expect. That we will have to assess and see as we move forward.

If you were the President of ISU right now, what would you be thinking then?
I think I can take the liberty of quoting Dr. Bowen. He said: "This is a great step forward for your institution. Congratulations! We look forward to working with you." I hope I would think the same thing.

This was all announced very suddenly. But, obviously, it sounds like a lot of ground work was probably laid behind closed doors. Can you explain why it did come as such a shock to everybody so suddenly? Why was it done this way?
Simply because the chairmen of the Board decided that is how to do it.

Is that how all the decisions are made here?
That is how the announcement was made.

Going back to the athletic program, you talked about the phaseout process. Is there a concern that kids who are on scholarship coming this year are going to see what is happening and decide that maybe they shouldn't use any of their eligibility at this point and go some other avenue?
Sure. That is why I said in terms of the phaseout and the timetable, "There is so much that we might anticipate." But the students themselves may say, I choose not to do that. Their decisions would have an impact on our ability to be able to fill those teams and participate. So that is a factor that we can't predict that will impact the phaseout schedule.

Is there a feeling that maybe the athletic program this year is going to be kind of working under a lame duck kind of atmosphere. Obviously, everybody here wants to compete and do well. You know, when the program is going to be over within a year, there might be a lot of dissension.
I do not think that would be characteristic of either the faculty, the coaches, or the students at Ricks College. In fact, I think we would find some incentive to compete better at the national level this year than we ever have.

You mentioned that you avidly for years have been explaining why this shouldn't be a four-year college.
Can I just jump in?

Yes.
Not that it shouldn't, but the rationale that had been explained as to why it likely wouldn't.

So can you explain some of the rationale that you offered to people and how have you been able to just move away from that? What was the rationale before, and what is the rational now?
The rationale was that in a rapidly expanding worldwide Church, the resources would be needed elsewhere; that it makes no sense to pour more and more resources into an institution that serves an ever-smaller percentage of the young people of the Church. Again, given that this is not a resource-based decision - it is more of an opportunity-based decision - given this physical plant, given some of the innovations, there is an opportunity to continue to bless more students. That is what the Board wants us to try and work with. What they have seen is a remarkable opportunity to bless people, and they are giving us the opportunity to develop a plan to achieve that objective.

So the original mission has changed then, is that correct - from the resources to an opportunity?
No, it is because it does not take a large increase in resources to make the shift. Throughout President Hinckley's remarks this morning was an indication that there will only be modest adjustments to the campus. There may be a need for a building and some other kinds of things. Those are relatively modest in the transition from two to four year. So it's not a resource-driven decision.

How about class size? Is it going to maintain that small student-teacher ratio that you mentioned?
That is our objective.

I don't know why, but this seems to be important. What's your shoe size?
A 9 1/2. That was one of the questions to President Hinckley today, and I have no idea where that came from. Anything else?

Let me conclude with this final observation. Thank you for coming. I indicated to our assembly this morning in the Hart Auditorium that as these evaluations are made, as the plans are formulated, and as we have information to share, we will be very forthcoming with that. So you can anticipate more information at the appropriate time. Thank you very much for being here this afternoon.
