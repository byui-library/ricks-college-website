Ricks College News Release
April 28, 2001

Emeritus Club honors two alumni couples

REXBURG, Idaho — The Ricks College Emeritus Club honored two alumni couples at its semi-annual breakfast Saturday, April 28..

Distinguished emeritus service awards will be presented by the Ricks College Alumni Association to William F. and Velda Campbell Lye of Logan, Utah, and Richard C. and Barbara Hegsted Rasmussen of Battle Creek, Mich.

The Rasmussens met at Ricks College in the 1935 production of "The Mikado.” They were also in a German class together. William graduated from Ricks in 1937.

Richard is a native of the Rexburg area and says, “I had hardly been outside Madison County, except to punch cows in Kilgore.” His father, Aaron, or "Happy” as he was more commonly known, was a basketball coach and teacher at Ricks Academy in 1912 and 1913.

Barbara Hegsted is from the Salem area.

He received his bachelor’s degree and a teaching certificate from the University of Utah. At the time he was an aspiring actor and appeared in many student productions. He taught in the Rigby High School for two years. 

Richard served for 40 years as a civil servant in the federal government with duties as director in adult education; as a 30-year veteran of the U.S. Navy and Navy Reserves, rising to the rank of a captain with active duty in the Korean conflict; as a former high school teacher following graduation from the University of Utah; and was a lifelong supporter of Ricks College.

Barbara was a homemaker and served in many church positions. They have been married for 63 years, raising three daughters and have eight grandchildren and nine great-grandchildren.

The Rasmussens were named Honorary Mayors of Battle Creek, Mich., for one day, March 23 of this year. They have been married for 63 years and are in the process of moving from Battle Creek to the Orem, Utah, area.

William Lye, a native of British Columbia, attended Ricks in 1953 and later in 1956. He graduated from Utah State University in history in 1959 and later earned a master’s degree at the University of California at Berkeley.

"When I earned my M.A. degree, President John L. Clarke (of Ricks) appointed me to the faculty though he had no openings (in 1959). By the time I arrived, he fit me into an open slot in my minor field, political science, and assigned me to also teach history, religion, English and geography, appointed me coach of the debate team and moderator of a weekly radio program. As a faculty member I taught 25 different courses, including upper division work at the BYU/Ricks Center – all of it an incredible preparation for my return to graduate school.” He left in 1963 but returned for one more year of teaching at Ricks in 1967 to 1968.

He later began work on his Ph.D. at Duke University and completed it in 1969 at UCLA in African history and ethnography. He taught at Utah State from 1968 until retirement in 1995 and is now an emeritus faculty member there.

He provided humanitarian service to the people of Africa in Sudan and Lesotho and served as chairman of the Cache Valley chapter of Habitat for Humanity. 

Velda Lye, originally from Ammon, has been a homemaker, church worker, had an interest in genealogy and served with the Daughters of Utah Pioneers. They have been married for 47 years and have five living children and 10 grandchildren.
