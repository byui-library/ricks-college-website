Prosecutors want death sentence if ex-boyfriend convicted

IDAHO FALLS, Idaho (AP) –– Prosecutors have served a notice that they will seek the death penalty if the ex-fiance of Melissa Mays Garcia is convicted of killing her.

But Bonneville County suffered a setback in its case against Todd Robert Briggs when 7th District Judge Gregory Anderson ordered a new preliminary hearing to determine whether Briggs should even be tried for the October slaying.

Anderson voided the earlier certification for Briggs to be tried because the Idaho Falls man had not been represented by a public defender with experience in death penalty cases as the State Supreme Court has directed.

Prosecutor Dane Watkins, Jr., opted for a possible death sentence after discussing the case with Garcia’s family.

No date was immediately sent for the new preliminary hearing. New defense attorneys have been appointed.

Briggs, 40, has been in the Bonneville County Jail since he was captured after leading police on a four-county chase following Garcia’s shooting.

Briggs allegedly shot the 25-year-old woman in the heart because she had broken off their engagement, Authorities said.

The couple had shared an apartment, but in October Garcia told Briggs to move out because she had been reconciling with her ex-husband, police said.

Briggs apparently fled to Nevada and did not resurface in Idaho Falls until that Sunday morning, when he sneaked into her parents’ home and killed Garcia as she started to apologize for breaking off their engagement, Officials said.

During his flight after the killing, Briggs wounded a college coed while in Rexburg. He is charged with aggravated battery in that incident.
