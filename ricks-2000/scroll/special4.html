Avoid accidents by driving safely in snow

by Mac Markstaller
mar95004@byui.edu
Scroll Staff

10 Tips for Driving in Winter Conditions

1. Drive slowly around corners.

2. Increase stopping distance.

3. Understand that four-wheel-drive does not improve stopping chances.

4. Turn the sterring wheel the direction the car is sliding.

5. Stay in the middle of the road.

6. When stuck, turn car wheels left and right to clear snow.

7. Do not let the car tires spin after car becomes stuck.

8. Don't stop on a hill.

10. Pump the car brakes when the car is sliding.
Cars in a ditch on the side of the road. Cars in parking lots covered with a few feet of snow.

These are common sights for those who have experienced winter in Rexburg.

Many students at BYU-Idaho have never driven in snow. This can create a dangerous situation if drivers are not informed of the precautions required for driving safely in the snow. Here are a few tips:
• Drive slowly around corners. The posted speed limit may be 25 miles per hour, but the practical speed limit may be 10 to 15 miles per hour because of icy conditions.

• Increase stopping distance. “Your tires have 0.7 pounds of co-efficient of friction verses the car’s weight on dry pavement. When there is snow or ice on the ground, your tires have 0.5 pounds of co-efficient of friction verses the car’s weight. This translates in the car needing 100 to 125 feet to stop on ice or snow verses 25 feet on dry pavement,” Tom Leeman, BYU-I campus safety officer, said.

• Four-wheel-drive does not make driving on ice and snow just like driving on dry ground. It does not help a car to stop; it only helps it get into motion more quickly.

• When sliding in the snow, turn the steering wheel the direction the car is sliding. “If the car slides to the right, turn right. If the car slides left, turn left” Leeman said.

• Stay in the middle of the road. If a car drives on the shoulder of the road, where people have not driven, it is easier to get stuck. If the car does get stuck, turn the wheels right and left to clear snow out of the car’s path. Do not spin the tires. Rocking the vehicle can also improve chances of getting out.

• Don’t stop on a hill. With icy and snowy conditions, the car may get stuck.

• Pump the brakes when sliding. Otherwise, they may lock up, causing the car to increase in speed.

Sources: Rexburg City Police, BYU-I Campus Police and Tom Leeman, BYU-I campus safety officer.
