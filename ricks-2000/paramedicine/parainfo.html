Paramedic Program

Ricks College


The Paramedic Program is a 12 month program designed to train students in prehospital emergency care as a mobile intesive care paramedic.  The Associate Degree in Paramedicine, when completed, offers the student National Registry certification as an EMT-Paramedic and a certificate of course completion.  The certification program includes two semesters of class work and one internship. Students must be accepted for admission at Ricks College in order to be accepted by the Paramedic Program.  Admission to the College does not mean automatic admission to the Paramedic Program. RICKS COLLEGE ADMISSION PROCEDURE
Admission to Ricks College is awarded by application.  Applications can be obtained from the Ricks College Admissions Office.  (208) 356-1001 or apply online.

Data required includes:

  1. The results of the American College Test (ACT).  If you have completed at least 24 credits of college course work, you need not submit the ACT profile.
  2. Scholastic record of all previous high school and college course work including GPA.

A completed Ricks application must be on file in the Ricks College Admissions Office by February 15.

Applications to the Paramedic Program must be received by February 15 for acceptance into the Paramedic Program fall semester of the following academic year.

SELECTION of CANDIDATES for the PARAMEDIC PROGRAM
Candidates are ranked according to GPA and ACT profile, however, this is not the deciding factor.  Letters of recommendation are considered and also pertinent information which the candidate should present in a personal letter of application.  A personal interview will be arranged during March.  This is a major importance in determining acceptance to the program.

CANDIDATE SELECTION STANDARDS
THE EMT-PARAMEDIC CANDIDATE SHALL BE:
  1.   At least 18 years of age
  2.   A high school graduate or equivalent
  3.   Able to speak, read, and write English
  4.   Be free of addiction to alcohol or drugs
  5.   Physically and mentally capable of performing the tasks required in emergency work as an EMT-Paramedic

The candidate must have satisfied the prerequisites for entering the program or be in the process of taking the required classes.

PROGRAM STRUCTURE

The student receives basic and advanced training in the following areas:
 

HOSPITAL CLINICAL
Hospital clinical training is performed at Eastern Idaho Regional Medical Center.  Clinical training includes rotations performed on 8-12 hour shifts.  Hospital clinical includes the following: Emergency Room, Operating Room/Anesthesia, ECG, IV Therapy team, Recovery Room/Post Anesthesia, Intensive Care Unit(ICU)/Coronary Care Unit(CCU), Pediatrics and Neonatology, Obstetrics/Labor and Delivery, Psychiatric Health, and Geriatrics and Rehabilitation.

The first year material will be prerequisite to the Paramedic Program and will contain all General Education required.  Special certifications may be acquired through community or Continuing Education credit locally or with other ambulance services.  This material may be acquired during the Paramedic year but it will be beneficial to you to acquire these certifications before beginning the Paramedic course work.

The Specialized Associate Degree has made a great deal of difference in the placement of our students.  Some states are beginning to require an Associate Degree in order for the student to be a Paramedic (i.e. Oregon, South Carolina, etc.). With the Associate Degree, many or our students go on to management positions.  Also, it is very helpful in assisting them to continue their education which we suggest strongly.

CAREER OUTLOOK
The new graduate can function as a Paramedic in any state that accepts D.O.T. training and National Registry Certification.  Because of current Paramedic shortages in most major cities, graduates have many choices, both as to geographical location and work preference.

PARAMEDICINE

EMPLOYMENT OPPORTUNITIES

Students graduating in Paramedicine and satisfactorily completing the National Registry Paramedic Exam have tremendous opportunity for immediate employment.

    PAY SCALES IN CITY AREAS INCLUDE:
        Paramedic starting salaries $21,000 per year up to $54,000 per year
          Crew Chiefs or Shift Supervisor's earn $30,000 to $75,000

    BENEFITS:(Benefits vary according to service but many are listed below)

  • Paid vacations
  • Holiday shift pay
  • Preceptor pay
  • Paid CE(continuing education)
  • Credit Union
  • PPTO (Paid Personal Time Off)
  • Medical, Dental, Vision and Life Insurance (Many health insurance plans for Paramedics pay 100%)
  • Retirement savings plan with employer contributions
  • Relocation allowance
  • Uniforms are usually provided

    PARAMEDICINE OFFERS:

Large Service Area:
Paramedic positions offer a variety of call volumes and patterns as well as varied geographic and demographic work locations.  Services offer varied work assignments designed with the employee in mind to provide a compatible, flexible and enjoyable work pattern.

Time:

Shifts can be 24 hours on 48 hours off; 10 hours, 4 days/week: or 13 hours, 3 days per week: or 8 hours 5 days/week. Shifts vary according to the service.

    MANAGEMENT POSITIONS:

Paramedics often have opportunites to increase their status in the system by a change of position and pay increase according to the degree of training.  Number of years in the field causes pay scales to increase by yearly increments.  Associates degrees or Bachelor's degrees enhance the possibility of obtaining management positions and often bring an immediate 1% to 4% increase in pay.  Some language experience often increases wages upon hiring usually around 1%.

    MANAGEMENT SUPPORT:

 
Many services have supervisors available 24 hours/day who respond to personal or professional problems and are extemely supportive of the paramedic.

    PLACEMENT:

Ricks College Students completing their program at Ricks, often move into positions as they finish their internships.  Currently, 1991 to 1996, 85% of Paramedic students who certified at Ricks were hired into Paramedic positions with major ambulance services or are continuing their education in the medical field.
