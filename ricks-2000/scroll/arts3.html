Choirs perform in Harvest Choirfest

by Peter Jones
sel00001@byui.edu
Scroll Staff

Nina Mangus, a sophomore from Richmond, Va., plans on haunting as the Lady of Liberty this Halloween.
Wonderful, great and amazing were a few of the comments people used to describe the Harvest Choirfest that took place Friday night in the Barrus Concert Hall.

The program started with the Men’s and Women’s Choirs combined performing Ron Staheli’s “A World About to Turn.” Women’s Choir, conducted by Eda Ashby, and accompanied by Devin Thetford and Chelsi Christenson, then sang “O Aula Nobilis” by William Mathias, followed by “Alleluia,” from “Place of the Blest” by Randall Thompson.

Next, Women’s Choir performed three mountain songs: “Lift Thine Eyes” by Felix Mendelssohn, “Carry On” by Janice Thompson and “Mountain Anthem” by Sue Neimoyer.

Collegiate Singers, conducted by Kevin Brower and accompanied by Holly Jeppesen, took the stage next. They displayed their musical talent by performing “A Prayer of the Middle Ages” by Howard Hanson, “O Magnum Mysterium” by Morten Lauridsen and “Go Where I Send Thee,” arranged by Andre Thomas.

Following Collegiate Singers, Men’s Choir, conducted by Randall Kempton and accompanied by Louisa Albrect, performed songs in a tribute to BYU-Idaho. They sang humorous, heart-warming tunes such as “Brothers, Sing On!” by Edvard Grieg, “You’ve Got to be a Football Hero” by Sherman/Fields/Lewis and arranged by Baird and “The Weed” by Ken Hicken. The choir proceeded to set a more reverent tone by singing “If Music Be the Food of Love” by David Dickau and“More Holiness Give Me,” arranged by Ron Staheli.

For the finale, the combined choirs with the Chamber Brass and Percussion performed Ron Staheli’s “Come We That Love the Lord” and Mack Wilberg’s arrangement of “Come Thou Fount of Every Blessing.”

Those who attended the choirfest seemed to enjoy the talent being displayed, Sean Hanberg, a sophomore from Brentwood, Calif., said.

“There were many times that the performers got a standing ovation, and all the people that I talked to said that they really enjoyed the show,” Hanberg said.

“The Harvest Choirfest is always a great concert. There is something there to interest everybody,” Kempton said.
