The Transition: Letter to students

June 30, 2000

Dear Student:

I am sure you are aware of last week's historic announcement about Ricks College becoming a four-year institution and having its name changed to BYU-Idaho. What an exciting time to attend school here! I am glad to know you will be part of this wonderful season of transition. I also anticipate that you may have some questions and hope the following information will alleviate your concerns.

Ricks College has long been recognized as a school where students receive personal, individualized attention. Some prospective students are wondering if that tradition will continue. At the press conference last week, Elder Eyring indicated that ". . . the character of the school will remain very much the teaching-oriented, student-oriented institution it has always been." I, too, want to assure you that as this transition takes place, one of our top priorities will be to preserve and enhance the caring environment that has always existed on this campus.

Many students are wondering if they will be able to finish their planned course of study and receive an associate degree. The answer is yes. It is important to remember that what President Hinckley announced was the beginning of a transition process, not a finished product. He said, "There are many, many details to be worked out. It isn't going to happen in a day. It will take some time." Your academic advisor will be able to help you develop a curriculum plan that will allow you to reach your academic goals; please do not hesitate to use him/her as a valuable resource.

At this point in time, decisions have not been made relative to what four-year degrees will be offered and when those degrees will become available. It may be that your specific program will be one of the first implemented, thereby giving you the opportunity to graduate with a baccalaureate degree from BYU-Idaho. If not, as indicated above, you can earn an associate degree and transfer to another four-year institution. During the transition period ahead, we do not anticipate any major changes to the articulation agreements that are already in place with other schools throughout Idaho and Utah.

You might also be interested in knowing that tuition and fees for the 2000-2001 academic year will remain at the levels already published in the current catalog.

Again, what an exciting time to be a student at Ricks College/BYU-Idaho. In a few days, you will be able to find additional information about this important change on the Internet at www.ricks.edu/pr/byu-i. If you have further questions after perusing that information, please do not hesitate to contact the Public Relations Office at (208) 356-1150.

Sincerely,

David A. Bednar
President
