Appendix B

A College-level Reading Strategy

Survey

Take a few minutes to explore the text. Read the beginning and the end. Then guess at an answer to these questions.

 Purpose Why is the writer writing? Is the writer writing to explain?
Is the writer writing to argue or persuade?
Topic What is the writer writing about?
Issue What is the central question the writer addresses?
Conclusion (Therefore?) How does the writer answer the central issue question?

What is the main point the writer makes?
Reasons and Information (Because?) If the writer’s purpose is to argue or persuade, what reasons does the writer give to support the conclusion?
If the writer’s purpose is to explain, what information does the writer provide to make the main point clear?
Read

Read the text at a comfortable pace for high comprehension. As you read, repeat this procedure for each sub-section.

 Underline Which of the writer’s ideas are most important? (Be selective; underline major points.)
Paraphrase How can I express these most important ideas in my own words? (Re-state what you have underlined in brief marginal notes.)
Vocabulary What words are essential to my understanding the writer’s most important points? (Circle these words and write definitions in marginal notes.)
Annotations How can I keep track of personal reactions to what I read? (Use ? to note questions you have about what you read; use ! to note ideas you react strongly to; use * to note ideas you particularly want to remember; and so on.)
"PTIC" & Block

Take a moment to reassess the writer’s purpose, topic, issue, and conclusion. Then divide the text into its logical sections and map it, that is, draw a diagram that shows how all the parts fit together. Be creative!

 Map Now that you have read carefully the text, re-ask the key survey questions:
P Why is the writer writing?
T What is the writer writing about?
I What is the central question the writer addresses?
C How does the writer answer the central issue question?
R What are the writer’s reasons?
Review

Review your marginal notes for retention.

 Recall (To hold key ideas in long-term memory, re-read your PTIC and marginal notes often; read "smart" the first time and you’ll avoid having to re-read full texts when you review.)
