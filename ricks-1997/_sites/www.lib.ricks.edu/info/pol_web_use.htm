Internet Access Procedures in the David O. McKay Library

When a patron comes into the library and requests access to the Internet, we conduct a mini-interview with the patron to determine how we can best be of service. Here are the most common scenarios:

Scenario A: Patron needs to find encyclopedia, magazine or newspaper articles for research.

Response: We show the patron the subscription databases they can access from the "Internet Resources" page, available on any computer in the library. Many of these resources can also be accessed from their apartment, if they have Internet service. Depending on the subject of interest, we also direct them to traditional (non-electronic) research resources which may be as good as or better than the electronic tools they have requested.

Scenario B: Patron needs to access information their teacher has made available for them to view.

Response: If we determine that the information is actually on (paper) reserve, we direct them to the Reserve Desk. If the information is on Electronic Reserve or on the teacher’s faculty Web page, we show the patron how to locate the information from the "Internet Resources" page from any computer in the library (or from their apartment).

Scenario C: Patron needs to access specific Web sites assigned by their teacher for a particular class.

Response: Ideally, the teacher will have contacted the library in advance and provided us with the URL’s the students will need to access. If so, we simply direct the student to the Electronic Reserve page as in Scenario B. If not, we scramble to add the Web sites to the Electronic Reserve page as soon as possible, so students can access the sites on any computer using the default Web browser.

Scenario D: Patron needs to search for Internet sites containing specific research-related information.

Response: We give them access to an "open" Internet browser, where they can type in a specific Web address, use search engines, etc., with a 30 minute time limit. We provide them with tips for efficient searching if needed. This level of access is available only in the Reference area on the first floor of the library. This allows us to provide maximum levels of support and at the same time, to manage open Web usage.

Scenario E: Patron wants to use the Internet to access their Hotmail account, spend time in chat rooms, do online shopping, play online games, etc.

Response: We direct them to the Games Center in the Manwaring Center.

Our underlying goal in the decisions we have made relative to Internet access in the Library is to aggressively support the needs of students and faculty with the resources available to us. To this end, we have developed a customized version of Internet Explorer, which is the default Web browser in the Library. This browser does not contain an "Address" window, so the student cannot type in a URL. This allows us to focus their Internet usage on the research-related sites accessible through the "Internet Resources" page, and to prevent them from accessing sites which are not relevant to research. This simple tool makes it possible for us to easily manage our resources and to serve the research needs of 8,600 students with only 300 computers in the library. This simply would not be possible if all library computers had open Internet access.
