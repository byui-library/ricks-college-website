OLYMPIC UPDATE-ALUMNUS WINS GOLD

Rulon Gardner won the gold medal in an upset victory over the previously unbeaten legend, three-time Olympic wrestling champion Aleksandr Karelin of Russia. In a nine-minute duel Karelin finally admitted his first defeat in thirteen years, and the official raised Gardner's hand in triumph with a score 1-0.

Gardner is the only Olympic athlete born and raised in the United States that has gone to Ricks College (1989-91). A native of Afton, Wyoming, Gardner attended Ricks and won the NJCAA heavyweight competition. As an Olympian, he competed in the Greco-Roman 286 pound (130 kg) division. He earned the opportunity to be on the U.S. team by defeating the legendary Matt Gharitti, Olympic silver medalist in 1996. It is the first time in three Olympics that Gharitti failed to make the team. In the elimination round in Sydney, Gardner was successful with a 7-2 victory over Omrane Ayari of Tunisia and 6-0 over Haykaz Galstyan of Armenia. In the next round, Gardner defeated Giuseppe Giunta of Italy by a 2-1 overtime referee's decision. With the win, Gardner advanced to the semifinals where he faced Yuri Jevseijtchik of Israel and won the match 3-2. (For more information: http://sportsillustrated.cnn.com/olympics/ or http://www.nbcolympics.com)

Ricks College has two alumni competing in the Olympics in Sydney. Both were outstanding athletes while at Ricks and became champions in finals of the National Junior College Athletics Association.

Marsha Mark Beard has completed the heptathlon. Competing for her native Trinidad, she ended the seven events with a score of 5627 and in 22nd place (compared with U.S. athletes Dedde Nathan at 9th with 6150 and Sheila Burrell at 26th with 5345). Gold went to Denise Lewis of Great Britain with 6584.

SERVICE A COMPONENT OF EDUCATION

"The purpose of Service Week was to embark in service and focused on the words heart, might, mind and strength," said ASRC officer Kayla Anderson, who spearheaded the event at Ricks College. A human chain of students strung throughout the library was the highlight of this year's Service Week. Tom Liau, director of the David O. McKay Library, said it wasn't very quiet in the library on Saturday. "It was lots of fun," he said of the approximate 800 students who showed up to help on their day off from school. He estimated the volunteers moved about 30,000 books while the library staff finished moving about 20,000 more books on Monday. Liau said they hope to open the East Wing about October 15. In the meantime if a patron or student needs a book that is located in the closed area, library employees will assist them.

Guitars Unplugged was another big success for the ASRC. Doug Smith, an advisor, said the event seems to get bigger and bigger every year. The event spans three nights, and on Saturday night drew a near-capacity crowd to the Hart. While donations have not been totaled, Smith said a similar event last year raised $8,000 for charity. Proceeds from this fall's Guitars Unplugged will be given to Upper Valley United Way.

FORUM SPEAKER DEFENDS FAMILY

A man who is rallying people to defend the traditional family spoke of the importance of this mission at the monthly Major Forum at Ricks College. Richard Wilkins, a law professor at Brigham Young University, is one of the organizers of last year's Second World Congress of Families in Geneva, Switzerland. He said the definitions of family and marriage are in danger of being redefined by international forces. He warned of forces that are attempting to diminish the traditional importance of the role of motherhood, advocate the legalization of homosexuality and same-sex marriage, and attack the importance of religious faith.

Wilkins said scientific research demonstrates that stable marital and familial relationships are vital to social progress. Research has documented that natural family structures benefit nearly every aspect of well-being for men women and children. "This includes greater educational opportunities, better emotional and physical health, less substance abuse, lower incidences of early sexual activity for girls, and less delinquency for boys. In short, stable marital unions promote the health, safety and social progress of women, men and children. Unstable marital relations promote poverty, crime, abuse, and social disintegration."

Wilkins said the LDS Church's proclamation on the family issued in 1995 is a warning to the people of the world. "We have an obligation to ensure that the world hears and understands the truths stated in that proclamation," he said. "As we raise our voices to confound the strong and mighty, we must not be ashamed to stand up for what some might deride as old-fashioned values."
