15-PASSENGER VANS FOUND TO BE SAFETY HAZARD

A new campus policy limits the number of passengers in campus vans to nine. The decision came from the NHTSA's finding that
15-passenger vans with 10 or more occupants had a rollover rate in single vehicle crashes that is nearly three times the rate of those that were lightly loaded.

James Ricks · Scroll

by Amanda Hansen
scroll staff


Fifteen-passenger vans are no longer allowed to leave the Ricks College campus fully loaded. On April 9, the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration(NHTSA) released an advisory stating that as the number of occupants in a 15-passenger van increases, the risk of rollover rises dramatically.

On May, 14 the Ricks Presidents Council decided that no more than nine people, including the driver, would be allowed to travel in the vans. To make sure that the new rule would be complied with, they instructed two seats to be removed from every van.

While ensuring the safety of campus students, the new rules are proving the be inconvenient for several organizations that must now schedule extra transportation for their activities.

However inconvenient the new rules are, they appear to be necessary for safety. According to NHTSA, vans with 10 or more occupants had a rollover rate in single vehicle crashes that is nearly three times the rate of those that were lightly loaded. Unlike light trucks or other similar vehicles, 15-passenger vans have a large payload capacity, and the occupants sit high up in the vehicles. NHTSA's analysis revealed that as more and more weight was added to the vans, the center of gravity shifted rearward and upward, increasing the likelihood of a rollover or loss of control in panic maneuvers.

NHTSA's research also reinforced the value of seat belts, stating that 80 percent of the deaths that occured from rollovers last year happened to individuals who were not wearing their seat belts.

In addition to removing two seats from every van, the Presidents Council is also cracking down on seat-belt wearing, tagging each van with a sticker that will state the seat belt requirement.

Programs such as Discovery that left the campus before the new rules were in place will be provided with more vehicles so they can be in compliance with the nine-passenger-per-vehicle requirement. The extra vehicles will be sent to each location where these programs are being conducted.

Departments have been told that they should overspend their budgets as needed to obtain the extra vehicles they need to meet the requirements of the new rules.
