SPORI BLAZES

Nearly a century ago, construction of a new classroom and administration building for the Bannock Stake Academy blazed the trail for educational commitment in the Upper Snake River Valley. The three-story building used rocks hauled by horses from a nearby quarry and was painstakingly constructed on the hill overlooking the budding community of Rexburg. The building became a historic icon and in 1964 was renamed the Jacob Spori Building in honor of the first principal at the academy.

In the wee hours of November 29, the Spori blazed once again -- an unforeseen fiery conclusion. On the scene by assignment to make an historic record of the demolition scheduled to begin at midnight, the college's press relations coordinator, Don Sparhawk, pondered if perhaps the fire was the building's way of saying goodbye in a more fitting manner than being pulled apart stone by stone. Photos of the demolition and subsequent fire were captured by the college photographer, Mike Lewis, and will be kept in an album in the Public Relations Office.

Much like the pillars of smoke pouring from the burning edifice, the impact from the final blaze has drifted far from the local community. Reporters called from news media throughout the Intermountain West and there has been substantial increase in the traffic to the college's web site. It seems the fire gave people one more chance to reflect on their memories that will continue to burn warm within the hearts.

Soon a new building will be constructed on the same location. It also will be based in a solid foundation of education, and it will inherit the name of the Jacob Spori Building. It is destined to become yet another image recognized for commitment to new generations as educators blaze trails of discovery in an era of expanding horizons.

VIKINGS WIN REAL DAIRY BOWL

Collegiate rivalry and a spirited drive for revenge of a former loss were key ingredients leading to a Viking victory over the Snow College Badgers in the 14th annual Real Dairy Bowl. The two squads faced each other in the contest sponsored by the United Dairymen of Idaho and held at Holt Arena on the campus of Idaho State University in Pocatello. After a close battle in the first half, Ricks College's game plan came together for a sweet 45-14 victory.

Ricks was inside the 30 yard twice in the first half of the first quarter but couldn't score. The Badgers drew first blood, facing 3rd and 22, Snow quarterback Casey Rehrer tossed a 25 yard TD pass to Aaron Boone and Snow led 7-0. Ricks was quick to respond, driving 85 yards and scoring on a 6 yard pass from Marc Dunn to Spencer Nead. Ricks missed the PAT and Snow led 7-6. Taking possession with four minutes left in the half, Ricks put together an 86 yard drive, capped by Dunn's 32 yard touchdown toss to Ben Holloway, and the Vikings led 14-7 at the half.

Snow opened the second half with two turnovers, the first a fumble on the kickoff return, but the Vikings failed to convert and had to punt. Two plays later Nick May picked off a Rehrer pass. It was first and goal at the nine. Snow held and forced a field goal attempt which went wide, but was flagged for roughing the kicker. Two plays later Brantley Oakley sprinted in from the one and Ricks led 30-7. Snow responded with an 80 yard drive, Landon Beus finishing it off with a five yard TD run and Ricks' lead was 20-14.

Ricks finished the Badgers off with 24 unanswered points. Reserve running back Scott Thompson scored twice on the ground. Doug Robinson took a screen pass 40 yards for a TD, and Blake Peterson added a 20 yard field goal. Key stats: Snow had five turnovers to one for the Vikings. Ricks had 162 yards rushing in the game. Ricks had negative 36 yards rushing against Snow during the regular season meeting between the two. All American QB Marc Dunn threw for 350 yards as Ricks totaled 518 yards in offense.


LADY VIKING NAMED ALL AMERICAN

Ricks College sophomore Trisha Pettingill has been named a Volleyball First Team All American by the NJCAA. She becomes the ninth Lady Viking so honored since 1980. Pettingill helped lead the Vikings through a 40-10 season, finishing third in the tough Scenic West Athletic Conference, a conference so tough that member Southern Idaho won the NJCAA championship, and member Utah Valley State took third. Ricks beat both of those teams during the regular season. Pettingill led the team in sets and serving, and was third in defense. She was co-captain of the team. Trisha is a sophomore from more Bonneville High School in Idaho Falls, Idaho. She was starting setter her freshman year in 1999 for Coach JoAnn Reeve. As a freshman she was second team All SWAC. As a sophomore she earned First Team All Conference honors and was named to the Region 18 All Tournament team. Her parents are Brad and Sherisse Pettingill.
