We asked our student leaders on the NSO Team to share their experiences in leaving home and coming to BYU-Idaho.

The students shared with us their feelings and concerns in starting school, making friends, and adjusting to college life.  

The students also shared with us what BYU-Idaho has done for them and offered advice to all incoming students.

 a       
             
             
   

 

     

What Was It Like To Leave Home And Come To BYU-I?

I was nervous about finding new friends, getting along with my roommates, and whether I had chosen the right classes.  I was also a little lost as to where to go to find a grocery store, bank, a job, the post office and other things in the community.

Nolan Perry

 


I have learned that BYU-Idaho offers so much to help me in my academic career and also spiritually.  There is such a strong spirit that surrounds this university and I can feel it everywhere I go.  I have also learned how important an education is and to take advantage of it.  Working hard and having fun have made my experience here at BYU-Idaho a great one!

Kim Clinger

What did you experience during the first few weeks of school?

I must admit that I was a little homesick.  The first week was the toughest.  I had to find my way around and get used to new people.  I was totally overwhelmed and thought that I wouldn't be able to handle everything that my professor's expected.   It got better though, honest!!!.

Becky Jepson

What BYU-Idaho has done for me?


I really love BYU-Idaho, now.  I wasn't sure before that I should come here but I made the decision and that choice has been a good one for me.  My experience here has been invaluable to me and my life.  

Charla Dietzel

What are your feelings about BYU-Idaho?

"My feelings for BYU-Idaho have definitely changed.  I believe that this school is constantly filled with the spirit.  No matter what stumbling block I come upon, whether it be academic, social, or spiritual, it can be conquered with the help of the Lord."

Becca Dumont

Advice to incoming students # 1.

"Arrive early and take advantage of all the campus orientation activities.  I never participated in these events and now wonder if they would have helped me feel more at ease.  Find where your classes are before the first day.   Finally, give everyone you meet a chance, you never know how much fun you can have when meeting new people."

Andrea Hooper

Advice to incoming students # 2.

"Use all the available resources on campus.  Everyone is more than willing to help you find your way around, but you have to ask questions."

Gage Putman
