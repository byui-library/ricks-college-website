PERFORMANCE TOURS ARE EXTENDING THE INFLUENCE

The influence of youth with a common purpose is being extended beyond the Ricks College campus through several tour groups. Each year with the approval of the Church Educational System and the Board of Trustees, Ricks College performers share their talents and enthusiasm for life. Five Ricks College performance groups are touring this year. The tour experience gives students higher expectations for themselves as they fulfill the role of being goodwill ambassadors. In addition to full evening performances, numerous mini-performances are held during the day at schools, malls, and major tourist attractions. The tour program helps create a bridge of better understanding as others come to understand that as Latter-day Saints "if there is anything lovely, or of good report, or praiseworthy, we seek after these things."

Extravadance is receiving enthusiastic response from its hosts as it tours stateside for the first time in a decade. The enthusiastic, skilled performance of the 36 ballroom and contemporary dancers are bringing rounds of applause from the audiences and the sponsors in the eastern states. Elliott Myers, the coordinator for the performances in Waycross, Georgia, declared the performance to be "twice as good as anything they have seen on Broadway. There are not enough compliments that can be said about them."

The 92 students in Collegiate Singers and Chamber Orchestra have just completed a two-week tour through the Pacific Northwest with "Expressions of Faith." Sander Larson, president of the Washington Seattle Mission, said the concert was "a touch of class. Beautiful music and talented soloists . . . made for a marvelous event." While presenting what some have classified as a "spiritual feast," the student performers add to their own depths of understanding which will help them face the yet-to-come challenges of life.

Showtime Company's performance carries a special spirit with them that encourages many as they learn to deal with life's "Lessons of Love." After seeing the show and being one of the host families in Three Lakes, Wisconsin, Chris Cunningham said, "When you have a lot of wonderful youth here . . . they give you the strength to go on again. Thanks for the work and the love. . . How thankful we are for the people they are."

The Symphonic Band toured in Montana and Idaho during the winter semester. The American Folk Dancers and Bluegrass Band have a tour scheduled to Spain and Portugal in July. The influence continues to be extended. The performance tour program has recently been approved for funding through the Endowment for the Performing and Fine Arts. SUMMER IN THE SNOW ENTERTAINMENT SERIES

Ricks College is gearing up for an expanded series of summer shows as part of its Summer in the Snow entertainment series in Rexburg. This year's series will include a variety of summertime concerts, several dinners, folk dancers, a musical revue by the theater department and even some history of the Oregon Trail. "We have been working hard to expand and improve our summer series," says Summer in the Snow coordinator Don Sparhawk. "We have tried to plan something most every Thursday evening during the summer."

Due to the popularity of a barbecue dinner held last year, Ricks is featuring three dinner shows this summer. "Many people - especially Sunbirds - liked the idea of having a nice dinner prior to a show," Sparhawk says. Tickets for paid events will go on sale beginning May 15 at the Ricks College Ticket Office. The office is open weekdays while school is in session from 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. For more information or to order tickets, call (208) 356-2230 or go online at www.byui.edu/tickets. IDAHO SUPREME COURT HOLDS SESSIONS AT RICKS COLLEGE

The Idaho Supreme Court heard three appellate cases of local interest at an out-of-town session held at Ricks College on May 3. The Court was presided over by Chief Justice Linda Copple Trout. These cases heard were: State of Idaho v. Michael E. Hosey appealing denial to withdraw guilty plea to charges of trafficking in methamphetamine. State of Idaho v. Robby Joe Mowrey, appealing felony judgement of lewd conduct with a minor child under age sixteen. Sondra Olson v. EG&G Idaho, Inc., and Constance C. Blackwood, an appeal by Sondra Olson contending that the district court erred in granting summary judgment on her claims of wrongful discharge, self-defamation, and age and disability discrimination when she was terminated from her position with EG&G in 1993.

These hearings provided a unique opportunity to see cases brought before the Idaho Supreme Court. The court sessions were open to the public. Each side was given 30 minutes to present their arguments to the Court. The Court now takes the cases under advisement. The average length of time before a written opinion to the issue comes back from the Court is about 90-120 days. The opinions are sent to the respective parties through their counsel, distributed to the trial judge, and copies distributed to the news media. The opinions are also posted on the Internet at www.state.id.us/judicial/judicial.html.

ASHTON FAMILY TO PRESENT RECITAL

Ted Ashton and his five children will be featured in a recital Friday, May 19, at 7:30 p.m. in the Snow Recital Hall. The recital is free to the public. They will be accompanied by another Ricks faculty member, Steve Allen. This family of string players will perform a variety of music ranging from Beethoven to Celtic music. Brother Ashton is a member of the music faculty where he teaches violin and directs one of the orchestras on campus. He also teaches music and the humanities and is a member of the faculty string ensemble, Serenata.
