Conceptions about women improve through the ages

by Janielle Gurney
GUR01003@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Historically, woman have been thought of as inferior to men and as a source of temptation and evil.

In Greek mythology it was a woman, Pandora, who opened the forbidden box and brought plagues and unhappiness to mankind. Early Roman law described women as children, forever inferior to men.

At the start of the 19th century female professions were limited to factory labor and domestic work. Now women are doctors, lawyers, accountants, preachers, writers and much more.

World War II had a profound impact on the accomplishment of women in the work force. With men fighting in the war, many wives and daughters had to take on husbands’ and fathers’ employment. Over 300,000 women began working for the Navy and Army, according to www.wic.org.

As the years pass, and specifically at the end of the 20th century, the thought of a woman being inferior to men has become extinct. Woman are increasingly carrying on the same or higher responsibilities that used to be a man’s dominion.

Woman are no longer the lesser sex and have been known to supersede or maintain many accomplishments of men.

Body-builder Marja Lehtonen from Finland has won two body-building Women’s World Championships in Mexico and Finland. She places 7th under 52kg and could probably overpower more than half the men in the world, according to www.wic.org.

Lehtonen’s motto is, “Keep your dreams high and go forward and up to reach them and do your best to make your way to the goal.”

Men’s jobs are now women’s employment, and women’s jobs are now men’s jobs. Women are becoming CEOs of major corporations, and men are becoming homemakers. Traditional roles are being flipped, making a woman’s world professional profile rise.

Women have even reached levels in which they are considered for presidential campaigns in the United States. Geraldine Ferraro was the first woman nominated by a major political power as its candidate for vice president in 1984.

In Ferraro’s acceptance speech to run while backing up former Vice President Walter Mondale, she spoke about her own American dream.

“Tonight, the daughter of an immigrant from Italy has been chosen to run for vice president in the new land my father came to love ... ” Ferraro said.

Although Ferraro lost the election, her example will be remembered and have a continuing impact for future women politicians.

Woman have more opportunities than ever before in the work force. The LDS Church encourages women to receive a college education, but a woman’s primary role is to take care of her children, according to www.lds.org.

Women can and will continue to grow in the work force. Depending on what employment a woman decides to dedicate herself to, the world will adapt to it as it has over the past decade, according to www.wic.org.
