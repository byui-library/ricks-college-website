Three Approaches

ARISTOTLEAN

 

C Exordium: the introduction

C Narratio: the statement or exposition of the case under discussion

C Divisio: the outline of the points or steps in the argument

C Confirmatio: the proof of the case

C Confutatio: the refutation of the opposing arguments

C Peroratio: the conclusion

 

TOULMINIC

 

C Claims: Claims represent answers to the question AWhat are you trying to prove?@

U Claims of fact
U Claims of value
U Claims of policy

C Support: Support for a claim represents the answer to the question AWhat have you got to go on?@

U Evidence
U Appeals to Needs and Values

C Warrants: The warrant is an assumption, a belief we take for granted, or a general principle.

U Authoritative
U Substantive
U Motivational

 

ROGERIAN

 

C Make a claim

C Consider opposition

C Present evidence

C Promote mutual learning and communication

U Avoid a confrontational stance
U Present your character as someone who understands and can empathize with the opposition
U Establish common ground with the opposition
U Be willing to change your views
U Direct your argument toward a compromise or workable solution
