15 YEAR OLD TEACHES FAMILY HISTORY SEMINARS

by Wesley Baugh
scroll staff

Fifteen-year-old Josh Taylor has an extraordinary talent, especially for a high school sophomore. He is the youngest person to instruct others in the exploration of past generations.

Genealogy has become his passion and has given him many opportunities to help older generations make their own ancestral discoveries.

Taylor's interest in family history began with a mini-course he enrolled in six years ago when he was nine-years-old. The other students at Madison Middle School in Rexburg were given the opportunity to either go skiing or take one of the many classes offered at the school that day.

"I took the [genealogy] class instead of skiing," Taylor said.

He is happy that he did. He began his own family history research with his grandmother Taylor's line, because his family was working with it at the time.

Since then, he has spent many hours in libraries searching for dates and names.

"I'm not just looking for a birth date or a marriage date," Taylor said. "I'm actually looking for a person's life and how they lived day to day."

He now teaches seminars for adult genealogists by working with the Utah Genealogical Association. He spent April 15 in Salt Lake City helping others with their family history research.

Taylor now spends at least one hour a day researching his family history. In college, he plans to major in genealogy at Brigham Young University and teach full-time.
