Ricks College News Release
May 29, 2001

Ground broken for new Spori Building
at Ricks College

REXBURG, IDAHO - Ground was broken May 29 for a new building to replace the former Jacob Spori Building at Ricks College.

Prior to the symbolic turning of soil, Elder Henry B. Eyring, a member of the Quorum of the Twelve and Commissioner of Church Education, paid tribute to faithful, pioneer Church members who built the original building and he challenged the younger generation to follow in their path.

In speaking to an audience of young college students and older community residents, the former Ricks College president (1971-1977) said Church members sacrificed to build the original academy building at the turn of the 20th century that later became known as the Spori Building. He said these Saints were poor and living on the frontier, but they sacrificed because they valued education.

In looking at the new Spori Building, he said, “When you see the (new) building go up, I hope you will see Latter-day Saints in Albania and Peru who have built it out of sacrifice,” referring to the use of tithing funds from Church members.

Elder Eyring paid tribute to Jacob Spori, a Swiss convert who served as the first principal of Bannock Stake Academy, which grew into Ricks College. He said Spori symbolizes many people who are seekers and willing to sacrifice everything for the gospel of Jesus Christ.

“Real seekers seek the kingdom of God and build it up. That’s the way Jacob Spori was, that’s what you are,” he told the students. He hopes that seekers like Spori will always continue to come to the college.

Elder Eyring added, “He (Jacob Spori) saw a great future. He saw that this tiny little academy would somehow have a worldwide influence.”

Ricks College President David A. Bednar said the building is intended “to look to the past with fondness and gratitude and to future with hope and anticipation.” He said there are many parallels between the old and new Spori buildings that will be built almost exactly 100 years apart. First classes in the original building were held in 1903 and first classes in the new building are expected to be held in 2003.

“I’m impressed by the similarities of what happened 100 years ago and what’s happening today,” he said. “We are walking on pathways which others have walked,” he said, referring to beginning a pioneer academy in the late 1800s and becoming a four-year university in 2001. “There’s a deep sense of history as we retrace these steps.”

The new building is designed to meet the needs of a growing campus as the college becomes Brigham Young University-Idaho and begins offering bachelor’s degrees starting in the Fall Semester.

The new 44,000 square foot building, which will sit at the exact location of the original building, has a striking resemblance to its predecessor and will feature a similar size and shape, rock exterior, tall windows and pitched roof.

Although the new building is reminiscent of the old building, it is not a replica, explained Ricks Administrative Vice President James Smyth, who serves as chairman of the building committee. Unlike the old structure, the four-level building will feature a skylight that will allow natural light to flow down through the center of the building to the first floor.

Some rock and woodwork from the original building were preserved and are incorporated into the design of the new building. A central focus of the interior will be the triangular wooden pediment from the front of the original building that will be mounted on the third floor and will be visible from the first floor by looking upward through the skylight. An outdoor terrace in the front of the building will be bordered by a wall of  rock from the original building. 

The building will house both the Art and Communications Departments at the college. Construction of the building designed by FFKR of Salt Lake City is expected to start in July and be completed in 2003.
