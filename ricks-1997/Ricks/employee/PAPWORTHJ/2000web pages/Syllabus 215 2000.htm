Overview:

The purpose of English 215 is to help you learn to write more clearly and convincingly, to understand the practices of good writers in social science fields of study, and perhaps to help you enjoy writing.

Particular Objectives:

Specifically, E215 will help you learn how to respond to common writing problems:

  • how to uncover your hidden writing voice

  • how to adapt your ideas to particular audiences

  • how to focus, develop, and organize your ideas

  • how to write personably and conversationally

  • how to revise and edit your writing

  • how to use language precisely

  • and how to work comfortably with library resources and formats of documentation.

University Credit: Most other colleges/universities accept E215 as the equivalent of a second-level writing course. Therefore, E215 must be approached as a junior/senior level class.

Requirements and Grading:

Grading is based on both process and development of 6 to 8 major papers (4 will be graded), and incidental assignments: in-class writing assignments, page-length papers, workshop sessions, editing, maps, responses, and class participation. I also rely heavily on personal assessment and evaluation; in other words, you must >grade= yourself and classmates. Your self grade probably reflects most accurately your strengths and weaknesses.

Major Essays:

Major papers will give you experience writing in situations typical of your field of study and academic preparation. Generally, these papers should be 4 pages long (1,000 words) and double-spaced. We=ll do two mid-sized documented papers instead of the traditional long research paper. Each assignment comes with easy to follow, snap together instructions.

Responses:

The response acts like a quiz: its worth hovers around ten points, unless it=s an obvious guffaw. It suggests to me that you have read the assignment and are prepared to participate in a discussion. Make them personal and informal, and allow them to fill at least a pageBkeyboarded. Please don=t dash these off: I=ll be searching for fresh ideas, mature thought, wisdom, metaphors, personableness, and attitude. Perhaps making notes as you go, revising, changing, deleting, thinking: these are the necessary steps. Do not SUMMARIZE the essay.

Workshops:

Pacing yourself through each essay assignment in English 215 is essential. Often we will gather in small groups to discuss essays in progress. Your participation in these workshops is essential to our class work. Your participation in these shops will be graded.

Submitting Papers:

Each time a paper comes due (4 pages minimum, double-spaced, titled, author-named, three-key-holed, unstapled), we=ll read and edit them. Type (keyboard) all essay assignments according to the format of your chosen discipline. Most of you will follow either the APA or MLA format.

Editing Process:

When papers are due, please turn in two copies. I'll haul these over to the Reserve Library and binder them. You'll have 2-3 weeks to read and edit the class papers. You'll applaud, gripe and whine, discuss, help--all those things a single-teacher-audience can't do as well as a collective, real audience. This process takes about 5 hours--5 HOURS? to complete, but it's the single best teacher of writing I've encountered. Trust me? You'll get back your two copies with a cart load of marginalia that will help you revise and rewrite. I'll pay you, of course, each time you go through this editing process with points.

The Stuff of Guinea Pigs.

Normally I assign a research paper project about 8 weeks from semester end. However, this semester we=ll act like guinea pigs. [I=ll explain this verbally in numerous class discussions we=ll have throughout the semester.] Essentially it=s this: all of the papers we write for class will be related and interconnected. By the end of the semester we=ll have put together what we=ll call our final project. This project is the culmination of all your writing; it will look and feel and have the heft of a research paper, but we will have sneaked up on it. In other words, it will be finished by the semester's end without your having to sit down to write it. Your individual topic will arise out of the rubble and ash of your thinking, discovering, writing, revising, and essaying, which you will have been shaping all along. This will all make sense after our in-class talks. Trust me.

I hope you enjoy our journey together. JP.
