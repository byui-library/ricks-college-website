Percussion


The Percussion Program at Brigham Young University-Idaho is designed to:

  • Prepare the percussion major with the skills and experiences essential to succeed in the professional world of percussion.
  • Provide a rich vocational percussion experience for the amateur percussionist who wishes to play for the inherent joy of playing.
  • Provide a rewarding introductory experience in percussion for the beginner who is interested in exploring the experiences of the percussion world.

Whatever your special interests, previous experience, or current ability, there is a place for you in the BYU-Idaho percussion program to give you the training, personal growth, and ensemble experience you are seeking.  There are many opportunities for percussionists to perform with the various wind, string, and vocal ensembles on campus.

THE PERCUSSION ENSEMBLES

Percussion Ensemble    This ensemble provides a challenging performance experience for percussion students regardless of playing ability.  Focusing on a wide variety of musical selections including standard repertoire, transcriptions of popular music and works written or arranged by students, the Percussion Ensemble offers at least one concert a semester and often appears as a special guest on concerts of other performing groups.  Basic training in percussion techniques is provided in this class and parts are assigned from this class to the Symphony Orchestra, the Chamber Orchestra, the Symphonic Band, and sometimes the choirs. Membership in the Percussion Ensemble is open to all interested percussionists 

RIXSTIX    A performing ensemble designed for percussionists only!  RIXSTIX is the heart of the football band drumline.  Combining the exciting sound of drumline with the percussion ensemble, RIXSTIX provides entertainment at football and basketball games.  RIXSTIX participants are required to enroll in the Viking Pep Band during fall semester.

Performance Back-up Bands    The Performance Back-up Bands are select instrumental ensembles who accompany such groups as Vocal Union and the American Folk Dancers.  They tour and perform on campus.

PRIVATE PERCUSSION STUDY


Whether you are a beginner or an advanced student, instruction is available in all areas of percussion.  Non-music majors receive 13 private lessons lasting 25 minutes each and a recital experience near the end of the semester.  Percussion majors are admitted to a specialized area but must also achieve minimum mastery on at least three areas of percussion over a four-semester sequence of study.  The first semester of study includes 13 private lessons lasting 25 minutes each and a weekly percussion master class.  The following semesters of study includes 13 private lessons lasting 50 minutes each and the weekly percussion master class.

A weekly percussion master class is required of all percussion majors.  Percussionists studying privately who are not music majors are also invited to attend these master classes.  In addition to student performances, weekly discussions focus on specific topics of interest related to the world of percussion.  A sampling of recent topics includes recovering timpani mallets, playing odd meter patterns on the drum set (specifically dealing with the music of Sting), the four mallet techniques of Leigh Howard Stevens, an introduction to the work of world-renowned percussionist Evelyn Glennie, and even replacing a calf-skin head on a tambourine.
