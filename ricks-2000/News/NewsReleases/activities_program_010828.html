August 28, 2001

New Activities Program 
will open opportunities to students

     
REXBURG, Idaho - The new Student Activities Program at Brigham Young University-Idaho is in the formulative stages and is expected to open new opportunities in sports, the arts, and service to everyone.

While intercollegiate athletics will be discontinued following the 2001-2002 school year, highly competitive athletics will continue within the institution and will actually involve a higher percentage of students. In the expanded Activities Programs students will be participants, not just spectators.

"At the present time we serve probably 280 intercollegiate athletes, and that's a great enterprise," President David A. Bednar says. "But we'll move toward a program where we can serve hundreds and even thousands with the same resources."

He adds, "Instead of a place where you buy a ticket and sit in the stands and watch, at Brigham Young University-Idaho, if you choose to, you'll be on the field doing it."

President Bednar says, "I believe people will find many of the events to be quite entertaining. Imagine the Hart Auditorium packed with students and community residents who have an interest in coming to watch a women's final four or a men's final four made up of teams that have been competing on campus. The community will be invited, and we hope they will be excited to come. This will be great fun both to participate in and to watch."

He also expects to see Viking Stadium used more in coming years. "It's presently used a limited number of times each year. We'll get much more use out of it. You can expect to see the lights on many nights."

This new concept broadens the access of competitive sports far beyond "intramurals." The program will include structured coaching, regular practices, full uniforms, and equipment¾a level formerly characteristic of only varsity athletics.

Students may also explore artistic and service opportunities within a similar structure.

The program is structured in three areas: service and social, visual and performing arts, and physical activities, giving students numerous opportunities for active involvement at various levels of interest and commitment.

Activities Program Director Devin Shaum gives an example of a student wishing to play the cello, but not wanting to be a music major. As part of the Activities Program, a cello class would be taught for recreational musicians. Currently, over 4,000 BYU-Idaho students participate in music on some level.

"This is a program that students can voluntarily select and choose from a palate of actually beautiful opportunities for them to be involved¾with a big key that all of these activities will make them more employable, and make them better fathers and mothers," Shaum says.
