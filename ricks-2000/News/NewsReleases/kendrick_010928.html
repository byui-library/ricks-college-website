September 28, 2001

Elder Kendrick speaks

of liberty

    
REXBURG, Idaho – A message on the importance of liberty was given by Elder L. Lionel Kendrick during Tuesday’s devotional at Brigham Young University-Idaho. 

            The member of the First Quorum of the Seventy of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints used the tragedies of Sept. 11 to demonstrate the battle between light and darkness.  “[These events] have given cause for all to pause and ponder the principle of liberty and to put life in proper perspective,” he said. 

He noted that the attacks in New York City and the Pentagon are not the first attacks on freedom.  “Each of Heavenly Father’s spiritual children had the opportunity to choose to follow the Savior or to follow Satan in the war in heaven.  This was the beginning of the battle between good and evil, between liberty and limits, between light and darkness, between freedom and force.”

            He said the United States is the land of liberty and was prepared as a promised land by the Lord.  “This nation is great because of its divine origin and destiny and because it was founded with deep spiritual roots.”  Elder Kendrick then listed examples of the spiritual roots that America was built upon.  Included in the list was Columbus, and an excerpt from his diary stating “Our Lord unlocked my mind, sent me upon the sea, and gave me the fire for the deed…who can doubt but that the Holy Ghost inspired me?”

            Also mentioned were the signers of the Declaration of Independence, and how several of these men sacrificed great personal fortunes and even their lives in the cause of liberty.

            Elder Kendrick also quoted prophecies from the Book of Mormon, such as 2 Nephi 1:7, stating “it shall be a land of liberty unto them.”  He added that “in the preservation of this choice land, we must not only have a love of liberty, but we must also be aware that the fulfillment of the Lord’s promises are predicated upon the avoidance of iniquity in this land.”

            He also explained why the Lord allows such things as the recent terrorist attacks to happen.  First, Elder Kendrick explained that it has always been the adversary’s goal to destroy agency, the souls of man, and God’s work.  In order to accomplish that, Satan has used secret combinations since the beginning. “His works of darkness are designed and delivered in secret.  The murder of Able by Cain was planned in secret,” he said.

            A comparison was then made between the events of Sept. 11 and events from the Book of Mormon.  “The shocking scenes we saw on our television sets September 11, 2001, are strikingly similar to those that were seen by Alma and Amulek.  Wicked men maliciously murdered innocent men, women and children because they believed in the living God.  They cast them into the fire.”

            In order to understand why “the agency of one person can be allowed to cause damage or death of another person,” Elder Kendrick referred to two scriptures.  The first is found in Abraham 3:25 and says “And we will prove them herewith, to see if they would do all things whatsoever the Lord their God shall command them.”  The second is found in D&C 136:31, which reads “My people must be tried in all things, that they may be prepared to receive the glory that I have for them.”

            Elder Kendrick then noted: “The events that we have seen are a part of the signs of the times what we live in during these latter says.”  He drew our attention to similar tragedies that the United States has overcome in the past 100 years, events such as two world wars, Pearl Harbor, the assassination of a president, the loss of the Challenger space shuttle, the Oklahoma bombing, and the first bombing of the World Trade Center.

            Along with trials come blessings.  “We must not be overwhelmed and fearful as a result of the tragedy that we have seen…The Savior counseled that “after much tribulation come the blessings (D&C 58:4).”  Then followed a list of some of the blessings that have come as a result of the attacks.  These include unity, patriotism, love, spirituality, service and sacrifice.  “It seems that we have been shaken and there has been an awakening in each of us of the important things that we have allowed to become dormant in our lives,” Elder Kendrick said, speaking about the nation as a whole.

            Elder Kendrick then reminded us that in these “times of need, we can take great comfort and confidence in the counsel that we receive from the scriptures…During the darkest days of our life we must always remember that we are never alone…One of the greatest roles of the Holy Ghost is to give comfort to those in need.”

            The devotional ended with a charge to the students of BYU-Idaho.  “The torch will soon be passed to your generation to protect the divinely inspired Constitution of prevail in this great land of the free and the brave…May God bless you and may God this great land of liberty…You must remember that the ‘gates of hell’ must not and cannot prevail in this great land of the free and the brave…May God bless you and may God bless America.”

            Next week’s devotional speaker will be Elder Vaughn J. Featherstone of the First Quorum of the Seventy.  He will speak at 2 p.m. in the Hart Auditorium.  The devotional will be carried live on KBYI, 100.5 FM, and rebroadcast at 9 p.m.
