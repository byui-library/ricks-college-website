REGION 18 WRESTLING CHAMPS

For only the third time in school history, Ricks College has won the Region 18 wrestling championships. For Head Coach Bob Christensen, it is his second Region 18 crown, the other one coming in 1991-92 when the Vikings went on to finish third at the NJCAA finals. Not only did the Vikings win the team title, all ten wrestlers qualified for the NJCAA tournament, only the second time in school history that the entire team will go to nationals. The NJCAA tournament is February 18-19, 2000, in Rochester, Minn. Coach Christensen was elated with the results of the regional competition. "We wrestled well. The kids rose up to the challenge when it was on the line."

Five Vikings won individual championships. Winning championships for Ricks were: Jason Blue (133), Andy Messersmith (141), Jeff Ireland (149), Nate Ploehn (165), and James Huml (Hwt). Messersmith was named the tournaments outstanding wrestler. Huml is the Region 18 and NJCAA defending champion at heavyweight and has lost only once this season. Runner-up honors went to Shane Wasden (174), and Dale Boone (184). Ty Rollins (125), and Brandon Ripplinger (157) finished third. Matt Walker (197) finished in fourth place, but received a wild card invitation to nationals.

ACCREDITATION REPORT

Ricks College officials are elated with the recommendations and commendations they received following an accreditation team's evaluation of the college that was conducted late last year. The positive evaluation, with only a few minor corrections needed, means the two-year college will continue to be fully accredited by the Northwest Association of Schools and Colleges. In a letter extending continued accreditation status, Sandra E. Elman, executive director of the association, stated: "The commission finds the college-wide interest in, sensitivity to, and support for effectively meeting student needs laudatory."

Max Checketts, the accreditation liaison officer at Ricks College, says Ricks officials are pleased with the commendations from the team. Accreditation has significant impact on whether or not students' credits transfer to other colleges and universities, as well as the availability of government loans and grants for students. College officials took the self study seriously and worked for three years in preparing for the once-in-a-decade visit by a team of 14 members. He says the evaluators were so impressed by the college's self study that they are using it as a model in their upcoming training sessions. They have asked Checketts to help train officials from other colleges in how to conduct and write a self-study report. Checketts adds, "We think accreditation is healthy, and we will address every one of the recommendations that were made." He says college employees will be taking a close look at the recommendations and addressing them in the coming months and years.

Scott Bergstrom, the Ricks administrator who coordinated the college's self-study, says the evaluation "forced us to look at ourselves and ask: Are we really doing what we say we're doing?" Its second purpose is "to show you really are a legitimate school of higher education." Bergstrom adds, "One thing that was really satisfying was for them to notice how student centered most of the organizations are on campus. They picked up on our unique environment, our friendliness and spirituality."

Checketts says Ricks officials were particularly pleased with the first two commendations, which are at the heart of what the college board of trustees, administrators, faculty and staff are trying to achieve. The eight main commendations and five recommendations are listed below:

Commendations

Commendation 1: There is college-wide interest in, sensitivity to, and support for effectively meeting student needs. Repeatedly one hears "our first concern is students; we try to meet their needs and expectations." There is near-universal student confirmation of this college position. Students find life direction at Ricks College.

Commendation 2: The college has created a nurturing, supporting environment for students, faculty and staff that is grounded in a sense of physical and spiritual well-being. The result is a welcoming, friendly campus climate that reminds one of an extended familial community, rather than a collection of strangers.

Commendation 3: The self-study document is to be commended for its overall organization, the excellent editing of content, and the visually-pleasing format.

Commendation 4: The support and mentoring of those faculty in non-continuing status is outstanding. The roles of the department chair and peer faculty go beyond perfunctory concerns and exemplify a true, caring stewardship. Evaluative measures include multiple indices and are systematic and regular.

Commendation 5: The commitment to and financial support of the institution by its governing board of trustees is exceptional. The commitment of the board is key to the college's successful fulfillment of its mission.

Commendation 6: In general, facilities, physical plant and landscaping are clean, attractive, well-kept and in excellent condition. A sense of security and safety is prominent, and obvious care has been taken to create an aesthetically appealing environment.

Commendation 7: The college believes in the growth and development of people, and it extends that belief to the development of faculty and staff, providing ample funds and opportunities for professional and personal growth.

Commendation 8: It is clear that all institutional efforts are guided by the mission. The mission, along with the honor code, is prominently displayed and infused throughout the entire curriculum and inspires the actions of the college community.

Recommendations

Recommendation 1: Despite the fact that the college has taken a forward-thinking position in outcomes assessment planning, the plan's implementation has slowed and is applied unevenly throughout the institution, especially in academic and professional/technical departments. Institutional documentation is needed to demonstrate that such assessment is incorporated in planning and leads to the improvement of teaching and learning.

Recommendation 2: The college would benefit by efforts directed at ensuring consistent and reliable open communication with faculty and staff. Methods of communicating effectively and working collaboratively with faculty and staff need to be established.

Recommendation 3: Continuing status faculty evaluation is inconsistent within departments, as well as across disciplines, in cycles and methods of review. It is recommended that the evaluation schedule and standards of measurement be published and monitored to ensure that every faculty member on continuing status is regularly evaluated in a timely manner.

Recommendation 4: It is recommended that the college review, strengthen and clearly define what constitutes college-level courses for general education requirements, especially in mathematics. In addition, the college needs to determine what constitutes related instruction course content for specialized degrees and certificate programs, especially in the human relations area. This should be done collaboratively between administration and faculty.

Recommendation 5: It is recommended that the college demonstrate evidence of clear and coherent systematic institutional planning and evaluation procedures to measure the achievement of institutional mission and goals.
