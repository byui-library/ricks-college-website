Welcome to our class! I hope you will enjoy learning and teaching this semester.

 

Aims

1. To deepen students’ understanding of the context and content of the revelations published in the Doctrine and Covenants.

2. To prepare and motivate students for life-long study and teaching of the scriptures.

Of the Doctrine and Covenants (and Book of Mormon), President Ezra Taft Benson said, “Virtually every page . . . teaches about the Master—His great love for His children and His atoning sacrifice—and teaches us how to live so that we can return to Him and our Heavenly Father. . . . Each [book] . . . contains the knowledge and the power to help us live better lives in a time of great wickedness and evil. Those who carefully and prayerfully search the pages of these books will find comfort, counsel, guidance, and the quiet power to improve their lives” (“The Gift of Modern Revelation,” A Witness and a Warning, 25).

I invite you to claim this promise, as a central (though ungraded) aim of this class.

 

Graded Activities

1. Preparatory study: The course calendar specifies a block of scripture to study before each class meeting. Much of your most valuable learning and growth can take place as you study on your own. Sections of special significance for this class are highlighted in bold print. You are welcome but not required to purchase the CES Student Manual. You should plan to take some notes related to the “Study Portfolio” and/or the “Essay Questions” each time you study (see below). Please report the quality of your preparation for each study assignment. I may help assess the quality of your preparation as needed. Basic expectation: read and mark the whole block of scripture before coming to class, use the study helps in the scriptures or another study resource, and take some notes.

2. Attendance and participation: Class presentations and discussions should complement your personal study of the scriptures. I hope to help highlight important principles, clarify complex issues, and promote understanding consistent with the course objectives. I invite you to participate in that process. Please report attendance and the quality of your participation for each class meeting. I may help assess the quality of your participation as needed. Basic expectation: be in class, on time, with scriptures, notebook, and intent to listen, write, and speak meaningfully.

3. Study Portfolio: You may choose a variety of study activities to report in a typewritten portfolio. The finished product must be your own original work and late submissions are penalized. In the portfolio you report a variety of study activities and the “fruits” of those activities. You should choose at least four of the following activities and report the fruits:

a.        Covenantal constructions: Identify promises of the Lord that can be paraphrased as, “If  you do this, then I will do that.” Report the ten covenantal constructions that inspire or warn you the most, including the promise and the relevant references.

b.       Converting principles: Discover principles in the revelations which, if understood by you, would have the greatest impact on your attitude and behavior.  Report ten such principles and the relevant references.

c.        Scripture chains: Develop “chains” of references that show the “unfolding” of a doctrine or practice, the development of priesthood organization, or the relationship between the Book of Mormon and the Bible and the Doctrine and Covenants. Each chain should be as complete as possible. Report your five best scripture chains, including the subject and all references.

d.       Footnote focus: Study at least three of the highlighted revelations, looking up every cross-reference and referring to some of the Topical Guide references. Report how this affected your study and the most important insights you learned.

e.        Divine Salutations: Identify introductory verses whose language claims your attention and reveals truths about the voice or character of the Savior. Report the ten introductions that claimed your attention most forcefully and note what they taught you about the Lord’s voice and character.

f.         One-line sermons: Identify short phrases in at least half of the revelations that inspire or warn you by the power of their language. Report the ten phrases that impacted you the most, noting the references.

g.       Revelation Titles: After studying each highlighted revelation, formulate a title that captures the key themes of the revelation. Report your title for each of the highlighted revelations.

Basic expectation: Do four activities meaningfully and report the fruits in correct form.

4. Essay Questions: I will ask you to turn in one or more typewritten essays during the term. Here are prospective essay questions (subject to revision as needed):

                a. The “Explanatory Introduction” of the book makes the following statement. “Finally, the testimony that is given of Jesus Christ—his divinity, his majesty, his perfection, his love, and his redeeming power—makes this book of great value to the human family.” As you study and ponder the revelations, can identify specific passages of scripture that best illustrate the Savior’s divinity, majesty, perfection, love, and redeeming power? Identify and classify revelations and references that best illustrate each highlighted attribute, discussing how the passages illuminate the attribute you chose, and stating why their illumination of each attribute is valuable to you.

b. Elder Neal A. Maxwell said, “What came through Joseph Smith was beyond Joseph Smith, and it stretched him” (BYU Speeches of the Year, 1985-86, 114). As you study and ponder the revelations, identify those that best illustrate Elder Maxwell’s statement. In your discussion, consider two kinds of “stretching”: first, those revelations that provided light and direction well beyond the question or problem that prompted the Prophet’s inquiry; and second, those revelations that were most likely to remind Joseph of his own weakness, the greatness of the work of restoration, and the need to obtain divine grace to accomplish that work.

c. The “Explanatory Introduction” states, “These revelations were received in answer to prayer, in times of need, and came out of real-life situations involving real people. The Prophet and his associates sought for divine guidance, and these revelations certify that they received it.” As you study and ponder the revelations and historical resources, what revelations provide the most inspiring and sobering examples of this statement? You should discuss the “real-life situations” in which the revelations came, comparing and contrasting the most joyous and sobering settings for revelation. You should also discuss the “real people” who sought to know God’s will and then responded, comparing and contrasting good and bad examples of people’s responses to revealed commandments.

5. Mastery Assessments: There will be at least two assessments of your mastery of the text—one will deal with the doctrinal content of the highlighted revelations; the other will deal with the context for highlighted and other sections. You can use your scriptures for each assessment, which will take place in the Testing Center on dates to be determined.

6. Additional out-of-class learning: Because the time constraints on this block class are severe, you need to do much of your learning on your own out of class. Your first outside assignment is to download and study the syllabus from my faculty web page. Your second outside assignment is to download and study “Principles and Promises to Guide Our Learning” from my faculty web page.  I hope these documents will inspire you to do your best and claim the blessings that follow. As you study each document, identify and record promises and principles that will help you most as a student and teacher of the scriptures.
