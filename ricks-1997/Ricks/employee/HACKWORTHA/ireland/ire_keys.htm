Major Players in Ireland's
Peace Process

Great Britain

Tony Blair, prime minister - Elected in 1997 as the first Labor prime minister in 18 years, Blair takes an active role in Northern Ireland. The province was the site for his first official prime ministerial visit.

Mo Mowlan, Northern Ireland secretary - Known as a blunt and irreverent politician, she caused a stir when she offered Sinn Fein a seat at early peace talks. She received her political science doctorate from the University of Iowa.


Ireland

Bertie Ahern, prime minister - At 45, Ahern is Ireland's youngest leader. He is the son of a man who fought the Irish Republican Army and he leads the party called Fianna Fail, or Soldiers of Destiny.

Northern Ireland's political parties
____________________________________________________________________________
Sinn Fein - The political wing of the militant Irish Republican Army, Sinn Fein is devoted to establishing a united Ireland. It has 18 seats in the Northern Ireland Assembly.

Gerry Adams, president - Since 1983, Adams has led Sinn Fein. Adams was interned in 1972 on a British prison ship and also held without trial from 1973-1977. In the 1990s, Adams was elected to the British Parliament.

____________________________________________________________________________
Ulster Unionists - The main Protestant, pro-British party in Northern Ireland. The party's main goal is to maintain the link between Northern Ireland and the rest of the United Kingdom. It has 28 seats in the Northern Ireland Assembly, making it the largest faction.

David Trimble, leader - Since 1995, Trimble headed up the party. Once a hard-liner in Northern Ireland politics, his views have tempered. In 1998, he shared the Nobel Peace Prize with John Hume.
____________________________________________________________________________
Social Democratic and Labor Party - Represents two-thirds of Catholics in Northern Ireland. It opposes violence in pursuing its political goals and is a critic of the British military presence in Northern Ireland. It is the second largest party in the Northern Ireland Assembly with 24 seats.

John Hume, leader - A member of the British and European parliaments, Hume has led the SDLP since 1979. He brought together Gerry Adams and the UK government. He shared the 1998 Nobel Peace Prize with David Trimble.
____________________________________________________________________________
Democratic Unionist Party - The party is strongly opposed to the Good Friday Agreement, is strongly anti-Catholic and is against any move it sees as an attempt to weaken the union with Britain. It has 20 seats in the Assembly.

Ian Paisley, leader - Co-founder of the party and a Free Presbyterian pastor, Paisley has been tied to radical anti-Catholic groups like the Ulster Volunteer Force. He has adamantly refused to talk with Sinn Fein.
____________________________________________________________________________
Other - Other countries, chief among them the United States, have tried to influence the peace process in Northern Ireland.

George Mitchell, U.S. Envoy to Northern Ireland - President Clinton named Mitchell, a Catholic, in 1996 to be his unpaid representative in Northern Ireland after Mitchell left the Senate, where he had been majority leader. Mitchell has been widely praised for respecting all sides.

John de Chastelain, head of the international commission on disarmament - A soldier turned diplomat, de Chastelain has ties to Canada, Britain, Scotland and Romania. He is a general in the Canadian army and was Canada's ambassador to the United States in the early 1990s.
