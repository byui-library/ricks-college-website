THE IMPORTANCE OF MEDICAL RECORDS



A medical record is a permanent document of the history and progress of one person's illness or injury. It is made to preserve information of medical, scientific, legal, and planning value and is a compilation of observations and findings recorded by the patient's physician and other professional members of the medical team. The entries and reports originate at various points in the hospital, clinic, nursing home, health center, or other health care facility. Through a network of communications systems they are entered in the individual's patient record. This vital medical profile constitutes the patient's unique medical history.



Just as schools and colleges keep transcripts of grades and employers maintain personnel records, doctors and hospitals set up a permanent file for every patient they treat. This file is known as the medical record or chart. It includes the patient's medical history, results of physical examinations, reports of X-ray and laboratory tests, diagnosis and treatment plans, doctors' orders and notes, and nurses' notes.



The medical record shows what the patient's symptoms were, what tests were ordered, and how the patient responded to treatment. Accurate and orderly records are essential for clinical purposes. However, medical records have other important uses as well. They provide background and documentation for insurance claims and Medicare reimbursement, legal actions, professional review of treatment and medications prescribed, and training of health professions personnel. Medical records are used for research and planning purposes. They provide data for clinical studies, evaluations of the benefits and cost of various medical and surgical procedures and assessments of community health needs.

Technicians' duties vary with the size of the institution in which they work. Medical record technicians are the people who actually handle records, organizing and evaluating them for completeness and accuracy. When assembling a patient's medical record, the technician ensures that all forms are present and properly identified and signed.



In a growing number of hospitals the medical and nursing staff use computers rather than the written charts to record blood pressure readings, medication dosages, test results, and other patient care information. In these hospitals, technicians use their own computer terminals to retrieve information from the patient's "chart" in the hospital's central computer.



Once they have all the necessary information (a process that may require them to track down physicians or nurses to fill in incomplete entries), technicians assign a code to each diagnosis and procedure documented in the record. In assigning codes, technicians consult a classification manual and also rely on their knowledge of disease processes. After the entries on the chart have been coded, technicians may use a packaged computer program to assign the patient to one of several hundred diagnosis-related groups, or DRGs. The DRG determines the amount the hospital will be reimbursed if the patient is covered by Medicare or other insurance programs that use the DRG system.



Because information in the medical record is used for reimbursement purposes as well as clinical decision making, it is doubly important that entries be complete and accurate. This has always been important for patient care; now it is important for the hospital as well. A coding error could mean a financial loss for the hospital since the amount reimbursed may depend on the diagnostic group to which the patient is assigned.



Technicians have other duties as well. For example, they assist the administrative staff of the hospital by tabulating and analyzing data from the medical records. Technicians might be asked to tabulate statistics that show differences in the average length of a hospital stay according to diagnosis, admitting physician, and procedures performed. Some technicians maintain special registries showing occurrences of disease by type, such as cancer, injury, or stroke. Technicians also maintain health record indexes and compile administrative and health statistics for public health officials, administrators, planners, and others. In response to inquiries from law firms, insurance companies, government agencies, researchers, and patients, medical record technicians may assemble medical records for legal proceedings or for meetings of oversight groups such as utilization review committees and medical review boards.



The day-to-day tasks of medical record personnel vary with the size of the facility. In a small facility, for example, a technician may have full responsibility for managing the record department, whereas in a large facility, technicians are likely to specialize in just one aspect of the work. If the department is large enough to employ medical record clerks, a technician would be responsible for supervising and training them. In many nursing homes a record clerk, working under a consultant who is a registered record administrator (RRA) or an accredited record technician (A.R.T.), is responsible for maintain the medical record system.

Hospital and nursing home administration differ in important aspects. Managers in HMOs perform all the functions of those in large medical group practices, plus one addition function--that of an insurance company. Licensure is not required in most areas of health services management, except for a nursing home or long-term care administration. Sixteen States currently require two years of college and an associate degree for licensure, while 25 require a bachelor's degree. Health services managers are often responsible for millions of dollars' worth of facilities and equipment and hundreds of employees. There are city, State, Federal, and other laws regarding sanitary standards in food, water supply, garbage disposal, sewage disposal, and housing maintenance, which sanitarian interpret and enforce. Sanitarian have also moved into many new types of positions and have assumed expanding roles within the field of_______________ health control. They are in a position, for example, to promote and help to secure such improvements as water supply extension, improved sewage disposal, safer recreational areas, and more_______ ____________ in nursing and convalescent homes. New sanitary problems are created as the population increases and as more and more people move into the cities and suburbs of expanding metropolitan areas. With technical training and experience, sanitarian are equipped to recognize and anticipate sanitation hazards. It is part of their responsibility to call these problems to the attention of the government bodies concerned, community leaders, civic groups, and the general public and to make recommendations for solving these problems.



WORK DESCRIPTION FOR SANITARIAN



Sanitarian collect samples and specimens for laboratory analysis and perform a variety of field tests, using sampling equipment. They also conduct environmental health inspections which involves going to food establishments, schools, hospitals, swimming pools, motels, parks, and many other locations to determine compliance with public health laws and regulations. In food establishments they check compliance with the "____ _______." The "Uni Code" contains 44 rules and regulations, 13 of which are critical. The scope of this code establishes standards of sanitation for all food processing; canning factories; cold storage units; ice manufacturing; water supplies; eating places; food vending; all food operations and their employees, equipment, utensils, and sanitary facilities; proper labeling; hazardous and solid wastes; and rodent and pest control. The sanitarian has enforcement duties and responsibilities, and the code contains provisions for enforcement. There are also procedures to be followed for mediation, allowing processors and manufacturers time to remedy infractions and receive a second inspection.



Sanitarian are familiar with working in courts, legal procedures, and law enforcement. They also reeducate in ways to gain compliance from those who are in violation. Reeducation takes precedence over compliance, but there are time limits involved.



After making inspections, sanitarian evaluate their findings and make recommendations to those concerned. Sanitarian also plan and conduct environmental health inspections and review and evaluate documents, license applications, and planning guidelines. Sanitarian frequently provide consultative services relating to environmental health problems. They consult with owners, operators, employers, contractors, school officials, government officials, hospital administrators, and others regarding environmental health equipment and related problems.



Sanitarian having supervisory duties analyze reports of inspections and investigations made by other environmental health specialists and advise on difficult or unusual sanitation problems. They conduct investigations and give testimony in court cases involving public health education regulations. In addition, they promote health laws and engage in health education activities. Those in top management positions are involved with planning and administering environmental health programs and coordinating them with programs of other agencies. Duties may include advising government officials on environmental health matters and drafting health laws and regulations. In many large local, State, and Federal agencies, sanitarian may become specialists in particular areas of work. They apply their skills in such areas as food and drug inspection, occupational health and safety, air and water pollution, institutional sanitation, insect and rodent control, refuse and waste control, housing inspection, land use and noise control, hazardous substances, product safety, and radiation safety. Sanitarian who work in rural areas and small cities do not usually specialize in one area and are often responsible for a wide range or environmental health activities.

EDUCATION, OTHER QUALIFICATIONS, AND ADVANCEMENT



A___________ degree in environmental health is the preferred preparation for a beginning job as a professional sanitarian, although a bachelor's degree in a basic science is generally acceptable. High level positions usually require a graduate degree in some aspect of public health. In most States, sanitarian cannot practice without a__________ degree. For a bachelor's degree in environmental health the individual must have a minimum of 30 semester hours in the physical and/or biological sciences. These include chemistry, physics, microbiology, intermediate algebra, statistics, environmental health, and organic chemistry, all with appropriate laboratory work and field experience. Courses in communications including English, writing, public speaking, and graphics are also recommended. In the second two years, the recommended program includes advanced courses in microbiology, chemistry, and physics. Over 60 colleges and universities offer undergraduate and graduate degrees in environmental health.



More than 10 percent of sanitarian are female, and they have been well accepted in the field. Public recognition of the essential role sanitarian play in the maintenance of a healthy environment is growing. Positive health and disease prevention through attention to the environment has received a high priority from government.



California passed the first Sanitarian Registration Act in 1945, in which specific educational and experience requirements were defined to designate who could use the title "sanitarian." Within the United States, registration is required in 35 States, and licensure is required in 1. To obtain State registration, the individual must meet specified educational requirements, have one to two years of work experience, and pass a written and/or oral examination. Since the requirements for registration vary considerably throughout the country, the appropriate State agency should be contacted for specific information. National certification is available through the National Environmental Health Association. This usually consists of a written examination administered by the association, and those who qualify are designated as R.S. (registered sanitarian). However, individuals possessing national certification must still conform to State regulations concerning registration. Professional certification is also available from the American Academy of Sanitarian (AAS). Certification as a diplomat in the academy is based on education, leadership, experience, and successful completion of an examination.



The sanitarian is a key_____ ________ professional who applies controls to prevent hazardous exposures to environmental agents. The sanitarian delivering environmental health services must be able to properly respond to changing community needs and problems if environmental health programs are to be effective.



To ensure that qualified professionals are available to respond to the complex and technical environmental problems within California, the Local Environmental Health Programs Section of the State Department of Health Services administers the Sanitarian Registration Program. The program assures employers that persons registered have a least the minimum qualifications of education, training, and experience necessary to provide the public with competent environmental health practitioners.



Most individuals who desire to pursue a career as a professional sanitarian major in _____________health. Students majoring in environmental health need a quality undergraduate and/or graduate education that includes a solid background in the sciences, social sciences, and humanities. Students graduating from undergraduate environmental health curricula approved by the Department of Health Services are eligible to take the sanitarian registration examination after completing _____months of full-time experience in an approved environmental health program, while students graduating from approved master's degree curricula are eligible to take the examination upon degree completion. Students in environmental health must complete a minimum of 30 semester units of basic sciences and 24 months of full-time experience in an approved environmental health program before they can take the Registered Sanitarian Examination. Students qualifying by 30 semester units of basic sciences may complete 15 semester units of specialized postgraduate environmental health courses and be admitted to the examination after completing 12 months of full-time experience.



Additional personal qualifications that mark the true professional in any discipline may be applied here also. These include the ability to work effectively with people, a commitment to service and a true concern for the public health and well-being. Prospective students should also consider if they have a strong interest in science.



There is a growing realization of a need for sanitarian aides and technicians. There is also recognition of the fact that these pre-professional students need a career ladder to advance to first-degree professional (B.S.) Sanitarian and to postgraduate levels if they desire. Accredited community colleges, therefore, frequently offer a two year associate of science, which includes the general education courses, and which students may transfer to a four-year college or university to complete the training.



Upper-division students must also complete an internship with a public health agency. Required elements of an internship program include experience in at last six elements, with three of them selected from the following: food sanitation, waste management, water supply, housing and institutions, bathing places, and vector control. The remaining three elements can be any of sanitation, safety, accident prevention, land development and use, disaster sanitation, ionizing radiation, milk and dairy products, noise control, occupational health, and rabies and animal disease control.



In the States where registration and/or licensure are mandated, a State board of environmental health specialist examiners certifies to the occupational licensing bureau that the student has qualified for registration, and the bureau then issues a certificate of registration. Grounds for suspension, or limitation of a registration rest with the board. If it finds that any holder is guilty of unprofessional conduct, practice of fraud or deceit in obtaining registration, gross negligence of duty, incompetence, malfeasance or misfeasance in office, or other serious infractions, a hearing will be ordered and both the board and accused may be represented by counsel. If the person accused is found to have violated any of the provisions, the board may suspend or revoke the certificate. A suspension carries not less than two months or more than two years, but a revoked certificate may not be reinstated unless a district court reverses the board's order. These serious judgments are discussed so the student can appreciate the scope and magnitude of the professional sanitarian's responsibilities.



Advancement possibilities in this field are generally good. Qualified sanitarian can move into supervisory or administrative jobs, or take teaching or research positions. They can also advance to higher levels within a position by assuming more complex tasks and responsibilities.



EMPLOYMENT OPPORTUNITIES



Graduates of various schools offering environmental health studies are flooding the traditional locations of employment namely local, State, and, to some degree, Federal health agencies. However, program areas such as occupational health and safety and institutional environmental health have recently received great emphasis and will provide greater employment opportunities in the future. Larger metropolitan areas will continue to offer the greatest number of openings.



The U.S. Department of Health and Human Services has a number of jobs for sanitarian.



EARNINGS



Beginning salaries range from $24,500 to $31,600 and progress to $40,000 or more. Agencies in larger metropolitan areas usually pay the higher salaries. Paid vacation and sick leave, health insurance, and a retirement program are the usual fringe benefits.



Sanitarian aide/technician salaries are appreciably lower, but these workers are gaining valuable experience that will serve them well as they return for additional education.



RELATED OCCUPATIONS



Environmental health sanitarian work to promote public health and to protect the public from exposure to communicable disease and other health and safety hazards. Other professionals with similar responsibilities include public health officers, physicians, nurses, and officers of the Food and Drug Administration and the U.S. Department of Agriculture.
