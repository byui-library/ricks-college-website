AUDIENCE ANALYSIS IN TECHNICAL WRITING

 

1. The Layperson:

-- reads and enjoys, files away a few interesting facts
-- not expert in the field
-- more concerned with what things do rather than how they work
-- likes drama, narration, relates experiences, stories
-- wide range of interests, skills, education, prejudices
-- needs background information--assumes knows little or no knowledge
-- definitions--specialized words and terms defined--don't force reader to the dictionary often
-- keep it simple, everything
-- use many illustrations
-- make conclusions clear

2. The Executive:

-- many ways like layperson
-- usually has college degree but not in writer's field
-- chief concern is practical
-- busy person-define terms
-- makes decisions on what read--establish conclusions
-- doesn't want to wade through the detail but wants it available
-- needs to know background information
-- explain alternatives
-- give recommendations
-- keep purpose clear


3. The Expert:

-- in love with facts
-- reading in field (composer/conductor hearing music)
-- emphasis on the procedure not the results
-- little background
-- observations of the writer--as complete as possible
-- facts must be substantiated
-- use jargon, shorthand abbreviations
-- complicated formulae and tables
-- make inferences--general conclusions
-- list qualifications


4. The Technician:

-- brings scientist's research and engineer's calculations into being
-- intensely practical
-- education varies, college or on-the-job training
-- won't follow complicated instructions
-- little theory
-- less than 17 words in a sentence
-- short, to the point
-- illustrations are important
-- some background


5. The Operator:

-- between layperson and technician
-- various educational levels
-- no necessary background
-- avoid mathematics or formulae
-- write instructions simple and clearly
-- write in active voice
-- consider reading conditions
-- trouble shooting charts and instructions
-- use warnings to draw attention
-- be generous with visual aids


Definition In-Class Essay

Your audience is your main concern, and it should always be. To illustrate this principle, you are to select a word or principle that is in your discipline which a layperson would not probably know or understand. Take this word and write a paragraph length definition using any of the methods we described in class to amplify or extend your definition to make it more clear for your reader. You are to write this paragraph length definition six different times for six different audiences. These audiences include:

3 Layperson

3 Executive

3 Expert

3 Technician

3 Operator

3 Child

Alter your definitions to meet the needs of each audience. You will have the class period to do this.
