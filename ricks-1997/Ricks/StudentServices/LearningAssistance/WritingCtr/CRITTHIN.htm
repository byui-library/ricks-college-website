Research Papers: Critical Thinking Skills

Good Research papers equal good thinking skills.   

  • Clarity: (What is this sentence saying?)                                                     

Elaborate further on this point? Can you express this point in another way. Can you illustrate? Can you give me an example?

  • Accuracy: (A statement can be clear but not accurate.)

Is this really true? How can we check it? "Most dogs weigh over 300 pounds."

  • Precision: (A statement can be clear and accurate, but not precise.)

Can you give me more details? More specifics? "Jack is overweight." (One pound or 500?)

  • Relevance:

How is this connected to the thesis or question? How does this bear on the issue?

  • Depth: (A statement can be clear, accurate, precise, and relevant, but superficial.)

How does your answer address the complexities in the thesis or question? How are you taking into account the problems in the question? ("Just say No." is a statement often used to discourage children from using drugs, but it lacks depth became it treats an extremely complex issue--the pervasive problem of drug use among young people--superficially. It fails to deal with the complexities of the issue.)

  • Breadth: (An argument should recognize both sides of the issue)

Do we need to consider another point of view? Is there another way to look at this question?

  • Logic:

Does this really make sense? Does this flow from what you've said above? How does this follow? Before you were saying this and now you're saying that; I don't see how both can be true.
