Students enjoy peculiar spring break

by Janielle Gurney
GUR01003@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Meremy Rincon, a freshman from Woodlands, Texas; Alecia Corkran, a freshman from Portland Ore.; Cindy and Nicole Eichelberger, freshmen from Florence, Ore.; Tyler Hornibrook, a freshman from Portland, Ore.; and Eric Radford, a freshman from Woodlands, Texas, enjoy the weather on Temple Square after the afternoon session of Conference, Saturday. James Ricks / Scroll
Suits and dresses take the place of bikinis and nudity for BYU-Idaho’s 2002 spring break.

While MTV’s spring break is associated with beer and sex, listening to the voice of the prophet and other Church leaders is the style for many BYU-I students.

Many BYU-I students spent spring break in Utah or with their families. However, the top five places for spring break are Cancun, Mexico; South Padre Island, Texas; Jamaica; the Bahamas; and Panama City Beach, Fla., according to www.MTV.com.

Tips for a safe BYU-I spring break include knowing where you are going, taking notes at conference and giving your parents a kiss and hug.

Contradicting tips for MTV’s spring break include where and when not to vomit from alcohol consumption and how to avoid sex disasters.

“Beaches, bikinis, bands and babes galore ... that’s right. Spring Break 2002 is here,” according to www.MTV.com.

This inviting phrase does not seem to affect many BYU-I students who still choose to go to conference or to their homes.

Salt Lake City may be the hot spot because of conference, but many students are still choosing to go to the beach.

California was a hot spot for BYU-I students like Jason Price, a sophomore from Rexburg; Jamilyn Crowder, a sophomore from Chino Hills, Calif.; Cassie Honn, a junior from Emmett, Idaho; and Brent Gardiner a junior from Diamond Bar, Calif.

While these students were on the beach, their intentions seem different from most MTV spring break participants. They at least follow the Code of Honor .

“We’re just two wholesome girls [who were] looking for some fun,” Crowder said.

Intentions seem to be the main distinction in activities for spring break, or maybe it is the difference in perception of what is fun.

Despite the cause for different choices in activities and location for spring break, BYU-I students will continue to be a separate and “peculiar people.”
