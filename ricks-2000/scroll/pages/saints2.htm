NEW PERPETUAL FUND OFF TO A GOOD START

by John Walker
scroll staff


The Perpetual Education Fund that President Gordon B. Hinckley announced March 31 is off to a good start.

Only a week after President Hinckley announced the new program, Latter-day Saints from all over have shown significant interest in donating money to the fund, according to The Church News.

Church officials "are very encouraged by the response of Church members who wish to donate to the Perpetual Education Fund," Church Spokesman Dale Bills said.

The new fund will help a substantial number of deserving Church members become monetarily independent, through a fronting of money towards an education made possible by the fund.

The Perpetual Education Fund will service Church members around the world in places such as Asia, Africa, Mexico, Central America, South America, and the Philippines.

Assistance will be given for the most part to return missionaries, who will pay back the loans compounded by minimal interest. The interest will serves as an incentive to pay back the loan.

The program will also require no new organization and will cost nothing to administer or maintain, President Hinckley said. The fund will be directed through the Church Educational System, which manages 1,950 institute programs. Institute directors will accept applications and make recommendations for loans for candidates.

The Perpetual Education Fund is not a welfare effort, but an education opportunity to be utilized by deserving Church members across the world, Bills said.

Designed as a revolving resource, the fund will administer loans, and once repaid, the loans will be made to others in need.

"Education is the key to opportunity. This training must be done where they live. It will then be suited to the opportunities of those areas," President Hinckley said at the 171st Annual General Conference Priesthood Session.
