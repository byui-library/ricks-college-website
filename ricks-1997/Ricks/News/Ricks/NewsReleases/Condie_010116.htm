Ricks College News Release
January 18, 2001

Devotional: Spencer J. Condie

REXBURG, Idaho —Elder Spencer J. Condie, a member of the LDS Church’s First Quorum of the Seventy, counseled students about the blessings that come from having the spirit at Tuesday’s Ricks College weekly devotional.

Condie began his remarks by paraphrasing the words of Charles Dicken’s novel "A Tale of Two Cities": "It was the best of times, it was the worst of times" and compared it to the world today.

"Never in the world’s history, except maybe the time of Noah or Sodom and Gomorrah, has there been greater wickedness in the world," he said. "But is its also the best of times, and (things) are better than ever."

Condie talked about using gifts of the spirit as a solution to overcome the obstacles of the world.

"Given such a comforting catalog of privileges with the spirit, it’s almost inconceivable that we would choose to live beneath our privileges," he said. "They are open to all."

He suggested six points of drawing closer to the spirit. They include keeping the Sabbath Day holy, paying an honest tithe, searching the scriptures, drawing closer to the Lord through prayer, claiming the blessings of fasting and partaking of the sacrament worthily.

Concerning Sabbath Day observance, he said, "How well we observe the holiness of this day signifies how much we love the Lord. At least one day a week we need to push back the world from our lives and remove the noise so we can listen to the still small voice of the spirit."

On paying tithing, he said, "A married couple who pay a full, honest, tithe will, through the persistent practice of paying tithing develop those desirable traits of unselfishness."

On searching the scriptures, Condie said, "When we feast on the words of Christ, we truly can claim the promise that the words of Christ will tell us all things that we should do. The scriptures will become our life’s script. Sometimes the answers we seek are not on the printed pages we’re reading, but rather in the promptings of the spirit we receive while we’re reading."

On fasting, he said, "As you come to him in your emptiness, he will bless you in his fullness, . . . the fullness of his love. The hollowing proceeds the hallowing . . . he will break every yoke."

Next Tuesday’s speaker will be Richard N. Holzapfel, a member of the Brigham Young University Department of Church History and Doctrine. He will speak at 2 p.m. in the Hart Auditorium. Devotionals are broadcast live and repeated again at 9 p.m. on KRIC-FM.
