Course Description: This is the second of a two semester series designed to give you a basic understanding of the structure and function of the human body. This course is designed for students seeking a career in nursing or one of the many other professions in the allied health sciences. In this course we will learn the basic science necessary to understand the functioning of the human body. We will be studying the body systems not covered in the first semester. There is a weekly 2 hour lab accompanying this course. This semester in lab we will learn the anatomy of the various organ systems studied. In addition, we will be conduction physiological experiments pertaining to these organ systems. We will utilize the cadavers and models to study the organs of the systems that we cover in class. Unlike last semester, you will not have to spend as much time in the lab outside of scheduled lab hours.

Grading Policy:
1)    There will be six lecture exams, each worth 100 points. The exams will cover material from lecture and from the labs. If you miss an exam it may be made up within one week of the closing date, however, there will be a 10 point penalty for taking the exam late. If you do not make up a missed exam you will receive a 0 for that exam. It is your responsibility to inform me why you are unable to take the exam and to make arrangements to make up a missed exam. Being unprepared is not a valid reason for not taking an exam. Dates for exams will be announced in class.

2)     Since we all have an occasional bad day you may retake one lecture exam, with the exception of exam #6.  The retake exam will be given within one week after you get the exam back. Since the retake will be the same exam you took the first time you will NOT be allowed to study the old exam before the retake.  The score for the retake will be the score recorded for that exam, even if it is lower than your original exam.

3)     Your lab grade will be averaged in with the lecture exams. It will be based on lab quizzes (~100 points), lab reports (~100 points), 2 lab exams (100 points each) and attendance (50 points). I will take roll the first 5 minutes of lab. You will start with 50 attendance points, you will lose 10 points for each the first three absences. A fourth absence from lab will result in a fail for the class.

4)     Extra credit (up to 15 points) can be earned by doing the "case study" discussed in class.

5)    There are ~1065 total points possible (1050 from lecture and lab  plus 15 extra credit points). The highest cumulative score will be scaled to 100% and all other scores adjusted accordingly. Grades will then be assigned according to the following scale:

  83-86  = B                    55-59 = D

Study Guide

Chapter 15 - The Senses
 

  1.  Know which senses are classified as general senses and which are special senses.
  2.  Know the difference between somatic and visceral senses.
  3.  Know the five classes of receptors and the stimuli to which each responds.
  4.  Understand the steps necessary for perception of a stimulus.
  5.  Know the location and function of each of the following types sensory nerve endings: Free nerve endings, Merkel's disks, Hair follicle receptors, Pacinian corpuscles, Meissner's corpuscles, Ruffini's end organ, golgi tendon apparatus, muscle spindles, baroreceptors
  6. Understand the anatomy of the olfactory organs.
  7.  Know the mechanism involved in olfaction (i.e. how is the action potential generated? how many primary odors are there?)
  8. What is unique about the bipolar neurons that are the olfactory receptors.
  9. Know the four major types of papillae of the tongue and their characteristics
  10. Understand the histology of the taste buds.
  11. Know the four basic tastes, what types of chemicals produce them and which parts of the tongue are most sensitive for each.
  12. Understand the role of olfaction in taste.
  13. Know the functions of the accessory structures associated with the eyes (eyebrows, eyelids, ciliary glands, meibomian glands, conjunctiva, lacrimal apparatus)
  14. Understand the anatomy of the eye including the structure and function of the 3 layers (tunics) of the eye.
  15. Know the two major compartments of the eye and the fluids that fill them. (What causes glaucoma?)
  16. Understand how light is refracted and which structures are involved in the refraction, also know how light is focused on the retina.
  17. How are the following three processes involved in focusing close objects: accommodation, pupil constriction, convergence.
  18. Know the structure of the retina (rods and cones, bipolar cells, ganglion cells, pigmented retina, sensory retina)
  19. Understand the role of the rods and cones in vision.
  20. Be able to explain what happens when light strikes the rods (Rhodopsin, opsin, retinal, bleaching, adaptation)
  21. Understand the theory of color vision.
  22. Be able to explain why the rods produce more sensitive vision but the cones produce more acute vision.
  23. Know the anatomy of the 3 divisions of the ear (external ear, middle ear and internal ear)
  24. Know the 3 divisions of the inner ear and the function of each (cochlea, vestibule, semicircular canals)
  25. Be able to explain how sound waves get from the external ear to the cochlea.
  26. Understand the mechanism involved in converting the sound waves to action potentials.
  27. Understand how the cochlea detects different pitches.
  28. Know the relationship between the amplitude, frequency and resonance quality of sound waves and the volume, pitch and timbre of the perceived sounds.
  29. Know the difference between conductive and perceptive deafness.
  30. Understand the mechanism by which we sense information related to balance
  31. Know the functions of the vestibule (static labyrinth) and the semicircular canals (kinetic labyrinth) in balance.
  32. Know the meaning or function of the following:  Phantom pain, referred pain, adaptation, tonic receptors, phasic receptors, exteroceptors, visceroreceptors, proprioceptors, sclera, cornea, choroid, ciliary body, ciliary process, iris, macula lutea, fovea centralis, optic disc, cornea, lens, far point of vision, near point of vision, perilymph, endolymph, basilar membrane, tectorial membrane, attenuation reflex, otoliths
Chapter 16 - Autonomic Nervous System
  1. Know the characteristics and function of the ANS
  2. Be able to explain how the somatomotor and autonomic divisions of the efferent system differ.
  3. Know the following structural features of the sympathetic and parasympathetic divisions of the ANS: Where do their neurons exit the CNS?   Where do the pre- and postganglionic neurons synapse?   What is the relative length of the pre- and postganglionic neurons?  What is the ratio of pre to postganglionic neurons?
  4. Know the 3 ways sympathetic axons exit the chain ganglia and what organs each innervates.
  5. Know the neurotransmitters employed by the ANS, where they are released, how they are classified, what their actions are, and the different types of receptors involved.
  6. Know how each of the following relates to the ANS: Dual innervating, opposite effects, cooperative effects, general vs. localized effects, functions of rest vs. activity.
  7. Be able to predict the effect of the sympathetic and parasympathetic divisions on different organs (i.e. sympathetic increases heart rate, parasympathetic slows heart rate)
  8. Know the meaning or function of the following: Preganglionic neuron, postganglionic neuron, cholinergic, adrenergic, nicotinic, muscarinic, alpha adrenergic, beta adrenergic.
Chapter 17 - Functional Organization of the Endocrine System
 
  1. Know the definition of a hormone and an endocrine gland.
  2. Know the names of the major endocrine glands.
  3. Know the different types of chemical messengers.
  4. Know the different possible chemical structures of hormones.
  5. Know the three major ways the secretion of hormones is controlled and understand the principle of negative feedback.
  6. Understand the role of carrier molecules in the transport of hormones in the blood.
  7. Understand the concept of biological half life.
  8. Know how hormones are removed from the system.
  9. Understand up-regulation and down-regulation of receptors.
  10. Know the different classes of hormone receptors and their basic functions (membrane bound, intracellular).


Chapter 18 - Endocrine Glands

  1. Pituitary gland
  2.     Know the organization of the pituitary gland, its different parts and its relationship with the    hypothalamus.
  3.     Know how the hypothalamus controls the pituitary.
  4.     Know the hormones of the posterior pituitary, what controls their release, and their functions (vasopressin, oxytocin)
  5.     Know the hormones of the anterior pituitary, what controls their release, and their function (GH, TSH, ACTH, FSH,   LH, Prolactin)
  6.     Know the results of hyper and hypo secretion of the various hormones - as discussed in class.
  7. Thyroid gland
  8.     Know how thyroid hormones are transported in the blood.
  9.     Understand the effects of the thyroid hormones on the body.
  10.     Know the result of hyper and hypo secretion of the thyroid hormones.
  11.     Know how thyroid hormone secretion is regulated.
  12.     Know the role of calcitonin.
  13. Parathyroid glands
  14.     Know the hormone secreted by the parathyroid and its basic function.
  15. Adrenal gland
  16.     Know the two main layers of the adrenal and which hormones are secreted by each.
  17.     Understand the actions of epinephrine and norepinephrine.
  18.     Know the functions of mineralocorticoids (aldosterone).
  19.     Know the functions of glucocorticoids (cortisol)
  20.     Know the function of the adrenal androgens (androstenedione)
  21. Pancreas
  22.     Know the cell types of the islets of langerhans and what hormone each produces.
  23.     Know the effects of insulin, glucagon, and somatostatin.
  24.     Know how insulin and glucagon secretion is regulated.
  25.     Know the symptoms of diabetes and the difference between type I and type II diabetes.
Chapter 19 - Cardiovascular System: Blood
  1. Know the three main functions of blood.
  2. Know the components of plasma including the functions of the plasma proteins.
  3. Know sites of Hematopoiesis during embryonic development and after birth.
  4. Know the stem cell that makes all of the formed elements and know what each of the following become: proerythroblast, myeloblast, lymphoblast, monoblast, megakaryoblast.
  5. Understand the structure and function of the erythrocyte.
  6. Know the basic structure of hemoglobin (ie., oxyhemoglobin, etc.)
  7. Know the steps in erythropoiesis.
  8. Understand the role of erythropoietin in erythropoiesis, also iron, Vitamin B12 and folic acid.
  9. Know how old red blood cells are removed from the circulation and how hemoglobin is broken down.
  10. Know the causes and types of polycythemia and anemia.
  11. Know the unique characteristics of leukocytes.
  12. Know the names, relative concentrations, and functions of the granulocytes and agranulocytes.
  13. Understand what happens during each stage of Hemostasis: Vascular spasm, platelet plug formation and coagulation.
  14. Know the 3 stages of clot formation.
  15. Know what factors trigger the intrinsic and extrinsic pathways of clot formation.
  16. What is the role of Vitamin K in clot formation?
  17. Understand the role of heparin and antithrombin III in clotting.
  18. What is clot retraction and what is its function?
  19. How are clots removed?
  20. Know the basis for the ABO blood typing system and the Rh blood typing system.
Chapter 20 - Cardiovascular System: The Heart
  1. Know the anatomy of the heart and the pathway of the blood through the heart.
  2. Know the histology of the heart including  1. The structure and function of the heart skeleton. 2. The characteristics of cardiac muscle (structure, energy source, intercalated discs, etc  3. The components of the conducting system and their functions.
  3. Understand the electrical properties of the heart, including: 1. How action potentials are initiated. 2. The function of the slow and fast ion channels.  3. The autorythmicity of the heart how action potentials are generated.  4. The characteristics of the action potential and how it differs from nervous tissue.
  4. Know the major coronary arteries and veins.
  5. Be able to explain the cardiac cycle, including; when and how the ventricles fill with blood, the pressures observed in the ventricals during the different phases of the cycle, when the valves open and close, the role of the atria in ventricular filling, what happens during isometric contraction and isometric relaxation, the aortic pressure curve, etc.
  6. Know the difference between intrinsic and extrinsic control of heart function.
  7. Be able to explain Starling's Law of the Heart and the effect of preload and afterload on heart rate and force of contraction.
  8. Know the effects of parasympathetic stimulation on heart function, what the neurotransmitter is and what nerve conveys parasympathetic activity to the heart.
  9. Know the effects of sympathetic stimulation on heart function, what the neurotrasmitter is and what nerve conveys sympathetic activity to the heart.
  10. Be able to explain the baroreceptor reflex, how it is stimulated, its neural circuitry, and the effect on the heart. (cardioregulatory center and autonomic nervous system).
  11. Know how pH, CO2 and O2 levels in the blood affect the activity of the heart and the reflex pathways involved.
  12. Know the effects of extracellular ion concentrations (Ca++ and K+) on the heart.
  13. How does body temperature affect heart rate?
  14. From lab know the basics of EKG, what each wave represents, what we can learn from an EKG, and the general types of problems that can be detected by the EKG.
  15. Know the meaning of the following terms: systole, diastole, ejection phase, isometric contraction, isometric relaxation, end diastolic volume, end systolic volume, stroke volume, cardiac output, cardiac reserve.
Chapter 21 - Peripheral Circulation and Regulation
  1. Know how veins and arteries differ.
  2. Know the structure of a capillary.
  3. Know the different types of capillaries, their features, and where they can be found.
  4. Understand the structure of a capillary network (arterioles, venules, metarterioles, thoroughfare channels, precapillary sphincters).
  5. Know the general structure of arteries and veins (composition of the three tunics).
  6. Know the distinguishing features of each of the following types of vessels: Large elastic arteries, muscular arteries (medium sized arteries and small arteries), arterioles, veins. (Focus on differences in the walls of these vessels)
  7. Know the structure and function of the valves in the veins.
  8. Know what atherosclerosis is and what conditions/activities might lead to premature development.
  9. Know major arteries and veins of the body (those on handout) as well as the hepatic portal and fetal circulation.
  10. Know the general structure and the function of the lymph vessels.
  11. Know the significance of each of the following as it relates to circulation: viscosity of the blood, Laminar flow, turbulent flow, peripheral resistance, vascular compliance, Critical closing pressure, Law of LaPlace (F=DxP).
  12. Know how the cross-sectional area of a blood vessel affects blood velocity and which vessels have the greatest total area, know how this affects the pressure in these vessels.
  13. Know what pulse pressure is and how it is affected by stroke volume and compliance.
  14. Understand how fluids leave and reenter the capillaries (capillary exchange).
  15. Know what edema is and how each of the following conditions can result in edema; high blood pressure, increased capillary permeablilty, decreased plasma proteins.
  16. Understand how blood flow is regulated in capillary beds.
  17. Know how the nervous system is involved in local control of circulation.
  18. Know the effects of elevated metabolic rate on the diameter and number of capillaries in a tissue.
  19. Know what the mean arterial pressure is and the factors that affect it.
  20. Know the mechanisms responsible for short-term regulation of blood pressure including: baroreceptor reflex, chemoreceptor reflex, CNS ischemic response, the fluid shift mechanism, the stress-relaxation response, and the hormonal mechanisms.
  21. Know the role of the kidney in long-term regulation of blood pressure including the Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone mechanism and the Atrial Natriuretic mechanism.
  22. Know the 3 stages of circulatory shock as well as causes of circulatory shock.
  23. Know the function or meaning of the following: vasa vasorum, skeletal muscle pump, arteriovenous anastomoses, venous return, laminar flow, turbulent flow, pulse pressure, vasomotion, vasomotor center, vasoconstriction, vasodilation, precapillary sphincters, thoracic duct, right lymphatic duct, arteriosclerosis, stroke volume, peripheral resistance, hydrostatic pressure, osmotic pressure, renin, antiotensinogen, angiotensin I, angiotensin II, aldosterone, ischemia.
Chapter 22 - Lymphatic System and Immunity
  1. Know the functions of the lymphatic system.
  2. Know the structure and function of each of the following types of lymphatic tissue: diffuse lymphatic tissue, lymph nodules, tonsils, lymph nodes, spleen, thymus.
  3. Know the difference between specific and non-specific resistance.
  4. Know the 3 components to non-specific resistance.
  5. Know the role of each of the following chemicals in non-specific resistance: Complement, Interferon, Histamine, Pyrogens, Leukotriens.
  6. Know the role of the following cells in non-specific resistance: neutrophils, macrophages, mast cells, basophils, eosinophils, natural killer cells.
  7. Understand the inflammatory response.
  8. Know the meaning of specific immunity.
  9. What is an antigen and what characteristics contribute to the antigenicity of a molecule?
  10. Know the difference between antibody (humoral) mediated immunity and cell mediated immunity.
  11. Know the origin of B-lymphocytes and T-lymphocytes.
  12. Know the types of T-lymphocytes and their functions.
  13. Know the types of B-lymphocytes and their functions.
  14. Understand how lymphocytes are activated.
  15. Understand the process of lymphocyte proliferate.
  16. Know the 5 classes of immunoglobulin and their functions.
  17. Know the structure of a typical antibody.
  18. Understand the effects of antibodies on foreign antigens (5 modes of action).
  19. Understand what happens during the primary response to an antigen and also during the secondary response to an antigen.
  20. Know how cell-mediated immunity destroys invading pathogens.
  21. Know the four types of acquired immunity.
  22. Know the meaning of each of the following: hapten, foreign and  self-antigens, major histocompatibility complex, tolerance, opsinization, clonal selection theory.
Chapter 23: Respiration
  1. Know what respiration is and the four processes involved in respiration.
  2. Know the anatomy of each portion of the respiratory system (nasal cavity, pharynx, larynx, trachea, primary bronchi, secondary bronchi, tertiary bronchi, bronchioles, lungs, alveoli).
  3. Know the structure and function of the pleura.
  4. Know the 2 blood supplies to the lungs.
  5. Know the muscles involved in ventilation.
  6. Understand the mechanism of ventilation and what forces are involved, including a basic understanding of the general gas law.
  7. What forces tend to cause the lungs to collapse and what mechanisms prevent the lungs from collapsing?
  8. Understand the concept of compliance in the lungs and what factors affect it.
  9. Know the meaning of the different pulmonary volumes and capacities (ie. vital capacity, tidal volume, etc.)
  10. Know what is meant by partial pressure (Dalton's Law).
  11. What factors determine the concentration of a gas in a liquid (Henry's Law)?
  12. Understand the composition of the respiratory membrane and the factors that influence the rate of gas diffusion through the membrane.
  13. How is oxygen transported in the blood?
  14. Know the partial pressures of O2 and CO2 in  the alveoli, blood and tissues and be able to explain how this results in gas exchange.
  15. Understand the oxygen dissociation curve, what its significance is on oxygen transport and how it is affected by pH (Bohr effect), PCO2, and temperature.
  16. Know how carbon dioxide is transported in the blood.
  17. Know the reactions involved in the conversion of carbon dioxide to bicarbonate.
  18. Know how the respiratory centers in the medulla and the pons are involved in the control of respiration.
  19. Know how each of the following influences respiration: pH, PCO2, PO2.
  20. Know the definition of the following: upper respiratory tract, lower respiratory tract, surfactant, Dust cells, intrapulmonary pressure, pneumothorax, apnea

  9. Know the functions of the liver.
  10. Know the composition of bile and understand its role in digestion.
  11. Know the function of the gall bladder and how it is controlled.
  12. Understand the role of the pancreas in digestion (know the pancreatic enzymes and their function).
  13. Understand the function of the large intestine.
  14. Be able to take carbohydrates, proteins, or lipids through the digestive system explaining where they are digested, what enzymes are involved in each segment of the tract, what the final products are, and how they are absorbed.
  15. Understand how water and ions are absorbed.
  16. Know the meaning of each of the following terms:  Ingestion, mastication, deglutition, peristalsis, chyme, zymogen.
