Production

Free-hand - This involves using a pen to draw directly on a sheet of plastic. Many types of plastic may be used including food wrap, but clear acetate of 5-10 mils is best. The latter with a blue-tint is also useful for glare reduction. All possible types of plastic vary in price and effectiveness. Some overhead projectors are able to carry rolls of plastic which may be useful when a steady supply of transparencies is necessary. When writing, keep in mind that neatness and legibility of print are important to the viewers. Overhead projector pens will produce the best image and can be erased by a damp cloth. Almost any permanent ink pen can be used but may not project in color and could also be damaging to the transparency film. Permanent ink is best for images that will be reused and can usually be removed with plastic erasers or alcohol-based correction markers. Highlighters for overhead projection are permanent but can be useful and effective. To prevent permanent markings, highlighting can be done on a second transparency sheet placed on top of the first. Wax-based pencils can be erased with a dry cloth, but they are rarely used because they only project in black.

Plain-paper copier - This involves the use of a plain-paper copier which operates by the electrostatic film process to print transparencies. Depending on the machine, black and white or color copies can be made from paper images or slides. Copies are made with electrically charged, light-sensitive film which responds to the darkness of the image being copied. Therefore, any opaque markings can be copied.

 

Uses

 

Advantages

Bright - Can be used with normal lighting when used with a high lumen projector.

Eye Contact - The presenter is facing the audience and can watch student reactions and assess learner reactions to instruction.

Availability - Many transparencies are available with texts and can be easily produced.

Manipulatives - Projectors can be used with manipulatives to model for the entire audience.

Advance Preparation - The instructor can prepare materials in advance rather than having to use writing boards (chalk or white) to convey information.

Impact on Attitudes - Positive effects can be produced by the use of overheads while making presentations seem well prepared, more interesting and more appealing to the viewers.

Organization and Discussion - Teachers who use transparencies as opposed to notes or printed outlines seem to have better organization and more student participation during discussions.

 

 

Limitations

Not Preprogrammed - The effectiveness of transparencies depends on the presenter as a projector cannot be programmed to do anything independently, nor is there any sound provided.

Production Process Required - Non-transparent items cannot be projected until they have been converted into transparencies.

Keystone Effect - Distortions often occur, usually because the projector is at a lower level than the screen which produces the "keystone effect".
