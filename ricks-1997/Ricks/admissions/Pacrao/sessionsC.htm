Session C1

Two to Four 

Facilitator:
Kathy Lucchesi, Truckee Meadows Community College

Recorder Name:
Su-Zan Harper, Western Nevada Community College

Presenters:
Danny A. Gonzales, Great Basin College  
Lynn Mahlberg, Great Basin College  
Stan Aiazzi, Great Basin College

The first-four year program students enrolled at Great Basin College in Elko, Nevada fall semester 1999.  GBC had been a community college offering associate degrees and certificate programs.  Some of the reasons GBC sought the authority to offer baccalaureate degrees were geographical isolation, diverse population, large service area and the need for more teachers, especially in elementary education!  At the same time GBC did not want to lose their community orientation and mission.

Some of the problems encountered were no four year model in the University and Community College System of Nevada (UCCSN); political and monetary schism within UCCSN; biannual legislature; lack of buildings, resources, ambience, personnel, faculty and sufficient size of the library; plus trying to gain community approval.  Approval was required from the Northwest Association. -- Nevada Department of Education and the Board of Regents.  GBC had to hire fourteen new faculty which greatly impacted the current faculty of thirty eight.  And there was always the need for money.  GBC went to the community as well s the legislature for additional resources.

As a safety measure for the future elementary education majors, GBC worked out articulation agreements with the University of Nevada - Reno just in case an approval did not come through.  Other implementation challenges encountered were federal approval for financial aid.  That required background checks, ID badges, immunizations, certification of personnel, hiring faculty "if the money is allocated," designing a unique curriculum with field experiences in the first year, maintaining communication and not losing sight of the original mission of the college.  All challenges were met and GBC had forty-nine applicants for elementary education for fall semester 1999.

Session C2

Enrollment Management:  The Art of Improving Student Persistence

Facilitator:  Maria Kohnke, Chapman University

Presenters:
Ruth Adams, Seattle Pacific University  
Janet Ward, Seattle Pacific University

This session focused on the “art” and goals of a successful enrollment management program.  The art of enrollment management should focus on critical success factors, be based on research and evaluation, help match the student with the institution, understand costs and have leadership commitment.  The goals of enrollment management include: 

1.     Stabilizing enrollment

2.     Optimize resources

3.     Improve services and quality

4.     Reduce vulnerability to environmental forces

5.     Stabilize finances, revenue streams

6.     Link academic programs and enrollment management

7.     Improve access to information

8.     Evaluate strategies and tactics

The vision of Seattle Pacific University’s enrollment management plan was reviewed.  This includes:

1.   Graduate more students of competence & character who will change the world

2.   Be one of the premiere Christian universities

Janet and Ruth also reviewed the basic components of an enrollment management team.  This includes the following:

1.     University marketing and admissions

2.     Student academic services

3.     Student financial services

4.     Institutional research

A primary focus of this session was to explore why students leave college and to discuss intervention methods to help students succeed.

Why Students Leave

1.     Unable to adjust

2.     Uncertain goals

3.     Competing commitments

4.     Incongruent match

5.     Finances

Helps or Keys to Improve Retention

1.     Learn the campus norms

2.     Build Connections (academic and socially)

3.     Transfer successful behavior from the past to college life

4.     Learn coping skills

5.    Learn connections between course requirements and     careers

6.     Attend class

7.     Become involved

After developing Seattle Pacific’s vision statement, the university implemented strategies, including price and financial aid leveraging, common curriculum, new student profile and intervention strategies.  Implementation required departments across campus to be collaborative to meet the enrollment management goals. Seattle Pacific developed several teams who focused on student persistence strategies.

The challenge for each individual institution is to develop their own vision, find their own strategies and develop collaborative teams focused on problem solving.  Enrollment management puts the students first and asks the institution to produce programs that support retention and graduation.  This is a paradigm shift for some institutions, but in the long run the school will have greater control over trends on their campus. 

Session C3

A Post-Modern Perspective: Recruitment, Admission and Retention of International Students

Facilitator:  
 Dorothy Everrett, Carey International University

Recorder Name:  
Judy Gorian, University of California at Los Angeles

Presenter:
Dr. Elena Garate, Santa Monica College

The "continuous enrollment" process in International Education was a look at who they are and prepare them to get an education and return to their countries.  It is involved in recruitment, application, admission decisions and information seminars.  SMC has the largest community college international program in the United States.

How do we interface with our domestic admissions office?  Students will file separate applications with admissions and records.  Admissions Offices also do immigration counseling with Immigration Coordinator who takes care of everything after the student is admitted.  They also work with Contract Education in setting up a one-unit course titled  "Welcome to American Education."  This helps students become more independent.

They do have plans for an interactive web application process.  The world of international students has changed. They are sophisticated consumers who go to he web asking very technical questions.  On the web students have access to the International Student Handbook.  Students can also connect with Housing Office by e-mail; since there is no housing on campus (mostly by referrals).  The web also gives other pertinent information; such as costs, majors, key faculty and e-mail addresses.

Session C5

Student Services Via the World Wide Web

Facilitator:  
Virginia K. Johns, UC Santa Barbara

Recorder Name:  
Julia Reisinger, Oregon College of Art and Craft

Presenters:  
Jim Day, Oregon State University

Linda Edwards, Oregon State University

Oregon State University has increased the services offered over the web.  The primary purposes of increasing web-based services were to increase ease of access for students, and decrease the workload for staff.  Pages were also designed with ease of use a primary consideration.  So instead of one long page of text, there were topics of interests on the side of the page, and a search engine for the OSU web site at the bottom.

Jim Day reviewed the services related to the admissions office, including: applying over the web, checking the status of an application, scheduling visits with admissions counselors or campus tours, or reviewing articulation tables from Oregon Community Colleges or State Schools.

When applying over the web, students needed to submit a valid credit card number, but could submit the application in parts, or its entirety.  The web program checked for missing information and/or errors, so admissions staff wouldn't need to spend time making follow-up contact.

There were also areas where prospective students would request information about: specific programs, financial aid, or housing.  In these instances, automatic e-mail messages are sent to the respective departments, and information is mailed without needed follow-up contact.  Students not wanting, or not able to apply over the web could also print out applications to mail later.

Linda Edwards presented additional services offered over the web including: registering for classes, reviewing course schedules and catalogs, checking grades and transcripts, searching for textbooks and searching the library for books and other material.

Students are all assign an ID# (usually their social security number), and a General Access Pin (GAP) number (usually the students birthday).  Students are required to change the GAP# before it automatically expires, and they also have the opportunity to request specific verifying questions in the event they forget their GAP number.

While OSU also has voice response registration, last year 79% of students utilized the web-based process to register for classes.  OSU also no longer mails grades, and since students can print out the most frequently used forms in the Registrar's Office, the amount of students needing to make multiple visits to the office has declined, and staff workload has decreased dramatically in these areas.  There are fewer phone calls, and fewer lines, especially during the traditionally busy first week of classes.

Session C7

A No File Cabinet Approach

Recorder Name:  
Alice Sparrow, Embry Riddle Aeronautical University

Presenter:  
Michael Dickson, Arizona State University

Arizona State University loads applications, and transcripts data into their electronic systems when they are received.  Once the applications are complete the transcript evaluations and equivalencies are entered as well.  Records are destroyed approximately 60 days after they loaded into the system.  No file is created for the records.

Advantages of this system: takes up less space, documents are easily matched, and information is instant and up to date.  Information can easily be shared with other staff and easily integrated with other systems.

Disadvantages of this system: more time is needed for data entry when the documents are received, and if a dispute over a transcript record comes up after the record is destroyed you may have to obtain another copy of transcript.  (ASU rarely has a problem with transcript disputes.)

Other issues: make sure a system such as this meets your state and federal reports clause and FERPA.  Provide training staff in admissions and other office who that will use the system.

Session C8

Veterans and Their Entitlements

Recorder Name:  
Brenda Simmons, Santa Monica College

Presenters:  
Dave Guzman, President, National Association of Veterans Program, and Registrar at Washington State University

Mr. Guzman gave a comprehensive overview of the various educational programs under the G.I. Bill.  Such bills as the Montgomery G.I. Bill, Post Vietnam, Survivor's Dependants and Vocational Rehabilitation Bills were explained in terms of the application process, certification procedures and institutional liability.

The Commission on Service Members and Veterans Transition Assistance known as the A Transition Committee is concerned with veterans transition assistance and readjustment benefits program.  The commissioners review the adequacy and effectiveness of the programs, review the allocation of responsibility for the administration of the programs among Government agencies and make recommendations to Congress regarding the need for improvements.

A VANetCert connection has been implemented enabling schools to view student information in the VA master record and send enrollment information over the Internet.  This project is scheduled to be piloted in February 2000.

Mr. Guzman stated that 385,000 veterans are attending colleges and universities across the country, yet vast majorities are not taking advantage of their G.I. Bill.  He urged schools to hire veterans as college work-study students because they have proven to be very skilled workers.
