February 2, 1997

News Release

REXBURG, ID - It's hard to believe the Ricks College baseball team is within a few days of opening their 1997 season especially after a glace outside and the realization that winter, and the weather it brings to Eastern Idaho, is at it's peak. Coach Jerry Schlegelmilch and his boys of summer have not let the wilds of winter set them back in the past and again this year they are prepared and ready for another big season.

I "We've been working hard and are starting to show some improvement," said Schlegelmilch last week after another practice in the tight quarters of the Hart Building field house. "We looked great today against ourselves. We'll see how we do against teams that have been practicing and playing outside all fall and winter."

Schlegelmilch won't have to wait long to watch his team perform out of doors. They open their annual California spring training tour this Thursday, February 6th, against Barstow College and have 12 additional games scheduled before their return to Rexburg. Their final stop will be in St. George, Utah, to participate in the Dixie College Invitational on February 13-15.

"We look forward to this trip all year," said the coach. "We see a number of different teams and some very tough competition. It really gets us off to a good start and helps the guys understand the level on which they are now competing. Hopefully we'll get a win or two while were gone."

The Vikings, since coming under the direction of Schlegelmilch, have had little trouble getting their share of wins. Last season they won 33 games and qualified for the Region 18 Championship Tournament for the fourth straight time. Five players are back from last year's team and joining these five are five additional sophomores with experience from either the 1993 or 1994 seasons.

"We have as strong a group back as we've ever had but some injuries have really hurt us. We hope a couple of our freshman can step in a make up the difference. "

The biggest hurt came late in the fall when Schlegelmilch's ace, Heath Ferguson, went down in a heap on the mound suddenly during practice. "I had only seen that happen one other time and that was years ago when I was playing professional ball. I knew immediately he would never pitch again. His arm was gone," Schlegelmilch said.

With Ferguson out of the rotation, Schlegelmilch is relying on veteran Tyler Holland and two rookies, Mark Heriehy and Scott Cannon, to pick up the slack. Holland went 6-3 for the Vikings in 1994 and both Herlehy and Cannon came into the program with impressive credentials. As a senior at Esperanza High School in California in 1993, Herlehy went 8-1 and led his team to second place in the nation. Cannon, who is from Blackfoot, was a standout in football, basketball and baseball and finished his senior season on the mound with a 1.86 ERA.

"Mark is our number two man right now," commented Schlegelmilch. "He is starting to make his move and look pretty strong. Cannon is our number one recruit. He's had a sore arm but he threw one inning - 13 pitches - last week and looked strong. If he can get healthy, we'll be alright."

The Vikings look solid in the field and their hitting stats also show great promise. The infield is made up of three sophomores on the bases. Dane Hatch, who hit .378 in 1994 is back at first base, Ryan Pond is at second and Adam Cluff will play third base. Pond hit .419 and was an All-Region 1 8 first team pick in 1994 and Cluff hit .315 for Ricks last year. Freshmen Dan Davis and Gary Smith will rotate a short. According to Schlegelmilch, both are talented and both will play.

The outfield is also filled with sophomores. Dave Smith and Ty Kidman will trade off in right field. Smith is a left-hand hitter and Kidman bats right. In centerfield lefty Spencer Holton will rotate with Todd Miller, who hits right-handed and Eric Wallace will have left field all to himself. Wallace hit .411 and was a second team All-Region 18 selection in 1993.

Complimenting the Viking's bats offensively is their speed and quickness on the base paths. According to Schlegelmilch, Miller could be one of the fastest players he's ever coached and Wallace, Holton and Smith will also be a threat to steal a base.

The only suspect position for Ricks is behind the plate. The Vikings have three catchers listed on the roster, but the coach is still waiting for one of them the step into the starting slot. According to Schlegelmilch Brandon Boyle of Rexburg is the most talented, but Dave Bagley, who was the Viking's builpen catcher last season, is the best receiver and handles the pitchers better. Matt Moss is also listed and could step into a starting role.

The Vikings will see only road action in February and March. Their first home game is not scheduled until April 7, when they host Colorado Northwestern Community College. Scenic West Athletic Conference action will open for Ricks on March 28, in Twin Falls against the College of Southern Idaho.
