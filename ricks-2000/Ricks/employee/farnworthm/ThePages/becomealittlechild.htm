To Become as a Little Child...

Take heed that ye despise not one of these little ones; for I say unto you, That in heaven their angels do always behold the face of my Father which is in heaven.  Matthew 18:10

I find it fascinating that as Christ visited the surviving Nephites after the devastation and destruction recorded in the Book of Mormon that He tells them three different times that they are to become as a little child!  ( 3 Nephi 9:22; 11:37, 38)  He told them that unless they did so, they would not be able to receive these things (the Father, the Son, the Holy Ghost, the Gospel of Christ- I will leave it to you to determine the importance of Christ saying the same thing three different times.)  This page is dedicated to exploring what it means to become as a little child in the Lord.

I think it important to acknowledge that the western industrialized culture that we have grown up in-- does not really value children and certainly has not spent much time in studying them or trying to understand them.   Children are looked upon with contempt and anger when they act in childish, needy ways.  Our whole emphasis in socializing and disciplining children is to teach and pressure them to behave in ways that are no longer childish.  

I think Alice Miller said it best in her book "For Your Own Good" that children historically have been seen as evil, wicked and not to be trusted.  She uses the concept of "Poisonous Pedagogy" that explains how severe, cruel and manipulative practices are zealously embraced with   by parents determined to raise good and righteous children.

In another of her books, "The Drama of the Gifted Child" she writes:

"Contempt is the weapon of the weak and a defense against one's own despised and unwanted feelings.  And the fountainhead of all contempt, all discrimination, is the more or less conscious, uncontrolled, and secret exercise of power over the child by the adult, which is tolerated by society (except in the case of murder of serious bodily harm).  What adults do to their child's spirit is entirely their own affair.  For the child is regarded as the parents' property, in the same way as the citizens of a totalitarian state are the property of its government.  Until we become sensitized to the small child's suffering, this wielding of power by adults will continue to be a normal aspect of the human condition, for no one pays attention to or takes seriously what is regarded as trivial, since the victims are 'only children'.  But in twenty years' time these children will carry within themselves an experience of cruelty to which they have no access and which remains hidden behind their idealized picture of a happy childhood."

 

If you are not shocked or offended by this paragraph-- please go back and read it again-- and this time-- think about what the lady is saying!

Here is an interesting read:  Little Children and the Gospel  by Elder Jay E. Jensen

What does it mean to become as a little child?  How much does the ordinary person even know about how a child processes information?  Or how a child processes energies and feelings?  Or what is important them?  Or what frightens and scares them and what makes them feel unsafe?  Our culture has spend billions of dollars on researching technology and scientific discoveries but remains, for the most part, clueless in the treatment and understanding of its children.  The typical adult, cannot remember much of anything, concerning their childhoods and what they do remember is either idealized or with little to no energy.  The information regarding children is often saturated with the masculine values and ideals of control, discipline and parents getting their own ways.  The dilemma remains:  What does it mean to become as a little child?

C. S. Lewis writes that to be as a little child is to come under the grace and scope of the atonement, completely dependent of the Savior for salvation.  Mercy, grace and innocence that are brought into play with sincere repentance is epitomized by the forgiveness of the Savior and becoming as little children.

Joseph Campbell writes in his book (The Power of Myth p. 153-154) about the three transformations of the spirit... 

"The first is that of the camel, of childhood and youth.  The camel gets down on his knees and says, 'Put a load on me.'  This is the season for obedience, receiving instruction and the information your society requires of you in order to live a responsible life.

But when the camel is well loaded, it struggles to its feet and runs out into the desert, where it is transformed into a lion-- the heavier the load that had been carried, the stronger the lion will be.  Now, the task of the lion is to kill a dragon, and the name of the dragon is 'Thou shalt.'  On every scale of this scaly beast, a 'thou shalt' is imprinted: some from four thousand years ago; others from this morning's headlines. Whereas the camel, the child, had to submit to the 'thou shalts', the lion, the youth, is to throw them off and to come to his own realization.

And so, when the dragon is thoroughly dead, with all its 'thou shalts' overcome, the lion is transformed into a child moving out of its own nature, like a wheel impelled from it own hub.  No more rules to obey.  No more rules derived from the historical needs and tasks of the local society, but the pure impulse to living of a life in flower."

Isn't it interesting that the final stage, in this parable of sorts, is that of a child living out of its own center.  A natural fulfillment of its nature, truth and spirit.

A more general explanation of becoming as a child is focusing upon the qualities of childhood.  Qualities such as humility, innocence, meekness, gentleness, faith, hope and love...  all characteristics of spiritual gifts.  The only way to receive these spiritual gifts is to come unto Christ and be begotten by him.  In essence,  being born again and  becoming little children... willing to submit to our father.

Movie Time:

There are two movies that I am going to suggest need viewing that illustrates the dilemma of  what it means not to awaken to your own history and childhood.

The first movie is Walt Disney's The Kid .  This is a wonderful portrayal of the stages of consciousness that Russ progresses through in the movie:  

  • The stage of Oblivion... living life without a clue.
  • The stage of Agitation... as the energies of his childhood start to intrude upon his life.
  • The stage of Denial... as his hostility and meanness are projected on the kid.
  • The stage of Softening... as his meekness invites the remembering  to unfold.
  • The stage of Compassion... as he embraces and accepts his own history.
  • The stage of Enlightenment... when he lives life with congruence and honesty.

I believe that unless one awakens to living their own life... that their adulthood will simply be a replay of what happened to them in childhood.

The other movie is entitled:  Drop Dead Fred .   In this movie, the energies of a young girl's shadow (her dark- unacceptable side) is portrayed by an imaginary friend by the name of Fred.  This 'guide' has to stick around in life until she "gets it" and is able to escape the historical bonds of living her life for her mother and not herself. Towards the end of the movie Lizzy embraces Drop Dead Fred and in doing so, embraces her own lost-fragmented self.  In that moment of embrace- a healing unity occurs which allows Fred to leave.

 Both movies deal with the metaphorical "waking up" that so many of us adults need to engage in.  You cannot watch them for their entertainment value (high as it may be).  I invite you to watch them in meekness and see what they have to teach you about yourself and your own history as a child. 

I believe that to become as a little child necessitates our undertaking a spiritual quest of awakening to our own histories as children.  This adventure invites us to embrace many of the energies which we have spent our life time trying to forget.  Energies of contempt for our own weakness and vulnerabilities.  This awakening puts us in touch with our own divine and sacred center and allows  an acceptance of lost, fragmented and unacceptable parts.  The contempt that is maintained by this culture regarding weakness and vulnerability fuels the contempt we have for our children. The painful events and memories of my own childhood and my refusal to suffer them-- prevent and insulate me from the compassion that I may feel toward my own children.  The idealized notions of my own childhood keep me prisoner in my peaceful slumber of being defended.  This defensive slumber allows a repeat of wounding the next generation of children.  Being afraid to suffer my own painful history will keep me dangerous to myself and to my children.  It is time to awaken and throw off the stilted and uncomely garments of self protection and forgetfulness that allows the insensitive and harmful treatment of children to continue-- ourselves included.  The conscious healing of childhood wounds is a hard and difficult work but its spiritual fruits will forever change you .  The Physician that once said, "Suffer the little children to come unto me..."  will show you the healing path.  Why not get started?

1st and 2nd Order Change:

First order change is common in life, insights are generated, behaviors change, education is obtained, emotions are experienced and yet, things remain much the same.  In first order change there are 1000s of changes that are possible but which do nothing to impact the system of the self.  The most vital relationship of all, remains safe and undisturbed, behind years of contempt, shame, fear and denial.  

It is the relationship of 'SELF' which needs to be changed before any other.  When the self is no longer feared, ignored, denied and fragmented then this relationship can change your whole life.  It comes as a spiritual healing from the Physician.  Christ taught us that a house divided against itself cannot stand and a self that is continually at war with itself cannot heal.  Even if you believe that part of yourself is the 'enemy'... what did Christ teach us about how to treat our enemies? Love them.  In love the greater power exists and the enemy is transformed by that power.

Second order change is the death and rebirth.  It is the transformation of the heart by the power of Christ.  It is a sweet and wonderful healing of your spiritual selfhood, body and spirit.
