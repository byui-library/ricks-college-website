Daughters of Utah Pioneers continue to honor legacy of state’s forebears

by Chelsea Summers
SUM00007@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

It has been over 100 years since Annie M. Taylor organized a small group of 46 women.

These women met in the Taylor’s home with the intention of setting up an organization that would be dedicated to the remembrance of their ancestors who came across the plains with the pioneers. They formed the Daughters of Utah Pioneers organization.

They were determined to make sure the memory of their relatives was not forgotten. The purpose of this organization is “to perpetuate the names and achievements of the men, women and children who were the pioneers in founding this common wealth by preserving old landmarks, marking historical places, collecting artifacts and histories, establishing a library of historical matter, and securing manuscripts, photographs, maps and all such data as shall aid in perfecting a record of the Utah Pioneers,” according to their constituition.

The number of women who are involved in this organization has jumped from the original 46 to over 2,000 members. This group has women from many different states, along with numerous members from different parts of Canada. Several of these women are members of other faiths.“You don’t have to be Mormon to join. You just need a Pioneer ancestor,” Colette Liddell, DUP’s public relations specialist said during an interview with Deseret News.

There are, however, a few requirements; one has to be a decendent of a pioneer who came before May 10, 1869. Also, no one under age 18 can join. Once a part of the organization, they are a member for life. There are members ranging from age 18 to 102, but the average age is 60.

This group of women has recently expanded their Pioneer Memorial Museum; this is one of the 86 museums that the DUP has set up in Utah. They also have many in Nevada and Idaho.

The Pioneer Memorial Museum can be found at 300 N. Main Street in Salt Lake City.

The new part of the museum will have archives that are very important pieces of Pioneer history, including the wagon Brigham Young used when he came across the plains. They have also found over 100,000 histories of Utah pioneers and added over 10,000 pictures which are displayed throughout the museum.

“This is a wonderful way to learn about the pioneers,” Mary Johnson, the International president of the DUP, said. “We hope that our program will connect people with their relatives and promote a feeling of continuity.”

These women have been responsible for many new improvements to a number of historical sites throughout Utah and many other states. So far, over 500 markers have been placed at these sites.

They have also printed many books on different pioneer experiences. Among these were six volumes of Treasures of Pioneer Heritage and 20 volumes of Our Pioneer Heritage.

It is the DUP’s goal to continue to honor the Pioneers by making sure the people who came across the plains are not forgotten.
