RICKS COLLEGE SOFTBALL PREVIEW

      With one year of varsity softball left, there’s nothing that would be more pleasing than to see the Lady Vikings return to Region 18 and NJCAA prominence. The first four years of the program were phenomenal as the Lady Vikings won four straight Region 18 championships and finished in the top three nationally three straight times. Year 2000 saw the Lady Vikings finish fourth in the SWAC, fourth in Region 18, and unranked nationally. Year 2001 saw the Lady Vikes fail to qualify for the Region 18 tournament for the first time. Head Coach Leli’a Roberts expects more from her troops this season. "We have seven players returning with experience. We’re more mature. We’re deeper at pitcher. The league is again, very good, but we have something to prove."

      Pitching should be a strength this year with Regina Molifua and Amy Cousin taking turns. Molifua is a freshman from Oceanside, CA. "She has a lot of pitches and power. She reminds me of Monica Hurley, very durable and mentally tough. She swings a big bat, too, and can do some damage at the plate." Back from last year is Amy Cousin. She saw limited playing time in 2001, but is expected to carry a big load this season. "Amy has experience. She has power. She’s pretty imposing at 6'1". We’re counting on her and Reg."

      Behind the plate, Alicia Yost of Mountain Home, ID, returns. She started most of last season and has shown the ability to handle the pitchers. "Alicia is a team leader. She’s one of our most disciplined players as far as her work ethic and practice routine goes. She has a strong bat." There is depth at first base. Emily Lister of Portland, OR, returns from last season. She has improved strength wise and handles the bat well. Freshman Nicole Steiner of Rexburg will see plenty of playing time here. "Nicole has a great attitude and is a hard worker. She can help us right away." Roo Bramwell of Orem, UT, walked on last year and saw playing time at third base. Coach Roberts has moved her to second base this season. "She’s one of the fastest kids on the team. She’s very quick, can play defense, especially on the bunt, and she is a strong hitter. She’ll bat in one of the first three spots. She can get on base, she can move runners, and she can switch hit." Jacie Summers of Parker, CO, will back her up. "Nicole is a freshman. She has a good attitude and works hard." At third base is Susie Goodman of Payson, UT, and a transfer from Barton CC in Kansas. "She’s got a quick glove like Sarah Breiner, a girl I played with. She fundamentally very sound. Kali Baird of Idaho Falls will back her up. Kali can pitch, too, when we need her." Shortstop belongs to Lisa Rackham, a sophomore from Brigham City, UT. She’s solid all-around. "Lisa started last year. She makes the tough plays. She’ll be a leader this year."

      There is also experience in the outfield. Sophomore Natalie Vance of Sandy, UT, will move from left field to center field. "Natalie sees the ball well when it comes off the bat. She really gets a good jump on the ball. She’s our slap hitter. She gets on base a lot and she’s a very smart and aggressive base runner. She’ll get a lot of steals." Sophomore Jennie Lockhart will move from right field to left field this season. "She has an uncanny way of making a play when we need it most. She finds a way to get on base, a way to advance, a way to score or drive in a run." In right field, the Ashley’s have taken over; Ashley Buchanan of Ontario, OR, and Ashley Wright of Payson, UT. "They’ll split time until one of them takes over the position. They both have good bats."

      The Lady Vikings look improved. They’ll have to be to get back to the playoffs. Salt Lake and Utah Valley are solid again. Dixie is improved. Treasure Valley returns one of the leagues top pitchers. "I think we’ll do well. I thing Treasure Valley is the team to beat. They have a great pitcher and want to prove that their first season was no fluke."
