Damage Deposits
Most housing complexes require a security deposit to cover any unusual damages, special costs, or charges you may incur.  If you are having a problem with your security deposit or other housing concerns, discuss it with your manager, or visit the Housing Office located in the Kimball Building.

Dance Office
The ASRC Dance Office is located in the Manwaring Student Center (Room 373) and coordinates and regulates all dances on the Ricks College Campus including ASRC, Church, Club, and Housing Unit dances.  Its purpose is to assist in providing wholesome music and dancing on campus.  The following policies and procedures apply.
Calendaring and Scheduling:  Each sponsoring organization must calendar and schedule its proposed dance with the Scheduling Office, Manwaring Center, (Room 250, phone 356-2248).  After scheduling the building and room, the next step is to schedule your Music and D.J.  (Call Doug Smith, Programs Director 356-2280).
D.J. and Music:  ASRC approved music and D.J.’s are required for all on campus dances.  The Dance Office will provide your dance with approved music and a disc jockey, for a minimal fee ($35.00 MC ballrooms, $45.00 for other buildings on campus) to be paid in advance.  Scheduling of a D.J. must be completed at least one week in advance of the dance or activity.
Lighting:  Strobe and flashing lights are not appropriate for campus dances.
Volume:  Music volume should be low enough to allow two persons standing side by side to carry on a normal conversation.  (Church Bulletin #24 - June 1982).
Security:  ASRC sponsored dances provide paid security to enforce the dress and grooming standards at dances.  Responsibility to maintain college dress and grooming standards at dances other than those sponsored by ASRC rests with the sponsors (bishops, advisors, dorm head residents etc.).
Sponsoring and Publicity:  Stakes, wards, clubs, organizations and housing units may provide dances for their own membership and in some cases, invite guests.  Under no circumstances should “invited guests” include an open invitation to the general student population.  Publicity and invitations to attend a dance should be directed at a target audience, such as a particular stake, ward, or club.  Announcements and publicity like “everyone is invited” is prohibited unless the dance is sponsored by ASRC.  Do not put advertisements for your dance in the Scroll, hang posters, or pass out flyers on campus.

Dances,  Dress Standards 
The Minimum Dress Standard for all campus dances is regular classroom attire.  This standard is intended to emphasize a neat, clean, dressy and modest appearance.  For a detailed list, look under dress and grooming standards, R Book.
Formal Dance Attire:  Men:  Tuxedo (optional), Suit, Sport Coat, or Dressy Sweater (Advised).  Shirt and Tie, Dress Slacks, Dress Shoes (Required).  Ladies:  Formal (Optional), Sunday Dress (Advised), Dress Shoes (Required).  Hemline and slits knee length or below.  No sleeveless dresses, spaghetti straps, low cut necklines, immodest use of sheer/see-through materials, open backs below the shoulder blades).
Special Theme Dances:  Should be consistent with campus standards, appropriate dress will be specified in the publicity.

Dean of Students Office
The Dean of Students Office assists students with a variety of needs.  These include questions regarding the Honor Code and dress or grooming standards, international student advising, withdrawing from college, and assisting with personal concerns.  Students found in violation of the Honor Code and dress and grooming standards will be required to report to and work with the Dean of Students Office.

Development and Alumni Relations
The Ricks Development and Alumni Relations Office (a division of LDS Foundation) provides services for alumni, friends, businesses, parents, and students associated with Ricks College.  It encourages and facilitates private contributions to enhance the quality of a Ricks education and to bless the lives of students.  This private support becomes the resources necessary to help Ricks provide the best possible teachers, facilities, and academic programs and makes available educational opportunities to deserving students through scholarships, student loan funds, and other programs.  The goal is two-fold:  to augment with private contributions the support the Church provides through tithing dollars and to serve those who want to give something of value back to Ricks as an expression of appreciation for the blessings they have received in their lives.

Disabled Student Services
Following admission guidelines, Ricks College is committed to the full inclusion of all individuals who can benefit from educational opportunities.  The Office of Services for Students with a Disability (SSD) provides academic accommodations for all qualified students who have documented learning, emotional, and/or physical disabilities (as defined by Section 504 and the ADA) and have need for accommodation.
The ultimate goal of SSD is to promote individuals’ independence within the academic atmosphere of the College.  Students are assisted in this process by SSD and by the support services and programs available to all Ricks College students.
When a student with a disability requests a specific accommodation they must submit (written) documentation of the disability and need for accommodation.  The following policies would ordinarily be followed:  The Office of Services for Students with a Disability may provide services so that a student with a disability can maximize their potential at Ricks College with a minimum of impact from the effects of the disability  “Reasonable Accommodations” may include (but not be limited to) such services as:  accessibility, extended time at tests, volunteer note takers, preferential seating, alternate testing, and adaptation for special needs.  The office for Students with a disability may also provide a wide range of services as outlined in the Ricks College Catalog.
The student will have on file with the Office of Services for Students With a Disability, independent professional documentation which supports the need for the accommodation.  Documentation must be filed prior to accommodation provision.  The Office of Services for Students With a Disability will evaluate the documentation and approve or disapprove the requested accommodation, or take additional action to secure more information.
A written description of the specific accommodation may be sent to the instructor if needed or requested by the student.  With the student’s permission, clinical information regarding the disability may also be shared with the instructor if necessary to provide better services.
Students and teachers may contact the Office of Services for Students With a Disability if problems and concerns occur.  An attempt will be made to resolve the problem or seek assistance from the administration.  Grievance procedure will provide for a discussion with the student and the Director of the Learning Assistance Labs and the Director of Services for Students With a Disability.  If agreement is not reached at that level, the student may request a formal hearing before the “Exception to Policy Committee”.  The results of that hearing may be reviewed by the President or President’s Council for final decision.  Technical assistance and consultation are always available.  For further information contact Richard G. Taylor, Ph.D., 2nd floor McKay Library 356-1158 voice/TDD.

Drugs
The use or possession of illicit drugs is cause for immediate dismissal from school.    There is no second chance.  The same policy will apply to any person having any part in supplying illicit drugs or assisting others to procure or use illicit drugs.  The school will not wait for the disposition of any court proceedings before continuing with student discipline in drug matters.  The Dean of Students Office will make its own timely, independent investigation.

Eligibility
The Dean of Students  makes grade checks on all students involved in activities or events at the beginning, mid-term, and close of each semester to determine their eligibility to participate.

Eligibility Requirements 

Academic Requirements:
Eligibility to participate in Ricks College events and activities is based upon cumulative GPA.  You will be considered making satisfactory academic progress if you maintain a cumulative GPA including transfer credit, above 2.0 and if your satisfactory progress is above 75%.

Departmental and Organizational Standards:
Some programs, groups, clubs, or organizations may establish a higher academic standard than that which has been adopted by the college.  When membership in a national organization requires a standard which must be met in order for a student to participate in that organization or activity and that policy is different than the college standard, the requirement set by the national organization may be followed.

Probation (Academic):
Should your cumulative GPA fall below 2.0 or if your satisfactory progress is below 75% you will be placed on academic probation.  The Dean of Students will notify you and the program advisor or sponsor, in writing, of your ineligibility to participate in the program in which you are involved.  You will be removed from probation when your cumulative GPA once again equals or exceeds 2.0 and your satisfactory progress is above 75%.

Emergency Telephones
A total of twelve emergency telephones are placed at various locations around the main campus area.  These telephones mounted on the walls of campus buildings are identified by a blue light directly above the phone box and the words “Emergency Phone” written in red letters on the door to the telephone box.  All campus elevators have emergency phones mounted inside.  These phones may be used to summon assistance in the event of an emergency.  These phones are directly connected to the campus 911 system and upon activation the Campus Police dispatcher is immediately advised of the location of the emergency telephone that is activated.  To activate the emergency telephone simply turn the handle in the direction indicated on the phone box cover, open the door and push the button.  When the phone is answered you will have two way communication with the dispatcher who will send the appropriate emergency service personnel to your location.  It is a criminal offense to use any emergency telephone to make a false alarm or to report false information.  Violators will be subject to criminal prosecution and/or college disciplinary action.
