      The purpose of English 215 is to help you learn to write clearly, logically, and convincingly.

Particular Objectives

Specifically, E215C will help you to respond to common writing problems. You will learn to do the following:

1. Break through "writer's block."                                                     

 2. Focus, develop, and organize your ideas.     

3. Write to others in your discipline (E215C focuses on the social sciences).

4. Use language precisely and effectively.

5. Revise and edit your writing.

6. Evaluate your own writing as well as that of classmate's.

7. Use legitimate logic.

8. Work comfortably and professionally with library resources, Internet, and documentation formats.

Requirements and Grading

  • Memo and Formal Proposal
  • Five critical papers (3-4 pages, double-spaced)
  • Research paper (8-15 pages, complete with Abstract, Table of Contents, List of Figures, etc.)
  • Formal Evaluation paper
  • Five Editing workshops
  • Formal Presentation of Research
  • Pop quizzes (sometimes daily)
  • Midterm and Final exam
  • Journal entries (at least 55)

Major essays: Major papers will give you experience writing in situations typical of your field of study and academic preparation. Generally, these papers should be about 1,000 words (four typewritten pages, double-spaced); some of them will require research.

  Preparation and Incidental Assignments: Occasionally, I will ask you to respond to an idea you have encountered in our reading materials. These responses, personal and informal, will require about 7-10 minutes per entry. We will write these in class on the computers. Why? You need practice, practice, practice to develop your writing process.

Submitting Papers

Please submit hard copies of formal papers. All other assignments will be saved to the network. Type (keyboard) all essay assignments according to the documentation format of your chosen discipline. Most of you will follow either the APA or MLA format outlined in numerous, readily-available handbooks.

Note                                                                                                                    

This is not a computer class.                                                       

It is a composition class. We will use the computers only as they support our writing. 
The class load assumes you have basic keyboarding skills.

1. We have only one printer and 25 machines; therefore, printing hard copies in the computer lab is not an option. Please arrange your schedule accordingly. Print your hard copies early.

2. Be wise and back-up every assignment you submit. If you lose a document and have no record of your work, you, obviously, can't get credit for the work.

Attendance

Absences affect your standing in the class. Quizzes and objective tests cannot be made up. Please--no late papers or assignments. Papers drop one full grade for every 24 hours late. Papers are due at the beginning of class on the designated dates. No extra credit.

BYU Credit

Students completing the course and earning a letter grade in E215 will have successfully met BYU's Advanced Composition requirement (E315). Several other colleges/universities accept E215 as the equivalent of a second-level writing course. Therefore, E215 must be approached as if it were for juniors and seniors.

Reasonable Accommodation

In compliance with applicable disability law, qualified students with a disability may be entitled to 'reasonable accommodation.' It is the student's responsibility to disclose to the teacher any special need she/he may have before the end of the first week of class.
