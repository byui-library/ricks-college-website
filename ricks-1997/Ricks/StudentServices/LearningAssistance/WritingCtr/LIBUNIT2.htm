MAIN LIBRARY CATALOG
Now go the library's main catalog to find books that relate to your topic.

  • Conduct a key words-search, a subject headings-search or an author-search, using the key terms and names of authorities that you have gathered from the LCSH and from your background reading. Although some books you find might be too general, you may be able to use a chapter or two.
  • Scan indexes to find references to your specific topic using both specific topic terms and general subject terms.
  • Pay attention to dates. Out-of-date information may weaken your research paper.

PERIODICAL INDEXES
Indexes list specific titles, authors and publishing information for articles, scholarly papers, or periodicals.

  • Popular literature indexes, i.e. the Reader's Guide to Periodical Literature indexes articles from Sports Illustrated, Time, U.S. News and World Report, etc. While articles from these popular magazines may give you an overview of your topic, often they tend to be too general for use in a formal college paper.
  • Paper indexes such as the Biological and Agricultural Index, or the Applied Science and Technology Index can give you help in specific areas such as statistics, specific examples, case studies and/or results from laboratory experiments, which may help you prove your thesis.
  • Computer indexes such as Academic Abstracts may also provide assistance. It has two types of indexes--full text (the index has the entire article) and summary. If an article is not a full-text article, be cautious about assuming our library will have the article. Scan titles listed under subjects to see if the index will be a good source for you. Check to see if the library has the indexed article.

NEWSPAPERS AND GOVERNMENT DOCUMENTS
Professional writers for the Chicago Tribune or the Los Angeles Times summarize the current affairs of your topic and often give names of authorities. Indexes to newspapers, such as Newsbank, are filled with articles that have been carefully chosen out of hundreds of established newspapers. The New York Times has its own index dating back to the 1800s. You may also find pertinent information in government documents which are arranged by the agency that produces them. The Index to Government Periodicals covers many articles in magazines published by various government agencies.
