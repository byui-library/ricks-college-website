TRACK AND FIELD GETS NEW HEAD COACH AND REGIONAL WIN

Doug Stutz has been named head track and field coach at Ricks College. "I'm excited. It's a nice opportunity for me." Stutz will replace Ferron Sonderegger. Sonderegger announced earlier that he was stepping down from the position to teach in the foreign language department and spend more time with his family.

Stutz arrived at Ricks College in 1988 as an assistant coach in cross country and track and field. In 1992, he was named head coach of the men's and women's cross country program. A dynasty followed as the men's team won the Region 18 crown five times in the Stutz's eight years, finishing second twice. The men placed in the top seven at the national finals on five occasions and won the NJCAA Championship in the fall of 1999. The performance of the women's team has been remarkable. In eight years they have won the Region 18 title seven times, finishing second on the other occasion. Stutz will continue as head coach of cross country and in his position in Continuing Education.

When Sonderegger officially closes out his career as head track and field coach at the end of this season, he will go out with 13 women's Region 18 championships and 12 men's championships, all of that in thirteen seasons as head coach. The Vikings repeated as men's and women's champions this past weekend at the Region 18 finals hosted by Utah Valley State College. "It's a great way to finish," said Sonderegger.

ATHLETIC HALL OF FAME INDUCTEES

Two outstanding athletes from the 1980s are the latest inductees into the Athletic Hall of Fame. Greg Hunter, football offensive lineman, and Georgia Johnson Palmer, track and field runner, were honored at the Athletic Awards Banquet in April and will be added to the Hall of Fame displayed in the Hart Building foyer.

GREG HUNTER--Football. Members of the offensive line in football often toil in anonymity. Hunter's talent as a guard, however, was impossible to ignore. Ricks placed 11th nationally in 1985 with a 7-3 record during his freshman year. A year later with Hunter as one of the team captains, Ricks was only four votes short of being named the national junior college champion. The 1986 team never lost a game, but had one tie to mar its season record. The highlight of the year came in a thrilling last-second, come-from-behind Jayhawk Bowl win in Kansas. For his efforts Hunter was named a First Team All-American at Ricks. He was a member of the Western States Football League's First Team two years and was given the Clyde P. Packer Ideal Male Athlete Award. A returned missionary from the Chile Vina Del Mar Mission, Hunter was an offensive starter at Brigham Young University for two years, graduating in 1989.

GEORGIA JOHNSON PALMER--Track and field. Running for herself, or as part of a relay team, Palmer knows her way around the track. As a freshman in the spring of 1986, she took All-American honors with a fifth-place finish nationally in the 400-meters. Earlier she had won the regional title in the 400-meters in a school record-setting 58.02 seconds. She also won the 200-meters at the regionals and was a member of the winning 1,600-meter relay team. As a sophomore she repeated for All-American honors placing second nationally in the 3,200-meter relay, running the anchor leg, with teammates: Bobbi Hawkins, Paul Egan and Karen Booker. As a member of the 1,600-meter relay, she placed fifth nationally. In the regionals, Palmer was a member of the championship 3,200 and 1,600-meter relay teams, the latter covering the course in a school record 3:57.33. During her sophomore season she also won a 400-meter race at a meet in Provo, Utah, in 58.97.

SOUTHERN UTAH HERITAGE CHOIR PRESENTS CONCERT AT RICKS

The Southern Utah Heritage Choir from St. George, Utah, will make its first appearance in Rexburg with a concert in the Barrus Concert Hall on Friday, May 12, at 7:30 p.m. With more than 160 voices, the choir is one of the rising choral stars in the country and has even performed in the famed Carnegie Hall in New York City. Tickets are $6 for the general public and available from the Ticket Office by calling 356-2230 or over the Internet at www.byui.edu/tickets.

The choir was organized in 1994 at the recommendation of the director of the St. George Temple Visitors' Center. Following a successful concert entitled "Celebrate America," planned for area citizens and a special gathering of governors from the western United States, it was decided the choir should become a permanent organization. Now consisting of 200 musicians from all walks of life, this community choir is comprised of dedicated people from throughout southern Utah and Nevada. The choir continues to enjoy a close relationship with the St. George Temple Visitors' Center.

WICKHAM NAMED PRESIDENT OF MPAEA

Larry Wickham of BYU-Ricks Continuing Education was installed as president of Mountain Plains Adult Education Association on April 27 at the annual conference held in Salt Lake City. MPAEA is a professional organization representing nine states with over 500 members. Wickham has served this organization for over 20 years as a member of the Board of Directors, secretary and treasurer.

WEYLAND HOSTS PHYSICS ASSOCIATION

Jack Weyland, a member of the Ricks College Physics Department faculty, had the daunting task of hosting the regional American Association of Physics Teachers meeting in Twin Falls this spring. As president elect of the chapter, Weyland was asked to host the meeting in conjunction with the Idaho Academy of Sciences. The two groups each had their own programs, with plenary sessions that include nationally known science figures in common. The AAPT meeting is a highlight of the year for many, as teachers and a few students swap successful classroom ideas, demonstrations, and research.
