                               RICKS COLLEGE
                     DEPARTMENT OF RECREATION EDUCATION
                            RE 102 - BACKPACKING

COURSE DESCRIPTION:  Minimal impact skills for high country 
expeditioning, including equipment selection, route planning, camp
procedures, and emergency preparedness.

STATEMENT OF PURPOSE:  The purpose of this course is to provide students 
with a working knowledge of the principles, techniques, and associated 
skills required to live in the wild outdoors.  The course will focus 
mainly on the needs of the individual or family camper, but will also 
apply to leaders who will be preparing for the management of extended 
outdoor trips.  The high country is a particularly fragile environment, 
requiring travelers to obtain knowledge of minimal impact skills and 
trail techniques, However, serenity often masks the violence of weather 
and other factors which have joined to sculpt the natural wonders the 
traveler comes to see. The untrained visitor may be a hazard not only 
to the wilds, but to himself as well.  By joining in an effort to raise 
the awareness and attitudes of backcountry travelers, this course may 
help provide a public committed to the adoption of a national wilderness 
ethic andperpetuate the careful use of wild lands.

COURSE OBJECTIVES:
     A. To give real experience to Advanced Outdoor Living Skills 
	students in the field of moving, extended, adventure-type camps.
     B. To train those who travel or will travel beyond the road head in 
	skills necessary to enjoy yet preserve our wilderness lands.
     C. To provide background experience and methods for teaching for 
	those wishing to share this field of study with others.
     D. To expose participants to country they may have never seen before 
	or may never see again.
     E. To develop within students a sense of responsibility to their 
	fellowman by learning to conserve a dwindling resource and to 
	acquire	those skills necessary to lend a helping hand in 
	emergency situations.

OUTINGS

GRADING BREAKDOWN:
The final grade will be computed according to the students completion of 
the following criteria (on campus):
	1. Lab Attendance - A minimum of two (2) nights and three (3) days 
	in a mobile camping situation.  NO EXCEPTIONS!  All required 
	theory is put into practice and competencies will be signed off.  
	All labs will leave from the parking area North of the Hart 
	Building at the prescribed time.  NOTE:  It is understood that 
	personal commitments may cause conflicts in some cases. If this 
	happens, students will make arrangements to attend another 
	outdoor activity.  It is difficult to make these up so the missing 
	of a scheduled class outing should be avoided.
	2. Exams - Two major exams will be given:  A written final and a 
	practical final.  NO unexcused make-ups will be given.  Early 
	testing will not be allowed.
     	3. Attendance - Two absences will be allowed.  Be There!

FIELD RELATED GRADING CRITERIA (off campus):
     A. ENVIRONMENTAL ETHICS - Goal:  For students to develop a personal 
	understanding of environmental ethics and practice those ethics 
	under field conditions.
     B. BASIC CAMPING SKILLS - Goal:  For students to learn those 
	basic camping skills necessary for all travel beyond the roadhead.
     C. RATIONS - Goal:  For students to be able to plan, secure, and 
	handle the foods necessary for nutritious meals in the field.
     D. EQUIPMENT - Goal:  For students to know about equipment and 
	be able to apply their knowledge to leadership situations.
     E. CLOTHING - Goal:  For students to develop judgement about purchase, 
	care, and design of outdoor clothing.
     F. HEALTH AND SANITATION - Goal:  For students to learn and practice 
	environmentally sound techniques of health and sanitation.
