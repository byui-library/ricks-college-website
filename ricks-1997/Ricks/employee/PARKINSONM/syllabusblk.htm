I. COURSE DESCRIPTION

Fitness for Life is an individualized approach to fitness for students of all abilities. During the scheduled class time, lectures will be given on preparing a fitness program, aerobic exercises, nutrition, stress, and strength and flexibility programs. In addition, all students must fulfill an 8 week personalized fitness contract consisting of at least 4 exercise sessions a week.

II. OBJECTIVES

During the semester, each student will be able to write a personalized fitness program, determine their fitness levels, increase their knowledge and appreciation for the human body and how to properly care for their body so they can remain fit for life.

SPECIFIC OBJECTIVE/PURPOSES

To develop in each student a higher respect for the miracle of the human body, therefore increasing the desire to care for body properly.

To teach each student how to plan a personalized fitness program.

To raise each student's level of endurance and cardiovascular condition.

To instill in each student a need to 'commit' to a healthy lifestyle.

To teach each student how to improve their strength level.

To teach each student how to improve their flexibility.

To teach each student a variety of aerobic activities and the pros and cons of each.

To teach each student how to relieve stress in their lives.

To teach each student about proper nutrition and how to eat better and feel better.

To teach each student a proper weight reduction program.

To increase each student's self-esteem through self- improvement activities.

III. PHILOSOPHY

My philosophy on Fitness for Life is that it should be an enjoyable, yet educational class improving each student in all five basic areas of health.

IV. GRADING

To qualify for any grade you must complete 8 weeks of exercising four times a week or 40 exercise sessions. I will accept a week of three and you can make-up with a week of five, but I never accept weeks of two exercises sessions only. If you do not exercise the 32 times, you will fail the class.

I am very picky about how the logs are to be filled in. Below is an example of how to fill them in before and after chapter three's lecture.



























Fitness evaluations will be given at the beginning, middle and end of class to check fitness levels and progress. To pass the class, you must participate in all three. Your final grade will be determined by your final fitness test. The grading criteria is based on the chart on page 20 of your book. (Superior and Excellent = A, Good = A-, Fair = B+, Poor = B, and Very Poor = B-)

If you do not take the final fitness test, you will fail the class. The only exception is with a Dr. excuse.

Standard chapter tests will be given during the first half of the semester at the testing center. All five tests are open book. A score of 80% or higher is needed to pass. Any score between 80% - 100% constitutes an A grade. Any score of 79.8 or below is an F and the test will need to be retaken. Take them when assigned as block classes move quickly. If you do not pass all five test with 80% or higher you will fail the class.

There are a few assignments for the class. They are all do or die's. If you do them you get full credit, and if you do not, you will be dropped one grade notch subtracted from your final run grade for each missing assignment.

This class will meet every day and you will be expected to attend and participate. If you miss more than four times, you will not qualify for a pass/fail. The major portion of the grade will come from the Fitness for Life standards and your individual C.V. Program.

Late work will not be accepted past its due date. I WILL NOT ACCEPT ANY WORK PAST THE LAST FRIDAY OF THE LAST FULL WEEK OF YOUR TERM. Make sure you arrange your schedule accordingly.

A Grade

1. Be in either Category V or VI for cardiovascular endurance in either the 1 & 1/2 mile run, 3 mile walk, 12- minute swim or 12-minute bike test.

2. Hand in both cardiovascular contracts (one is a computer contract) and at least 8 weekly exercise logs, totaling 32 days of exercise.

3. Hand in the fitness appraisal and the strength appraisal.

4. Have passed all five test with an 80% or above.

5. "Aunt Edna"

6. Hand in a nutritional analysis.

1. Be in category IV for cardiovascular endurance.

2. Hand in both contracts and at least 8 weekly exercise logs, totaling 32 days of exercise.

3. Hand in both fitness appraisals and the strength appraisal.

4. Pass all 5 tests with a score of 80% or above.

5. "Aunt Edna"

6. Hand in nutritional analysis.

Since there are 8 weeks in a block, you must exercise each week to get full credit. If you become ill, go to the medical center and either copy the receipt or a Dr. excuse to your weekly log. If you do not feel super a couple of the days, I will also give exercise credit for going to the media center and watching Covert Bailey's 20 minute video's. The choices are Body Fat in America 3768, Crash Diets don't work 3769, The Target Diet 3771, Apples and Pears 3772, or 12 Years to Getting Fat 3773. On your log for the day you watched the video, write it what video you watched and 2 things you learned from it.

PASS/FAIL

Students have the option of either selecting a letter grade or a pass/fail. In order to qualify for a pass/fail grade you must pass all tests, turn in both contracts and at least 8 logs and the fitness appraisal, strength, appraisal, nutritional analysis and "Aunt Edna" and have less than four absences.

Plan to make room in your lifestyle for out-of-class fitness activities. Each weekly program must consist of exercising on your own at least 4 times a week.

V. DRESS

When we dress down to participate in class activities, Ricks College clothing must be worn. You may wear biker shorts underneath or sweats on top. Bring your activity card to dress down. Lockers are not provided but may be purchased if you feel one is necessary.
