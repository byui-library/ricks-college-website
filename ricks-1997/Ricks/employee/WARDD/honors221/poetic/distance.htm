At a Distance

What do they think,
these students;
these members of community
when they walk by
on their way to college
with backpacks slung naively
over one arm or
drive by on their way to work
in new or old cars
and look casually in our
direction, noticing us
leaning against the steel siding
and side window of our
rusting LDT-still, motionless,
wrapped in each other's embrace?

Could they perceive our expressions-
two faces turned in opposite direction-
would they understand
the faithfulness involved
as we hold one another
so close,
like statues,
without stirring,
holding back breathings
that could only intrude,
because the tension to separate
is becoming too great to
stand side-by-side,
alone,
and stay together?

I'm sure they smile
to themselves, feeling a bit
embarrassed that they intruded
upon our intimacy by nothing
more than a glance turned
toward us. They probably feel that
here, in this house
for this couple,
life is good, and take from the sight
of our embrace a spark of hope,
perhaps to rekindle their own
commitment to love more perfectly-or
to love at all.

But we,
we who feel the weight of the other's
arms around us,
who smell the accustomed scent
of skin and hair,
who feel the intimate curves
of our bodies touching and untouching;
we know that our love
burns anxiously within us . . .
no . . . burns us within
by a tormenting flame
that promises to purify,
but not without destroying
something of me,
something of you,
as we are . . . here . . . now . . .
holding each other
in place of letting go.

David L. Ward
September 23, 1993
