RICKS TO HOLD GRADUATION ON SATURDAY

Ricks College will hold its 111th graduation Saturday, April 22. Elder Neal A. Maxwell of the LDS Church's Quorum of the Twelve will speak at the commencement exercises at 9 a.m. in the Hart Auditorium. Commencement will be preceded by the traditional academic march at 8:40 a.m. by faculty and graduates. Diplomas will be handed out at the various division convocations, all scheduled for 11:30 a.m. at various locations. The Class of 2000 of 3,012 graduates has 1,156 males, 1,856 females, 432 married students and 77 foreign students. This year's graduating class is slightly less that last year's record-breaking number of 3,039 students. Graduation activities will be held Thursday through Saturday. (Please see list below for details.) The semi-annual Emeritus Breakfast will be held in conjunction with graduation on Saturday in the MC Special Events Room. Alumni who attended Ricks during or before 1950 and retired Ricks employees are invited. The cost is $5 and reservations are not required. Distinguished emeritus service awards will be presented to Ivan and Helen Miller and Hal and Carma Ferguson.

PREMIERE OF SACRED WORK TO BE PERFORMED

An evening of sacred music, including the premiere of a work by Gaylen Hatton and Elder Neal A. Maxwell, will be presented on the eve of Ricks College commencement. The "Expressions of Faith" concert on Friday, April 21, will feature the Ricks College Collegiate Singers and Chamber Orchestra, which will leave the following day for a two-week tour through the Northwest. The concert will be held at 8 p.m. in the Barrus Concert Hall of the Snow Center for the Performing Arts. Tickets are $2 for the general public and $1 for Ricks students. They may be purchased at the Ricks College Ticket Office by calling (208) 356-2230 or over the Internet at http://www.byui.edu/tickets. The highlight of Friday's performance will be the premiere of "Apotheosis" composed by Hatton, an emeritus BYU music faculty member, with words by Elder Maxwell. Several of the works will be recorded in the Barrus Concert Hall during two May 5 concerts at 6 and 8 p.m. The recordings are being done for the LDS Archival Recordings Project, whose mission is to produce archival recordings of compositions and musicians representative of the LDS heritage of music.

BEDNARS IN HIGH DEMAND AT GRADUATION TIME

The calendar of President David A. Bednar and his wife Susan is very full these days B and nights. Recognition banquets, graduation ceremonies, and final concerts fill their time to capacity. And yet, they go with smiles on their faces, warmth in their hearts, and final departing words of wisdom. Speaking at the ASRC Recognition Banquet, President Bednar encouraged the student leaders to use their education experiences to be more effective in life. As the new ASRC officers were sworn in, President Bednar taught,  "The mark of a leader's effectiveness is the involvement that occurs in the lives of other people." Leaders facilitate the involvement of others, and it may take years before results are evident or recognized. For example, parents should not judge their effectiveness until they see the values passed on by their children to their grandchildren. "In 15 or 20 years, you will come to understand what it meant to have been a student at Ricks College," said President Bednar.

EXEMPLARY TEACHERS HONORED BY FACULTY

Making math understandable and enjoyable are two main goals of Steve Terry, who was honored Friday evening as Exemplary Faculty of the Year during the Faculty Association. Terry was selected from his teaching peers for the award which is given annually to a teacher who exhibits a positive influence in the lives of both students and colleagues, who exemplifies the highest standards and is liked by students and fellow workers, while "staying alive" in his or her teaching area. The five honorary award recipients were John Galbraith and Mel Sanford in foreign languages, Dorla Jenkins in English, Phil Packer in business, and Lynn Smith in sociology-criminal justice.

The Honorary Non-Classroom Faculty Award was presented to librarian David Butler and the Honorary Adjunct-Part-Time Faculty Award went to Susan Grover of the English department. This year's Innovation In Teaching Awards were presented to Jeff Anderson in humanities, John Thomas in religion and Lane Williams in communications. Kendall Grant was voted by the students as Academic Advisor of the Year. Grant has taught English at Ricks since 1989. Grant will be leaving Ricks to be head coach for the women's basketball team at the College of Southern Idaho in Twin Falls.

Retiring faculty members who were honored at the banquet included Jack Bond, William Conway, Gary Griffeth, Roger Harris, Larry Hibbert, Dorla Jenkins, Bud Johnson, Robert Oliphant, Blair Pincock, Janel Smith, Lynn Smith, Dean Sorenson, Lynn Speth, Ralph Thompson and Allen Wilson.

FORMER ATHLETES HONORED BY RICKS

Two former Ricks athletes Mari Burningham Carpenter and Dan Robinson were honored as Athletes of the Year during last week's Athletic Banquet. Carpenter, who played volleyball for BYU during the past year, was described by Ricks Coach JoAnn Reeve as "the best, smartest, and most dominating hitter we've ever had." As a junior outside hitter at BYU she played for the fifth-best NCAA women's volleyball team in the nation. As a senior last fall her team reached the final 16 nationally with a 28-5 overall record. Robinson, who played football for the University of Hawaii, was at Ricks during the 1995 and 1996 seasons. As the starting quarterback in 1998, he saw the Rainbow Warriors shut out without a win at 0-12. Then came last year's 9-4 season that included an Oahu Bowl victory against Oregon State. The wins are the most ever for a football team in NCAA history after coming off a winless season the previous year.

RICKS PIANIST TO PERFORM IN TEMPLE SQUARE CONCERT SERIES

Stephen Allen, director of piano studies at Ricks College, will be featured in the Temple Square Concert Series. Dr. Allen holds degrees from Eastman School of Music and San Jose State, as well as a doctoral degree in piano from the University of Indiana. The free concert (no tickets required) will be Saturday, April 22, in the Assembly Hall on Temple Square at 7:30 p.m.
