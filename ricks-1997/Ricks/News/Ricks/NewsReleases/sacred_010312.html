Ricks College News Release
March 12, 2001 
 

Daniel E. Gawthrop

New sacred work to be performed
in Washington, D.C., Virginia, Idaho, Utah 

For additional information, see http://www.ricks.edu/pr/performinggroups/sacredmusicseries.htm.

REXBURG, Idaho — A new sacred work written by composer Daniel E. Gawthrop will be premiered by Ricks College in Washington, D.C., Virginia, Utah and Idaho during April.

"The Passion and the Promise of Our Lord Jesus Christ" was commissioned by the college as part of its biennial Sacred Music Series. The oratorio utilizes scriptures in a musical setting to portray events in the Savior’s life.

The work will premiere in the Barrus Concert Hall at Ricks College in Rexburg, Idaho, April 3 at 7 p.m.; the historic Mormon Tabernacle in Salt Lake City April 7 at 7:30 p.m.; the Barrus April 10-12 at 7:30 p.m.; the Hart Auditorium at Ricks April 15 at 7 p.m.; and the Hart April 27 at 8 p.m.

For the first time, the Ricks choir and orchestra will travel to Washington, D.C., to perform Thursday, April 5, at National City Christian Church in Washington, D.C., at 8 p.m. and Friday, April 6, at Fairfax High School in Fairfax, Va., at 8 p.m. During these two performances, they will be joined by the Mormon Choir of Washington, D.C., and Southern Virginia Chamber Choir from Southern Virginia College in Buena Vista, Va.

Tickets are complimentary and available from the Ricks College Ticket Office at (208) 356-2230 or 1-800-71RICKS. Tickets are not required for the performance in Salt Lake City or the April 15 Sunday evening fireside at Ricks College.

The work will feature soloists Michael Belnap of Bloomington, Ind.; Kristine Ciesinski of Victor, Idaho; and Norman Bailey of Victor. They will be joined by the Ricks College Collegiate Singers and Symphony Orchestra in the two East coast performances, as well as the Men’s Choir and Women’s Choir in the Idaho and Utah performances. They are conducted by Dr. Kevin Call of the Ricks College Music Faculty.

Gawthrop, who lives in the Washington area, says the work portrays events in the Savior’s life from his entry into Jerusalem, through the crucifixion, resurrection, post-mortal ministries, and ending with Peter’s powerful testimony at Pentecost.

He says the "passion" traditionally refers to the part of Jesus Christ’s life from his triumphant entrance into Jerusalem through the crucifixion. Passions were written by many composers and were typically performed as part of church services before Easter.

"We didn’t want to end the story with the crucifixion," he says. "We wanted to include the his resurrection and post-mortal ministries. The ‘promise’ refers specifically to the Apostle Peter’s testimony at Pentecost."

"I try to write in an approachable, accessible way," Gawthrop says. "I feel very strongly that I need to touch, change, uplift and move the audience."

Gawthrop has been working on the oratorio for about a year, working full time on it the past four months. He says it’s the longest piece he has ever written. He typically writes similar, but shorter, pieces for church, schools and community choirs.

Gawthrop served for three years as composer-in-residence to the Fairfax Symphony Orchestra in Virginia and has been the recipient of four grants from The Barlow Endowment for Musical Composition. He has been commissioned by dozens of institutions including the American Choral Directors Association through its Raymond Brock Memorial series, and has had works premiered in such distinguished venues as the Concert Hall of the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts in Washington, D.C., the Mormon Tabernacle and Washington National Cathedral.

His choral pieces have been performed by such eminent ensembles as the United States Air Force Singing Sergeants, the Gregg Smith Singers, the Turtle Creek Chorale, the Paul Hill Chorale, the Mormon Tabernacle Choir, the Cathedral Choral Society (of Washington National Cathedral) and hundreds of other groups in the United States and abroad.

In addition to his work as a composer, he was a morning radio announcer on KBYU-FM in Provo, Utah, from 1985-87 and an evening radio announcer on WETA-FM in Washington, D.C., from for 12 years before leaving two years ago to compose full time. He has been active as a clinician and adjudicator, church musician, conductor, teacher and writer (including a period as music critic for The Washington Post).

Ricks College is the largest privately-owned junior college in the United States with 8,955 students from all 50 states and 40 foreign countries. It was recently announced that it will become a bachelor’s degree granting university to be called Brigham Young University-Idaho. The college is affiliated with The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. Funding for the Sacred Music Series comes from private donations with generous support from the Ricks College Endowment for the Performing Arts and the Snow Society.
