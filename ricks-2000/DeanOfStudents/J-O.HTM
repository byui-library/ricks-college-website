KBYI-FM 100.5
The BYU-Idaho owned radio station broadcasts NPR and other public radio programs 24 hours per day to Southeastern Idaho and by way of 8 translators, to Southwestern Montana, Western Wyoming and Southern Idaho. Leadership and Service Institute
The Leadership & Service Institute (LSI) in Manwaring Center 380 provides learning and service opportunities for all interested students. LSI emphasizes three main areas of involvement. For more information about these programs or to use our resource library visit the LSI office or see www.byui.edu/lsi/.

1. Leadership & Personal Development
LSI sponsors evening workshops, overnight retreats, and Saturday conferences to help students develop leadership skills, enhance their self-esteem, and strengthen their testimonies. These seminars are held about an hour from campus at a rustic ranch with a view of the Teton Mountains. All students can participate. Transportation is provided and programs are at a minimal cost. Clubs and other campus organizations can also use the lodge for training purposes.

2. Community Service
LSI helps students find meaningful service projects in the community and acts in a supportive role to encourage reflection and learning. One-time service projects are posted in our referral center. More involved service opportunities are offered through local agencies and campus clubs such as Adopt-A-Grandparent, Family Crisis Center, Friendship/Homework Tutoring, Special Olympics, Blood Drives, Humanitarian Service, Head Start, etc. Student program directors coordinate these projects and can answer questions and help students get involved.

3. Marriage Enrichment
LSI provides overnight retreats at the Teton Lodge that are designed to assist recently married students at BYU-Idaho.

Math Study Center
The purpose of the Math Study Center (MSC) is to provide math support for all math students on campus by using the following three fold effort:

1. The Drop-in Math Study Center
The MSC is open 53 hours a week where a staff of tutors are available to answer math questions. In the math study center students may work on their math homework and ask questions about what they do not understand. Hours are 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. on Mondays, Wednesdays, and Fridays, and 9 a.m. to 9 p.m. on Tuesdays and Thursdays. The center is open from 9 a.m. to noon on Saturdays. The MSC is closed from 2 to 3 p.m. for devotionals and forums.

2. One-on-one Tutors
Tutors are available for all math classes offered at BYU-Idaho, and can be obtained outside the Math Study Center at the Learning Assistance secretaries desk which is open from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., Monday through Friday. This is a free service funded by the university.

3. Lecture-based College Prep Math classes.
MSC offers three courses taught in a lecture based setting by a dynamic group of professors. These classes are Arithmetic (Math 100A; fractions, decimals, percent, negative #’s), Beginning Algebra (Math 100B; No previous algebra needed--work from basics up) and Intermediate Algebra (Math 101; need some prior algebra knowledge--this is the prep class for Math 108 or 110).

Name Changes
Names of currently enrolled students can be changed only for the following reasons: marriage, divorce, legal adoption, legal name changes, and misspelling. Certified documentation is required for all name changes, except misspellings and marriage. International students cannot anglicize their names without permission of the International Student Advisor. Name changes are made at the Registrar's Office in Kimball 190.

Orientation
The office of New Student Orientation (NSO), located in Rigby Hall 144, exists to better prepare incoming students and parents for a BYU-Idaho learning experience. Orientation focuses on three main areas of preparation. See www.byui.edu/orientation.

1. Pathway Pre Campus Orientation
Pathway is an internet course offered to all incoming students to better prepare them for a BYU-Idaho learning experience. Students can receive one credit for successfully completing the course and during a transition period, fulfill the online learning requirement needed for graduation from BYU-Idaho. The course is located at www.byui.edu/precampus.

2. Campus Orientation
Six separate orientations are offered to incoming students throughout the year. Campus Orientation covers fall, winter, fall/winter blocks, and first and second summer terms. Students are encouraged to participate in the campus orientation for which they are admitted.

3. Parent Orientation
Parent orientation activities and programs are offered to parents of new students at fall, winter and 2nd summer term orientations.

Outdoor Recreation
1. Outdoor Equipment Rental
The Outdoor Equipment Rental Center, located in Hart 115-B, provides outdoor equipment rentals at reasonable prices. Students can rent equipment for outdoor activities including camping equipment such as backpacks, sleeping bags, camp stoves, tents and cooking gear. Cross country skis and snowshoes are also available for winter.

2. Hours
Fall and Winter hours are from 2 to 6 p.m. on Mondays, Wednesdays, and Fridays and from 3 to 6 p.m. on Tuesdays, Thursdays, and Saturdays. During the ski season the center is open from 8 to 10 am. on Saturdays. Summer hours are 4 to 6 p.m. on Mondays, Wednesdays, and Fridays. During the summer we may be closed holidays. Please call ahead at ext. 2100. Students may also call the main office at ext. 2225 on weekdays between 8 a.m. and 5 p.m.

3. RAAP
An adventure program for the physically challenged. To join or assist, call ext. 2225 or stop by Manwaring Center 150.

Overnight Activities
Overnight co-ed activities such as spending the night at the dunes, camping, staying in motels or cabins and similar activities are prohibited. No overnight guests of the opposite sex are permitted at any time in unmarried student housing. Violations of this rule may result in suspension.
