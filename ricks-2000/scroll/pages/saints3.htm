MODERN DAY REVELATION

Sunday School curriculum designed to teach about the past and give hope for the future

Doctrine and Covenants section 109 contains the dedicatory prayer of the Kirtland Temple in Ohio. Section 110 was received by the prophet Joseph Smith within the temple.

by Melanie Ball
scroll staff

The course of study for this year's Sunday school curriculum is the Doctrine and Covenants and Church history.

The Doctrine and Covenants is a compilation of prophecies, visions, commandments and teachings given through the Prophet Joseph Smith and some of his successors in the presidency of the Church.

Doctrine and Covenants sections 124 through 135 were received in Nauvoo, Ill.

The Prophet referred to this book of scripture as "the foundation of the Church in these last days, and a benefit to the world, showing that the keys of the mysteries of the kingdom of the Savior are again entrusted to man."

The revelations within this book are interwoven with the history of the Church, coming "in answer to prayer, in times of need, and . . . out of real-life situations involving real people," the introduction to the Doctrine and Covenants, said.


Liberty Jail, Clay County, Mo. is where Joseph Smith received sections 121 through 123.

Rather than taking a sequential or section-by-section approach, the lessons in this course focus on major themes taught in the Doctrine and Covenants and Church history.

The themes are designed to teach the doctrines, ordinances and covenants of the restored gospel, which are necessary for individuals and families to come unto Christ and inherit eternal life.

The curriculum is also designed to teach the ongoing history of the restored Church and help members understand the importance of their day in the history of the Church - that they have inherited a great legacy and that they can find joy in their responsibility to continue to move forward the work of the Lord.

Adam-ondi-ahman is a sacred site mentioned in three different sections of the Doctrine and Covenants.

As members attend weekly classes and study the material, they can gain an appreciation for the past, strength for the present and hope for the future.

"How glorious is the past of this great cause. It is filled with heroism, courage, boldness and faith. How wondrous is the present as we move forward to bless the lives of people wherever they will hearken to the message of the servants of the Lord. How magnificent will be the future as the Almighty rolls on his glorious work, touching for good all who will accept and live His gospel and even reaching to the eternal blessing of his sons and daughters of all generations through the selfless work of those whose hearts are filled with love for the Redeemer of the world," President Gordon B. Hinckley said.
