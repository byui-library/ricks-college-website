The David O. McKay Library Welcomes You
 

The David O. McKay Library is located in the center of campus, just north of the Manwaring Center. The Library maintains a well-balanced collection of traditional materials and new technology to serve the needs of the students, faculty, and staff of Brigham Young University - Idaho. The Library also houses a Family History Center.
 

Mission Statement:
The David O. McKay Library will play an active role in supporting the academic needs and mission of BYU-Idaho, including the educational and economic advantages of new technology by acquiring, organizing, processing, and making readily available collections in all media; also by assisting patrons in finding and using information at this College and elsewhere.
 

This virtual tour will acquaint you with the services and technologies that are available for information retrieval. A similar printed walking tour is available when you arrive on campus. Becoming acquainted with these services will help you choose the best sources available in the Library and those available through remote access.

The main Information Desk is located near the east entrance to the Library. At this desk student workers and / or  trained professionals are available every hour the Library is open. A walking tour can be obtained at this desk when you visit the Library in person. Local telephone directories and printed college catalogs are among the materials shelved at this desk. Help is available at this desk or by calling (208) 356-2367. 

Books are located on the shelves near the Information Desk. Dictionaries, encyclopedias, almanacs, manuals, handbooks, yearbooks, directories,  and bibliographies are types of reference books found in the Reference Collection. Reference books do not check out in order to have them available for other users. The Library uses Library of Congress call numbers. The Library of Congress Subject Headings volumes are found by reference desks. The subject headings are words used to describe contents of books and many other materials. Like a thesaurus, this list helps identify other terms to use when doing research.  Books are shelved by the collection abbreviation and the letters assigned to subject areas and numbers associated with them.

    Circulation  maintains patron records and checks Library materials out. Circulation policies follow: 
    • A Ricks College ID card serves as the Library card for students. Off-campus patrons can obtain a Library card at the Circulation Desk for $1.
    • Most Library materials check out for two weeks. Books may be renewed two times. Faculty may keep these items for as long as a semester, if needed.
    • Music CDs check out for one week, with a limit of 2 CDs per patron.
    • Framed pictures circulate for 105 days or one semester.
    • Calculators and headphones can be checked out for a maximum of two hours.
    • Reference books, newspapers, and periodicals do not circulate.
    • Fines for overdue items are assessed at a cost of $0.25/item/day. Fines for Reserve items are $0.25/item/hour.
    Reserve: Teachers may put materials on reserve for students to use. 
    • These items check out for two hours at a time. 
    • Reserve materials include audiocassettes, books, and file folders that Instructors on campus make available to their classes. 
    • Electronic copies of materials can be placed on "Electronic Reserve," which students can then access on or off campus via the Internet.

Periodical (magazine & journal) subscriptions to 800 titles are found on the shelves and in the cabinets in the windowed room on the first floor. 

    • Church Periodicals are found just inside the door by circulation. Titles include copies of the French, Spanish, and German translations of  the Ensign, New Era, Friend and Church News. 

        • Popular magazines and scholarly journals are arranged alphabetically by title on the Periodical Collection shelves.  Periodicals in paper format are generally kept for five years. Periodicals and newspapers do not check out but remain in the Library for all to use. Please treat these and all library materials with care. Do not mark in them, mutilate them, or otherwise make them unusable for the next patron.

    • Older copies of many popular and some current scholarly periodical titles are available on microforms in cabinets near the door by the stairs and elevator to save space and preserve them. 

        • Microfiche are sheets of film used for some periodicals &  government documents. 

        • Microfilm are rolls of uncut film which are used for newspapers and some older periodicals. 

        • Local newspapers on microfilm are located near the Family History Library on the second floor.

        • Reader / printers for the microforms are located in a room near the cabinets. Paper copies can be made at  ten (10) cents per sheet. Lenses for the reader / printers allow various enlargement ratios.

    • Copies of print editions of local and major newspapers are kept from 1 week to 3 months.

        • Our subscriptions to newspapers are generally by mail which results in delays of several days between publication and receipt. 

        • Indexes to the New York Times are located on the last aisle of the periodicals.

        • Index to the Periodicals of the LDS Church in print format covers the Church News. Church periodicals since 1971 are available using Internet Resources. The Deseret News has an index to the Church News which requires passwords by subscribers.

        • Current newspapers are on shelves between the east and west wings.

Printed Indexes  to popular magazines and scholarly journals are found on the last row of periodicals and the shorter shelving units by the door. Internet Resources also has indexes and full text databases of newspapers and periodicals.

The Government Documents Librarian is Shane Cole. The McKay Library has been a Federal Depository Library for government documents since 1946. This library receives less than a fourth of the documents available to depository libraries. The University of Idaho regional depository receives all documents and serves our state by lending their documents. Other depository libraries with home pages include Brigham Young, Idaho State, and Boise State Univerisites. They are listed in the online Library Catalog and are arranged using the Superintendent of Document call numbers. Many documents are available over the Internet. Maps of neighboring states are located by the drinking fountain. Maps are stored in drawers which allow them to be flat. Topographic maps are received from the United States Geological Survey. An index accompanying these regional topographic maps is filed in the first drawer for the state. The index gives the names for quadrangles. Topographic maps are filed in alphabetical order. A topographic map for Idaho in the catalog will have a call number like: Map #.ID 

Maps issued by other groups are arranged by Library of Congress call numbers. An example of a LC call number for a map is: Map G7061.F-1992.U5. The Central Intelligence Agency produces international maps. National Geographic publishes thematic maps.

    The Student Copy Center or Copy Spot is a branch of the Campus Press and is located in room 172. 

    • All of the photocopy machines are administered by the campus press. 
    • The press can print, collate, and staple materials on a choice of white or colored paper. 
    • A one card system is being established using your student identification card. Money can be added to the card in the Copy Spot and other locations on campus. 
    • Other services include laser color photocopies, T-shirt transfers, and hot glue binding. 
    • Discounts for multiple copies and billing copies to campus departments and groups are also services of the Copy Center. 
    • A computer station in the Copy Center also allows users to print in color or directly to the copy machines.
    • Facsimile or FAX copies may be sent and received  at the Copy Center. The fax number is: (208) 356-2385. Receiving a fax costs $0.25 per page.

    • Materials can be picked up at the Interlibrary Loan office in McKay 164. 
    • Faculty Document Delivery is a service available to faculty.

Technical Services offices are located at the north end of the second floor near the elevator. Librarians,  professional staff, and student workers order and prepare library materials in this area. Technical Services includes Acquisitions and Cataloguing. Acquisitions personnel order materials for the Library collections, handle the invoices and billings, and  process periodicals received. Cataloging personnel prepare the material for use by entering information into the online catalog, by attaching labels and barcodes, by performing minor repairs to damaged materials, and by sending worn materials to a bindery company for more extensive work.
 
 
 
 
Family History Center is located in the glassed in room by the main stairs. Sources for family history and genealogical research, using the latest computer materials, can be found in the Family History Center. Expert genealogists are also available to help patrons. Campus wards and stakes will have orientations to this facility. As a branch of the Family History Library in Salt Lake City, materials can be ordered. A branch family history library has the same computer programs available as stake family history libraries. The Center is open during regular Library hours, as well as 3:00-7:00 p.m. on most Sundays during the regular school year. For more information, visit their home page: http://abish.ricks.edu/fhc/.
Special Collections are located in rooms behind the Family History Center. Special Collections include campus archives, rare books, and local manuscripts. Archival collections include copies of the devotional audiocassettes in a digital format. Microfilming or digitizing some campus publications are ongoing projects to preserve the history of this College. Many Special Collections materials have been moved to Brigham Young University. Special collections are materials which require special handling to preserve them.
 
A reference desk on the second floor has personnel that shelve materials and answers questions.
 
    Comfortable chairs for reading, relaxing, and studying are located on the second and third floor landings. Circular stands contain the paperback book collection. Mormon fiction paperbacks by Orson Scott Card, Anita Stansfield, and others are located in these two paperback book collections.
    Notice the public telephone on the south wall. Each floor landing has a telephone for public use. To reach local off-campus numbers, dial 8 followed by the 7 digit number. Long distance telephone calls, including toll free numbers, should be made at the pay telephones in the Manwaring Center.

The Department of Instructional Technology has offices in room 321. Instructional Technology personnel assist the faculty and other campus departments with presentations and electronic publication. Many campus events and productions are videotaped and copies are available for purchase. Online course CDs are also available in this room. Instructional and promotional videos are produced both on location and in the video studio. Other services include editing, special effects, character generation, duplication, and off-air and satellite taping. A large collection of stock music and sound effects are available for use.  (See their homepage for more information.)
  • The Instructional Technology Center for faculty use is located in room 337. Multimedia presentations are demonstrated and created by faculty in this room.
  • At the end of the hall in room 360 is the Media Lab. Classes and individuals may use this self-serve, hands-on area to produce media materials. You can laminate materials, bind a report, make an overhead transparency, mount a visual, create lettering for a sign, add clipart images to a chart, enlarge a visual, make a banner, cut paper, etc. Student lab assistants are available to help if needed. Contact media specialist Richard Nelson at ext. 1209 for further information.
  • Room 343 is a self-serve audio room. Students can make their own tape recordings or make copies within the copyright law.
  • A self-serve video room is found in room 339. Students can use this room to edit and add music to video productions or create a video from still photographs or slides. A video studio in room 328 can be scheduled for class projects only. Scheduling for the self-serve rooms and studio is done in room 320.
  • The Photo Studio  is in room 342.  A photographer and student assistants in the photo and imaging area shoot portraits, passport photos, group pictures, and promotional photographs. Additional services include copying and duplicating slides, producing copy negatives, making black and white prints, and photo scanning  and restoration. A picture file of campus personnel and other useful material is maintained.
