Note Taking

Really, the method you use to take notes is up to you. Make sure, however, that whatever method you use, you find a way to accurately record source information and page numbers so that you can cite your sources correctly in your essay. Try to paraphrase or summarize as much of the information as you can, putting the facts and ideas you find into your own words. Keep direct quotations to a minimum. You want to quote a source only if the author's words add significant weight to your own point or are so memorable and brilliant that you can't possible say it any better. Remember, however, that you don't want to use lengthy quotations keep your quoted passages down to two or three lines.

Many writers suggest you use index cards to take notes, limiting each card to one fact or thought on any given topic. You put the author's name and the page number where you got the information in the upper left-hand corner, the topic of the note in the upper right-hand corner, and the information on the card itself.

Along with your note cards, you also prepare works cited cards, a separate card for each source that lists all the publication information you'll need for your works cited list. This is a very important step. You can't use source information you cannot accurately document, so take the time to record publication information before you jump into taking notes.

One reason I like index cards is that you can use them later to help you organize your essay. You can easily group your source information by topic and then shuffle your cards, rearranging ideas and source material in a way you can't shuffle and rearrange sheets of notebook pages. I don't require you to use this method or any other, but you might find it offers you a flexible, time-saving way to organize and accurately record your notes.
