Most Mormon funeral sermons included, but were not restricted to, reminiscences on the life of the deceased. ...the Mormons loved and valued their dead for simpler, more rudimentary virtues. They praised them for having been kind, trughful, unselfish, patient, and cheerful; for having been obedient, duriful children; or for having given selfless service as parents. Fortitude in the face of life's challenges was also often commended. This naturally required mentioning some of those trials, including, often, the final cause of death.

The most characteristic Mormon virtues seem to have been being faithful and true. ...

...

... Funerals for Latter-day Saints who had not been faithful and true could be awkward. The speakers would probably mention happy memories or other positive qualities of the deceased, perhaps giving a general statement about the justice of God and the reality of the future life. But such sermons generally were not those that were preserved in printed form.

Because their primary urpose was to omfort the bereaved, Mormon funerals were not occasions to dwell on the decease'd misdeeds or otherwise condemn him or her. ...

...

One purpose of praising the dead was to urge others to do likewise. ...

...

In the course of eulogizing the deceased, speakers often recalled specific esperiences in order to evoke fond memories and therby involve family members and the rest of the congregation in a collective "reappreciateion." ...

When speaking of the purpose of life and the different stages of existence, Mormon preachers naturally called upon their religious faith. Lookng forward in time, they described the spirit world, resurrection, judgment, and eventual reward in one of the three degress of glory. ...

...

The Mormon comception of life in paridise was not one of simply basking in eternal glory. ...the immense task of preaching the gospel to the spirits in spirit prison was seen as the primary activity in the spirit world. ...

The knowledge of missionary activity inthe spirit world enabled survivors to envision their departed loved ones as still active and striving in the work of the Lord. It also provided a possible explanation for untimely deaths. ...

...

The earliest sermons do not indicate that because the spirit survived and retained its individual identity, it might still be present on earth, even in thevery room in which the funeral was hel. However, this idea appears in later sermons. ...

...

...typically, scriptural references were incorporated into the sermon. ... [Common: 2 Samuel 3:38; Job 1:21; Job 19:25-6; Matthew 25:21; Luke 2:29-30; Luke 2:26; John 11:25-6; 1 Corinthians 15:19, 22, 55; 2 Timothy 4:7; Revelation 6:9; Revelation 7:9; Revelation 7:15; Revelation 14:13; Revelation 20:12; Alma 40-2; Doctrine and Covenants 76; Doctrine and Covenants 88; Doctrine and Covenants 132; Doctrine and Covenants 42:46.]

...

... Were Mormon funerals simply the same as those common throughout the Christian world? Of course there is overlap, but two significant differences should not be overlooked. First, the virtues describing a model Latter-day Saint were not those of a crusader of ascetic. ... Second, after 1850 Mormon preahers drew upon modern scriptures in their funeral sermons, thus amplifying the undrestanding of the premortal existence, the future life, and the purpose and meaning of the earthly probation. ...
