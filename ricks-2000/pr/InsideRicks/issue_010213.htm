TELEVISION PROGRAM FOCUSES ON TRANSITION

"Stunning" is how President David A. Bednar described his initial reaction to last June's announcement that Ricks College would become a bachelor's degree granting university known as Brigham Young University-Idaho. President Bednar made the remark during Sunday morning's broadcast of "Eye on Idaho" on KIDK TV 3. During the 30-minute program hosted by Steve Cannon, he reviewed the transition of the past seven months.

Since June 21, college and Church leaders have been developing plans for BYU-Idaho. The college plans to add approximately 100 more faculty members along with additional support personnel during the next five years. A total of 46 programs will be added with the first eight of these programs to begin junior level classes in this fall.

President Bednar said that growth will be incremental. To better utilize the campus, students will enroll in one of four tracks (fall/winter, winter/summer, summer/fall, or the year-round Fast Track program). The unique track system will make it possible to serve one-third more students. As students enroll in junior and senior level classes, the student body is projected to grow from 8,600 to 11,600 over five years. The impact on the community and upper valley will be "a series of waves, not a giant tsunami," President Bednar assured.

When asked by Cannon what friends and alumni can do to help with the transition, President Bednar responded, "Be patient. Do not expect it all to happen at once. We have a five-year, phase-in process." He encouraged interaction as he told the television audience, "Ask questions. Seek information." And he promised, "We will be forthright with information when we have it." President Bednar invited listeners to visit the Ricks College web site which includes current information about the changes. Public information meetings also are being held, and campus question-and-answer sessions are scheduled monthly. (See http://www.byui.edu/pr/byu-i).

NEXT GENERATION OF TV TO BE TOPIC AT MAJOR FORUM

The next generation of television will be the topic of the Major Forum set for Thursday, Feb. 15. Steven R. Lindsley, chief executive officer of WOW!TV, will present the lecture at 2 p.m. in the Taylor Chapel. It also will be carried live on KRIC radio, 100.5 FM. A question-and-answer session for interested students and faculty will follow in Taylor 120 from 3 to 4 p.m.

In February of last year Lindsley left his position as president of KSL-TV in Salt Lake City to develop new communication technology in his own interactive and digital TV company. During his 12-year career at KSL, Lindsley served as president, vice president general manager, vice president station manager and sales manager. A graduate from Ricks College and Brigham Young University in business management, Lindsley played football for both schools. He was the starting quarterback at Ricks in 1983 and at BYU in 1986. He serves as executive producer of the LDS Church's 2002 Conference Center event to be held during the Olympics. He has served as chairman of the Utah Substance Abuse and Anti-Violence Coordinating Council

RUSSIAN MUSICIANS TO PLAY AT RICKS

Three Russians who play classical and ethnic music on traditional folk instruments will perform at Ricks College. Trio Voronezh will perform in the Barrus Concert Hall on Saturday, Feb. 24, at 7:30 p.m. Tickets for the evening event are $6 for the general public. A pre-show Russian dinner will be available at 6 p.m. in the Manwaring Center East Ballroom for an extra $10. Early in the day, the trio will give a workshop at 10:30 a.m. at the college and then present a 30-minute show following lunch at the Rexburg Senior Citizens Center at about 12:30 p.m.

Classically trained at the Conservatory in Voronezh, Russia, this true Cinderella-story began in the streets, subways and small clubs of European cities. Discovered playing Bach in a Frankfurt, Germany, subway station, Trio Voronezh made its U.S. debut at the 1996 Oregon Bach Festival. The members, Valerie Petruchin, Vladimir Volochin (National Champion, All-Russian Domra Competition) and Sergei Teleshev, still make their home in the working-class city of Voronezh. They play traditional Russian folk instruments: a double-bass balalaika (the three-stringed Russian national instrument, with a triangular body made of fir), a domra (a three-stringed short-necked ancestor of the mandolin), and a bajan (a chromatic-button accordion, with various registers). The trio's diverse repertoire ranges from classical works of Bach, Vivaldi, Tchaikovsky and Stravinsky to popular songs by Gershwin, Russian folk and gypsy dance music.

EXPLORATION WORK AT SITE FOR NEW MULTI-USE BUILDING

Heavy equipment has been digging a large hole at the site of the proposed multiple-use building southeast of the Kimball Student and Administrative Services Building. This is all part of preliminary exploration work that is being done by the engineers and contractor to study the rock conditions underneath the topsoil. The official groundbreaking for the building is anticipated to be held this spring, says Administrative Vice President James Smyth says. Because a standard plan already is designed, the building can be built quickly. Completion will take about 14 months and should be ready for use by the fall of 2002. The contractor is Jacobsen Construction of Salt Lake City, and the architect is Jensen Haslem of Logan, Utah. Unlike other buildings on campus that were principally designed for academic needs, the building is principally designed for religious needs. During the week, it will be used by the college for classes. The building will have space for 20 to 25 faculty members, who will share offices with ward and stake leaders.
