Juggling the dual roles of manager and mother

by Sarah Lunt
LUN99013@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Rosalie Evans, the head resident of Royal Crest apartments and a full time mother, plays with her children while keeping an eye on the apartments.
Scott Cameron / Scroll
Every college-approved housing complex in Rexburg has on-site managers who live and work with their tenants. Though students, as managers’ tenants, encounter these people on a regular basis, most students have no idea what goes into being a manager or how it affects daily life.

Rosalie Evans, the manager of Royal Crest Apartments, has four children 8 years old and under. In fact, all of her children have been born while she has been managing at different apartment complexes. She and her husband have been managing apartments for about eight years — over six of those years have been at Royal Crest alone.

Being a wife, a mother of four young children and an apartment manager can be pretty demanding, Evans said.

She shared her schedule to show how all the elements in her life fit together in her day.

At 6:30 a.m., Evans and her family have scripture study. Her husband leaves for work, and she prepares two of her four children for elementary school.

After they leave, she spends time cleaning up her apartment before office hours begin.

Evans’ office hours begin at 8 a.m. during the week. At 10 a.m., things start to pick up as the maintenance worker comes in to pick up repair orders. In addition, apartment tenants come in with concerns.

At noon, Evans prepares lunch for her younger children while listening for the office bell. While her children nap, she takes her one-hour break before returning to the office.

Her two older children return from school at 2:30 p.m., and she cares for their needs while trying to be available for tenants.

By 5 p.m. Evans’ office closes, and she is able to prepare dinner and get her children to bed. Although her office hours are officially over in the evening, many times tenants will call with immediate concerns.

Even though her schedule seems demanding, she enjoys her job, Evans said.

“The thing I enjoy most is getting to know the girls,” she said.

In addition, her children really enjoy the attention they receive from the girls, she said.

Although she is working, she also enjoys being able to spend time with her young children. When she first began managing, that was the most appealing aspect of the job, she said.

Another perk, Evans said, is the many benefits an apartment manager gets. A big advantage is that she and her family live in an apartment where the rent and utilities are paid for already.

However, Evans said, there are some drawbacks to the job. She feels that sometimes her duties make her children “second place,” in that she has to make sure she is available for the tenants at all times. She is also so busy when her husband comes home that she has less time for him.

Another disadvantage is the lack of privacy that she and her family have, she said. Often, at just the time her children wish to sleep, the complex begins to get noisy.

She also wishes that her children had more room to run and play.

The job can be especially difficult when girls in the complex have problems getting along. Evans finds that emotionally trying, although working with the girls who struggle and helping them is a big benefit to her job, she said.

Although she will be ready to move on when her time at Royal Crest is done, she will greatly miss the girls and the experiences she has had as a manager, Evans said.
