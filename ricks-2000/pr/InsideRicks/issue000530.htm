3,000 PEOPLE EXPECTED AT CAMPUS EDUCATION WEEK

Ricks College officials expect more than 3,000 people to attend the third annual Education Week set for Thursday to Saturday, June 1-3. "There are more classes and more evening events this year," says Kristen Foster, coordinator of the event. "We have also added some ticketed events at night so more community people can attend." The event is squeezed in between first and second summer terms at Ricks College. More than 355 classes will be taught by 73 instructors during the three-day period. While many of the classes are aimed at adults, classes also are held for youths 14 to 18. Foster says the purpose of Education Week is "to instruct, uplift and teach principles of living for growth and enrichment."

It is become a popular time for families and friends to do something together. "I'd like to extend an invitation to entire community, regardless of religious affiliation," she says. "There are many classes and events that will help families and enrich personal lives." Registration is under way through the Department of Continuing Education. Walk-in registration is possible May 31 from 7-9 p.m. and from 7:30 a.m. to 7:30 p.m. June 1-3 in the Manwaring Center Special Events Room. Registration cost is $35 for the full three days or $15 for one-day or evening passes.

The event will feature favorite teachers from the Ricks faculty and other popular speakers addressing spiritual, family, homemaking, self-improvement and human relations topics. While there is a cost to attend all classes, devotionals at 9:45 a.m. each morning are free and open to the public in the Hart Auditorium. Speakers will be Ricks President David A. Bednar and his wife, Susan Bednar, on June 1; Elder L. Aldin Porter, senior president of the LDS Church Quorums of the Seventy, on June 2; and Anne Wirthlin, former counselor in the Primary general presidency, on June 3.

SIFE PLACES IN INTERNATIONAL COMPETITION

A group of enterprising college students from Ricks College won two top awards at the Students in Enterprise International Exposition and Career Opportunity Fair May 21-23 in Kansas City, Mo. The Ricks SIFE team, which was formed last fall, won the International Rookie of the Year award and was listed among the Elite Eight finalist teams among two-year colleges. The team received a $2,000 award for placing in the Elite Eight. Each team at the international competition had competed earlier in the school year and won at one of the regional or national competitions. During the year, the Ricks team completed more than 42 projects in the community including a credit fair, Internet web page design fair for local businesses, investment seminar and visits to schools. The Ricks team's mission is to provide positive community programs to ensure an in-depth knowledge of the business world through mentoring and serving the people at Ricks and Rexburg.

Ricks business faculty member Mark Nygren, who serves as advisor for the Ricks team, says, "SIFE is an amazing organization to work with and it is great to see what a difference Ricks SIFE students are making." SIFE is a nonprofit organization that works in partnership with business and higher education to provide college students the opportunity to make a difference and to develop leadership, teamwork, and communication skills through learning, practicing and teaching the principles of free enterprise.

RESEARCH COLLECTION DONATED TO MCKAY LIBRARY

A collection of research on the history and culture of Polynesia and Hawaii has been donated to the McKay Library. The donation was made by a retired sociology and anthropology professor who had the foresight to save his students' research papers. Hal Hunter of Rexburg has spent the past two years retyping research papers that were written for his classes between 1964 and 1972 and has compiled them into four volumes of material. Hunter recently presented a set to the McKay Library at Ricks College and will travel to Hawaii in October to present other sets to Brigham Young University Hawaii and the Polynesian Cultural Center in Laie, Hawaii. The 208 essays capture the life and history of the islands of Hawaii,Tonga, Tahiti, Fiji, Samoa, Easter Island and New Zealand. Topics range from pineapple and poi to the impact of war on the immigrant Japanese culture in Hawaii.
