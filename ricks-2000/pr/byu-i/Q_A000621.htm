The Transition: Questions & Answers - June 21, 2000

A question-and-answer session was conducted by President David A. Bednar with members of President's Council at 5 p.m. on June 21, 2000

Good evening. It has been quite a day. We will be pleased to respond to your questions. We will begin with an invocation offered by Brother Dave Richards.

I will begin with a question that I have been asked a lot today.

Question: Why was this announcement made when there are no extensive details available about timetables and so forth?
Now, since we have primarily the campus community here, I can be fairly direct in responding to that question. If, today, there had been an announcement with timetables and specific programs, it would have caused a great deal of frustration within our campus community. Everyone would have wondered why they were not consulted in establishing those timetables and programs. We are not to the point where we know exactly what programs on exactly what timetable until we have consulted with academic and other leaders on campus.

I will reiterate one statement that was made earlier today: This is the beginning of a transition process, not the announcement of a finished product. This is something that will be phased in over time, and that phasing requires consultation and planning with a lot of people on this campus. Therefore, today we have not had an extensive announcement of "x" programs in this year and so on and so forth. That is the single, most frequently asked question I have had today, so I figured I would just do that one right off the bat.

We have two microphones, and we have some folks who will be distributing those. If you will raise your hand, we will call on you and get you a microphone and do our best to try to respond to questions.

Student: I was wondering, when it starts, will those of us who are already here just be able to register as if we are up here for another year or will we have to reapply?
President Bednar: That will really be determined by the programs that we offer, recognizing that there will be a relatively small initial number of degrees as we phase things in. It will really be a function of the students in the pipeline and the particular programs that would feed those. So it is unlikely that you would have to reapply. Is that responsive to your question?

Student: Yes, thank you.

President Bednar: You will notice that I use all kinds of weasel words like "likely" so that if somebody comes back and says,"Well you promised this," I'll say, "No, there was a weasel word in there."

Employee: Just a question to go along with the last one: The students who are here now in a two-year program, that will probably continue? They can continue and finish that degree, can't they, while you are phasing in the four-year program?
President Bednar: Oh, absolutely. Again, let me see if I can be clear about one thing. For all intents and purposes, the functioning of this institution the remainder of this summer and next year doesn't change. Programs are not eliminated. What we are doing is the homework and the evaluation and the assessment and the consultation. So as we move forward, keep in mind that this is going to be gradual, incremental, and done over time. Does that help?

President Bednar: I have a couple of e-mail questions that I will intersperse along the way. Here is the question:

E-mail Question: Is there an idea about the anticipated lead time that will be given for those of us who may be asked to leave the college?
President Bednar: That is premature. In the announcement that President Hinckley made today, there was an indication that there would be a thorough analysis and perhaps some very significant adjustments that will need to be made. That is not going to happen overnight. And the other thing is that as we adjust resources within the campus, to the degree possible, we will help people with retraining and retooling. We are not just going to say, "You are out the door."

Student: Will graduated students be able to apply or reapply for a four-year degree?
President Bednar: I will go back to the same basic answer, and then you can tell me if it is not satisfactory. That is really going to depend on the availability of the particular degree that you are interested in. So I could say yes or I could say no. What it will really boil down to is that initially, once we have done the homework, there will be "x" number of degrees moving to four-year programs. And if one of those degrees happens to be in your area of interest the answer is going to be yes, you will have that option. But we do not know how extensive that option is going to be at this point in time.

Question: When will further information be available?
President Bednar: Without trying to get into a lot of detail, there are two oversight bodies. Now, given the scope and the magnitude of some of these changes, they are clearly going to be presented, reviewed, and ultimately approved by both the Executive Committee and the Board. I suspect that some of our initial work will first be presented to the Executive Committee in September. And after they have had a chance to look at it, review it, critique it, and make adjustments, then we will be in a position to say: Here is what the specific action plan is beginning to look like.

President Bednar: At this point I am not willing to say the specific number of students because once such a number is spoken, it takes on a life of its own. There are evaluations and assessments that we are going to be making as we go forward. Down the road, I will probably be able to answer that with much more specificity. Your second question was about tuition. That is something that is examined not just in isolation at BYU-Idaho, but we have to take a look at tuition rates in connection with the other schools as well. For example, at the present time, tuition at BYU-Hawaii is somewhat less than it is in Provo. So that is a new world for us, and that is something we are going to have to evaluate. It is understandable that you want answers; you may even get frustrated because we don't have a lot of specific answers. Our objective is to make sure that at the present time we are asking the right questions.

Employee: I have a three-part question. As we phase in new programs, will more faculty need to be hired?
President Bednar: Let me outline the strategy of what we have to do through this evaluation process. First, we have to ask the question: In our academic programs, what do we need to be very good, to be an extraordinarily effective teaching-oriented institution? What are the academic programs? As we begin to answer that question, we then move to adjustments that make the answer possible. So there will be a recapturing and a reclaiming of resources. It is not the case that the recapturing, alone, is going to cover all of our needs. So the answer is yes. It is inevitable that we will need additional faculty. Now, in terms of exactly how many, that will grow out of the evaluation and the answer to the question: What is it we really need in that phased process?

Employee: The second question is: For those who will be hired in the future as faculty members, will they be required to have doctorate degrees since it will be a university?
President Bednar: Accreditation is predicated on our statement of mission and our assessment of how well we are achieving that mission, so we have a key role in defining what percentage of the faculty would have to be Ph.D. qualified. There is not some body somewhere that says it has got to be "x" percent. We have a role in defining that. So, is it the case that a faculty member will be required to have a Ph.D. to be hired at BYU-Idaho as we move into this new world? The answer is, as a matter of an absolute requirement, no.

Employee: Will those who are here as faculty now, with only master's degrees, be required ever to pursue doctoral degrees?
President Bednar: Using the word "required," I think the general answer is no. Perhaps encouraged, if it makes sense to achieve the quality that we need in a particular content area. But, as a general rule, there is not a universal mandate that says everyone now has to finish a Ph.D. and we will only hire Ph.D.s in the future.

Student: In the broadcast, the Internet was mentioned. I was wondering what changes you anticipate there will be based upon the use of the Internet.
President Bednar: That is a good question. Beginning this fall, there is a requirement that every student during his or her program here would complete one class via the Internet in order to graduate. That will still be in force. A number of Internet learning experiences are available to help students complete that requirement. We will never be an Internet-based learning experience such that it dominates everything and is the only way we get the information and take tests. That is one extreme. The other is to not use the Internet at all. So where we are going is an appropriate balance. In today's world, not knowing how to navigate the Internet and not knowing how to use the Internet as a lifelong learning tool, would be fatal flaws in one's preparation and training. We are experimenting with that now. How do you do that? You don't just rush in headlong and adopt it in everything, but you have got to experiment and learn how to use that tool. So, on campus we have a number of faculty who are incorporating the Internet into delivering material to students. There are a number of classes where you will receive information. It is also the case you can get that information before you get to class. The Internet can disseminate the information and make it available to you on your schedule. Then, when you get to class, you ask questions, you discuss, you work on cases, you do other things, not just take notes. So it produces the opportunity for enriching and revolutionary changes in how learning occurs, and that is a part of the innovation and the experimentation that we are going forward with. Is that responsive to what you are asking?
