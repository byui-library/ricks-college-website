 The Excitement of Photoshop 5.0

Photoshop 5.0 offers some exciting new features.

 

Let's introduce a few of the more common ones:

1. The Effects submenu under the Layer menu. The most common layer effects, some of which we've already taught you in earlier lessons, are now available under Photoshop 5.0 with one simple menu selection.

They are Inner Shadow, Bevel and Emboss, Inner Glow, Outer Glow, and Drop Shadow.

To follow along, merely drag and drop this image below and then open it in Photoshop 5. Let's start with our image of a Hurricane that hit south Florida:

If you look closely, I've selected portion because I want to make a button that has some parts of the storm. Make sure you don't feather your selection. Go ahead and copy the article and create a new document. The new document should be the width and heighth of your circle.

Hit OK to create the new document. Then, paste your copied item into the new box. It should look something like this:

 

(Make sure the new, copied layer is selected in your layers pallette for all of these effects:)

Next, you want to select the inner shadow. When you do, this dialog box should appear:

You can change the angle, distance, blur and intensity to increase your effect. Click on the black box next to normal mode (the way it should be set) to change the color of your shadow.

When you apply those effects, it looks three dimensional, something that took four layers and several minutes to do in Photoshop 4.0:

 

Isn't that nice?

In the dialog, you can increase the amount you've raised the item by according to taste and desired effect.

You might apply Outer Glow to text layer or regular layer. This gives the halo effect we've discussed in earlier lessons. You, of course, can pick the color that surrounds it.

You might apply Inner Glow. This also is a 3-D effect, but not as effective as the inner shadow.

 

Note the dialog box

You can alter the effect selected where it says Inner Glow. When you check apply, you can see how an effect will look without actually running it. Increasing the blur, as shown, increases the width of the glow on your picture.

Three other things to mention in Photoshop 5:

First is the Magnetic Lasso Tool. This tool is part of the lasso tool elements on your tool pallette. This tool automatically finds the edge of a sharply shaped object rather than using the marquee lasso tool and clicking each corner. The magnetic lasso tool finds clear edges for you quickly and fairly acurately.

The second is that power of dynamic text editing. You can go back into old text layers and re-edit them rather than having to delete the text layer and start over.

The third is the history pallette. This pallette allows you to see all of the actions you have completed in your image.You can cut out a step you don't like rather than starting from scratch. This can save huge amounts of time.

Well enjoy the power of creation with Adobe Photoshop 5!
