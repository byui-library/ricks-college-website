November 8, 2001

BYU professor speaks at

BYU-Idaho devotional

REXBURG, Idaho –Camille Fronk, a professor of ancient scripture at Brigham Young University in Provo, Utah, spoke to BYU-Idaho students and faculty members about putting God first during Tuesday’s devotional in Rexburg.

Fronk began her devotional address by citing the words of Jesus Christ saying, "And we will prove them herewith, to see if they will do all things whatsoever the Lord their God shall command them.’ Did you hear that? The Lord said, ‘All things whatsoever he commands us.’ How do you know what to focus on?"

Using scriptural examples, she showed examples of how the lives of those who put God first have been blessed. "Captain Moroni’s people fought for many righteous causes, but the first one listed on the title of liberty was: ‘In memory of our God.’ Captain Moroni’s army remembered to pray, to heed the direction of righteous leaders, and they were victorious in miraculous ways," Fronk said.

She then discussed manners in which these principles apply in the lives of Latter-day Saint college students.

First she spoke to the young men, saying, "When you young men seriously commit to put God first in your life, you will experience a reverence for acquiring knowledge by study and by faith that opens up opportunities and a desire to continuously serve the Lord long after your full-time mission is completed. More than looking slick on the outside, we need young men reverence the priesthood because of their deep, abiding love for God that comes before anything or anyone else in their lives."

Fronk next spoke to the young women about putting God first, particularly in the areas of serving missions and marriage.

"In many ways the mission decision is more complex for young women," she said. "Prophets have not given definitive directives for young women, generally, concerning the Lord’s expectations for full-time missionary service."

She then quoted President Hinckley’s words, saying that if a young woman has a persisting interest in serving a mission, she should counsel with her bishop as well as her parents.

Speaking on the topic of marriage, she said, "I would hope for your sakes, young women, you have had a clear experience of pouring out your heart to God and receiving his answer before you have to ask him how to answer a marriage proposal. You will find it hard to put God first when you don’t know how his voice feels."

Another topic covered in the devotional was the physical care of each person’s body. "When we put God first in our lives, we see his gifts to us as more precious than anything money can buy," Fronk said. "Take our bodies, for example. We were created in the image of God."

"The manner in which we clothe that body provides another opportunity to show the Lord that he is first in our lives," she said, speaking about modesty. "I like the definition of modesty – dress or demeanor that does not draw undue attention to one’s self.

"Furthermore, God has revealed that these very bodies will be restored to us in the resurrection," she continued, emphasizing the importance of the physical care of each body. "Does knowing that you will have that very body for eternity alter the way you care for your bodies here? Does wanting to be physically fit so that you can better serve God, whenever or wherever he calls, increase your ability to choose what is good for your body?"

Next week’s devotional speaker is Elder Bruce C. Hafen, a member of the First Quorum of the Seventy and a former president of Ricks College. Devotional addresses can be heard live on KBYI 100.5 FM Tuesdays at 2 p.m. and are rebroadcast at 9 p.m.
