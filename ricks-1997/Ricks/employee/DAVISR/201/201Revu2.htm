Philosophy- questions were more important than the answers; questions always the same, but answers change with new knowledge

Thales - basic stuff is water

Anaximander - all is separating out or going back into the boundless

Heracleitos - Logos guides all change / is in all things “nothing is, everything is becoming” “can't put foot in same river twice”

Pythagorus - that which is permanent is real

      reality exists apart from substance / “Reality is only in the mind.” / trust the mind, not the senses - use math, it's always correct.

      - there is a permanent Pattern for each Type (forerunner of Plato)

Democritus - all matter is composed of atoms

      - we live in a blizzard of atoms. They hit our brain (make us do things) thus we are not in control of ourselves. This leads to Fatalism.

      - atoms are controlled by Fate (or Gods) so you're not in control

      - leads to today's existentialism

Socrates - wrote nothing/all we have is from Plato

      - loved any discussion (dialectic) to show that truth is hard to define (“Truth” is usually what men choose to call it, not what it “really” is.)

      - taught rhetoric, the art of persuasion

      - “The unexamined life is not worth living.”

      - the establishment doesn't like him / trial on phony charges

- condemned to death / drinks cup of poison

Plato - student of Socrates/nickname;means “wide”

      - in the beginning we started with God/Creator, in the world of Ideas or Patterns.

      - divides all into two realms: perfect: (world of Ideas) and imperfect:(this world)

      - God molds things in shape of patterns. Since things change, none is perfect like its pattern.

      - Before this life, Man lived as a soul in the world of Ideas / he had a divine origin

      - at birth he was made to forget / soul was imprisoned in the body. We can remember, somewhat, of the beauty which surrounded us, and this life is the search for that beauty and truth we once knew.

            - it can be found through education

      - at death the soul will return to the realm of perfect ideas (heaven) / Soul is good. Remove the body and the mind/soul will soar / body limits you

      - the soul consists of 3 parts: appetite (which must be confined), courage (to make us honorable), and reason (to govern the body and make it perfect). He elaborates on these in The Republic, giving a picture of an ideal, socialist state, which defines the nature of Justice. Three groups in the perfect state: 1) workers, 2) soldiers, 3) philosopher kings

Aristotle - student of Plato

      - lived in northern Greece / son of a physician, so he had an empirical (scientific) background

      - is personal tutor to Alexander the Great

      - tries to reconcile the duality of Plato

      “idea” cannot be separated from the “object”

      substance + idea = reality / both are of equal importance

      - enteleche  “that which has purpose”

            is in all things / light of Christ / all move toward perfection

      - God is “unmoved Mover”

      - each object works for its own good, its summum bonum

      Man's “good” is to live in harmony with others, which can be done in two ways:

            1) life of virtue / achieve Golden Mean / work for balance in all you do

            2) life of contemplation. Thought is better than action. (will lead to monasteries in middle ages)

Art Greeks believed the laws of Nature were not to be abused.

- copy nature/Gods and use reason / 7‑head‑proportions / Golden Mean or Divine Proportion

- copy Egyptian - post and lintel / don't use Minoan inverted columns

      - frontal or profile views / statues are formal, not sensory

Geometric - geometric pottery / angular figures / no architectural style yet, only cella, Mantiklos

Archaic (archaios = source, beginning) / Kouros (youth) / Kore (maiden)

      - all nude (clothes do NOT make the man)

      - Greek post and lintel starts / copy Egyptians

Classic natural / serene / idealism / Kritios Boy / Spear Bearer

      - Parthenon / perfect building (like a perfect body)

      - Hermes and the Infant Dionysus / smaller / thinner / replace ideal with naturalism

Hellenistic at first petty / theatrical / not heroic / not true to life

-         later it shows start of realism

-         Roman influence  (if human is not perfect, why show him that way?) / Dying Gaul / Laocoon

      - statues start to look like real people / start of individual art /Romans will use this

Architecture  - temple style starts in 600's - early is Doric from mainland (plain capital) - then Ionic from Ionia/Turkey (scroll or “eyes”) - then Corinthian from town of Corinth-in Sparta (leaves / most elaborate)

      - floor plan follows style of Jews / Moses?

      - room in temple center is cella, a cell (inner chamber/Holy of Holies)

      - usually built to a single god

      - best temple is called the Parthenon, to Athena (her statue was pure gold and ivory), in Athens

Music  name comes from “the Muses” or musika / starts with Apollo, but best singer ever is Orpheus (went to Hell seeking his wife)

      - early music was to embellish / never heard music and words together

      - no Greek music has survived; we have none

      - our present musical scale comes from Pythagoras

      - orchestra has nothing to do with music—it means “to dance” and was what actors did in the area in front of the main set (stage) of the theatre

      - Music for a Greek meant one’s total education, not just music as you and I know it.

      - 4 functions of Music: (1) how to play an inst. (2) music will create a balance in your soul (3) the math side/acoustics (4) key to understanding ALL music (of the spheres/Nature).

      - represents the tension in each man: intellect (Apollo/lyre) vs. passions (Dionysus, aulos)

      - music was the most important of all the arts, for it produced a balanced person
