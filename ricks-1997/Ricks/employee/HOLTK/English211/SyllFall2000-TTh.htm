PREREQUISITE SKILLS: E-211 assumes that students:
1. are familiar with the writing process (pre-writing, drafting, revising, and editing) and the conventions of writing (grammar, punctuation, mechanics, and spelling);
2. have reading skills appropriate to the college level
3. that students have been introduced to the library and forms of documentation.

COURSE DESCRIPTION: E-211 emphasizes the application of critical thinking skills to college reading and writing. Readings focus on expository and argumentative discourse drawn from various disciplines. Writing assignments spring from class readings and research. (Unlike the prerequisite E-111, which may in part employ the personal experience essay, E-211 focuses on written texts as sources for summary, analysis, critique, synthesis, and argument.)

COURSE AIMS: E-211 aims are based on the assumption that reading and writing are connected and necessary to the development of critical thinking skills.

Reading Skills

Related Writing Skills
to develop a strategy for reading college-level expository and argumentative prose to mark and annotate text; to take Cornell notes with summaries; to organize and map text; to write summaries
to see connections among ideas from different readings to write essays synthesizing related materials from several sources
to understand arguments: claims, support, warrants, assumptions, patterns of logic, fallacies of logic, distinctions between statements of fact and opinion, methods of persuasion to write analyses of arguments and position papers
to master essential research skills: finding, evaluating, and using resource materials to write papers documenting quoted, paraphrased, and summarized material; to write a resume' with cover letter
