Married students struggle with housing

by Elizabeth Hatchett
HAT01004@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Lane and Celena Gekler of Rexburg are among the 1,338 married BYU-Idaho students this semester.
Sarah Burt / Scroll
Dorm 5 has opened its doors to married students, marking the first time on-campus student-family housing has been offered.

“As the announcement was made, we knew our student body would change. There is a greater need for student family housing,” Sharon Tuckett, housing director, said.

Lane and Celina Gekeler have been married for three weeks now and are currently living in Dorm 5. Their search for housing, though, was not an easy one.

“There isn’t enough married housing,” Celina said. “Most of the housing units had a six-month to two-year waiting list. We thought for a while there that we were going to have to live separately until housing became available.”

According to the BYU-Idaho Housing Office, getting into the housing really depends on the student turnover. The waiting list for Dorm 5 consists of about a dozen people.

The dorm is appealing to couples “because it is new, nice, and in our price range,” Natalie Mills said.
The price for this new on-campus housing ranges from $250 a month for a small wheelchair accessible one bedroom apartment to $550 per month for a large two bedroom apartment.

The dorms are furnished and include couches, a kitchen table and chairs, two desks, a queen-size bed, refrigerator and dishwasher. Gas, electric, cable and telephone utilities are also included in the price.

Jared Roberts and his wife Rebecca choose to live on campus because of “the reputation of the college. We knew they were responsible and that they would manage the dorms,” he said.
