QUARTERBACK SELECTED AS NJCAA OFFENSIVE PLAYER OF THE YEAR

Ricks College quarterback Marc Dunn has been named the Offensive Player of the Year in the NJCAA football. The award is equivalent to major college football's Heisman Trophy. Dunn is the first Ricks College football player to ever receive the award. 

Dunn was the back-up quarterback for Ricks during the 1999 season. He quickly earned the starting position as quarterback for the 2000 season. He led the Vikings to an 8-3 season, a victory in the Real Dairy Bowl, and a #9 ranking in the final NJCAA poll. Meanwhile he rewrote the schools' record book. He now holds the distinction for individual games of the most passes completed (34 - old record 33 set in 1981 and met in 1994; most yards passing (605 - old record 508 set in 1998), and most TD passes (8 - ties with record set in 1981). He also set records for the season in most total offense yards (4237 - old record 3410 set in 1994), most yards passing (4351 - old record 3450 set in 1994), most TD passes (42 - old record 37 set in 1997), most passes attempted (465 - old record 417 set in 1994), and most passes completed (281 - old record 240 set in 1994). Dunn also set the NJCAA record as the individual passing leader. Defensive end Josh Rollins has been named Honorable Mention All American by the NJCAA. FRAMEWORK SET FOR NEW EXPANDED ACTIVITY PROGRAM

The Future Activities Committee has concluded its assignment to construct the framework and concepts on the new activity program for Brigham Young University-Idaho. As they gave their report to President's Council, it was deemed necessary to dissolve the committee and identify an organization which will help deliver the activity program in a timely fashion.

Garth Hall chaired the Future Activities Committee and has been appointed by President's Council to head the new student activity program. During the transition year as intercollegiate athletics is being phased out, Hall will continue to serve as the athletic director. Justin Garner, assistant track and field coach, will serve as the assistant to the athletic director.

Within the new activity organization, there are three divisions planned to give students a wide diversity of opportunities to be involved. Jim Brague represents the visual and performing arts, Lori Woodland represents physical activities, and Ric Page represents the student life and ASRC.

"As intercollegiate athletics are phased out, there will be a proportionate increase in the number of activities," says Hall. The new organization will work towards implementing the program and concepts outlined by the Future Activities Committee starting with the 2001-2002 academic year. Preliminary details will be forthcoming after the first of the year.

(For additional details, please see news release dated January 2, 2001 at http://www.byui.edu/Ricks/News/Ricks/NewsReleases/Activities_010102.htm)

 

CHANGES MADE IN REGISTRAR AND INTERNATIONAL STUDENTS ADVISOR

Kevin Miyasaki has been named the new registrar of Ricks College. He replaces Kelly Hymas who died of cancer on Nov. 22. Miyasaki is a Sugar City native and served as an assistant dean in the Dean of Students Office since 1992 and was the international student advisor. The registrar oversees registration, grade reports, transcripts, transfers and graduation. "It is a different field, but I like to learn and I am enjoying it," he says. "My background will be very helpful in working with students and other people."

He graduated from Sugar-Salem High School in Sugar City and attended Ricks before transferring to BYU. He received a bachelor's degree in social work from BYU 1977 and a master's degree in social work from Arizona State University in 1979. Prior to coming to Ricks, he was director of treatment at the Behavioral Health Center in Idaho Falls for seven years. He and his wife, Darla, live in Sugar City and have six children. He was honored in 1999 as Ricks exemplary employee. He currently serves as bishop of a married student ward at the college.

David Macbeth, has accepted the assignment as the international students advisor. He has served as an assistant dean in the Dean of Students Office for 7½ years. In his new assignment, he counsels 247 students from 48 countries helping them with cultural adjustments, government rules and regulations, assistance with finding on-campus employment, and financial needs.
