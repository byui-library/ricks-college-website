WOMEN WIN REGIONAL BASKETBALL

The Ricks women's basketball team sent a chill to the Snow College home crowd on Saturday evening (March 4) in Ephraim, Utah. After an 11-point Ricks lead had evaporated to only three points with 1:09 left in the game, four Ricks women stepped up to the free throw line and iced an 80-76 regional championship with six tosses from the charity stripe to earn a fifth trip to the national finals in the last eight years. Jamie Critchfield, Candis Wilcox, Mandie Little, and Lindy Peterson connected on six of nine opportunities to bring the regional trophy to Rexburg. Little had six consecutive points in the first half to spark a Ricks rally from a nine-point deficit to a two-point lead at the half. The Lady Vikings padded the lead in the second half before Snow rallied to cut the margin to only three points. Little had 17 points overall.

Shaking with emotion, coach Lori Woodland had to compose herself on the team bench prior to the awards ceremony. "We were focused," she said of her team's effort. "We've really come on in the last three weeks." Woodland was named Region 18 Coach of the Year. Ricks will compete in Salina, Kansas, March 14-18.

ALUMNI AWARDS

"Ricks College is founded on the firm belief that there are extraordinary possibilities in ordinary people," surmised John L. Clarke as he served as president of Ricks College for 27 years. That phrase continues to be demonstrated as Ricks students graduate and go on to live exemplary lives of service. Over the next several weeks, the Ricks Alumni Council will announce this year's recipients of alumni awards.

The Eliza R. Snow Award for the Performing and Fine Arts was given to Mary Beth Cooper Cheney. Music has been a driving force in Cheney's life. An alumna of Ricks College in 1971, she is a homemaker in Livermore, Calif., where she is ward organist, choir director, and music chairman and directs the stake choir. She also heads Valley Voices, a private children's chorus. About four years ago, she and two others founded the Blankenship Memorial Project in honor of an amateur pianist and fellow piano instructor in the Bay Area. The project provides piano lessons and singing experiences to children from low income families who would not otherwise be able to afford lessons. Cheney graduated Summa Cum Laude in piano performance from Brigham Young University and obtained a master's degree in piano pedagogy from Holy Names College in Oakland, Calif., in 1991. She is married to Ricks alumnus Nolan Dean Cheney, and they have four children.

Dr. Rex E. Head was recognized with the Distinguished Alumni Award for Humanitarian Service. After several years of visiting orphanages in Mexico, Peru, and Ecuador to provide medical assistance, Head started the non-profit organization Semesters of International Service in 1998. In January he resigned as physician at the Ricks College Student Health Center, and this month he and his family will return to Ecuador to direct the program personally and bring much-needed medical care to the orphans. His hope is to be able to help abandoned children throughout the world. He is a native of Salt Lake City but grew up in the Teton Valley of eastern Idaho. He attended Ricks College 1980-81, graduated from Brigham Young University, and obtained his doctorate degree from the University of Utah. He and his wife, Melodie, are the parents of five children.

JAZZ FESTIVAL

A popular a capella group from the Ukraine, a nationally known saxophonist, and Ricks College's own jazz musicians will highlight the second annual Ricks College Jazz Festival set for Saturday, March 11, at 7:30 p.m. in the Hart Auditorium The evening finale will feature a concert by invited guest artists Ed Calle, a tenor saxophonist from Miami; and the vocal sextet Man Sound from Kiev, Ukraine. The Ricks College jazz band, Sound Alliance, will also perform during the show. Admission for chair seats is $4 for general public. All bleacher seats are $1. Tickets are available through the Ticket Office by calling (208) 356-2230 or online at http://www.byui.edu/tickets.

Man Sound, which sings in both English and their native language, was formed in Kiev in 1995 and will be on the Rexburg campus from March 7-12. They were brought to the United States to perform in the recent Lionel Hampton Jazz Festival in Moscow, Idaho, as well as at jazz festivals at both Brigham Young University in Provo, Utah, and Ricks College.

Saxophonist Calle has been heard on thousands of recordings both as backup musician and soloist. He recorded with Gloria Estefan in her early days with the Miami Sound Machine. He's heard on Grammy-award-winning recordings by Arturo Sandoval, Vicky Carr and pop singer Jon Secada. He has also performed with Julio Iglesias, Vanessa Williams and Frank Sinatra just to name a few. In addition he is heard on many television and motion picture soundtracks.

During the full day of activities, area junior and senior high school bands will participate in an afternoon clinic where they will perform. Performances will be critiqued, and judges will work with and offer suggestions to the students. The jazz clinic will begin at 12:30 p.m. in the Snow Drama Theater. Admission is free. Those who attend will have the opportunity of watching Calle and Man Sound work with the students and talk about the music business.
