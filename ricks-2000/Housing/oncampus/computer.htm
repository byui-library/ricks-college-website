On-Campus Housing Internet Services Agreement

Students who live in the Perkins, Kerr, Barnes, Lamprecht, Ricks, Rigby, or Biddulph Halls and have “supportable” computers, can have them connected to the network, giving them Internet connectivity at no extra monthly cost.  Other Halls will be connected at a future date.

A “supportable” computer is one that has Windows 98, 2000, ME or MAC OS 8.6 - 9.1 and a USB port or a IMAC or Macintosh with built in NIC and system 8.7 or greater. For Windows PCs, students will need to purchase a 3Com USB network interface card (NIC) for approximately $60 and a patch cable for $3.00, available at the Bookstore.  Other brands of NICs may work, but are not officially supported.  The only exception to a 3Com USB NIC is a NIC that comes built in internally within a Compaq PC, such as those sold by the Bookstore, or a current Macintosh Computer with built in NIC.

Those who live in one of the connected halls and have a supportable PC need to contact the Help Desk in the Library, MCK 306 or call x2361 or email at helpdesk@byui.edu to arrange to be connected.

Here are some things you should be aware of and agree to:

  • Network jacks are not active until the HelpDesk sets up your PC.

  • T he network is a privilege and can be taken away if you break the   rules.

  • The student’s PC network configuration will be changed, including its name to reflect its location and owner, making it easily identifiable.  Students agree not to modify the network configuration, since it can cause network failure or problems.

  • Each network jack will be used only for one network connection.  The student will not attempt to connect multiple systems to one network jack, but they are allowed to switch back and forth where there is only one jack and two computers.

  • Students will not host servers nor provide services on the network.  For example, audio servers, video servers, games, web business, etc.

  • Students will not provide any dial-up services to the network.  They will not simultaneously use their modem and network connection; they will physically have one unplugged while using the other.

  • Students are expected to understand and comply with the Computer Use policy at http://www.byui.edu/standards.html (printed on the other side of this page).

  • BYU-Idaho administration actively monitors Internet use.  Anyone found abusing the privilege might face disciplinary action, including dismissal from school.

  • BYU-Idaho administration prevents access to resources that are considered either a waste of expensive bandwidth or inappropriate to its mission.  These sites include but are not limited to web chat, audio download sites, and online games which are blocked.   Students will not have unrestricted access to the BYU-Idaho network or the Internet.  Services deemed necessary will be identified and made available.

  • There are times when network maintenance must take place, usually nighttime and weekends.  During such times the network will be down.  Every reasonable attempt will be made to minimize and inform of such outages in advance.

  • Students will not use the network for any kind of business or commercial advertising.

Computer Use Policy

BYU-Idaho owns, operates, and maintains a private computing network for purpose of meeting the goals of its mission statement. Although the network connects with public networks it is not for public use. Patron access is restricted to authorized faculty, staff, students, and other patrons who have been approved by appropriate computing administrators.  Exceptions to this policy are restricted computer resources that have been explicitly identified for public use. Patrons who are not students or employees must receive written authorization from the Computer and Technology Council (CTC) for approval.

The network and computing resources at BYU-Idaho empower students and employees with many capabilities. With these benefits come responsibilities and obligations that are expected of those who use these university provided resources. Any assistance will be directed to the David O. McKay Library Helpdesk Room 306 (helpdesk@byui.edu or phone 496-2361. Patrons are expected to use resources in an ethical manner and in accordance with the guidelines found in the BYU-Idaho Honor Code. 

Patrons shall:

·   Use resources for authorized purposes

·   Protect individual user accounts and passwords and not share them with others.

·   Access information that is owned by themselves, or information which they have been given explicit authorized access.

·   Use copyrighted material in accordance with copyright law, respect the intellectual property of others, and abide by the provisions of applicable license agreements.

·   Abide by Internet usage guidelines (RFC 1281), which are available through the Internet.

Patrons shall not:

·   Use another person's files, system, user account, password, software, or data without permission.

·   Use computer programs to decode passwords or access control information.

·   Attempt to circumvent or subvert system security measures.

·   Knowingly engage in any activity that causes harm to systems or to any information stored thereon, such as creating or propagating viruses, disrupting services, or damaging files.

·   Use systems for personal or political purposes.

·   Make or use illegal copies of copyrighted software, or store or transmit such copies on BYU-Idaho systems.

·   Use mail or message services to harass, intimidate, or annoy another person. (See Electronic Mail Policy.)

·   Waste computing resources, such as, but not limited to, computing power, storage space, and printing paper.

·   Use the network in a manner not authorized by BYU-Idaho to gain commercial or personal profit or advantage.

·   Engage in any other activity that does not comply with the Honor Code, with the principles of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, or would injure the reputation of BYU-Idaho.

BYU-Idaho considers any violation of this policy to be serious. BYU-Idaho reserves the right to copy and/or examine any files or information that resides on the network or on BYU-Idaho property that is related to inappropriate use of its resources. Violators are subject to disciplinary actions as prescribed by BYU-Idaho Policy and may also be prosecuted under applicable local, state, or federal civil or criminal law.
