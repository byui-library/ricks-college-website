STUDY GUIDE #1

Entrepreneurship & Small Business Management

Part 1 Exam, Chapter 1-5

TRUE/FALSE

  1. ViaSat provides an example of a successful entrepreneurial venture based on technical expertise.

  2. An entrepreneur who operates a repair shop and whose experience consists of work as a mechanic is an opportunistic entrepreneur.

  3. The name of the company started by a woman after losing her sales job is C&B Industrial Safety.

  4. Any entrepreneur with the drive to succeed will do well.

  5. Bequeathing equal shares of ownership to children in a family business will probably create havoc.

  6. One weakness of a family business is the tendency of a family members to leave quickly when the business "falls on hard times."

  7. In a family business, there is a possibility for either conflict or harmony between business goals and family goals.

  8. Some entrepreneurial couples manage to build strong businesses while strengthening the business at the same time.

  9. Because of unique characteristics of franchising, the success rate for franchises is higher than the success rate for non-franchised businesses.

  10. The price of the product and the location of the business affect the amount of training required for the franchisee.

  11. The failure rate for independent small businesses is comparable to that for franchised businesses.

  12. Small business firms have little opportunity to franchise in other countries.

  13. An entrepreneur who starts his or her own business, rather than buying an existing business, avoids the undesirable precedents, policies, and procedures of the existing firm.

  14. Stacey Butterfield, a prospective buyer, decides that the best way to value a business in which she is interested is by estimating the amount of potential income that may be produced by the business in the future.  She is using the earning-based valuation approach to business valuation.
