Elect ladies receive enriching instructions

by Tammy Walquist
WAL01018@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Christina Woolf, a freshman from Flower Mound, Texas, attends her enrichment night in the 41st Ward. They scrapbooked with pictures of their own. Carrie Garrett / Scroll
Once a month, women are given the chance to learn skills such as cutting men’s hair, creating and maintaining a personal budget and learning safety techniques. This is made possible through Home, Family and Personal Enrichment Nights.

Enrichment Nights usually consist of a lesson and an activity. They normally last from an hour to an hour-and-a-half, according to The Church Handbook Book 2 for Priesthood and Relief Society

The activities provide girls with an opportunity to grow.

“I think that lots of people don’t take advantage of [Enrichment Night], and it’s a good opportunity to grow. Girls should give it a chance,” Rebecca Keller, a junior from Lewisville, Texas, and an enrichment teacher in the BYU-Idaho 13th Ward, said.

Enrichment Night activities are planned by the Relief Society Enrichment counselor, the ward Enrichment leader and the Enrichment teacher, Elizabeth Allen, a sophomore from Richland, Wash., and the Enrichment counselor for the 13th Ward said. Assignments are given to each member of this committee.

“My job is mainly to oversee that everything is going well and everyone’s job is going smoothly,” Allen said.

Activities for Enrichment Nights are supposed to fall under categories such as principles of the gospel; homemaking; strengthening marriages; spiritual, emotional and physical well-being; personal reliance; family living; literacy; and temple work, according to the Handbook.

Also, since the topics are so broad, it is up to the Enrichment leader’s discretion to determine which topics will be covered.

“[We] have topics [we are] supposed to be under,” Celeste Beattie, a freshman from Ammon, Idaho, and an Enrichment Leader from the 38th Ward, said. “I went through and had [ward members] mark which ones they like and will be most helpful to them. Then I plan a lesson and get an activity to help out with it.”

Some activities that Beattie’s ward has participated in include learning how to cut men’s hair, having a guest speaker on relationships and doing a service project.

However, since many college students are busy with homework and school, it may be hard for them to find time to come to Enrichment Night. Homework is probably one of the main causes for girls not coming, Sami Hansen, a freshman from Dallas, Texas, and an Enrichment Leader for the 13th Ward, said.

“Everyone has so much going on that it’s hard to spend an extra hour or hour-and-a-half to do something else,” Hansen said.

One thing to help out with this problem is to get the word out about activities early and also to have a good atmosphere, Hansen said.

“I try to get publicity out as soon as possible when I find out what we’re doing,” she said. “I also try to do the lesson first so girls can leave if they need to. I also try to have a fun atmosphere.”

Enrichment Night can provide many different experiences that can help women.

“I think Enrichment Night is a good time to bond, and it teaches us good skills for our home and family,” Allen said.

Enrichment Night is also helpful to girls in getting to know one another’s talents and abilities.

“I’ve really grown to love it and have come to enjoy it,” Hansen said. “It’s a great time to get to know girls and their talents and abilities.”
