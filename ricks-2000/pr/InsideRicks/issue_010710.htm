AMERICAN FOLK DANCERS AND BLUEGRASS BAND PREPARE FOR 25TH EUROPEAN TOUR

Ricks College is proud to announce the twenty-fifth European tour by their well-known American Folk Dancers and Bluegrass Band. They will be performing in the Kirkham Auditorium on Friday, July 20, at 2 p.m. prior to the departure on a month-long tour to Poland, the Czech Republic, Austria and Hungary. Ticket revenue goes toward travel expenses for the group. Tickets for the public are $6 from the Ticket Office at (208) 356-2230 or www.ricks.edu/tickets. The matinee performance has become a popular feature of the Summer in the Snow Entertainment Series.

This colorful team of 24 dancers and six musicians presents a variety of exciting dances that reflect the heritage of folk dance in the United States. While on tour, performance locations include street parades, festival competitions and full-length concerts. As an emissary of the United States and American folklore, the group has become internationally famous at the most prestigious folk dance festivals abroad, where they perform with many of the finest dance companies from throughout the world.

Festival performances will be presented in cities near Gdansk in northern Poland before moving to the Bielsko-Biala region of southern Poland for the Highlander Festival which is the oldest and biggest folkloristic event in the country. The team will also appear in Krakov, Poland, then Vienna, Austria, with stops in Gyor and Budapest, Hungary, before participating in the Summerfest Dance Festival in Szazhalombatta, Hungary.

Members of the American Folk Dancers and Bluegrass Band are students at Ricks College. The team is directed by Charles West, an instructor for the Ricks College Dance department and past president of the National Folk Organization of the United States. The band has been instructed by Eileen Wilcox.

LaNae Poulter, coordinator of performance tours at Ricks, explains the significance of this year's tour, "There is a history that comes with the expressions of dance. Each culture of the world has a unique past that is preserved through folk dances just as in architecture, paintings, sculptures, and other folklore. It is an honor for our group to be invited to perform in festivals that celebrate the world's folk heritage."

Funding for tours comes from a combination of student fees and generous support from the Endowment for Performance Tours.


OPUS IV RETURNS FOR ENCORE

The Montana singing group Opus IV will return to Ricks College for an encore performance Thursday, July 12. Because of their popularity last summer, the Summer in the Snow Entertainment Series at Ricks will present the group again in the Barrus Concert Hall of the Snow Center for the Performing Arts at 7:30 p.m. Tickets are $3 for general admission and available from the Ricks College Ticket Office or at the door. Call (208) 356-2230 or order online at www.ricks.edu/tickets.

The program features music, comedy and scenes from Broadway's great musicals, interwoven with classical selections by the world's great composers.

The two married couples met while singing together with the Montana Chorale and now are Montana's only professional vocal quartet. James Aiken and Nita Hamilton live in Missoula while Robert Worobec and Linda Terry live in Livingston.

They are joined by accompanist Kenneth Christensen, a piano instructor at Montana State University in Bozeman. He has served for many years as pianist for the Intermountain Opera Association. He also is an accomplished ragtime pianist and for eight years has appeared as pianist and musical director of the Virginia City Players production of The Brewery Follies.


SOUTH FAMILY PERFORMANCE WILL DEMONSTRATE THE "LEGACY OF MUSIC"

The South family of Idaho and Utah will present a concert entitled "Legacy of Music" Saturday, July 21, at 7:30 p.m. in the Barrus Concert Hall at Ricks College.

The large ensemble of musicians includes descendants of Barney and Marjorie South, including the families of David, Barry, M'Jean (Lund), Myrna, Susan (Crandall) and Randy South.

Tickets for the Summer in the Snow presentation are $3 and available from the Ricks College Ticket Office at (208) 356-2230 or from the website www.ricks.edu/tickets.

Music has played an important role in the South family from the log cabin days at the family sawmill in Island Park when violins were played by lamplight and a player piano accompanied the family chorus, to the present when family members have performed before thousands in concert halls on three continents.

The program will feature violin, cello and piano classics by Vivaldi, Dvorak, Wieniawski and Liszt. Broadway tunes by the cousins, old-time fiddling and choral numbers characterizing the spirit of our pioneer and American heritage round out the program.
