RON HAUN

    Ricks College head coach Ron Haun has concluded his twentieth season, and his career at Ricks College.  In the transition to BYU-Idaho, the school is dropping all intercollegiate sports.  Haun and the Vikings went out in style.  With an 10-1 overall record in 2001, and a 9-1 record in the WSFL, the Vikings won a share of the WSFL championship once again.  The Vikings won back-to-back WSFL titles in 1997 and 1998.  The Vikings finished 3rd in the final NJCAA poll both years, and met Butler County CC of Kansas for the NJCAA championship in the 1998 Real Dairy Bowl.  Butler won the contest 22-18 in front of 11,856 fans, a Dairy Bowl attendance record.   Ricks spent five consecutive weeks as the number 1 NJCAA football team during the 1997 season, and 9 weeks as the top rated team during 1998.   They began the season as the number one ranked team in 1999 and held the top spot for four weeks. The Vikings finished with a 10-1 performance in 1999, a 59-26 thrashing of Garden City in the Real Dairy Bowl, and a number four ranking in the final NJCAA poll.  Year 2000 followed with an 8-3 record, a 45-14 win over Snow in the Real Dairy Bowl, and a number nine ranking in the final NJCAA poll.  In the Vikings, and Haun's, final season in 2001, Ricks went 10-1 and beat Lackawanna, PA, in the Real Dairy Bowl 49-21 in front of 11,000 fans.

    Coach Haun has enjoyed an enviable 178-40-2 record in his twenty years as the Vikings head coach. Since 1984, his teams have been ranked nationally in the top 15 of the National Junior College Athletic Association's final season polls sixteen times. In 2001 the Vikings won a share of the Western States Football League championship for a ninth time.

    In December of 1992, Ron was named the Intermountain Area Collegiate Coach of the Year. The award is given annually by the Universal Athletic Service of Utah and the recipient is selected by a committee made up of newspaper and radio sportswriters from throughout the intermountain west. Prior to 1985 when the Vikings competed in the Intermountain Collegiate Athletic Conference, Haun-coached teams had never finished less than second in the league.

    Nationally, Coach Haun is second among active JC coaches in win-loss percentageat 82.3 percent.   He ranks fourth nationally among active and inactive coaches.

    Ron was born and raised in Murray, Utah, where he attended high school and was the starting quarterback for the Murray High School Trojans. After his high school graduation, he served in the United States Army for one year and in 1962 began his college playing career at Dixie College in St. George, Utah. He won the starting quarterback job both his freshman and sophomore seasons.

    In 1964, Haun transferred to Weber State College in Ogden, Utah, and as a junior and team captain, again won the starting quarterback job. After receiving his bachelor's degree from Weber in 1966, he was hired as a graduate assistant at Brigham Young University in Provo, Utah, where he eventually earned his master's degree in 1967.

    Haun's first coaching position was as an assistant at Skyline High School in Salt Lake City, Utah. He coached at Skyline from 1967-1974 during which time Skyline won three state championships.  In 1975, he returned to his alma mater, Murray High School, where he was the head coach until 1978, leading the Trojans to two state titles.

    Ron came to Ricks in 1979 to coach the quarterbacks and receivers. The following year, he became the offensive coordinator under Coach Ken Schmidt and in 1982 was appointed the Vikings head coach when Schmidt accepted a position at BYU.

    Since becoming the head coach at Ricks, Ron has led his Vikings to thirteen post-season bowl appearances and has won seven of those thirteen games.

    He is married to the former Beverly North of Heber City, Utah, and they have two children.

    ASSISTANT COACHES

    COACH RICK DIXON
    Defensive Coordinator, Linebackers
    Brigham Young, 1970
    10th Season
    Rick Dixon joined the Viking coaching staff as a volunteer assistant and is now a full time assistant coach and physical education faculty member at Ricks College.
    He played football at Brigham Young University where he was an All-WAC player. He later coached at BYU, as well as at Layton High School, North Texas University, and Oklahoma State University. Rick was coaching at Oklahoma State when the Cowboys played BYU in the 1976 Tangerine Bowl.
    Coach Dixon received his bachelor's and master's degrees from BYU and has a Utah Secondary Professional Certificate. He spent many years in the oil business and from 1981-1991 was an independent oil producer.
    Coach Dixon continues as the defensive coordinator as well as the linebacker coach.

    COACH ED LARSON
    Offensive Coordinator, Quarterbacks, Receivers
    New Mexico, 1988
    5th Season
    Ed Larson stepped right into the hot-seat as the offensive coordinator and position coach of quarterbacks and receivers in 1997. In his first year, the Viking offense set a Junior College record for points scored in a season at 544, according to "1st-N-Goal College Football Resource Services". In his second season, the Viking offense became the first junior college football team to score over 500 points in consecutive seasons. Larson’s offense made it three years in a row in 1999.
    Coach Larson calls San Jose, California, his home town. His football skills began to shine in junior college as he quarterbacked San Jose City College to a national championship in 1986. He moved on to the University of New Mexico as quarterback for the Lobos. Larson graduated in 1988 and was honored as a WAC Scholar Athlete.
    Ed’s coaching career began in 1990 as a graduate assistant at BYU. In 1992 he moved to Southern Utah University as quarterback coach. In 1995 and 1996, Larsen found himself at Snow College as the offensive coordinator. Under his tutorship, the Badgers offense ranked as one of the top five in NJCAA football both years.
    Coach Ed Larson and his wife Jeanne have five children.

     COACH WAYNE STARTIN
    Offensive Line
    Brigham Young, 1959
    15th Season
    In 1983, Startin came to Ricks and in nine years put together one of the most explosive offenses in junior college football. In 1983 and 1984, the Vikings were ranked first nationally in total offense and in 1988 they finished second in the nation in pass offense and were third in 1989.
    Coach Startin played at BYU where he earned his bachelor's degree in mathematics. He was an assistant football coach at BYU for five years and coached high school football in Utah. In 1976, he was selected as the Utah High School Coach of the Year.
    He and his wife Susan have four children.

    COACH JIM EGGLESTON
    Running Backs
    Ricks College, 1982
    7th Season
    Jim Eggleston grew up in Stockton, California. In 1980 he joined the Ricks College football team as a tight end. The Vikings won the ICAC crown that year. Eggleston returned to Ricks in 1981 and helped the Vikings to a second consecutive ICAC championship, a victory in the Valley of the Sun Bowl game, and a number two ranking on the final NJCAA football poll.
    While owning and operating a successful private business, Jim has continued his association with athletics by coaching football and basketball at four different high schools in the Sacramento, CA, area.
    He and his wife Peggy are the parents of 2 children.

     COACH MARTY DUFFIN
    Defensive Line
    University of Montana, 1996
    5th Season
    Marty Duffin knows about winning. At Idaho Falls High School, his Tigers won 23 consecutive football games and two straight State Championships. As a defensive tackle for Ricks, the Vikings went 20-2 during Duffins two years in 1992 and 1993, winning consecutive WSFL titles and finishing ranked #2 nationally in 1992. Marty then moved on to the defensive line at the University of Montana where he started as a junior and senior in 1994 and 1995. Those Grizzly teams went 24-5 and won the NCAA Division 1-AA Championship in 1995.
    Along the way, Coach Duffin was named Student of the Year at Idaho Falls High School and the University of Montana.
    Coach Duffin’s wife Melinda, is a graduate of BYU. They have one child.
