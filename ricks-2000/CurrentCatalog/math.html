THE MATHEMATICS PROGRAM
We provide students the opportunity to explore and use the beautiful and powerful language of mathematics.

Students who major in mathematics and go on to complete a Bachelors degree or higher can choose from a wide variety of careers ñ from forecasting next month's weather to predicting the winner of an upcoming election, from designing insurance policies to planning large scale irrigation projects, from teaching students the Pythagorean Theorem to creating new theorems. Mathematicians discover and explore the properties of abstract structures and apply them to almost every area of human endeavor.

Need Help?
If you need to acquire or renew mathematical skills, we offer courses in beginning levels of algebra. The department takes primary responsibility for the administration of the mathematics graduation requirement.

Please study carefully the program and course descriptions that follow and become as excited as we are about offering them.

THE COMPUTER SCIENCE PROGRAM
Computer Science is "the study of computers, including their design, operation, and use in processing information. Computer Science combines both theoretical and practical aspects of engineering, electronics, information theory, mathematics, logic, and human behavior. Aspects of Computer Science range from programming and computer architecture to artificial intelligence and robotics." (Microsoft Press Computer and Internet Dictionary) The Computer Science curriculum teaches students how to develop software applications but not how to use applications such as word processors and spreadsheets.

Students graduating with a Bachelors degree in Computer Science are prepared to enter the work force and develop applications as a software engineer. These applications impact all aspects of our lives. Students may also choose to continue their education and research some of the many facets of computer science. Regardless, Computer Science majors will have a challenging and rewarding career. This major provides a solid background in computer science that facilitates transfer to a four year university by providing experience in:
- algorithm development
- procedural and object oriented
design and programming
- software engineering practices
- data structures
- computer organization
- theoretical foundations

New Computer Science majors should be prepared, at a minimum, to take calculus (Math 162 or

COURSE DESCRIPTIONS
Credits

Math

Math 100A Arithmetic 1
Three contact hours per week

A study of arithmetic and applications using arithmetic. This course is only for those needing a review of elementary school arithmetic including signed numbers, fractions, decimals and percents.

Math 100B Beginning Algebra Part I 1
Three contact hours per week

The arithmetic of integers and rational numbers as well as an introduction to algebra. Success in the next math class requires a grade of "B" or better. This course is recommended for those needing basic algebra before taking progressively higher math courses.

Math 100C Beginning Algebra Part II 1
Prerequisite Math 100B and consent of instructor
Three contact hours per week

This is a continuation of beginning algebra introduced in Math 100B. Roughly equivalent to the second half of the first year of high school algebra. Linear equations and their graphs, systems of linear equations, and quadratics.

Math 100R Introduction to College Math .5-2
Prerequisite: Acceptance into summer CSS Program.

Preparation for college level math classes. A variety of topics will be covered depending upon the Instructor.

Math 100S Agricultural Mathematics 3
Prerequisite: Admission to the technical agricultural programs Three lecture hours per week or offered in a lab setting
Fee $15 for supplies
Mathematics applied to agricultural problems.

Math 101* Intermediate Algebra 3
Prerequisite: Ability to demonstrate proficiency in first year algebra or Math 100B and C with a grade of "B" or higher
Three contact hours per week

Fundamental operations of algebra, properties of exponents, solving linear, fractional and quadratic equations, graphing linear and quadratic functions. Math 101 may not be taken for credit if Math 110 has been completed with a grade of "B" or higher. We strongly recommend a grade of "B" or better before taking a higher math class.

Math 108* Quantitative Reasoning 3
Prerequisite: Math 101 or two years of high school algebra
Three contact hours per week

A course designed to meet the mathematical needs of the liberal arts student. Topics may include mathematical modeling, regression, finance mathematics, probability, statistics, logic, and mathematical patterns and aesthetics. This course will satisfy both the Ricks College and Idaho Core math requirements but will not serve as a prerequisite to other mathematics courses.
Graphing calculator may be required. (See class schedule.)

Math 110* College Algebra 3
Prerequisite: Math 101 or two years of high school algebra
May be taken daily for 10 weeks or MWF for the full semester

Theory and applications of polynomial, rational, exponential, and logarithmic functions, and systems of linear equations. (This can be followed in the same semester with Math 111.)
Graphing calculator required.
(See class schedule)

Math 111* Trigonometry 2
Prerequisite: Math 101 or equivalent
May be taken as a full semester class or daily for five weeks beginning at the 10th week of the semester.

Circular functions, triangle relationships, identities, inverse trig functions, complex numbers, and applications.
Graphing calculator required.
(See class schedule)

Math 162* Plane Analytic Geometry and Calculus 4
Prerequisite: Math 110 and Math 111
Five hours of lecture per week

One-dimensional differential and integral calculus with applications.
Graphing calculator may be required.
(See class schedule)

Math 205* Basic Concepts for Elementary Education Teachers 3
Prerequisite: Math 108 or Math 110
Three hours of lecture per week

This course is only open to Education majors. The content focuses on important mathematical ideas and their interrelationships. Problem solving, sets, relations, functions, systems of numeration, operations on the real numbers and number theory are included.

Math 206* Math Concepts for Elementary Education Teachers 3
Prerequisite: Math 205
Three hours of lecture per week

Enrichment or extended study of the concepts covered in Math 205 plus the study of geometry, probability, statistics and logic for elementary education teachers.

Math 209* Elementary Calculus 4
Prerequisite: Math 110
Five hours of lecture per week

A one-semester terminal course of single and multi-variable calculus designed primarily for students in biology, agriculture, and business. Topics include derivatives, integrals, and their applications.
Graphing calculator may be required.
(See class schedule)

Math 221* Principles of Statistics 3-4
Prerequisite: Math 110
Four hours of lecture per week

Frequency distributions; measures of central tendency and dispersion; elementary probability; regression and correlation; sampling, statistical inference and estimation including the normal, t-test, chi-square, and analysis of variance tests; multiple contrasts and multiple regression.
Graphing calculator may be required.
(See class schedule)

Math 243* Linear Algebra 3
Prerequisite: Math 162
Three hours of lecture per week
*GE Credit in Math
Linear systems, matrices, vector spaces, linear transformations, determinants, eigenvalues, eigenvectors.

Math 263* Solid Analytical Geometry and Calculus 4
Prerequisite: Math 162
Four hours of lecture per week
Applications and methods of integrations, hyperbolic functions, infinite series, polar coordinates, parametric forms and vectors.

Prerequisite: Math 263
Four hours of lecture per week

Partial derivatives, n-dimensional vectors, multiple integrals, integral theorems of vector calculus including Green's and Stokes' theorems.

Prerequisite: Math 243 or concurrent registration
Four hours of lecture per week

An introduction to the theory of ordinary differential equations with applications, linear systems, series solutions, the Laplace Transform, and numerical methods.

Prerequisite: Math 263 or consent of instructor (Winter Only)

Reading, understanding and creating rigorous mathematical proofs. Fundamentals of set theory,
relations, functions and cardinality will be covered. Instructors may choose to cover additional topics from algebra, analysis or topology.

CS 100* Computer Basics 0-2

This self-tutorial option prepares the individual for challenging the knowledge and competency exam's to meet the General Education Computer basics requirement. Areas covered by this option include: hardware and operating systems, word processing, spread sheet, database applications and ethical use of computers.

CS 100L* Computer Basics Lab 2

This computer lab prepares the individual for challenging the knowledge and competency exam's to meet the General Education Computer basics requirement while applying these skills to a specific discipline. Areas covered by this option include: hardware and operating systems, word processing, spreadsheet, database applications and ethical use of computers.

CS 102 Introduction to Computer Science 1
(Fall, Winter, and Summer)

Introduction to Computer Science is designed to help a student determine if he/she would like to major in this field. Topics include: Basic concepts of Computer Science, software engineering, and software development and review of curricula at Ricks College and selected transfer schools.

CS 144 Introduction to Programming 3
Prerequisite: Math 101 or high school equivalent
(Fall, Winter, and Summer)

Introduction to programming using the C++ programming language, Algorithm development, problem solving, testing and debugging, C++ language, and classes. All programming is in a UNIX environment. This class is for non-Computer Science majors and those with no programming experience.

CS 165 Object Oriented Programming 3
Prerequisite: CS 144 and instructor permission
(Fall, Winter, and Summer)

Software design and development using an object oriented language, Algorithm formulation, an introduction to software engineering, and object oriented programming. Utilizes C++ in a UNIX environment.

CS 170 Introduction to Software Development 4
Prerequisite: CS 102 or CS 144,
Math 110
(Fall, Winter, and Summer)

Introduction to software design and development, Algorithm formulation, introduction to software engineering and structured programming, procedural and object oriented programming. Utilizes C++ in a UNIX environment.

CS 173 Web Programming 2
Prerequisite: CS 144 or CS 170
(Fall, Winter, and Summer)

Introduction to Web programming in a client-server environment. Web site design, HTML forms, and CGI programming with Perl .

CS 233 Introduction to Computer Organization 3
Prerequisite: CS 144 or CS 170
(Fall only)

Fundamentals of computer organization and operation. Memory structure, registers, arithmetic and logical functions, instruction formats, addressing modes, machine and assembly languages, and internal-external data representation.
CS 235 Foundations of Computer Science I 4
Prerequisites: CS 170 and Math 162
(Fall only)

Iteration, induction, recursion, lists, trees, sets, relations, functions; mathematical analysis of algorithms and data models; object-oriented implementation of abstract data types.

CS 236 Foundations of Computer Science II 4
Prerequisite: CS 235
(Winter only)

Continuation of CS 235; relations, graphs, automata, grammars, propositional and predicate logic. Implementation of object-oriented algorithms

CS 246 Advanced Object Oriented Programming 3
Prerequisite: CS 235
(Winter only)

Continuation of study of object oriented programming. Advanced software development and object oriented design. Code larger programs. Utilizes the Java language.

CS 290 Special Topics 1-3
Prerequisite: consent of instructor
Faculty/student consultation determines an area of research study beyond the subject matter covered in standard courses. Content and credit are approved by the instructor.
