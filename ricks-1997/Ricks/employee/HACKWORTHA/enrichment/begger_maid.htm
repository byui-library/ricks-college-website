The Begger Maid

by Alfred Lord Tennyson

Tennyson's poem inspired the painter Sir Edward Burne-Jones.

Victorian painters often found inspiration for their work in historical events, ancient myths, and literature.

The information below comes from www.webmagick.co.uk.

"Cophetua was an ancient African king who disdained women and was immune to love. However, one day he saw a beggar girl, and, falling instantly in love with her, vowed to make her his queen. Burne-Jones drew upon two sources for the subject:

(1) An Elizabethan ballad by Richard Johnson - A Song of a Beggar and a King (1612)

(2) Tennyson's poem - The Beggar Maid"

 

 Her arms across her breast she laid;
She was more fair than words can say;
Barefooted came the beggar maid
Before the king Cophetua.
In robe and crown the king stept down,
To meet and greet her on her way;
‘It is no wonder,’ said the lords,
‘She is more beautiful than day.’

As shines the moon in clouded skies,
She in her poor attire was seen;
One praised her ankles, one her eyes,
One her dark hair and lovesome mien.
So sweet a face, such angel grace,
In all that land had never been.
Cophetua sware a royal oath:
‘This beggar maid shall be my queen!’

 

King Cophetua and the Beggar Maid 1884
Tate Gallery, London

Read a significant statement by Burne-Jones.  He tells how hard he worked on the painting.
"The anemones in the girl's hand symbolize rejected love, while the characters represent Burne - Jones himself and his wife Georgiana; . . .  "

"Burne-Jones started working on the painting in about 1861 and laboured at it for another 20 odd years."
