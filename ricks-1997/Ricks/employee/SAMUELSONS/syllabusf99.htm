Purposes of this class:

To build Zion by increasing faith, deepening commitment to and understanding of the Word.

To increase the ability to think, read, feel, and express ideas and emotions.

To understand and practice the terms, forms, themes, and mentalities of poetry.

To understand poetry and the poetic impulse by experience as well as by word.

To make an interest in poetry connect with life habits.

Some of my educational/pedagogical assumptions:

A good, hard, fair class is better than an easy class.

In the long run, analysis increases rather than decreases appreciation. True appreciation has a strong element of cognitive understanding to it.

Readers (interpreters) of poetry have not only the right but the obligation to participate with the author in creating meaning.

Not all interpretations are created equal; those based on evidence in the text are superior to those which exists merely as association within the interpreter.

There is a field of admissible interpretations, a variety of "readings" of a poem based upon textual evidence that may contradict each other but which contain possible truths or meanings. (This class is not about replicating my interpretation, the authors' or critics', but about being able to responsibly creating your own valid interpretation).

Understanding should precede judgement. "I understand this poem" is a greater, more valuable assertion than "I like this poem."
