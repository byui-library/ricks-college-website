Session A2

Technology in the Recruitment and Admission Process

Recorder Name:

Kimberlie Moock, Chapman University

Presenter:
Marc Meredith, California Lutheran University

In our profession the utilization of technology is not only becoming increasingly more important, but also increasingly more diverse, and changing rapidly.  In the last 15 years we have gone from rotary phones to voice mail to pagers; from paper applications to disk applications to web applications.  This change in technology has allowed admission officers to reshape how we work everyday.  Has technology made recruitment easier?  Yes.  Has technology made recruitment harder?  Yes.  With technology enhancing our operations and changing the way we do business where do we go from here?

At CLU we have been working at utilizing technology tools that are at our fingertips.  It is not always necessary to spend the entire budget on technology enhancements.  Utilize what you have.  At CLU we have reorganized how we operate in order to maximize technology and reach our enrollment goals.  Streamlining our admissions activities now allows us to focus on corresponding with students via phone and e-mail.

We have developed a tracking and rating system utilizing our administrative software.  This system allows us to rate our prospects and spend quality time sending the right message to the right student.  Our counselors use a customized access program to rate and comment on students they come in contact with.  We download and upload the information on a daily basis allowing for both systems to remain current.  The access program allows the counselors to travel and recruit with all of the information we know about each student.

Session A4

Creating Effective Workplace Cultures 

Facilitator:
Julia Pomerenk, Pacific Lutheran University

Recorder Name:
 Rose Smith, California State University Polytechnic
Presenter:
Clifford A. Ramirez, University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA)

Rarely do we stop to think about workplace culture -- unless it's to complain about the way things are or to confess the bosses sins.  Yet, we are all leaders because we influence our co-workers through actions and through language.  We have an impact on our culture.  Blaine Lee says, “the principles you live by create the world you live in; if you can change your principles, you can change your world."  We have the power to change our workplace culture, but first we must become conscious of how we influence our culture.

Many theories have been developed to explain our interrelationships and all of them focus on two areas - my relationship with myself and my relationship with others.  These are the two components of emotional intelligence as well as countless other philosophies.

In a 1992 book entitled Empowering Innovative People, Gretz and Drozdek suggested a model for understanding our relationships from the perspective of self-esteem and our regards for others.  At one end of the spectrum was low self-esteem and regard for others -- evident in bureaucracies that revolve around policies and procedures rather than human judgment.  At the opposite end, trust and interdependence lead to a culture of collaboration.

The Franklin Covey Film Max and Max illustrates the way in which we affect each other.  Our attitudes and a behavior do not really change from our personal to our professional relationships.  Failure and frustration in one area essentially yield the same in the other.  How do you break the cycle?

Self-awareness and a critical examination of our principles is the beginning.  We want to act in adherence to the principles we profess in mission statements and personal declarations.  We want to empower and collaborate for a better world.  As Dr. Margaret Wheatley asserts in Leadership and the New Science "Relationship is all there is."

Purpose: An opportunity to meet and discuss the enrollment status verification process.

In order to simplify the process, in 1993 an electronic process was proposed.  The clearinghouse compares information with borrowers and guarantors and reports information both to them and the federal government.  In 1994, the process was endorsed by AACRAO - it works!  About 2800 institutions of higher educations (with enrollments over 11 million) are using the clearinghouse - that's over 80% of all enrolled higher education students.  The NSLC is doing the work that was done by hand at no cost to us and more students are being correctly accounted for.  All guarantee agencies are participating with the clearinghouse.

Session A6

Becoming a Leader (Part I)

Recorder Name:
Georgia McRae, Seattle University

Presenters:
Roger Swanson and Faith Weese, Publishers of "Becoming a Leader in Enrollment Services"

Enrollment Services personnel are in increasing demand to help lead institutions as they face the challenges of changes in technology, demographic shifts and changes in the market.  This session focused on four essential qualities needed to become such leaders:

  1.  Maturity - which includes self-responsibility, self-discipline, vision, value of people, a positive attitude, and the desire to continually grow.  

  2. Integrity - includes such things as being a good model (walk the talk), committed to the university and it's mission, honesty, ethical in your dealings with all contingencies, exhibiting discretion and keeping confidences, and sharing credit when it is due.  

  3. Judgment - in the five areas of planning (thinking ahead) seeing and understanding the big picture (from top to bottom), understanding yourself - discernment and setting priorities.  Judgment is summed up in 3 words - Planning, Deciding, and Action

  4. Skills - the most important skill any leader can have a knowing how to get results using effective interpersonal communication and time management practices.

Finally, this session's lesson can be summed up in "The Oz Principle."  People hold inside themselves the power to rise above their circumstances and to get the results they want.  As leaders it is our job to have the courage to "see" it (the Lion), the heart to "own" it (the Tin Man), the wisdom to "solve" it (the Scarecrow), and the power to "do" it (Dorothy).

Session A7

Arizona Course Applicability System: Support for Transfer Students

Facilitator:
Linda Burns, Arizona State University

Recorder Name:
Donna Fischer, Phoenix College

Presenter:

Amy Fountain, CAS Transfer Analyst

CAS, A national project, is a web-based product which uses degree-audit and transfer articulation software to assist students transferring from a two-year institution to a four-year college or University.  CAS development is lead by Miami University's Degree Audit and Reporting System (DARS) Project in partnership with the State of Arizona.  CAS is an advisement tool and only shows equivalency not applicability.

CAS provides a "course equivalency guide" that will replace the Annual Publication in use at this time.  It also provides "academic programs" and "transfer planning aids."

The academic program in CAS is color coded to help make areas of deficiencies or completions easier to read and understand.

The transfer planning aids are both interactive and pro-active. Students will be able to enter course work and have it apply to program requirements, the planning aids show what requirements remain unsatisfied.  Students can choose the college they wish to attend and CAS will show the courses available with that school which would satisfy requirements for a specific program.  It will also show what the student still needs to fulfill the requirements for completion of the program.  The course equivalency guide provides customized views of sets of equivalency rules that have been established between participating schools.

CAS offers Admissions officers and registrars help in facilitating recruitment by providing world wide web access to your school's academic progress, by linking students directly to your on-line admissions applications, and giving perspective students hints for maximizing transferability of course work.  CAS also offers help in facilitating completion, and assisting with the "swirling student" automation of transfer evaluation and decision support.

There are technical requirements needed for CAS.  The student must have access to the Internet, Microsoft Internet Explorer Version 4.01 or higher, or Netscape Navigator version 4.0 or higher.  The schools must have access to or a commitment to become a CAS server site and should also purchase the CAS product, including licensing for DAR win.  CAS will interface with your existing system.  Administrators must use a PC (windows 95 or better) and should use a Pentium processor with 32 megabytes of RAM or better.

CAS is a wonderful tool for advisement and should enhance and interface with your degree audit system.
