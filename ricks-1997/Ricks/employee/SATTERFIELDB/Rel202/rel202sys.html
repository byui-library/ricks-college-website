COURSE OBJECTIVES

1. To be able to identify and use a variety of resources that aide in the study of the Old Testament.

2. To understand the nature and characteristics of the Canaanite religion. Be able to identify the various gods worshiped by the Canaanites.

3. To understand the history of Israel from Solomon to the return of the Jews from Babylonian captivity.

4. To understand the history of the scattering of Israel as well as the purpose of the scattering and gathering of Israel.

5. To understand the Old Testament prophets teachings concerning the scattering and gathering of Israel.

Texts

The only texts required for this class are the scriptures of the LDS church (Bible, Book of Mormon, Doctrine and Covenants, Pearl of Great Price). Bring all four standard works to class every time!

C. Quizzes There will be several pop quizzes given during the semester. They will be given at the beginning of the class concerning the material discussed in the previous classes. The quiz can only be taken during the class it is offered in - there will be no make up quizzes! The quizzes will be worth 20% of the overall grade.

D. Commentary You will be required to write a verse-by-verse commentary on a chapter of your choice from the prophetic writings of Old Testament. The commentary is due at the beginning of class on April 3 - NO LATTER!! This assignment is worth 40% of the overall grade.

Objective: To take an unfamiliar chapter from the prophetic writings and, by using various resources, discover on your own what the Lord through his prophet is teaching us in that chapter.

Audience: The audience to whom you are writing are members of the church who have a fundamental knowledge of the gospel but who know relatively nothing regarding the chapter you are discussing.

Grading: The commentary will be graded using the following criteria.

1. Introduction. The commentary should begin with an introduction containing the following: (1) briefly tell about the prophet who wrote the chapter; (2) any historical information needed to understand the chapter; (3) any other information that is needed for the reader to appreciate the chapter. (10 Points)

2. Verse by verse commentary. Keeping paragraphs in mind, comment on the various verses within your chapter. (60 Points)

3. Resources. Use various resources to help you understand the chapter. Cite those resources in your commentary using either endnotes, footnotes, or within the text itself. You must have a least one references to the following resources: (1) Bible Dictionary, (2) Hebrew Dictionary, (3) Scholarly commentary, (4) General Authorities, (5) your own opinion. (10 Points)

4. Conclusion. The commentary should end with a conclusion stating what has been learned from this chapter that is valuable for Latter-day Saints. (10 Points)

5. Bibliography. There must be a bibliography at the end of the commentary of the resources you used. (10 Points)

1 Kings 17

1-7 Elijah sealed the heavens shut that there would be no rain except at his command. He hid in the wilderness near the brook Cherith and was fed by ravens.

8-16 The Lord sent Elijah to Zarephath near Zidon (Canaanite territory). He met a widow woman who feeds him at his command. Her barrel of meal and cruse of oil last a year at Elijah's command.

17-23 The widow's son dies. Elijah raises him from the dead.

24 The woman exclaimed, "Now by this I know that thou art a man of God"

1 Kings 18

1-6 The Lord commanded Elijah to return to the court of Ahab. As he came near to Ahab's palace, Elijah met Obadiah, the governor of Ahab's house, looking for food to keep Ahab's animal's alive.

7-16 Elijah asks Obadiah to go and get Ahab.

17-40 Elijah challenges Ahab's belief in Ba'al. He has Ahab gather all Israel to Mt. Carmel. He asks, "How long halt ye between to opinions? if the LORD be God, follow him: but if Baal, then follow him." He then asks for a contest. Both he and the priests of Ba'al would perform a sacrifice to their respective god. The god that answered with fire (lightening) would be god. Jehovah, Elijah's god, answered with fire alone. Elijah then had all the priests of Ba'al killed.

41-46 Elijah tells Ahab to return to his palace for rain was coming. Ahab flees in his chariot but Elijah runs and beats Ahab to the palace - he was there waiting for him!

1 Kings 19

1-3 Jezebel, Ahab's wife, sends a message to Elijah warning him that she is going to have him killed for destroying her priests. Elijah fled south to Beer-sheba.

4-8 Elijah left his servant and went a day's journey into the wilderness to die - he is discouraged! An angel comes and tells Elijah to leave. But he doesn't. The angel returned and once more told him to get going. Elijah does but goes to Mt. Sinai or Horeb.

9-18 The Lord chastises Elijah for coming to Mt. Sinai instead being in Israel to his prophetic work. He is told to get a companion who will help him in his work and take over his prophetic calling when Elijah is to die.

19-21 Elijah returned to Israel and found his new companion, Elisha.
