I. COURSE DESCRIPTION

The course is designed to provide the student with drafting training and experience in detailing of construction problems dealing with wood, steel, concrete, and masonry. Property descriptions, construction assemblies, cabinet elevation and detailing, construction terminology, drafting technique, and current detailing technique practices will be emphasized.

II. INTRODUCTION

The concept of detailing becomes an interesting part of architecture when it is realized that detailing is a means of controlling the total building process. Sentiments are usually expressed from either the architect or contractor, when quality construction is involved, that there is never enough information on a set of drawings or not enough details drawn. The word "detail" covers the broad category of isolated and enlarged drawings which, together with the plans, elevations, and other drawings, and specifications form the complete set of construction documents. Since the knowledge of details and how parts are assembled is absolutely necessary for a drafter, a designer, or a construction manager, these areas will be covered in the process of creating the "detail".

This course is intended to follow ARCH 155 - Residential Drafting and Design. Therefore, the prerequisite of ARCH 155 or consent of the instructor is required.

A. Credit hours 3
B. Class lecture hours/week 4+
C. Class lab hours/week 4+
D. Individual lab hours/week 15+

Drafting lab time is required each week outside of the regular class time to do the drafting assignments. All outside lab time will solely depend on individual need to complete the assigned drafting problems. Give yourself ample time so skills and technique can be developed and not rushed just to get an assignment completed. A general guideline is two hours outside of class for each hour in class for reading and doing your drawing assignments.

III. COURSE OBJECTIVES

The following objectives give the overall goals that each student should seek to accomplish during the semester.

A. Discover the items that necessitate details.
B. Approach nontypical details logically and systematically.
C. Discriminate details that deal with structure from aesthetics, and from the environment.
D. Be able to detail within certain confined limitations, such as building codes, manufacturing limits, and the ability of the workers out in the field.
E. Establish an appreciation for a comprehensive set of details in a set of working drawings.
F. Comprehend the total scope of detailing as an integral part of the preparation of full service in a project.
G. The student will demonstrate the skill of architectural detailing by completing drawings of industrial quality in the following areas: lumber and metal connections, welding, masonry, footing details, exterior wall sections, roof connections, door and window details, stairways, fireplaces, interior elevations, and cabinet details.
H. The student will develop line quality, techniques, and presentation skills in drafting the details.
I. Develop a portfolio of architectural detail drawings that show architectural lettering and line technique and dimensioning practices.

V. ORGANIZATION OF THE COURSE

This course is organized into units of study. Units are established by the time frame and complexity of material, they are noted on the assignment sheet. Each unit has assignments to be completed and noted references for reading. Study questions and terminology are listed at the end of each chapter of the text, and students are expected to spend time researching the answers and understanding the material of each chapter BEFORE the lecture.

VI. TEACHING METHODS

There will be lectures and discussion periods during each lecture period. Students are required to be in attendance at all lectures. The failure to attend the lecture could bring about a failing grade in the course, see Attendance below. There are planned labs with each class period, but additional outside drawing time will be required. Advanced students are encouraged to help slower students. Examples of technique and solutions are available in the text. Students are encouraged to prepare adequately, before attempting the drawing assignments, by reading the text material and reviewing notes of lecture material.

VII. EVALUATION AND GRADING

To satisfactorily complete the course, a performance of 70% or better is required on the examinations and course work. The students performance in the following categories will determine the final grade:

A. Attendance and Class Participation: The college attendance policy applies to all students enrolled. Roll will be taken each class period by a seating chart. The following comments relate to attendance:

1. Grading and attendance have a direct relationship.

a. Missing lectures significantly lowers comprehension of course material.
b. Class participation is essential to the goals of the course.
c. In past years, students with more than a few absences had the lowest grades.
d. In questions of a close grade, students with unexcused absences will not be given extra consideration.

2. Only excused absences may be made up.

a. The instructor will determine what is an excused absence based on the following:
   1. The student providing requested information about the absence.
   2. The validity of the excuse, illness, field trips, etc. are valid.
   3. The circumstances being unavoidable by the student.
   4. Notification to instructor in advance before absence.
b. All absences will be considered unexcused until the student notifies the instructor otherwise.
c. The student must notify the instructor within two class periods after his return if the student wants the absence considered as excused.
d. Work on the day of an unexcused absence will be recorded as a zero in the grade book. This includes quizzes, tests, and drawing assignments due.
e. It is the students responsibility to request make-up work for excused absences.
f. Signed "excused absence slips" must be given to the instructor before the absence.
g. All work not made up within two weeks will be recorded as a zero, unless direction from the instructor has granted more time.

3. A point system for class attendance is used:

a. Each student is given 100 points at the beginning of the semester.
b. For each absence, 5 points will be deducted.
c. For each tardy, 2 points will be deducted.
d. For leaving class early, 2 points will be deducted.
e. Total points are added to student's point tally at the end of the semester.

B. Detail Drawing Assignments: In order to acquire practical experience, the student must draw many details that will help develop good construction practices. This requires utilization of correct drafting practices and a thorough understanding of how parts go together. Each unit of details will help supply this exposure and will be the vehicle for learning how parts are assembled in the field. It is very important to keep current with the drawing assignments, so follow the class schedule very closely. Each given assignment has one week to be completed. After this time, a 10% late penalty will be assessed. Two weeks after the assignment was given, the drawing is dead and no points will be given. All drawing assignments will be graded on a scale of 1 to 10, with 10 being the best evaluation. Point exceptions will be given in class.

C. Detail Drawing Booklet: The students are to compile the detail drawings into a booklet and present their booklet to the instructor at the end of the semester. This booklet is much like a portfolio. There will be 26 drawings/exercises in the booklet to be evaluated. THIS BOOKLET WILL CONTAIN ONLY THE CORRECTED OR MODIFIED ORIGINALS. However, the marked-up blueprints must be included separately. So keep all your evaluated drawings. The H.O. Line Exercise will be the booklet cover sheet.

D. Quizzes: Quizzes will be given, regularly, dealing with concepts from lectures, reading assignments, chapter terminology, and review questions of each chapter. Quizzes should be used to review for unit tests. Except in the case of an excused absence, no make-up will be accepted.

E. Unit Examinations: Unit text concepts, lecture material, terminology, codes, and text study questions will be the basic material tested. There are four unit tests. True/ False and multiple choice questions will be the method of evaluation. Unit examinations are given in the testing center. Only one make-up test is permitted. No retakes are permitted.

F. Final Examination: Comprehensive objective type examination: true/false and multiple choice test will be given at the conclusion of the course to evaluate knowledge and skills gained in the course.

G. Grade Determination: The final grade is established by points acquired on drawing assignments, attendance, drawing booklet, quizzes, unit test scores, and the final examination. The students score will be compared to the total points possible, adjusted (curve), and a percentage will be determined and compared to the school percentage system given below:

A   93% above              C-   72%-70%
A- 92%-90%                D+  69%-67%
B+ 89%-87%                D    66%-63%
B   86%-83%                D-   62%-60%
B-  82%-80%                F     59% below
C+ 79%-77%                I      Incomplete
C 76%-73%

For mid-term grade: A straight percentage is used, no curve is figured for the mid-term grade. The mid-term grade gives the student a "rough" estimate of his/her standing in class.
