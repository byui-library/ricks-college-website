USEFUL WEBSITES FOR BUSINESS

http://www.sec.gov/edaux/searches.htm -- The Edgar Database.

The forms you would search include the 10Q, the 10K and the 14 forms -- the Proxy statements. These are the official forms submitted by companies about who owns stock in a company, about revenues, ownership, board of directors, and executive compensation about a company. This is the motherlode of all data. It is official and legally binding. Saying you know how to do basic SEC research can be a plus in getting hired.

http://www.prnewswire.com/home.shtml -- PR Newswire.

This database is updated continuously and features the latest press releases from companies as the come out. Though not as good for investors, it is still helpful. For journalists, you can find an occasional photo and numerous story ideas. If I were a business journalist, I would make this a weekly, if not daily, place to look for story ideas on major competitors.

http://university.smartmoney.com/ -- A guide to investing.

Smartmoney.com is Dow Jones guide to investing. It's university portion has one of the best places to learn about the basics of investing anywhere on the web. Smartmoney is opinion-heavy, less strong on hard data, but still an excellent secondary source. Departments/Investing101/ in the university may be the best place to start learning I have seen.

http://www.monsterboard.com/ -- The classic online job center.

This, along with some others such as careers.com and the WSJ's free career site, is a premiere place to go when looking for a job. It can often have as many as a quarter million websites.

http://www.census.gov/ -- The source for great demographic data.

You can find out detailed information about people at this site. On the first page of census.gov, simply go to the county/state data as listed at the top of the page. Click in a couple of times and -- you are in specific detail about all counties in Idaho or any county in the United States. This can be significant in determining how successful a business may be in Idaho. It also has an American factfinder feature that can be useful. Regardless, this is a terrific website for demographic data. Its only real fault is that data is sometimes a little bit on the old side.

http://www.pathfinder.com/money/ -- An excelling money site.

This site remains filled with significant financial information. Again, much of its information is opinion/secondary data. You can track you stocks and also have your portfolio examined by a computer for its strengths and weaknesses. That is one thing to make it unique.

http://www.fool.com -- A good place for starters.

A good place for beginning investors to learn about the basics. Still, in many ways, it differs little from some of the other money sites with strong doses of advice and opinion.

http://finance.yahoo.com/ -- A source from primary data on stocks, etc.

The quickest way to primary source data on stocks. Less is available on mutual funds. Though others exist, this free service on stocks and companies can give all the primary, logical data you might need to make a decision on what stock to buy -- or sell. It has much less "opinion." This is significant.

http://www.financecenter.com/ -- The online calculator center.

This helps you plan your own finances a variety of ways using calculators. It is simple, straightforward, and regularly seen as one of the most useful financial websites online.

http://www.hoovers.com/ -- The online information center about businesses.

Again, a source of primary data on businesses. It gives incredible detail for subscribers and non-subscribers to its service about businesses.

BY THE WAY, all of these businesses, and others like them, employ writers. This is where business journalists can and do work and can and do make decent money.

 

Good luck on your research.
