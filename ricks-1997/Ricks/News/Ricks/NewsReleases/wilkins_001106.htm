Ricks College News Release
November 6, 2000

Find, develop and give gifts

REXBURG, Idaho — Finding, developing and giving of gifts was the theme of BYU Academic Vice President Alan Wilkins’s talk Tuesday at the weekly Ricks College devotional.

"Today I want to talk to you about making something of yourselves, about developing the gifts you have been given, and giving them in ways that bless others rather than in ways that merely conform to the world’s definition of value," he said. "I also want to consider with you, in contrast, what occurs in our lives when we look beyond the mark and try to make something of ourselves that is other than what we can or should make of ourselves."

Wilkins illustrated his point of finding, developing and giving of talents and gifts through scripture, personal experiences and two Broadway plays.

"I have chosen to comment briefly on two plays: Arthur Miller’s ‘Death of a Salesman’ and Peter Shaffer’s ‘Amadeus.’ In each of these plays, we see the main character is near the end of his life. We see people considering lessons from life from which we can learn much," he said.

In "Death of a Salesman," the main character, Willy Loman, had wanted fame and recognition, but was eventually fired and committed suicide. "It seemed to me that Willy Loman could have contributed so much more to life and his family if he had only been able to accept who he needed to be and was able to be," he said. "He was afraid of his greatest gifts, perhaps because they seemed so small in the eyes of others. "

In "Amadeus," the message was similar. Antonio Salieri wanted fame as a musician, but knew he could never be as good as Wolfgang Amadeus. Salieri would only serve God if he could be famous, but he was mediocre and he knew it.

"We can’t really serve, then, if we are selfish. Our own selfish needs keep getting in the way and we are likely to give the wrong gifts like Salieri tried to do. Or we may give our gifts in a way that blesses us more than the recipient," Wilkins said.

He related an experience from his own life that we had with his grandfather, former LDS Church President Harold B. Lee. At the time, young Wilkins wanted to know answers concerning his future, but his grandfather taught him a lesson instead. Lee recounted to Wilkins his own personal history of how he arrived at where he was in life and then related this counsel, "Don’t live too far into the future, live for today. You don’t have tomorrow; all you have is today. You don’t have yesterday. All you can do is repent or feel good, depending on your yesterdays. So make your yesterdays days to feel good about living fully for today."

Wilkins concluded with a call to action. "May you become what you must become. May you develop your God-given gifts. May you work hard and persist even when it seems your gifts are only a widow’s mite. May you seek the inspiration of God, to know when to take the twists and turns that he would have you take, as you confront life’s challenges."

Next Tuesday’s speaker will be Elder James Paramore, an emeritus member of the LDS Church’s First Quorum of the Seventy. He will speak at 2 p.m. in the Hart Auditorium. Devotionals are broadcast live and repeated at 9 p.m. on KRIC-FM.
