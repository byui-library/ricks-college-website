John 1: 14:  Learning Grace

And Christ became real to me,
no longer just a Sunday School concept;
no longer someone spoken of to shame me
into fits of obedience carried out
because I couldn't bring myself to offend
someone
whom my parents called
Jesus.

He became real to me,
a brother,
someone who lived for me,
longed for my success,
cried with me when my heart was broken
by misunderstanding and rumor
lashed upon me by unkind friends.
He took time for me,
hanging on each word of my awkward,
stammering prayer,
sympathetic of my silent plea
to let some things pass,
to change me even when I lived to resist
obedience.

Then, his grace was something of a mystery,
something promised, intangible, barely
hopeful.

Now he lives as graceful as anyone I've
known,
supporting my hand in his,
arm around me,
slightly touching the small of my back,
moving with me,
gently directing my steps with slight
nudging and delicate pressure
to turn first this way then that,
around as I follow his lead, learning
the dance only he can teach.

Glorious?
The word is too strong, too grand,
too exultant to name the one I am coming to
know.

One day, no doubt, glorious
will fit him until it grows too weak, too
mortal
to bespeak the splendor of one supremely
adored
for nothing less than atonement and
redemption.

But now,
sensitive, understanding,
these words do nicely
to name a brother,
who encourages my best,
patient with me
as I learn to live beyond weakness:
a friend true to me
as only the Son of God could be.

David L. Ward
September 4, 1996
