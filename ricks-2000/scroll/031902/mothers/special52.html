Finding a hair color to dye for

by Sarah Lunt
LUN99013@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Shaunna Jensen, a freshman from Allen, Texas, bears a head of hair that has recently been dyed. Scott Cameron / Scroll
Many women decide to dye their hair when the grays start to appear. Others will dye just for a little change.

Whatever their intent, women should take caution when experimenting with their precious tresses.

It is suggested that the most success comes when a woman chooses a dye as close to her natural hair color as possible, especially when covering grays, according to www.garniercolouradvice.co.uk.

If one desires a change, she should know what kind of shade she is looking for. If she wants to have less gold or red tones, she should choose a shade with the word “ash” included.

Ash-browns tend to be a cooler color (without so much red and gold), while golden-brown will have more of the red and gold tones.

The best way to determine which kind to use is to stand under bright lights or in the sun and figure out if your hair naturally has red highlights, golden highlights or no highlights at all, according to www.newbasics.com.

There are different levels of permanence in hair colors as well. It is recommended that women who have never before dyed their hair use a more temporary dye. These range from a one-wash formula to the semi-permanent, which washes out after six to 12 washes. For those more daring newcomers, long-lasting semi-permanent dyes last for 24 washes.

Permanent dye should only be used after a woman has found a color she likes and is comfortable with dying her hair. Usually the more permanent the color is, the better the results. However, the color will not stay the same as when first applied. Most of the time the color lightens and fades with time.

Once a woman decides on a hair color, she should definitely read the directions on the box. Many may feel that they have dyed their hair enough to know how the process works, but not every brand is the same.

It is also recommended that anytime a hair dye is used, an allergy test should be performed so the woman knows if her scalp is allergic to the chemicals, Paula Hamberlin, an instructor at Vogue Beauty Salon and College in Idaho Falls, said.

The test can be performed by applying a very small amount of the dye to a portion of her skin and not washing it for two days. If there is no irritation after those two days, then the woman is probably not allergic to the dye.

A good place to perform the sensitivity test is behind the ear. It is also a good idea to apply the colorant two or three times.

Another step to take before actually dying the hair is to not wash one’s hair one day or more before coloring it.

This is a good idea because hair products can inhibit the performance of the dye. The natural oils from the scalp can also keep the dye from dying the scalp as well as the hair, according to www.newbasics.com.
