Family 210 is a course which will explore major issues of children's development, from pre-birth through adolescence. Our discovery tools will be theory, research, revelation, religion, observation and discussion.
The following are specific course objectives:
1.To gain enough understanding of the normal growth and development processes from conception to adolescence so one can identify obvious deviations from the norm and can state clearly what are reasonable expectations at various stages of development.
2.To identify and describe the developmental needs and tasks of children and the physical, social, intellectual, and emotional environments that are most conducive to optimum development at each stage.
3.To show awareness of the importance of early human experience, particularly the quality of interpersonal relationships in the development of concepts of self and others: to identify some of the variables of the environment and interpersonal associations that have significant impact: to describe elements which show continuity from one stage of development to another.
4.To demonstrate a rudimentary awareness of the contributions of the various frameworks or theories for examing child development such as the psychoanalytic, the biological-maturational, the environmental- learning, the constructivist and the cultural context frameworks.
5.To relate more positively to others, both children and adults, as a result of increased understanding of a child's perspective and of one's own feelings and behavior.
6.To have experience in observing, recording, and interpreting childrens and/or caretaker behavior.
7.To increase appreciation for the roles of the family and the Gospel of Jesus Christ to guide the eternal development of the child.

TEXT: Exploring Child Development:Transactions & Transformations, (2000), by Fabes & Martin
