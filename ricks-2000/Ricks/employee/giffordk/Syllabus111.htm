COURSE OBJECTIVE

 

            Through a framework of facts, principles, and models, the student will be able to identify and analyze the basic economic problems which every society face.  The student will be able to explain how a mixed economy such as the U.S. solves these problems. 

            The course will introduce the student to the aggregate variables used in simple models of the U.S. economy.  Using these models, the student will be able to predict and explain the effects of changes on the U.S. as well as other economies. 

            In addition to aiding in passing this course, a mastery of the concepts and ideas will assist the student in making informed decisions regarding current economic issues.  Without a basic understanding of economics, issues such as social security, taxes, and foreign trade cannot be understood.

Midterms:            Two testing-center midterms will count for 100 points each.  The midterm exams will consist of multiple choice, problem solving and essay questions.  Only under extenuating circumstances will makeup midterms be given and only if arranged prior to the scheduled exam days.

 

Final:                An in-class final will count for 100 points.  This non-comprehensive exam will consist of multiple-choice questions. According to University policy, no early final exams may be given.  Any exceptions must be approved by Max Checketts, Assistant Academic Vice President.

 

Quizzes:            In-class quizzes will be given periodically throughout the semester. These quizzes are worth 5 points each and will be based on the online quizzes associated with each chapter. In order to do well on these quizzes, it will be necessary to read the chapter and complete the appropriate online quiz prior to coming to class.  Under no circumstances will makeup quizzes be given.

 

Homework:            Homework assignments will be given out at least one week prior to the due date.  Each assignment will be worth a maximum of 20 points and consist of either an in-class problem set, a take-home problem set, or a short research project.  All homework assignments are due by the assigned Friday at 5:00 p.m.  Please put your completed assignment in the appropriate box in Smith 127.  In the event that you neglect to turn in an assignment by the specified deadline, one mercy homework will be allowed. A mercy homework information sheet is attached to the syllabus for this purpose. Other late homework is worth a maximum of 10 points each and will be accepted only on the last class period of the semester. There are no exceptions to this policy, so please do not ask.
