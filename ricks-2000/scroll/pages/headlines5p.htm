HOT SPRINGS PROVIDE WHOLESOME SUMMER ACTIVITY

Hot springs for a cool summer

by Munro Murdock
scroll staff


Hot springs throughout the nation range from muddy and murky pits, to clean and chlorinated concrete pools. Unless in search of a natural environment, public hot springs offer an accessible alternative.

Lava Hot Springs, 25 miles south of Pocatello, channel more than three million gallons of hot water through their pools daily. This water, bubbling up from an ancient volcano, contains no chlorine or sulphur and is virtually odor-free. The Hot Springs offer four outdoor pools with temperatures ranging from 102 to 112 degrees.

"It was so dang hot, but not hot enough to kill you," Bill Swann, a sophomore from Preston, Idaho, said. "It has a gravel bottom, and all the hot water just comes up from underneath so there's no cold end in the pools."

Located about 105 miles south of Rexburg, Lava Hot Springs is 11 miles off of Interstate 15. "If students are on their way to Utah, they can just make a little stop, and hang out for awhile," Swann said.

Many students from Idaho State University, in Pocatello, visit the hot springs regularly, according to Mark Lowe, executive director of Lava Hot Springs. "If students haven't had the opportunity to come, they need to. It's a great place to come and have some fun, wholesome entertainment."

Lava Hot Springs also offers a separate Olympic swimming complex with two pools, seven diving boards, and a 33-foot diving tower. Additionally, two new water slides will be completed within the next two weeks and ready for use, according to Lowe. Additional charges apply to use the swimming complex. Call for details.

"We're reasonably priced and a good place for the family to get away," Lowe said. "It's a lot of fun in the summer time and a great place to meet new people."
