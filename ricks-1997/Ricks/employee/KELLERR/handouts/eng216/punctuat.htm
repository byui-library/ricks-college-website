Punctuation

"Punctuation, to most people, is a set of arbitrary and rather silly rules you find in printers' style books and in the back pages of school grammars. Few people realize that it is the most important single device for making things easier to read." --Rudolf Flesch

 

Comma ,
1. Coordinating conjunction (and, but, for, so, or, nor, yet)
2. Items in a series
3. Introductory elements
4. Parenthetical information

 

Semi-Colon ;
1. Weak period
2. Conjunctive adverb (however, moreover, for example, hence, consequently, therefore)
3. Items in a series containing commas

 

Colon :
1. Introduce list
2. Introduce definition
3. Introduce long quotation
4. Avoid using after "be" verb, prepositions, namely, such as

 

Parenthesis ( )
1. Separate clarifying information
2. Punctuation usually follows parenthesis mark unless information is a complete sentence separated from initial sentence.

 

Dash --
1. Serve as dramatic parenthesis
2. Indicate sudden break in thought.
3. Use sparingly

 

Brackets [ ]
1. Indicate editorial parenthetical information
2. Use only within quoted material

 

Italics/Underlining
1. Italicize titles of books, plays, pamphlets, long musical works, television and radio programs, long  poems, periodicals, published speeches, movies, works of art
2. Italicize names of ships, aircraft, spacecraft, and trains
3. Italicize foreign words and phrases
4. Italicize words, letters, numbers, and phrases named as words
5. Italicize to indicate emphasis

 

Apostrophe '
1. Indicates contraction (it's)
2. Indicates possession (its)
3. Positioning of apostrophe indicates number (boy's bike, boys' bike.)

 

Quotation Marks " "
1. Indicate direct quotation
2. Indicate titles of songs, short poems, articles, periodicals, short stories, essays, episodes of television and radio programs, chapters of books
3. Indicate words with unusual connotations
4. Periods and commas always inside quotation marks
5. Semi-colons and colons always outside quotation marks

Question marks and exclamation marks on inside of quotation only if part of the quotation.

 

Ellipsis Points . . .
1. Indicate omission of quoted material
2. Indicate of dramatic pause (use seldom)
3. Only three points (fourth is a period)
