Admissions Checklist

Each year, more than 8,000 students apply for admission to Ricks College. While Ricks has always had a tradition of being a friendly and caring school, a growing increase in the number of students wanting to attend has made it more competitive. While we desire to keep the admissions process simple and fair, it can still be complicated and frustrating. While most students who desire to attend Ricks are accepted, some will be turned down. Often, students not admitted could have been if they had planned better or paid more careful attention to such things as ACT (American College Test) dates or deadlines.

The following checklist is designed to help the prospective student better understand areas we consider and help improve his or her chances of being accepted:

CHURCH ACTIVITY -- As a church college, one of the factors we consider is an applicant's church activity, regardless of denomination. LDS applicants, who are endorsed by their bishop or branch president and are active in church, will be given the most consideration. Students who are not active or have unresolved worthiness issues will not be considered.

SEMINARY AND INSTITUTE ATTENDANCE -- One of the main areas we consider is an LDS applicant's seminary attendance. As a church-sponsored college, we support the seminary program and feel seminary graduates make great contributions to our school. Students who attend all four years of seminary and graduate will have the strongest chance for admissions. Likewise, students who have had access to seminary, but have not attended, will have a harder time being admitted. We also encourage students who attend other colleges before Ricks to be actively involved in an LDS institute.

HIGH SCHOOL AND COLLEGE GRADES (GPA) -- High school and college grades are also considered when a student applies to Ricks. A student's GPA is used to determine if he or she has the necessary preparation to succeed in college. We encourage students to take their academics seriously and work toward having above average grades. Applicants are required to send an official transcript of high school and college work to the Admissions Office. Since transcripts received after the deadline will not be considered, students should have their school send transcripts well before the deadline.

ACT (American College Test) -- The ACT is another criteria we use in determining a student's ability to succeed in college. The ACT is also used in advising and placement once a student is enrolled at Ricks. We encourage prospective students to take the ACT the spring of their junior year in high school. This will allow the student time to retake the test the fall of his or her senior year if desired. The admissions office will consider the highest composite score. Since our admissions deadline for fall semester is February 15, the December ACT is the last test an applicant can take and still have scores sent to Ricks in time for consideration. Ricks College will also consider an SAT (Scholastic Aptitude Test) score.

ESSAYS -- The essays are an important part in the evaluation of a student's application. The essays are designed to help the Admissions Committee determine a student's background and preparation to attend Ricks. Well written and typed essays are given the strongest consideration. When preparing essays, we encourage students to write a preliminary draft before submitting the completed and typed essay in the application.

DEADLINES -- The Admissions Office will accept an application up to one year prior to the term or semester the student is planning on attending. Unfortunately, many applicants procrastinate and delay sending materials until just before the deadline. Our policy is that all application materials must be received before the deadline to be considered. The deadlines for the summer terms and fall semester are February 15. The deadline for winter semester is October 1. We encourage high school students applying for the fall semester to apply during the fall of their senior year. In addition, all recommendation forms to be completed by church leaders or seminary teachers should be given to the leader well in advance of the deadline.
