Ricks College News Release
July 13, 2000

Ricks art department moving downtown

REXBURG, Idaho — The art department at Ricks College will be moving downtown this summer as the college vacates the Spori Building in anticipation for it being torn down later in the year.

Because there was no room on campus, the art department will be based at 16 E. Main St. in a building most recently occupied by Sandra and Co.

The college is leasing the building until a new structure is built where the Spori Building now stands.

Department chairman Kelly Burgener says art classes in drawing, painting and illustration will be taught on the main level. Five faculty members and the department secretary will be housed in the basement.

A gallery area also will be set up in the basement and the storefront windows will be used to display student work.

Design classes that require computers will be taught in a remodeled area of the Manwaring Center on campus.

Burgener says the department plans to move out of the Spori Building in early August and will be ready to teach classes when the Fall Semester starts Aug. 29.

Double E Construction of Ririe currently is building three classrooms and upgrading the restrooms inside the building.

The Spori Building had been a major concern to college leaders for several decades. Determining the appropriate course of action was particularly challenging because of the historic value of the building, and much time and effort was expended in trying to find ways to preserve and/or restore the facility. After extensive research and deliberation, it was determined that restoring the building was not feasible.

Ricks officials have selected the architectural firm of FFKR of Salt Lake City to design the new building because they believe it has the vision and capability to design a building that will reflect the heritage and legacy of the aging building. The design process slowed down last month while Ricks officials determine if there are additional needs that may be required when the college starts offering upper division classes.

Administrative Vice President James Smyth hopes design work can be completed in early 2001 with construction of a new building starting in the spring. It is estimated that the new building will take from 20 to 24 months to complete.

The Spori Building has housed the Communication and Art departments; along with KRIC and KWBH radio stations, Graphic Services; and the campus newspaper, the Scroll. Communication classes will be moved to other locations on campus, the Scroll has been moved to the Manwaring Center and a new building currently is being constructed for the radio stations and Graphics Services.
