Flatter yourself
Pick the right wedding gown

by Sarah Lunt
LUN99013@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Timothy Robison / Scroll
A big part of the happiness for the bride on her wedding day is attributed to how well her dress fits her and if she feels beautiful in it.

Obviously, not every dress will look fantastic on every woman or every kind of figure.

There are certain guidelines, according to weddingchannel.com, that can be followed to find the best dress for each woman’s figure.

For women with the “inverted triangle” figure, meaning that their shoulders and upper-bodies are wider than their hips, the goal is to create the illusion of a narrower upper body.

Tops of dresses that do not add volume to the upper body and that possibly break the horizontal line of wide shoulders include fitted sleeves, deep necklines, wide collars, princess lines and vertical stripes. These will de-emphasize the broadness of the upper body.

Styles to avoid for the inverted triangle figure are tops with shoulder pads, dropped shoulder seams, boat necks, narrow collars and horizontal stripes.

Another figure type, the rectangle, includes those with balanced hip and shoulder width and without a defined waist. The goal here is to avoid drawing attention to the undefined waist.

Women with this figure should go for straight tops that fall over the hips, princess lines, vertical stripes and empire waists. They should avoid gathered waistlines and very fitted dresses that draw attention to the waist.

A third type, the triangle, has hips that are wider than the shoulders and upper body. Here, the woman should try to create the impression of wider shoulders and reduce the appearance of volume at the hips.

Women in this category should search for dresses that have boat necks, mock necks, shoulder pads, wide shoulder lines and narrow collars. Lightweight fabrics for the dress are recommended.

These women should avoid vertical necklines, wide collars, bottoms with pleated waistlines, large prints and stiff or heavy fabrics.

A fourth type of figure is the hourglass figure. This type has balanced hips and shoulders and a defined waist. The objective here is to show off the figure.

Most dresses will look good on the hourglass figure, but these women should really go for princess-cut, fitted, semi-fitted and tapered tops.

The final figure type is the circle figure. These women have a waist that is wider than the bust and hips. These women want to avoid drawing attention to the waistline.

Circle figures should seek for straight tops that fall over the hips, princess lines, vertical stripes and empire waists. They should not buy gathered waistlines or fitted clothing.
