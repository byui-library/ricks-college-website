A Once in a Ten-lifetime Experience
Garth Hall's Story
by Jennifer Hunter
 
It was about 9:00 in the morning when Garth Hall unloaded his trusty mountain horse, Nebo, from the trailer and prepared for a Saturday morning ride. Garth had always enjoyed this solitary time; it was a nice retreat for him and a chance to escape from the hectic world for at least a few hours. On this particular day Garth had decided to ride down the Logan Canyon trail on to Tony Grove Lake and Green Canyon.

This would work out to be about a four to five hour ride. Garth saddled-up, kissed his wife good-bye, and promised to meet her at the bottom. Then he was off, packing only his bundled up yellow rain jacket along with him. He mounted and rode away, standing out against the white background as he went in his faded blue Levi’s, Levi jacket, and brown cowboy boots and hat. Alone and unarmed, Garth made his way along the snowy trail.

The sky was a blanket of dense clouds so thick and low that you could literally see them sitting right on top of the mountain peaks. The earth was sparkling white with six inches of soft, new snow. Garth rode along the isolated trail, drinking in the solitude and beauty of the world around him. Everything was so quiet, so serene. Not even the birds dared to disturb the silence. It was almost as if the world were sleeping and Garth was softly tiptoeing by so as not to wake her.

For nearly an hour Garth and Nebo rode along enjoying the tranquil morning. Soon they came to a huge rock in the shape of a chimney. Thinking nothing of it Garth proceeded to pass. Just around the rock the trail came into a large, open basin area on the spine of the mountain. Here the clouds seemed to lift and Garth had a clear view of what lay ahead of him. Suddenly his eyes fell upon a gray animal there on the trail in front of him.

The bobcat had already seen Garth. It just sat there with its black eyes riveted on him. Garth was surprised by the size of the bobcat, he hadn’t expected one to seem this big. It sat about two feet above the ground with it’s big, pointed ears making it seem even taller. The silver-gray fur was flecked throughout with black, giving the animal a very dark appearance.

Garth was astonished; he was sitting just twenty yards away from a bobcat! For a few moments the two just stared at each other, neither one fearing, but both curious and surprised. Seconds went by, then, just as suddenly as the bobcat had appeared, it ran off. Before the animal was completely out of sight, it stopped, turned around, took one last look at its intruder, and then disappeared from view.

Before Garth even had a chance to reflect on what had just happened, he noticed something moving in the snow. A more intent look revealed a white face, and a sleek brown body. This animal was much bigger than the first. Then, like a lightning bolt, Garth was struck with the realization that he was looking at a cougar. At that same moment Nebo also sensed the danger. The horse danced around nervously, but was kept under control by Garth’s soft commands.

The cougar sensed that he was not alone and a split second later was running swiftly across the wide-open basin away from Garth. The cougar’s body stretched out as he darted across the snow. He ran so gracefully, so effortlessly, it was almost as if he were actually on dry ground. Garth could have sworn that the cougar was twelve feet long and going seventy miles per hour. The cougar ran for about a mile and a half at an incredible speed before it reached the safety of the trees.

Garth was in awe; he tried to follow the cougar, but he lost track of it once it was out of the basin. At last he gave up his search and continued on the trail. The entire remainder of the ride Garth’s thoughts kept going back to his encounters with the cats. How did that happen? He was so lucky. People didn’t even think there were cougars in these mountains anymore. Boy, were they wrong. He had seen one, not only a cougar, but a bobcat too, almost simultaneously.

The cats must not have heard him coming. He remembered the big chimney rock. That must have blocked out the sound he made as he came down to the basin. That’s why the cats were there; they thought they were all alone.

The animals were so beautiful, what an experience! He had seen some impressive things on his many rides, but nothing quite this extraordinary. He was bursting with excitement and a desire to share his story. He hadn’t felt threatened at all, the animals had been afraid of him. Why were others so scared of cougars?

They seemed more timid of humans than humans are of them.  What a wonderful experience that was!  He felt his appreciation for nature triple just from that one experience. The scenario kept replaying itself over and over again in his mind.   Garth became anxious to reach the bottom of the mountain.  He couldn’t wait to tell his wife about his adventure.
