Rexburg, national post offices prepare for possible anthrax

by Rebecca Long
lon01004@byui.edu
News Assistant Editor

Local citizens of Rexburg continue to use the local post office. The post office is prepared with equipment in case anthrax were to become a local problem.
The U.S. Postal Service is going to great lengths to assure that employees and customers are safe and to keep the mail securely under control, according to a guide sheet distributed by the postal service.

The postal service has advised employees to maintain safe working methods; employees can request and will be given dust masks and gloves to work with.

Federal authorities in impacted areas are working diligently with the postal service to educate employees and citizens, thus mobilizing their common sense. The postal service has also created a Mail Security Task Force to assist in keeping the American public safe from contaminated mail.

The fact that first-class mail is sealed against inspection is a privilege that Americans have enjoyed for 226 years. Until the recent anthrax outbreak, the Postal Service had never experienced the strain being sent through the U.S. Mail, according to reports from the postal service.

The postal service has delivered over 20 billion pieces of mail across the country since Sept. 11. Nearly 700 million pieces of mail are delivered to 136 million businesses and residential addresses daily — more than 210 billion pieces annually, Massachusetts postal official Bob Cannon said. This equals almost half the world’s mail volume.

Workers have been advised to look for abnormal boxes, which does not mean a whole lot, Leon Moser of the United Parcel Service said.

Employees are looking for “stuff coming out of [boxes] that may look like anthrax,” Moser said. Other than that, there aren’t many other precautions that can be taken, he said.

The postal service indicates that citizens should be suspicious of any mail that is unexpected, from unknown sources or bears no return/legitimate address. Other reasons for concern include packages of unusual size, packages that are marked with restrictive endorsements such as “confidential” or “personal,” or packages that have an outdated address.

Suspicious mail should not be handled. People who encounter such mail should notify local authorities, isolate the piece of mail by putting it in a sealable bag and wash their hands with soap and water. However, it is important that people do not become paranoid, Post Master Bill Scouten of the Rexburg Post Office said.

“Awareness is the big issue but not to go into panic state. [Anthrax] is designed as terrorist activity to breed fear and panic into people. It is very effective as a psychological weapon,” he said.

Scouten believes that the chances of anthrax being found in Idaho are slim. “Nothing [has been found] in the West entirely, not to say it won’t [be found],” he said.
