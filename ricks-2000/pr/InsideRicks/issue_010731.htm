DEDICATION CEREMONIES SCHEDULED FOR RADIO AND GRAPHIC SERVICES BUILDING

Dedication ceremonies for the Radio and Graphic Services Building will be held Thursday, August 2, at 11 a.m. Elder David A. Bednar will preside. All interested are invited to attend.

The outdoor ceremony will be held on the south side of the RGS Building which is located east of the Kimball parking lot adjacent to the tennis courts.

The 4,225 square foot, one-story building houses the Graphic Services Department, KRIC-FM and KWBH-FM. The building was designed by architect Rulon Nielsen and constructed by Vern Clark and Sons Construction of Idaho Falls.

KRIC is part of the public radio network and broadcasts to southeastern Idaho, western Wyoming, and southwestern Montana. KWBH is a low-wattage student laboratory radio station. The Graphic Services Department is the college's design studio for publications, brochures, and logos.

KRIC-FM BECOMES KBYI-FM

KRIC-FM will be getting a new name consistent with changes at Ricks College, which is becoming a four-year university known as Brigham Young University-Idaho. The station's call sign will change to KBYI-FM on August 10, the same day Ricks College officially changes its name.

The letters, representing BYU-Idaho, are reflective of the changes taking place at Ricks College, which owns and operates KRIC-FM.

"We are eager to be part of the transition as Ricks College becomes BYU-Idaho," says general manager James Clark. "And we're pleased to have a call sign representing the institution which sponsors us."

He emphasizes that no format changes will accompany the name change. "We are the same station that our audience is accustomed to," Clark says. "We will continue providing classical music, news, and information programming."

KRIC-FM was established as a 10-watt station in 1973. It remained at that power for the next 11 years and was used as a lab by broadcast majors in the Communications Department at Ricks College. The station's coverage was limited to a small section of Rexburg.

On May 1, 1984, KRIC went on the air at a power of 75,000 watts and frequency of 100.5 FM.

Today, the station's 100,000-watt transmitter is located on the South Menan Butte southwest of Rexburg. A translator network carries the signal to other areas including Pocatello, Burley, and Twin Falls Idaho; Jackson, Afton, and Freedom, Wyoming; and Dillon and West Yellowstone, Montana.

INSIDEOUT BRINGS A CAPELLA TO RICKS

A six-man, all-vocal group with roots in the upper valley will perform on Saturday, August 11. InsideOut will perform at 7:30 p.m. in the Kirkham Auditorium as part of the Summer in the Snow Entertainment Series.

Tickets are $3 for Ricks student and $5 for the general public at the door or from the Ticket Office ( 356-2230 or over the Internet at www.ricks.edu/tickets).

Local members are Kimball Brown, Ben Haacke and Layne Haacke, who grew up in Sugar City, and Jason Smith from Idaho Falls. The group was formed in the winter of 1997. InsideOut entertains audiences of all ages. Their musical ability and vocal percussion create an invigorating performance. They have produced three CDs including "Reverence," runner-up in the best religious album in the 2001 Contemporary A cappella Recording Awards competition.

The group performs at universities and community events. They have appeared on local network television channels in Arizona, Utah and Idaho, as well as a national cable television appearance.

Members of InsideOut are also currently pursuing college degrees at BYU, work professionally, and are actively involved in student leadership and service.
