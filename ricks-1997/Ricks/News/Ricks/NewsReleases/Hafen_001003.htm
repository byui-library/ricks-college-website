Ricks College News Release
October 3, 2000

Former Ricks 'first lady' tells students to avoid extreme

REXBURG, Idaho — Avoiding extremes was the topic of Tuesday’s devotional address by Marie Hafen, former "first lady" of Ricks College and wife Elder Bruce C. Hafen, a member of the LDS Church’s First Quorum of the Seventy and former president of Ricks College.

She said, "In the safety of these moments here today, I would like, with candid words of warning and hopeful words of cheer, to offer advice on how you might grow instead of wither in the midst of your extremes . . . and be encouraging.

"The crux of my prevention message to you against dangerous extremes can be summed in two words: ‘get real!’ " Hafen said.

She used the example of dating to illustrate some of her points.

Answering the question of how society succumbs to the cheap extremes, Hafen said, "There are many who are not as careful as (they) should be about when, how, and to whom they show affection . . . in short, save you kisses for when you really mean them. Real love depends on your integrity being intact."

She encouraged listeners not to forget how much they are worth. "It’s not unusual to wonder at times if other people love you or even if the Lord loves you," Hafen said. "Those times of doubt will pass much faster if you don’t fall for the seeming external symbols like popularity, money or fashion to determine your worth."

Hafen counseled that deep, meaningful relationships don’t come quickly, but evolve over time. She said that she and her husband had known each other at least three months before romance dawned on them. After taking a class together, she remembered feeling a spark ignite while they danced one night to the music of Nat King Cole.

"I remember feeling a little zinging flare like someone had struck a match," she said. "We had discovered in our college landscape the natural growth of being in friendship first, and in romantic love second. That progression is worth seeking for and working for."

She said, "Walk the higher road of self-restraint. Develop the power to deny yourself and learn when to say no to your passions, in all their varieties, and when to say yes. A person with that kind of self-restraint will discover the truest kind of love."

Next Tuesday’s speaker will be Stanley A. Peterson, an LDS Church Educational System administrator. He will speak at 2 p.m. in the Hart Auditorium. Devotionals are broadcast live and repeated at 9 p.m. on KRIC-FM.
