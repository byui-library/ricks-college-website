Ricks planetarium up and running after a year of renovation

By Trent Toone
Ricks College News Bureau

REXBURG, Idaho — The Ricks College planetarium has never looked so good.

After being renovated for much of last year, the planetarium is up and running, says Ricks College physics instructor Brian Tonks.

Located in the basement of the Romney Building, the planetarium is used to teach students of all ages about astronomy through projecting images onto a domed ceiling.

"The shows are more smooth and have more special effects," Tonks says of the renovated planetarium. "Everything has been computerized. We’ve replaced slide projectors and added more options. Things are less complicated and easy to operate."

Tonks says the changes keep the planetarium right on track with the BYU-Idaho transition. "By the time we’re a four-year institution, we want to be able to have students running the shows and having at least two shows a week," he says. "At first when I started, it took me more than 30 hours to learn how to run everything and that’s too much time."

The planetarium’s first showing took place in 1963 under the direction of Gordon Dixon, who was a longtime faculty teacher. "Things were quite simple then," Tonks says.

The college estimates more than 87,000 people attended shows during the first decade. He estimates more than 10,000 attend every year now.

In the years that followed, the planetarium became more sophisticated, adding more than 75 special effects projectors, 70 plus panoramic projectors, two expensive zoom projectors and a video project. The recent renovations added to this.

Planetarium public showings take place weekly at 7 p.m. on Thursdays or also by special appointment. Cost for admission is $1.25 per adult and $1 for students and children. For groups of more than 25, a $25 group fee is charged.

During February, "The Worlds Unknown" will feature a visit to planets in the solar system. March will feature a new show entitled "Is There Life on Mars?" if it’s ready. If it’s not finished, the planetarium will show "I Will Show Thee the Workmanship of Mine Hands (Moses 1)." April will feature "Adventures Along the Spectrum."
