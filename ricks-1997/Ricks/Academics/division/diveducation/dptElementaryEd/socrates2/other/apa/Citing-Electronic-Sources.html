    Citing Common Print Sources

    APA Crib Sheet

    The information in this handout, taken from the _MLA Handbook for Writers of Research Papers_ (4th Edition), and the _Allyn And Bacon Guide to Using and Documenting Electronic Sources_, cover many of the cases in which you'll need to give credit to sources that are found on-line. If you need information about other cases, you can consult the Handbook or Guide in the Writing Lab (226 Heavilon Hall).

    Note:
    Because electronic sources (especially those on the Internet) change rapidly, citations for these sources are not completely standardized. For example, Janice Walker has a page that covers MLA citations for specific types of electronic publishing and she includes citations which differ somewhat from the examples below. Another such site is the International Federation of Library Associations' page. Because electronic sources vary, you may also have to adapt the citation structure to what you need in order to accurately represent the material.

    MLA Style for Unchangeable Sources Available in Print:

    For books, pamphlets, or text accessed electronically-- Provide author, title (underlined), normal publication information, medium, name of archive of text, name of computer network, date of access, and supplementary information. 

    Melville, Herman. _Moby Dick, or The White Whale_. Ed.
  Howard Vincent. New York: Viking Press. 1957. Online. U. of Virginia
  Lib. Internet. 10 Mar. 1995. Available FTP: etext.virginia.edu.

    For journals or periodicals accessed electronically-- Provide the author, title (quotations), journal title (underlined) issue, date, page citations, location, publisher (if available), name of database, medium, name of vendor, date of electronic issue, any other supplementary information. 

    Jones, Jerry. "Congressional Investigations of the
  Waco Incident," _The New York Times_, May 8, 1995: 2, 10.
  New York: New York Times. _America OnLine_. Online. May 8, 1995.

    MLA Style for Unchangeable Sources Not Available in Print:

    A one-time electronic issue or release on disk--

    Provide author, title (underlined) edition/release number, medium, location or city of publication, name of vendor, date of issue

    _Microsoft Word_. Version 6.0. Diskette. Everett WA: Microsoft Corp. 1994.

    A journal or periodical--

    Provide author, title in quotation marks, location, publisher, name of database for electronic text, medium, name of vendor, date of electronic issue, and supplementary information 

    Taubes, Gary. "Peer Review in Cyberspace."
  _Physics Journal_ 14 (1994). Online. Internet: WWW. http://xxx.lanl.gov/

    MLA Citations from Changeable On-Line Electronic Sources:

    An information service databank--

    Provide authorship and title (in quotes), date of material, name of database for electronic text, medium, name of computer service, date of electronic issue, any supplementary information 

    Smithsonian Institute. "The Sound of Whales."
  _The Smithsonian Online_. Online. America OnLine. Released 10-11-94.
  AOL/Education/Smithsonian.

    Material from an electronic journal, newsletter, or conference--

    Provide author, title (in quotes), year or date of electronic issue, number of pages or paragraphs, the medium, computer network, date of access, and any supplementary information 

    Shimabukuru, Jim, ed. "Internet in Ten Years--Essays."
  _Electronic Journal on Virtual Culture_ 3.1 (1995): 62 par. Online.
  BITNET. 5 Feb 1995.

    A person-to-person or bulletin online message--

    Memos and e-mails should be treated as print sources. Include author, descriptive lable for the document (or title) in quotations and give a notation that the document is an e-mail letter, and to whom addressed, date of electronice posting 

    Rushdie, Salman. "My concern about the Fatwa."
  e-mail to author. 1 May 1995.

    A bulletin board posting--

    Provide the author, descriptive label or title in quotation marks, date of electronic transmission, medium, name of computer network, any information about the bulletin board, address or path for electronic access, date of access 

    MacDonald, James C. "Suggestions for promoting collaborative
  writing in college composition." 10 Nov. 1994. Online: America
  OnLine. NCTE Forum/current topics/bulletin posting. 12 Mar. 1995.

    APA Citations for Unchangeable Electronic Sources:

    For books, pamphlets, or text accessed electronically, or an abstract on cd-rom or a computer program--

    Provide same author, date, and title elements required for print books or other non-periodicals, as well as the medium, location, name of publisher/producer/distributor of database program in brackets 

    NCTE (1987) _On Writing Centers_[tape database]. Urbana:
  ERIC Clearinghouse for Resolutions on the Teaching of Composition,
  II. SilverPlatter. [Producer].

    For a computer program--

    Add any additional informatin needed for retrieval or identification. 

    Microsoft Corporation (1994). Microsoft Word. Version
  6.0. [Computer software] Everett WA: Microsoft.

     

    For an on-line journal available for general access--

    Provide same author, date, and title elements required for print, date of search or issue, insert statement about availablility or retrieval location 

    Kettel, Raymond P. (1994). An Interview with Jerry Spinelli
  Thoughts on Teaching Writing in the Classroom. _English Journal_
  83 (1994): 61-64. Urbana: ERIC Clearinghouse on Elementary and
  Early Childhood Education. 1966-1995/Feb. _ERIC_ ipps.lsa.uminn.edu

    APA Citations from Changeable On-Line Electronic Sources:

    For all online material--

    Provide author, date, title, date of issue or search, statement about availability and retrieval location (in place of publisher and location), method for finding material, path of retrieval (for network material) 

    Shimabukuru, Jim, ed. (5 Feb. 1995) "Internet in
  Ten Years--Essays." _Electronic Journal on Virtual Culture_
  [On-line] _3.1_ (1995): 62 par. Available FTP:138.122.118.1
  
    Blythe, Stuart. "Why OWLs? Value, Risk, and Evolution"
  _Kairos_ [On-line] _1.1_ (1996) Available URL: http://english.ttu.edu/kairos/1.1/tocnf.html

    For a memo, e-mail, bulletin board, or user network posting online--

    Letters, memos, and temporary postings online are treated as for print sources in APA style. All unpublished letters, notes, bulletin postings, or unrecorded private conversations, in print or electronics, are not listed in APA's formal source citations in the "References" section, since they are not easily retrieved. They are acknowledged in a parenthetical in-text citation.

    --The novelist has repeated this idea recently (Salman Rushdie, e-mail to the author, 1 May, 1995).
