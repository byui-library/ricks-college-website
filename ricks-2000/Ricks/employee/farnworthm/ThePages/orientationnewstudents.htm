An Orientation For New and Continuing Students.

(The most profound lessons you will ever learn in life-- will be about yourselves.)

The following is the first lesson you will need to preview for class. It will orient you to my style and content.  Please come to class ready to discuss the lesson along with a well thought out question.

Lesson:   A Teaching Paradigm: Three Degrees,  Orders and  Realities...

This page is for the student who has never had me for a class before.  I will try and give you an idea of what I'm about and  what you can expect in my class.  I suppose it will also act as a filtering process for some who may decide another kind of teacher would be better suited for their learning styles.

I desire to create a class room where it is ok to feel and think and to ask questions.  I want it to be a class room where you can discover things about yourself-- or at least be aware of them if they are too difficult or frightening to process at the present.  I would like to produce a class room of peers who engaged the materials in a serious and mature way in the hopes of making a real change in their lives.  And, who would use me as a coach of sorts, to help them through some of the labyrinth-- we call life.

I use lecture and power point lessons.  As a student you will preview the lessons before coming to class and we will go through and discuss the concepts in class.  Questions and discussion are always invited and makes for a much more interesting experience for the both of us.  Audio and visual will dominate the learning scene with  little kinetic-hands on experience.  I am always inviting students to think, question and ponder their inner realities and assume responsibility for their education!  I pattern my class after life and play by the same rules as the student does when it comes to the class contract.  I focus upon deconstructing the traditions and philosophies popular in the culture that I see as contradicting the Gospel of Christ.  Since all of my classes deal with the nature of family and relationships- there is a lot of deconstructing to do.  Some students are threatened by new paradigms that challenge their own, but as Parker Palmer once said, "First we must see that not knowing is simply the first step toward truth." 

Now I come to my 'pet peeve' of teaching: a student who is not in a class to learn but to simply judge the teacher and materials.  Self righteous prigs is what Hugh Nibley calls them.  A prig is "an over precise, smug, narrow minded person".  Please spare yourself (and me) a semester of irritation.  Teaching is hard enough without having to deal with students who are defended and closed off to learning.  Learning requires a humble heart and if you think you already know - please do not take my class.

                          Please read the sign on the door.

The sign on the door...

(by Emily Young a past student)

...by the way, be prepared to enter an environment where reality is acknowledged and respected. where energy is high and paradigm shifts are encouraged.  Things won't always make sense, because within the walls of this room we leave the culture behind and deal with things sacred, secret, spiritual, sensual, superb, sorrowful and supremely important. Pain is almost inevitable, but that is just because tearing down the walls of a meticulously built house of cards is hurtful to our ego.  Work is inevitable because with all the cards left flat on the table in a chaotic pile, we are left with the passion to rebuild our house (hopefully with some stronger and more lasting materials).  Thankfully, supreme joy is also inevitable.  Just dwelling in a house with a firm foundation, sturdy walls and lots of windows feels good and right and contented.

The metaphor of house building and remodeling will serve us well.  If you have your house just the way you want it and would be imposed upon to deconstruct and tear down so that a rebuilding could occur-- then maybe this is not the class for you.
