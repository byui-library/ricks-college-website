    November 30, 2001
    Ricks 49    Lackawanna College 21  (Real Dairy Bowl, Pocatello, ID)
    The 'Last of the Vikings' went out in style Saturday night with a 49-21 win over Lackawanna College.  The Falcons got off to a bad start, fumbling on their first offensive play.  Ricks recovered and two plays later, Robbie Haas hit Damon Mortensen from 22 yards out to start the scoring.  As the first quarter closed, Ricks scored again, Haas connecting with Jon Clark from 9 yards out and it was 14-0 at the end of one.  The Vikings exploded for 28 points in the second quarter.  Haas threw TD passes of 9 yards to Chad Harrigfeld, 17 yards to Jon Clark, and 16 yards to Jared Doman.  Dallas Stevens connected with Damon Mortensen on a 48 yard touchdown.  Ricks led 42-0 at the half with just under 400 yards of total offense.  The Falcons scored twice in the third quarter.  A Robbie Haas pass was deflected and picked off by Joe Porter who returned it 36 yards for a touchdown.  Backup quarterback Brandon Hoover connected with tight end David Kenner for 71 yards and it was 42-14 at the end of three quarters.  Hoover connected with Kenner again in the fourth period before the Vikings closed out the scoring as Mike Udy punched the ball in from the one yard line with 12 minutes left in the game.
    Game MVP's were Robbie Haas for Ricks and DeAndre Mickelberry for Lackawanna.  A crowd of 11,000 witnessed the final Real Dairy Bowl, and the final game ever for Ricks College as the school becomes BYU-Idaho and drops intercollegiate sports at the end of this school year.

    November 3, 2001
    Ricks 28    Dixie State 35 (OT in St. George, UT)
    In the final meeting between arch rivals, Dixie State prevailed over previously unbeaten Ricks College 35-28 in overtime. After a scoreless first quarter, Ricks took control in the second on touchdowns by Nick Hyde and Jon Clark. Dixie countered with a second quarter score and it was 14-7 at the half. As the third quarter opened, Dixie’s Singa Iloa picked off a tipped Viking pass. The interception turned into a Waylon Bond touchdown tying the score at 14-14. Three minutes later, the Vikings tried a trick play with receiver Brantley Oakey attempting a pass to QB Robbie Haas. Dixie’s Jason Lee picked off the throw and returned in 70 yards for a touchdown and a 21-14 Dixie lead. As the third quarter ended, Dixie’s Aaron Sain scored from the one and it was 28-14 Dixie. With 13:27 left, Ricks began a drive at their own 5. Six minutes later, Ricks QB Robbie Haas hit Oakey on a screen pass for a 26 yard touchdown cutting the Rebels lead to 28-21. On Dixie’s next offensive play, QB Waylon Bond fumbled the ball at the Dixie 20. Ricks recovered and three plays later, Haas hit Nate Hahm from 29 yards out and the game was tied, and stayed that way through regulation. In OT, Dixie was on offense first.  Ricks  was called for pass interference at the two. Dixie scored and took the lead. Ricks took it’s turn in overtime and promptly dropped a TD pass. After picking up a first down, QB Robbie Haas was sacked and fumbled the ball. Dixie recovered, ending the game and the Vikings undefeated season.

    October 27, 2001
    Ricks 37    Arizona Western 6 (in Rexburg)
    With  6,500 fans attending the final home football game ever at Ricks College, it was the Viking defense that keyed the win, and sent hundreds of students and fans onto the field to tear down the goal posts as the final gun sounded.  While the Viking offense sputtered throughout the first half, the Ricks defense fought off the Matadors offense, giving up field goals in the first and second quarter.  Ricks trailed at the half 6-0.  The defense quickly stirred the pot as the second quarter opened, forcing Arizona Western quarterback Foster Brodus to throw an incomplete pass from the endzone.  Intentional grounding was called and Ricks had its first points on a safety.  The Matadors ensuing free kick was returned by Brantley Oakey from the 20 to the Vikings 37.  Four plays later, Robbie Haas hit Jared Doman on a 21 yard TD pass and Ricks led 9-6.  The Viking defense asserted itself two minutes later when Mike Overly intercepted a Foster Brodus pass and returned it 45 yards for a touchdown and Ricks led 16-6.  After forcing another Matador punt, Haas hit Jon Clark on a 38 yards pass for six more and it was 23-6.  On AWCC's next offensive play, Viking DT Carl Tuitavuki recovered a fumble at Western's 31 yard line.  Three plays later, Haas and Doman connected again, this time from 11 yards out, and it was 30-6 at the end of the third quarter.  Doman and Haas connected one more time, from 62 yards away in the fourth quarter.  The score followed another Western fumble.  Western's offense was shut out in the second half.  Haas completed 16 of 26 passes for 256 yards and four touchdowns.  Tuitavuki totaled 12 tackles in the game.  The Viking defense forced four turnovers.  The win clinches at least a tie for the WSFL title.  The Vikings close out the season next Saturday night at Dixie.

    October 20, 2001
    Ricks 59   NM Military 35 (in Roswell)
    The Vikings got off to a strong start with 24 points in the first quarter, and with 10 minutes to go in the first half, Ricks led 31-7. That’s when NMMI found it’s running game, scoring four times in less than seven minutes, and taking a 35-31 lead into the locker room. Jamil Porter scored three times in the spurt, once on a 90 yard run. A Viking fumble and interception added fuel to the second quarter onslaught.
    Ricks came out of the locker room and immediately took over the game. The defense solved the Broncos option running attack, shutting them out of the endzone the entire second half. On offense, Ricks got rolling on their first possession, covering 54 yards in three plays, Robbie Haas hitting Jared Doman from 39 yards out and Ricks had the lead back, 38-35. Brock Williamsons’ fumble recovery ended the next Broncos possession at the NMMI 20 yard line. Haas hit Nate Hahm from 3 yards out and Ricks led 45-35. In the fourth quarter, the Vikings offense held the ball twice, and scored twice. The two drives involved a total of 21 plays, 20 of them on the ground, and consumed 10 minutes to wrap up the game. Ricks totaled over 600 yards of offense, almost half of it on the ground led by Brantley Oakey and Scott Thompson. Nick Hyde scored four touchdowns, all on short runs. New Mexico Military, on the strength of their second quarter rally, totaled over 400 yards on the ground.

    October 13, 2001
    Ricks 46   Scottsdale 14 (in Scottsdale)
       The Ricks College Vikings improved their record to 7-0 with a 46-14 win at Scottsdale Saturday evening. Ricks is ranked second in the NJCAA poll, with three games left in the regular season, two of them on the road.
    Neither team started out strong offensively, with the first quarter ending in a 0-0 tie. With 2:24 to go in the first period, Dallas Stevens came off the bench to quarterback the Vikings. He took over with the Vikings at their own seven yard line. Two minutes into the second quarter Stevens hit Jonny Harline on a 42 yard touchdown pass to put Ricks up 6-0. Jon Clark then hit Nate Hahm for the two point conversion and an 8-0 Viking lead. The Viking defense forced Scottsdale to punt and the Vikings took over at their own 48 yard line. Seven plays later, Stevens connected with Harline for a second touchdown and a 15-0 Viking lead.
    Scottsdale’s offense then woke up, using the screen pass and the draw play, and going 78 yards in 12 plays, Jack Tomco hitting Shannon Holcombe from 5 yards out for the score to make it 15-7. Ricks had no problem answering, driving 72 yards, with Stevens and Harline connecting for a third time in the quarter to make it 22-7 at the half. Harline had a highlight reel full of great catches in the second period spurt.
    Scottsdale opened the third quarter with a 74 yard scoring drive, 250 pound fullback Justin Holmes finishing it off with a 4 yard run. The Vikings then put the game away, exploding for three touchdowns in less than five minutes. Robbie Haas hit Jon Clark from 46 yards out....Luke Knuppenburg hit Brantley Oakey for a 27 yard score....and Haas and Clark connecting again from six yards out. It was 43-14 at the end of the third period. Jim Marshall added a 40 yard field goal in the fourth to finish the scoring. Viking quarterbacks connected on 30 of 44 pass attempts for 385 yards, with Ricks rolling up 438 yards total offense. Carl Tuitavuki led a defense that gave up only 45 yards rushing.

    October 5, 2001
    Ricks 41   Snow 14 (in Rexburg)
    The Vikings got off to a quick start, scoring on their first two possessions of the game, and beating Snow College 41-14.  Robbie Haas directed both drives, connecting with Jon Clark on TD passes of 7 and 24 yards to put Ricks on top 13-0 midway through the first quarter.  A Mike Overly interception gave the ball to the Vikings again as the first quarter ended.  Moving into Snow territory, Badger linebacker Joe Katoa picked up a Viking fumble and returned it 60 yards for a touchdown, cutting the Vikings lead to 13-7.  The Ricks offense then blew the game open, scoring on consecutive one play drives.  The first was a 77 yard TD pass from Haas to Jonny Harline, then two minutes later, Haas connected with Brantley Oakey on a 64 yard pass and Ricks led 28-7 at the half.  The Vikings first possession of the second half also resulted in a touchdown, Dallas Stevens hitting Jared Doman from 22 yards out and it was 35-7.  Fourth quarter scoring consisted of two Jim Marshall field goals.  Meanwhile, safety Mike Overly was having a career night with four interceptions, one short of tying a school record.  Snow's offense managed a fourth quarter touchdown.  Haas completed 18 of 30 passes for 315 yards and four touchdowns in less than three quarters of play.  Harline had four catches for 148 yards as the Vikings totaled 510 yards total offense.

    September 29, 2001
    Ricks 64     Phoenix 14 (in Phoenix)
    Ricks College football put its best foot forward Saturday, routing Phoenix College 64-10. The offense rolled up 495 yards total offense while the defense limited Phoenix to minus ten yards rushing, ten completions in 38 attempts, and picked off 4 Bear passes.
    It took the Vikings one quarter to adjust to the 104 degree heat. Phoenix led 3-0 at the end of the first period, then stood stunned as the Vikings reeled off 27 points in the second quarter. Leading 14-10, Ricks scored twice in the final 1:11 of the second period to lead 27-10 at the half. Ricks added 23 more in the third, and closed with 14 in the fourth.
    Quarterback Robbie Haas threw for 277 yards and three touchdowns and rushed for a fourth. Late in the third quarter, Luke Knuppenburg took over and ran for two TD’s while throwing for a third. Jon Clark caught nine passes for 160 yards and two touchdowns, and Brantley Oakey added 209 all purpose yards.
    The defense was led by the D-Line that forced Phoenix into bad passes and haunted Bears receivers into numerous drops.

    September 22, 2001
    Ricks 35    E. Arizona 14
    In front of a homecoming crowd estimated at over 7000, Ricks picked up its fourth win of the season, beating Eastern Arizona 35-14.  Neither team scored in the first quarter as defense set the tone.  Eastern recovered a fumble and Ricks intercepted a pass.  On the Vikings first possession of the second quarter, Robbie Haas directed a 4 play, 54 yard drive, ending in a 19 yard touchdown pass to Chris Nash.  A Viking defensive stand followed, forcing EACC to punt from their two, Ricks taking possession at the Gila Monsters 30.  Dallas Stevens then directed a four play drive, Scott Thompson carrying the ball in from the nine and Ricks led 14-0.  Eastern then put together a 69 yard drive that got them a 33 yard field goal by Kessley VanDeGraff, making it 14-3.  The Eastern defense followed with a safety and it was 14-5 at the half.  In the third quarter, Ricks fumbled at the Eastern 16, but Viking linebacker Brigham Tomco followed with an interception and return to the EACC five.  From there, Scott Thompson got his second TD of the day and it appeared Ricks was in control, leading 21-5.  But Eastern came right back with an 8 play, 92 yard drive, Josh Todd connecting with Evans Hyacinthe for a touchdown.  The conversion attempt failed.  Ricks drove deep into Gila Monster territory again, only to fumble the ball.  Eastern  followed with a 22 yard field goal and it was 21-14 as the third quarter ended.  Again the Vikings turned the ball over, as an EACC cornerback stripped a receiver of the ball at Eastern's 36 yard line.  The Viking defense held, forced a punt, and return specialist Brantley Oakey broke the game open with a school record 86 yard punt return for a touchdown.  A Ben Allen interception led to a Jon Clark touchdown and a 35-14 lead that held.

    September 1, 2001  
    Ricks 24   Pima 14
    Ricks College opened the season with a 24-14 win over the Pima Storm.  After a scoreless first quarter, the Vikings took the lead as Travis Christensen capped a 37 yard drive, scoring on an 11 yard pass from Dallas Stevens late in the second quarter.  The Vikings scored three times in the third quarter.  Kody Kraus opened the second half by intercepting a Blake Kinart pass at the Pima 30, returning the ball to the eleven. Five plays later, Christensen had his second touchdown of the night on a one yard run, making it 14-0.  Pima was soon forced to punt again.  Robbie Haas then connected with Jon Clark for 54 yards.  Pima stiffened and forced Ricks to take three points with a 37 yard field goal by Jason Wood.  Down 17-0, Pima used the scrambling and passing of quarterbacks Kinart and Dan Camacho to start a comeback.  Driving to the Viking 23, Kinart was intercepted for a second time.  This time Viking cornerback Ben Allen picked off the pass and returned it 90 yards for a touchdown and a 24-0 lead.  Pima came right back, driving 92 yards and scoring on a four yard run by Andre Gayles.  The Storm defense held, and the offense moved again, this time 78 yards in six plays with an 18 yard pass from Camacho to Kyle Lunsford.  The Vikings kept Pima's comeback hopes alive by muffing a punt attempt, but time ran out as Pima missed a 22 yard field goal.
