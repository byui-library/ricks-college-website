Sleep is not a luxury

Recent studies show that 2 out of 3 Americans get less sleep than they need to feel rested and fully alert. Sleep is no small matter. In fact, many motor vehicle accidents can be attributed to sleep deficits. Dr. John Sheperd, Jr. of Mayo Clinic=s Sleep disorders Center writes, AAny time you are relying on an alarm clock to wake you up, you are not getting enough sleep, your brain is voting >yes, you need more sleep.=@

He continues, AThe vast majority of people need 8 hours of sleep a night. A small fraction of people are fully alert all day with a routine of 6 hours a night, while others actually need 9 or more hours a night to feel rested. Genetics play a role in these differences. If you are not feeling rested, you may be running up a sleep debt that accumulates over the course of the week. It probably takes about 2 weeks to recover from a sleep debt that has gone on for a week or more.@

There are several problems that can interfere with sleep including stress, tension, noise, barking dogs, depression, pain, heartburn, work shifts, too much light, extreme temperatures, among others. If you snore loudly, you may also have a condition known as sleep apnea. In sleep apnea, the person actually stops breathing for several seconds. The irritability, memory problems, and tiredness (common to a variety of sleep disorders) are daily concerns for the sleep apnea patients. Consult your physician for any sleep problems that persist for more than two weeks.

A few strategies for dealing with sleep problems include retiring and arising from bed at the same time each day, avoiding naps longer than 15 minutes, relaxing before bed, doing something quiet before bed like reading, getting up if you are not asleep after laying in bed 15 -20 minutes, exercising at least 4 hours prior to bedtime, and setting aside a planning/problem solving time earlier in the day instead of bedtime. And finally, if you are staying up watching TV, set your VCR to tape your favorite show, or consider doing without it altogether. Your body will thank you in the morning.
