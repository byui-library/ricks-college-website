1.  Are there any absolutes?

Max Plank gave us Quantum Theory

Small bits of matter (sub-atomic particles) move about and use energy, measured in units he called quantum.

Though small, this amount is a constant, and can be calculated.

Principle of Uncertainty:  (another constant) 1927, Heisenberg — you can predict how large amounts of material or matter will react,

but you can never be sure about individual particles.

He also found the more you try to measure a particular atom or particle, the more it varies.

He concludes: something which is purely physical cannot be measured.

You can't say this loaf of bread weighs exactly 2 lbs. or this pencil is 10” long.

In Mormon terms this is “free agency.” We can predict  (a constant) how most of the students on campus will react to a certain message from Salt Lake, but we cannot foretell how each student will respond. They have their agency or Principle of Uncertainty (constant).

Here's where Einstein comes in.

He says that most things are relative. A number like 10 is only something we mortals use, or the word for an object can be a car or vehicle, or wagen or voiture, etc.

There is only one absolute/constant:

the velocity of light.

He then adds that to increase speed will also increase mass. And if something were to move at the speed of light, it would fill the universe in both mass and energy. (Light of Christ fills the universe .  .  . )

Einstein concludes there are some absolutes in nature, and that they govern everything in the universe, no matter what state they may be in. (Mormons would agree.)

The same holds true for time. And space is not finite, but infinite. Mormons would agree, saying that God has no time, in our sense. He Himself has said that “all things are present.”
