Course Objectives:

The primary objectives of English 109 are:

               1. To develop writing skills

               2. To develop reading skills

The secondary objectives of English 109 are:

               1. To continue developing speaking and listening skills

               2. To continue aiding in the acculturation process

Course Description:

English 109 is designed to help students improve their speaking, listening, reading, and writing skills. The focus of the class, however, is on college writing. One of the main objectives is to prepare students for English 111. The processes of prewriting, drafting, rewriting, and editing will be practiced in class and with your reading partner in the Reading Center. Students meet 3 times a week in class and once a week with an assigned reading partner in the Reading Center.

The reading partners will help students work on the basic skills of standard English such as spelling, punctuation, capitalization, vocabulary building, and sentence structure. Students may meet more than once a week with a reading partner if they desire. The reading partners serve as both academic and cultural tutors. Please feel free to ask them questions about English, BYU-Idaho, and America.

Coherent thinking leads to coherent writing. To be good college writers, students must be good college thinkers. Writing reflects thinking and is, therefore, an excellent tool for developing English proficiency. Success in English 109 will prepare the students for increased success in all college courses.

The four main objectives of the class are:

1. To practice the writing process by prewriting, drafting, rewriting, and editing papers.

2. To improve basic skills of standard English through group and individual instruction.

3. To Improve English "thinking" skills by listening, speaking, reading, and writing in authentic situations.

4. To help students make a smooth transition to a new culture by discussing the "expectations" of American writing teachers.

What can you expect?

You can expect the following types of activities in English 109.

Journal Writing

You should write in your journal everyday. You should include your feelings and thoughts of each day. Letters to your family and friends may be included as part of your journal. You may write in English, or in your primary language. Your journal grade will be calculated by the number of days you have written during the semester. Entries will be completely confidential. Label each entry with a number and a date.

Formal Writing Assignments

You will write 13 papers during the course of the semester. These will be approximately one page each. The purpose of each paper will vary depending on the assignment. These will all be typed (12pt), double spaced, and have one inch margins unless otherwise instructed.

Grammar Practice

Grammar concepts will be discussed in class. You will then complete grammar homework by the assigned due date. When you complete the homework, you will correct your own using the back of the book. Every Friday, you will have a short quiz covering the concepts we have discussed in class. The papers you write from then on should be free of errors in the areas covered.

Discussions/Readings

You can expect to discuss all of the assigned readings in class. You are expected to come to class prepared to discuss the assigned readings, and to make connections between your own life experiences and the readings. You will also read orally in class from time to time.

Portfolio

The portfolio will be a representation of your best work. It will include what you believe are your three best papers and a letter. The portfolio will be turned in towards the end of the semester.

Conversation Partners (tutors)

You will meet once a week with your conversation partner (tutor) in the reading lab (Clarke 329). Together, you will practice and review the class assignments. You will read your papers out loud to your partner. Your partner may help you edit your papers. You will also continue to practice and improve your spoken English.

Mid Term and Final
