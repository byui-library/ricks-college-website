Rome founded 750BC Romulus and Remus/born in village founded by Aeneas (who came from Troy)/need wives “Rape of the Sabines”  /citizenship to husbands

Republic 500-133BC (Early Rome)

            - law of the 12 Tables/Plebians & Patricians

            - family organization/father controls all/chooses husband for bride/is the law

            - everyone is a farmer or soldier

            - loyalty to the state above all

            - very conservative lifestyle

Punic Wars 264-146BC  Carthage vs. Rome/Hannibal and elephants in Alps

            - three wars (about 50 yrs apart)/ Rome gets all Carthage's possessions

            - more slaves/decline of small farmer/city mob rising middle class/more luxury

Late Republic 133-30BC

            - General Pompey vs. General Julius

            - Julius wins, but is later assassinated on Ides of March/calendar due to him

            - Augustus (Caesar's nephew)41 yrs of power/good man/Pax Romana

            - followed by Tiberius/Caligula/Claudius/Nero (all bad)

                        - Coliseum not built until 72-80AD, after Nero's death

The Decline 100-200AD

            - military dominates everything/violent lifestyle

            - excesses/vice/corruption/decadence

The Fall 476AD (no battle, empire just dies out)

            - imperialism (“I rule everyone”)

            - changes in the economy

                        - too much money leaving country/foreign markets close

                        serfs created/heredity status/social

                        stigma against work/land worn out/disease

The Arts - Romans were doers, not thinkers

            - made copies of Greek statues/(copies, not originals)

            - architecture is the most Roman art/the round arch

Philosophy

            Epicurians - excess today will lead to a bad tomorrow (Catullus)

                        - Prudence is the pursuit of pleasure

                        - avoid anything which creates fear: religion/death

            Neoplatonism - all comes from God/Forms

                        - despise matter/world but revere soul

            Stoics - Logos controls all, so submit (Arrian)

                        - do your duty and you'll be happy; if you can’t change the situation then change your attitude about it

Transition to Christian World

            - old Roman conventions changed to Christian ones

                        Apollo=Christ;cupid=cherub;nike=angels/Roman Christ has no beard

                        - symbols (except cross)/icthys/orant/sarcophagus

            - Christians everywhere/adopt them/Constantine says they're OK

                        Edict of Milan 313 legalizes them

            - Constantine moves capital to Constantinople 323AD

                        - splits empire/East is Byzantine style/mosaics

                        - Western capitol moves to Ravenna/later Justinian will rule there

            - Council of Nicea 325/defines God for entire Christian world

            - St. Augustine c.400 - City of God, evil is a privation/saved by grace/duality of time (Man vs. God)/world is changeable/man has free agency

                        - only the Jews had truth/so avoid Greeks readings

            - Ascetics (religious extremists)/self-martyrdom

                        - denigration of the body/roll in thorns

                        - too much for most men (they go crazy)

            - Monasteries start instead/vows of poverty/labor

                        - choose worst lands, away from cities, yet make them productive

                        - best wines/metal shops/brewing/weaving

                        - division of clergy: regular/secular

            - church shuns scientific/materialistic world

                        - faith based on miracles and spiritual values, not science

                        - innovation and originality were discouraged

                        - final authority lies in Church

            - Iconoclastic Controversy 726

                        - Pope Leo III says no more icons

                        - protest against worldliness (and monasteries making to much $$)

                        - finalizes split between East (Greek Orthodox) and West (Roman Catholic) churches

                        - but edict reversed a century later

Feudalism - starts in 500's

            - strict stratification of society/land ownership

            - no upward mobility

            - knights 1000 acres/central village/“common” meadow

            - no education/short life span (30 yrs max)

            - no travel (abt 30 miles)/no money so use barter system instead

            - Christian outlook: this world is the 'vale of tears'

Arabs - Mohammed 570AD in Saudi Arabia/unites Arab world/gives equality to all men

            - no clergy/no images/becomes symbol of 'infidel' to Christians

Germans - opposite of Christian view

            - the good life is here, not later

            - eat, drink and be merry

            - honor ONLY on the battlefield

            - comitatus/Beowulf

Charlesmagne (Charles the Great)

            - first to unite all Europe/emperor of Holy Roman Empire

            - brings scholars to his capital/copy manuscripts

                        - learning is popular/study everything

            - forces all his conquests to become Christians (unlike Muslims)

                        - Song of Roland - tells of reconquest of Spain, take back from Moors

            - he dies/grandsons divide empire/Europe recedes back into the dark.

Beowulf - oldest English poem/500/written down 700? 1000?/Christian author

            - fight between Good and Evil/serious/glory is earthly

            - typical German/cruel/rough/a man’s world

Everyman - morality play/didactic/man's struggles here on earth/no joy in life

            - not same as 'mystery' play/ministerium 'service' which teaches history of Man.

            - popular until Shakespeare's day.

Aucassin - complete opposite of Everyman

            - a lover's story/life is fun/it’s OK to enjoy it

            - the worldly approach, rather than church approach
