Prospective International Student Information

About Ricks College:

Ricks College is a unique school affiliated with the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, Ricks College requires all students, faculty, and staff to abide by a Honor Code. Adherence to the Honor Code promotes a spiritual setting, as well as academic growth and social harmony.

All students agree to live by a code of conduct both on and off campus. These standards include clothing, grooming, and housing regulations. The curfew at Ricks College requires all students to be in their apartments by 11:00 p.m. on nights proceeding school days, and 1:00 a.m. on weekends. Overnight co-ed activities such as spending the night, camping, in motels, etc. are prohibited. No overnight guests of the opposite sex are permitted at any time in unmarried student housing. Abstinence from alcohol, tobacco, illegal drugs, coffee, and tea is also required of all enrolled students.

President David A. Bednar of Ricks College stated, A...attending Ricks College is both a privilege and responsibility...the honor code is a lesser law preparation to enter the house of the Lord and make sacred covenants, and the dress code is a lesser law preparation for how you will dress and should act after you have entered into those covenants.@

Often a phrase associated with the Honor Code is the ASpirit of Ricks College@. That spirit is gained by a unified group of students, faculty and staff dedicated to live and learn in harmony with principles that build character and promote mutual respect of each individual.

All International Students except Canadians:

Once you are accepted by Ricks College, you must go to the U.S. Embassy and apply for an F-1 visa. You should not come to the United States without first acquiring F-1 status. You should never come to the United states on a visitor visa hoping to change to an F-1 visa. There are a few unavoidable circumstances in which a B-2 is issued. In those cases, be sure the B-2 status is noted as Aprospective student@ (a change of status will be required which takes time and costs extra money).

The following are suggestions to assist you in your preparation to visit the consulate:

$In some countries, applicants need not apply in person and may mail their applications to the consulate. Whether the applicant should apply in person or by mail varies by post. Contact your local embassy for specific instructions.

$Take I-20, financial documents, letters of admission, and any other type of document that might be required for the application.

$Remember that you must prove that you are coming to the United States for Anon-immigrant@ purposes. You will be presumed to be an immigrant by the officer until you prove that you are entitled to non-immigrant status. You must prove that you have sufficient ties to your home country to cause your return after your studies. You must prove that your reasons for returning to your home country are greater than your reason for remaining in the United States.

$There will be a processing or application fee, which is currently $45 (U.S. dollars). You must pay the application fee each time you apply or reapply, as it is non-refundable. There is also issuance fee which will vary based on what your home country charges U.S. citizens for a similar visa.

$Because of the volume of applications received, visa officers must decide after a brief interview whether someone is qualified to receive a visa. Make sure that you are prepared to present your case clearly and concisely. The average interview lasts two to three minutes.

Housing:

Rexburg is a small city by most standards. There are many options of housing both on-campus and off-campus. Most off-campus housing is within walking distance. You are responsible to secure your own housing arrangements. It is highly recommended that you obtain housing prior to coming to the United States. It is best to apply early, as many housing units fill their vacancies quickly. For more information write the Ricks College Housing Office, Kimball 196, Rexburg, ID 83460-1645, or call (208) 356-1045 or Ricks College Housing.

Financial Planning:

It is essential that you plan the financing of your entire U.S. education prior to your departure. There is very little financial aid available for foreign nationals. Eighty-one percent of the international students in the U. S. finance their education through personal and family sources at the undergraduate level. You should explore all possible home country sources of funding, such loans or grants, or resources available through the U.S. Embassy. Although you are eligible for some limited employment options, it is unwise to plan on employment as a means to finance your education. Please remember to carry travelers checks and/or bank drafts when traveling. Do not carry cash in substantial amounts.

Students who have solicited sponsors for financial assistance, please understand the significance of being honest with your sponsor regarding their financial obligations. The sponsor=s signature and statement on the affidavit of support signifies the amount of financial support that person will offer during the period of your education at Ricks College. Do not leave your sponsor with an impression that their obligation is only superficial in nature. Students will discover that they may need to return home due to poor financial planning and lack of honest communication with their sponsor.

Scholarships and Financial Aid:

International students are not eligible for most scholarships until they complete 24 credit hours. There are a variety of scholarships available to students. Most are related to academic achievement. The grade point requirements for these scholarships vary each semester, and only the highest achievers receive these awards. There are also scholarship awards related to leadership, academic divisions, athletics, and some performing arts.

International students of nonimmigrant status are not eligible for any U.S. Federal grants or loans. There are very limited grants available through the Financial Aid Office. Grants are available for international students who choose to attend our summer school sessions. Summer school attenders are eligible for a tuition discount of substantial significance. If any student has financial difficulties they are to first visit with the International Student Office.

About Rexburg: Rexburg is a small city located in the Northwestern part of the United States. It is surrounded by farms, and close to the famous national parks of Yellowstone and Grand Teton. Our climate ranges from beautiful warm sunny summer days to the cold winter blizzards. Being somewhat inland, we have a dry climate. The wind tends to blow quite frequently. Clothing purchased needs to accommodate the climate extremes and conform with our dress and grooming standards. We also lack a public transportation system in Rexburg. Most businesses are within walking distance of apartments. Some students purchase bicycles, which can be used most of the year.

Registration: Once you are accepted, you will be able to register for classes at a designated date. The order of registration is based on the number of credit hours previously taken. Being a new student, you will be one of the last to register. Therefore, many of the classes desired will be full, and you may need to look at attending classes mainly offered in the afternoons or evening hours during your first semester.

All international students from non-English speaking countries are required to sign up for English 108. This is a class specifically designed to assist your adjustment to learning in the American college system, and learning reading and writing skills. An assessment is given during the first days of class to determine your skill level in English. Students may be placed in higher level English courses if competency is indicated.

It is recommended that in your first semester, you register for general education classes, and limit your credit hours close to the required minimum of 12 credit hours. This allows you the adjustment period needed. Classes can be added and dropped during the first few days of school. Feel free to contact the International Student Office for guidance and advice.

Employment: All international students are limited in their employment options. New international students may not work off-campus. They may work on-campus. Students may not work more than 20 hours per week while school is in session. There are some off-campus work experience programs offered after students complete one academic year (two full semesters).

In order to be employed on-campus, a social security card must be obtained. You must check with the International Student Office, and receive a permission letter, and other instruction prior to applying for a social security card. The Social Security Office is located in Idaho Falls, about 25 miles south of Rexburg.

Health Insurance: You are required to have major medical health insurance while attending Ricks College. The cost of the insurance will be assessed at the beginning of each semester. Only those students who are already fully and continuously covered by a medical policy of their own will be exempt from this requirement. Your policy must guarantee payment of medical cost incurred by you while you are in the U.S.    Those who hold such a policy must provide a notarized copy to the Accounting Office in order to have the insurance requirement waived, and sign designated forms.

Orientation: Each semester, the International Office provides a formal orientation for all new international students. If you have not received a notice of this orientation, please contact the International Student Office

Questions: If you have any questions or concerns, please feel free to contact the International Student Office.
