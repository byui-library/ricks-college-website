Research and Writing
As you start researching and writing, consider what this activity will do for you.  Your goals are:

(1) to practice the process of learning on your own,
(2) to find information from traditional and electronic sources,
(3) to synthesize this information from several sources into a focused paper,
(4) to document properly the information you have found,
(5) to practice principles of good writing,
(6) and to follow directions.

As you improve in these areas, you will become more competent and skillful, and this will build your self esteem and confidence.  Write your research paper from a position of knowledge. Don't just string together quote after quote collected from another person's writing.  Don't plagiarize. Learn. Digest. Then write what you have learned IN YOUR OWN WORDS.

Paper Requirements

1. Five pages in the body
2. Cover sheet which includes: Paper Title, Your Name, Class Hour, Paper Number
3. Works cited page
4. At least six separate sources
5. A bibliography page is not required.

Television or Radio Program
The information in an entry for a television or radio program usually appears in the following order:

1. Title of the episode or segment, if appropriate (in quotation marks)
2. Title of the program (italics)
3. Title of the series, if any (neither italics nor in quotation marks)
4. Name of the network
5. Call letters and city of the local station (if any)
6. Broadcast date

Example:
"Frankenstein: The Making of the Monster."  Great Books. Narr. Donald Suterland.  
     Writ. Eugenie Vink.   Dir. Jonathan Ward.  Learning Channel.  8  Sept. 1993.

Basic Format
Author. "Title." <Internet address>.  (Access date).

Example:

Godiva Chocolatier Inc. "Age Old Obsession: A Brief History of Chocolate."  <http:// 
     www.godiva.com/resources/history.html>.  (30 July 1996).

E-Mail Site
Basic Format
Author or sender. "Subject." Recipient's e-mail address.  (Date of message).

Example

Day, Martha. "Review of Film-Bad Lieutenant." <XLI@MOOSE.uvm.edu>. (30 July
     1995).

Listserv Site
Basic Format
Author. "Title or subject." Listname@e-mail address. (Access date).

Example:

Smith, Joseph. "Daily Schedules." LIBREF-L@Kent.edu.  (10 May 1995).

Journal
Basic Format
Author. "Title of article." Journal Title. Volume issue (year): paging or indicator of
     length. <Internet address>.  (Access date).

Citing Materials Inside the Research Paper MLA Style

In your paper, all borrowed information needs to be cited.  Such information includes quotations, summaries, paraphrases.  Cite any information considered not to be common knowledge.  Cite this information in either of the following two ways:

1. Using a signal phrase.  Here is an example:

According to Hackworth, "Herman Melville used his great, white whale as a symbol of God. Ahab was actually on a God-killing mission" (53).  

The signal phrase is "According to Hackworth."  In this example, note the location of the quote marks, the parenthesis, and the period.

2. Naming the author in the parentheses.   Here is an example:

In his lifetime, Melville came to see God as one divinely depraved. Consequently, he felt alienated from God and chose to fight against the Creator.  In the book, Moby Dick, "Herman Melville used his great, white, sinister whale as a symbol of God. Ahab was actually on a God-killing mission" (Hackworth 53).
