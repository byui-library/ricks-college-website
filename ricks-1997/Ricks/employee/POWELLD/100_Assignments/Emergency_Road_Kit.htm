Car Emergency kit

In Idaho the weather can be BITTER cold (Sometimes to -30o F or lower). If you are traveling and get caught in a snow storm or blizzard, or slide off the road, this emergency kit will probably save your life. If you get in trouble, STAY WITH YOUR CAR! It's easy to get lost or disoriented in a snowstorm. Cars are easier to find than lost people. Conserve your battery. If you are in trouble, flash your lights three times when a car passes. Don't run lights or electrical accessories (radio) if no one is around. Batteries go dead fast in cold weather.
1. Warm Clothes:
    A. Warm, water-proof shoes or boots                                         C. Change of clothes, preferably wool
    B. Extra Socks (wool)                                                                 D. A REALLY warm coat
                                                                                                       E. Warm gloves or mittens
2. A warm sleeping bag (best) or heavy wool blanket. Get something that stays warm when wet. Down-filled bags are warm when dry, but you'll freeze if they're wet.
3. High Energy Food (Candy bars, granola, raisins, etc.)
4. A can or something to melt/carry water in. Don't eat snow. It takes more energy to melt it than the benefit you get from the moisture.
5. Water-proof matches or Emergency Road Flares to light a fire.
6. A good flashlight with spare batteries.
7. A good first-aid kit.
8. Battery Jumper Cables.
9. Tire Chains or cables. Have them fit to your tires before you need them.
10. Some clean rags.
11. Never let your car get below ½ tank of fuel. You may need to run your car for a few minutes every ½ hour to keep the heater (and you) warm until help arrives. An empty gas tank doesn't produce much heat. Be sure to roll down a window a little to prevent carbon monoxide poisoning if you're sitting still.
12. A tow strap or tow chain.
13. A snow shovel. (You may want to dig a snow cave or dig yourself out if stuck.)
14.  A cellular phone is a really good idea if you have access to one. You can use it to call for help or to report someone else that is in trouble. Make sure the batteries are kept charged and that you know how to use it. The cost of the call is cheap if you need help.
