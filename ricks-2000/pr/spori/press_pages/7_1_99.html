R News & Notes
July 1, 1999

After months, even years, of study and concern, it has been determined that the Jacob W. Spori Building will be replaced with a structure that will maintain the legacy and "Spirit of Ricks." The announcement was made on June 23 through a press conference to the media, a published letter to the public, a campus-wide email to employees. The building has stood quietly on the hill as the terrain has transformed from sage brush to manicured grounds, the students have grown from hundreds to thousands, and technology has taken us from dittos and switchboards to Internet and fiber optics. Each will have personal reflections of the gray stone edifice; perhaps a look back at history is appropriate for all:

1899 LDS Church authorizes first building for Fremont Stake Academy, previously Bannock Stake Academy, now known as Ricks College.

1900 On June 25, cornerstone laid by President George Q. Cannon, first counselor to President Wilford Woodruff.

1903 In October the building was used at start of 1903-04 school year. It had 20,565 square feet of floor space and was built of rhyolite, a soft stone quarried nearby, measures 60 feet by 120 feet. Estimated cost of construction was $63,475 (includes furnishings and grounds; much of the labor was donated.)

1906 On Nov. 12 the building was dedicated by John Henry Smith of the Council of Twelve Apostles

1911-1912 Building was plumbed for running water. The auditorium converted into multi-purpose stage. A gymnasium and dance hall were on third floor.

1915-16 Lighted, large sized "RA" placed on roof of building

1963 Dec. 11, Building was named Jacob Spori Administration Building

1976 Administration offices moved from building.

1989 Building was placed on National Register of Historic Places.

1995 Building was re-roofed and north dormers removed.

1999 Decision was made to replace the building.
