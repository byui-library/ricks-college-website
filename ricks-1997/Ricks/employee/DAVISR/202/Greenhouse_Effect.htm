The Impending Renewal

of the Earth

Forum at Ricks College,November 17, 1994

Shenvood Idso, Arizona State University

It's a real privilege for me to be at Ricks today and to be able to visit with you about a subject that encompasses both scientific and religious concepts at one and the same time. The topic to which I refer is briefly enunciated in verse 10 of The Articles of Faith, which lists four extraordinary events that are to occur in close proximity to the Second Coming of Christ. Three of these phenomena are people‑oriented: the gathering of Israel, the restoration of the Ten Tribes, and the building of the New Jerusalem. The fourth, however, and the one that I will discuss today, concerns the world of nature. Simply stated, it is “that the earth will be renewed and receive its paradisiacal glory,” which transformation, according to one of the footnotes to this verse, will return the planet to “a condition like the Garden of Eden.”

The writings of the prophets, both ancient and modem, describe this transformation in some detail. In Isaiah, for example, it is written that the forlorn and desolate areas of the globe shall become as “a fruitful field” (Isa. 32:15), and that “the desert shall rejoice, and blossom as the rose.” (Isa. 35:1) In fact, Isaiah categorically declares of the lone and dreary world, that the Almighty “will make her wilderness like Eden, and her desert like the garden of the Lord.” (Isa.51:3)

The early saints of our dispensation were well acquaint­ed with this doctrine. In a song entitled “Adam‑ondi­-Ahman;” which was included in the first LDS hymnbook published in 1835 and which we still sing, William W. Phelps, who was a contemporary of Joseph Smith, wrote that “this earth was once a garden place,” and after rhap­sodizing about its former glories, he concluded by stating that these blessed conditions would be restored at the time of the Savior's Second Coming, “when,” as he put it, “all the earth in glorious bloom affords the saints a holy home.”1

Phelps reiterated this doctrine in two other hymns we still sing. In one he wrote that “when the Lord returns with Zion, . . . then the earth will be as Eden,”2 while in the other he declared that “we'll watch for the day when the Savior will come . . . and earth will appear as the Garden of Eden,”3 providing us thereby with a clear and unequivocal affirma­tion of faith in the impending renewal of the earth that is repeated in all three verses of this latter composition.

In more recent times, Elder Joseph Fielding Smith has reaffirmed the reality of this imminent event and described it in some detail. In Volume 1 of Doctrines of Salvation, he states that “when our Savior comes, the earth will be changed to a terrestrial condition;” adding that “this condi­tion will last until after the close of the millennium.”4 Quoting the tenth Article of Faith, he then says that “too many have the idea that this has reference to the celestialized earth, but this is not the case.” It refers, he states, “to the restored earth as it will be when Christ comes to reign.”4

Expanding on this concept, President Smith asked the following question and gave this answer:

What is meant by the restoration of the earth?

This earth is to be renewed and brought back to the con­dition in which it was before it was cursed through the fall of Adam. When Adam passed out of the Garden of Eden, then the earth became a telestial world, and it is of that order today . . . . When Christ comes, the earth will be changed and so will all upon its face. It will become a terrestrial world then and will so remain for 1,000 years.5

That this statement means exactly what it says is made clear by a subsequent statement of President Smith in Volume II of Doctrines of Salvation: The earth is to be renewed or restored to its primitive beauty and condition... This is not mere­ly a figure of speech, but a literal condition that will prevail.6

So what will be the nature of this planet‑wide transfor­mation? According to Elder James E. Talmage, the enhanced biological state that will characterize the earth throughout the millennium will not be like anything cur­rently familiar to man; for as he expresses it in his book, A Study of the Articles of Faith, “the blessed conditions under which the earth shall exist and man shall live during the millennial era are almost beyond human powers of compre­hension, so different are they from all to which history testi­fies and experience confirms.”7 Indeed, Talmage states that “the description of Eden is to us as the story of another world, an orb of a higher order of existence, wholly unlike this dreary sphere.”7 Yet, as he continues, “Eden was in real­ity a feature of our planet.”7 And such conditions shall return again, he affirms, “in the regeneration when the Son of Man shall sit on the throne of his glory” (Matt. 19:28), as is also indicated by the previously quoted words of Joseph Fielding Smith.

In broad outline, then, the impending renewal of the earth would appear to consist of some tremendous change in the basic biology of the planet that will likely enhance plant growth far beyond anything heretofore experienced, except, of course, for Eden itself. What will be the immedi­ate cause of this change? And when will the transformation occur?

Before proceeding to provide what I believe to be both possible and plausible answers to these questions, I want to make it perfectly clear that I do not know for a fact that what I am going to suggest is correct. A prophet, which I am not, may indeed prophesy, and prophesy correctly; but a scien­tist, which I am, can only theorize, and that often rather imperfectly. Nevertheless, even tentative theories can be useful, if they are recognized for what they are (a sophisti­cated form of speculation) and if they provide an impetus for further inquiry of either a religious or scientific nature.

What I would like to suggest, then, or theorize, if you will, is the possibility that a dramatic increase in the carbon dioxide or CO2 content of the atmosphere may be the means employed by the Lord to reinvigorate earth's biosphere. We do know for a fact, for example, as a result of literally hun­dreds of laboratory and field experiments, that a doubling of the air's CO2 concentration typically increases the pro­ductivity of nearly all herbaceous plants by 30 to 40%8-10 and many scientists feel that a CO2 concentration of twice the atmosphere's pre‑industrial level may be reached some­time in the coming centuryl1.l2 as we continue to burn large quantities of coal, gas and oil, all of which substances release copious quantities of CO2 into the air as a major by­product of their combustion.

This “aerial fertilization effect” of carbon dioxide derives from the fact that atmospheric CO2 is the primary raw material used by plants to produce food via the process of photosynthesis, and the fact that the more CO2 there is in the air, the better plants perform this vital function.13-14

addition, augmented concentrations of CO2 reduce vegeta­tive water loss by partially closing the small pores in plant leaves through which water escapes to the atmosphere. In most non‑woody plants, the resultant water savings is also on the order of 30 to 40% for a doubling of the air's CO2 con­tent.l6,l7 And combining this effect with the CO2‑induced increase in plant productivity leads to an actual doubling of the efficiency with which such plants utilize water to pro­duce organic matter.15

Think of what this improvement in vegetative economy could do for the planet. As the air's CO2 content rises ever higher, earth's plant life should become more and more effi­cient at conserving and utilizing moisture, enabling plants to grow where it is presently too dry for them and empow­ering them to reclaim great tracts of land previously lost to desertification,16 in literal fulfillment of Isaiah's prophecy that the desert shall 'blossom as the rose.” (Isa. 35:1)

Greater vegetative cover should additionally reduce the adverse effects of soil erosion caused by the ravages of wind and rain. And with greater plant productivity, both above­ and below ground, there should be an increase in soil organ­ic matter, which may produce even further biological bene­fits.l9

Creatures that live in the soil, such as earthworms, for example, are greatly stimulated by additions of organic mat­ter; and an increase in their activity would likely lead to the creation of much new soil, while at the same time improv­ing the fertility, structure, aeration and drainage of existing soils. These improvements, in turn, would likely boost plant productivity higher yet, putting still more organic matter into the soil, and so on, with the several effects feed­ing back upon each other in a positively reinforcing fashion that lifts the whole biosphere to a new level of activity 20

As incredible as this scenario may appear, however, it is only the beginning of the good news about atmospheric CO2 enrichment; for not only did Isaiah say that the wilderness would become a fruitful field, he said that the fruitful field would “be counted for a forest” (Isa. 32:15) and that “instead of the thorn shall come up the fir tree, and instead of the briar shall come up the myrtle tree;” (Isa. 55:13) a theme of an emerging preeminence of trees that he repeats over and over again. (Isa. 13:7, 29:17, 41:19, 60:13)

These prophecies are particularly insightful; for a care­ful reading of Genesis and the Book of Moses in The Pearl of Great Price reveals that the Garden of Eden was composed primarily of trees, and that it was from the fruit of those trees that Adam and Eve freely received their sustenance. In fact, it was not until they were cast out of the Garden and the ground was cursed that they were forced to labor for their food, which the Lord then directed should be “the herb of the field.” (Gen. 3:18) Hence, the return of the earth to Eden‑like conditions that will accompany the removal of the curse under which the biology of the planet has operated since the time of Adam will likely lead to major changes in the composition and functioning of earth's ecosystems, reducing the roles played by herbaceous plants while expanding those of woody species.

This transformation of the planet—the development of an increasingly robust “global forest”—is precisely what is predicted by recent research that shows trees to be consid­erably more responsive to increases in the air's CO2 concen­tration than are non‑woody plants.21 In the world's longest continuous CO2 enrichment experiment, for example, my colleagues and I at the U.S. Water Conservation Laboratory in Phoenix, Arizona have found that a simple doubling of the air's CO2 content more than triples the long‑term growth rate of sour orange trees, and that a tripling of the atmos­pheric CO2 concentration may cause them to grow almost seven times faster.22 What is more, we have observed equiv­alent growth responses in a number of other tree species,23,24 and there is evidence in the annual cycle of the air's CO2 content that suggests that the vast majority of earth's trees may behave similarly 25,26 In addition, we have observed the photosynthetic rates of certain pine trees we have studied to rise by a factor of ten in response to a ten‑fold enhancement of the air's CO2 content 27 which is the degree of CO2 enrich­ment that scientists have estimated would result from the burning of all fossil fuels in the earth's crust28 which is something that could well occur when the earth shall “burn as an oven” at Christ's return. (Malachi 4:1) And for such an increase in photosynthesis, it is possible that the actual growth rates of the pine trees we studied might be as much as fifteen times greater than they are today, due to the com­pound interest effect caused by the CO2‑induced increase in photosynthesis producing additional foliage, which then also photosynthesizes at this greatly elevated rate and pro­duces even more foliage, and so on.29

Now I must admit that such potential increases in growth rates sound incredible, almost unbelievable, in fact. But to fulfill the prophecies that pertain to this phenome­non, the biological renewal of the earth must be incredible; for as Elder Talmage has described it, the renewed earth, like Eden, will truly be “an orb of a higher order of existence, wholly unlike this dreary sphere.”7 And if my projections fly in the face of “all to which history testifies and experience confirms,”7 well, that is exactly what Talmage suggests they should do.

As amazing as are the prophecies of earth's impending renewal, then, science has shown that many of them may well be readily fulfilled. But just because increased concen­trations of atmospheric carbon dioxide may be able to bring about what would appear to be paradisiacal conditions, does this mean that this phenomenon is in fact the means that will be employed to do so, or that the rising CO2 con­tent of earth's atmosphere is truly a manifestation of the hand of the Lord in preparing the way for his millennial reign?

In considering these questions, it is instructive to note that the Lord has generally set the stage for great events by orchestrating the occurrence of important preparatory phenomena. Christ's birth, for example, was heralded by a magnificent sign in the heavens. John the Baptist came before and prepared the way for the Lord's mortal ministry. The restoration of the gospel was made possible by the Reformation in Europe and the establishment of the Constitution of the United States in America. And as the scriptures declare, the Lord's Second Coming is also to be preceded by unique events in both the affairs of men and the world of nature.

In this regard, and in reference to the unparalleled polit­ical reforms that have taken place in recent years in Eastern Europe, Elder David B. Haight of the Council of the Twelve has solemnly declared that “the Lord is now preparing the nations of the earth to receive the truth He desires them to have.”30 And if the Lord is “now preparing” the peoples of the world for the times of refreshing that shall flow from his presence, why should he not also be preparing the earth itself for the tremendous transformation that it will experi­ence at the time of his coming?

One of my primary purposes in visiting with you today is to describe the considerable scriptural and scientific evi­dence that suggests that the Lord may be currently doing just that. This recital begins with the revelation of September 23, 1832, wherein the Savior declared through the Prophet Joseph Smith that “I, the Lord, have put forth my hand to exert the powers of heaven; ye cannot see it now, yet a little while and ye shall see it, and know that I am, and that I will come and reign with my people.” (D&C 84:119)

In this scripture, the Lord says that he has set in motion certain events. Although already occurring in 1832, these events were not to be perceived by man at that time; but the Lord says that they would be discerned in “yet a little while.” He also says that these events would serve as a sign of his Second Coming, strengthening the faith of his people in the reality of his imminent return.

The rising CO2 content of earth's atmosphere meets all three of these criteria. With respect to the first point, it was not perceived to be occurring in 1832, although it had already begun, as we now know from studies of the compo­sition of air of that period trapped in bubbles imbedded in the polar ice caps31‑33 Second, it is known to be occurring at the present time, having been continuously monitored by direct measurement since 195934 And finally, it would appear to be a necessary antecedent of Christ's Second Coming, based on the following scriptural analysis.

In verse 37 of the Joseph Smith History in The Pearl of Great Price, the Prophet records the Angel Moroni as saying that “the day cometh that shall burn as an oven, and all the proud, yea, and all that do wickedly shall burn as stubble; for they that come shall burn them, saith the Lord of Hosts, that it shall leave them neither root nor branch.”

The important point to be made from this scripture, within the context of our current discussion, is that “they that come” will do the burning that shall cleanse the earth and usher in the Lord's millennial reign. And in describing this event in more detail, the prophet Joel tells how “a great people and a strong” will accomplish this task: “a fire devoureth before them; and behind them aflame burneth: the land is as the garden of Eden before them, and behind them a desolate wilderness.” Joel 2:3) Hence, it would appear that even before the Lord returns, the earth will have experienced at least a partial return to Eden‑like conditions; for the fire produced by those who accompany him is prophesied to destroy Eden‑like vegetation.

Other scriptures that provide support for this point of view are found in the Doctrine and Covenants. In D&C 38:17-­18, for example, the Lord says that the earth is his footstool and that he will soon stand upon it. And it is stated that at that day it will be “a land flowing with milk and honey, upon which there shall be no curse when the Lord cometh.” This concept, the idea of the Lord renewing the earth to a significant degree in advance of his second coming, is fur­ther elucidated in D&C 61:17, where it is written that “as I, the Lord, in the beginning cursed the land, even so in the last days have I blessed it, in its time, for the use of my saints, that they may partake the fatness thereof.”

Joseph Fielding Smith also appears to suggest that the renewal of the earth may already be in progress, when he says that “we are living in the great day of the restoration.”6 He further notes that “in this dispensation we are informed the Lord will bring back all things to the condition in which they were before the curse came upon the earth,” adding that “the earth is to be renewed or restored to its primitive beauty and condition.”6

Finally, we go back to the Lord himself and his words of September 23, 1832, wherein he declared that the events he had set in motion that would serve as a sign of his second coming would be discerned by man in “yet a little while.” Focusing on these four distinctive words—yet a little while—we turn to the scriptures to see if they are used anywhere else in describing the occurrence of a particular event. And we find that they are, and that the event is indeed the very one we are considering: the renewal of the earth, with par­ticular reference to the role that will be played by trees in this remarkable transformation.

The words are the words of Isaiah saying “Is it not yet a very little while, and Lebanon shall be turned into a fruitful field, and the fruitful field shall be esteemed a forest?” (Isa. 29:17) Nephi also records this statement of Isaiah, and even more forcefully: “But behold, saith the Lord of Hosts: I will show unto the children of men that it is yet a very little while and Lebanon shall be turned into a fruitful field; and the fruitful field shall be esteemed as a forest.” (2 Nephi 27:28)

When will this transformation occur? Both prophets agree that it will be coincident with the flooding of the earth with the Book of Mormon; for they both state that “in that day shall the deaf hear the words of the book, and the eyes of the blind shall see out of obscurity and out of darkness.” (Isa. 29:18; 2 Nephi 27:29)

But, one may ask, would the Lord ever allow us to com­prehend such things? Is it proper to identify them as overt signs of his impending return to earth? And ought we def­initely attribute them to him? According to the scriptures, not only does it appear that the Lord will allow such identi­fication and attribution, they seem to suggest that he actual­ly expects it of us!

Consider, for example, the admonition of the Lord received through the Prophet Joseph Smith, “that he that feareth me shall be looking forth for the great day of the Lord to come, even for the signs of the coming of the Son of Man.” (D&C 45:39) Surely, if commanded to so search, “even by study and also by faith” (D&C 88:118 and 109:7), it would seem only logical that the Lord would allow the faithful to recognize the signs of his imminent return. Else, what would it profit the Lord, or anyone else, if he provid­ed a sign that no one could recognize or interpret? Indeed, he has forcefully declared that “against none is his wrath kindled, save those who confess not his hand in all things” (D&C 59:21); and it is stated explicitly in the most correct book on earth, in 2 Nephi 25:7‑8, that “in the days that the prophecies of Isaiah shall be fulfilled men shall know of a surety, at the times when they come to pass . . . for in that day shall they understand them.”

So what do we see with respect to the predicted renew­al of the earth, of which Isaiah said “shall ye not know it?” (Isa. 43:19) Are there any “signs” that it is already in progress?

First and foremost is a series of ongoing measurements of the seasonal cycle of the air's CO2 content that is reveal­ing a steady intensification of the yearly “breath of life” of earth's biosphere. By way of explanation, as the Northern Hemisphere's thin veneer of vegetation draws great quanti­ties of CO2 out of the air in the spring and early summer, when it awakens from the dormancy of winter and begins a new season of growth, and then releases equally huge quan­tities back to the atmosphere when it senesces in the fall, it creates a small ripple or wave that is superimposed upon the steadily rising mean value of the air's CO2 content; and the difference between the high and low points of this sea­sonal oscillation is growing measurably larger each year. Several groups of scientists have studied this phenomenon in detail; and each and every one of them have acknowl­edged that the primary candidate for its explanation is the likelihood that the photosynthetic activity of the world's plant life is growing greater and greater each year as a result of the steadily rising CO2 content of the atmosphere and its ever‑increasing aerial fertilization effect

Evidence of a more direct nature for increased plant growth in response to the rising CO2 content of the air is to be found in a number of tree‑ring studies. When compared to their growth rates over the previous millennium, for example, long‑lived bristlecone pine trees at high‑altitude sites in California, Colorado, Nevada and New Mexico have all shown tremendous increases in growth over the past two centuries, some of them nearly doubling their productivi­ty.41,42 Studies of other forest species in other parts of the world have produced similar results,46,47 and all around the globe there is evidence of woody plants beginning to grow in profusion on lands that previously supported only desert or prairie vegetation.48‑52

A classic example of this phenomenon has been meticu­lously documented on the Jornada Experimental Range in New Mexico. When surveyed by the U.S. Land Office in 1858, no shrubs were evident on 60% of the land, and only 5% of the range contained what could be described as dense stands of brush. One hundred and five years later, howev­er, none of the area was free of woody plants, and 73% of it was dominated by thick stands of mesquite and creosote­bush.53

In addition, in that same year of 1963, the USDA's Soil Conservation Service found 88 million acres of Texas range­land to have been overrun by brush and shrubs.54 Ten years of fighting this invasion of woody plants with bulldozers and herbicides did little to slow their advance. By 1973, for example, the total land area covered by brush was no longer 88 million acres, but 92 million acres,55 and by 1982 it was 105 million acres.56

Similar statistics could be provided for other states; for the return of the trees has been as widespread as it has been rapid. To quote from a 1990 publication of the USDA's Agricultural Research Service, “the North American grass­lands are believed to have been in place for thousands of years, but it took less than 150 years for the woody plants such as mesquite and creosote bush to win out.”53 The arti­cle goes on to state that the same phenomenon has occurred on former grasslands of other continents, such as South America, Africa, and Australia; and it suggests that “some global change such as increasing atmospheric CO2 is involved.” Two of the world's foremost authorities on rangelands are even more direct. As they see it, “an experi­ment assessing the consequences of increased CO2 levels has already been conducted on a global scale, and we should consider the possibility that the recent increases in abun­dance of woody plants on rangelands is its most visible result.”

But what about places that have always been covered with trees and woody plants? What about the tropical forests of the world that have been highly productive from time immemorial?

Earlier this year a major study of forest turnover rates broached these questions. Its authors noted that turnover rates of mature tropical forests correlate well with estimates of forest net productivity and that tropical forest turnover data, therefore, “may provide a novel test of the hypothesis that global forest productivity is increasing.”58

After analyzing historical studies of 40 tropical forests from all around the globe, the authors of the report discov­ered that there has indeed been a significant upward trend in turnover rates of tropical trees since at least 1960. They then noted that the global consistency of their findings sug­gests a worldwide causative factor; and they identified the concurrent buildup of atmospheric CO2 as the factor most likely responsible for the worldwide enhancement of forest growth rates. Other experts subsequently agreed, stating that “enhanced productivity induced by increased CO2 is the most plausible candidate for the cause of the increased turnover.

Yes, there is an amazing array of facts—even much, much more than I have been able to relate here—that can be marshaled to support the theory that the biosphere is truly in the process of awakening from the great lethargy of the telestial state in which it has basically slumbered through­out all of human history since the fall of man. This evidence further suggests that our humble planet may currently be in the midst of being prepared for the tremendous transforma­tion that will occur at the time of Christ's Second Coming, when the entire world will be burned, and the resultant jump in atmospheric CO2 concentration caused by the com­bustion of the great stores of coal, gas and oil locked away in the earth's crust could well propel the planet into a boun­teous biological state closely akin to that which graced the earth's surface at the time of the Garden of Eden.

Could it be that this on‑going phenomenon is what David of old foresaw when he declared in a psalm of thanksgiving (1 Chr. 16:33) that “the trees of the wood [shall] sing out at the presence of the Lord, because he cometh to judge the earth?” Or, could it be that it is what Isaiah saw when he declared that “the mountains and the hills shall break forth . . . into singing, and all the trees of the field shall clap their hands?” (Isa. 55:12) Or, could it be that it is what the Prophet Joseph knew, either consciously or subcon­sciously, when he exulted “let the woods and all the trees of the field praise the Lord?” (D & C 128:23)

It is indeed a plausible theory; but before concluding, it must be noted that in spite of all of the evidence for the bio­logically rejuvenating properties of atmospheric CO2 enrich­ment, many people claiming to be concerned about the envi­ronment are clamoring for reductions in CO2 emissions to the atmosphere. Their basis for concern is the much-publicized prediction—another theory, if you will—that higher atmos­pheric CO2 concentrations will intensify earth's natural “greenhouse effect” and lead to catastrophic global warm­ing.60

Most such people are sincere in this advocacy, believing what they have read in the popular press or seen on televi­sion, both of which news sources typically emphasize the “doomsday” side of such stories; and there is, in fact, enough uncertainty surrounding this particular topic for reasonable and thoughtful people ‑ even scientists, no less ‑‑ to be found on both sides of the issue.61 Many people worry, however, that some of the leaders of the movement to cur­tail CO2 emissions may have a regressive socio political agenda they hope to advance by promoting this as yet unproven theory as fact. Among other things, this agenda has been described by its own proponents as including the development of a centralized “Planetary Management Authority;” designed to cope with what they claim is an unprecedented threat to the biosphere.62 But according to those who have pondered its legal ramifications, this inter­national regulatory body could pose a grave danger to some of our most fundamental freedoms, as it has been portrayed, again by its own advocates, as requiring powers so strong and pervasive that it would even be able to limit the num­ber of children a couple could have, which scenario is not at all far fetched, particularly when it is remembered that many people concerned about environmental problems lay almost all of them at the feet of the world's burgeoning pop­ulation.63,64

The truth of the matter, however, is that the theory of catastrophic CO2‑induced global warming is based on the predictions of computerized climate models that, although incredibly complex and sophisticated, are still in their infan­cy and are as yet unable to adequately represent all of the real‑world processes of significance to global climate change.65-68 As depressing as this state of affairs may be—getting the cart (international energy policy) before the horse (proof of the theory on which it is based)—we can take solace in the fact that all the schemes of man to impede the earth from achieving its divine destiny are doomed to fail; for the Lord has declared that he shall bring to pass the regeneration of the earth and that none shall stay his hand: “Have I not made the earth?” he asks. “Do I not hold the destinies of all the armies of the nations of the earth? Therefore, will I not make solitary places to bud and to blos­som, and to bring forth in abundance? saith the Lord.” (D&C 117:6‑7)

Surely, the Lord will fulfill his word; and the great “greening of the earth” that is presently in progress may well be part of the process of planetary renewal he has set in motion. Our goal, clearly, should be to work in harmony with him, in harmony with truth; but the truth is often ,a dif­ficult commodity to acquire, and to do so on the scientific side of this subject, we must study deeply the many facets of the CO2‑climate controversy, as well as the many results of the numerous botanical CO2 enrichment experiments that have been conducted, to be sure we are on the right side of the issue. For the Lord has told us to be “wise as serpents” (D&C 111:11), and so we should be; for we must ultimately do what the Pharisees and Sadducees could not and answer the question put to them by the Savior. “Ye can discern the face of the sky;” he said, 'but can ye not discern the signs of the times?” (Matt. 16:3)

In conclusion, I leave you with my testimony that God lives, and that he will in very deed, and possibly very soon, completely rejuvenate the biology of this lowly sphere we call the earth. And I leave you with my theory of one pos­sible way that he might do so. I hope it inspires you to believe whole‑heartedly in the words of the prophets and the divinity of this great latter‑day work, wherein the Lord will truly redeem both man and nature alike.
