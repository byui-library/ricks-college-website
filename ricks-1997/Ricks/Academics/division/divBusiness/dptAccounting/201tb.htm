Course Description and Objectives: This introductory course in financial accounting provides a basic foundation for other courses in accounting as well as in many business and information systems courses. Topics covered in this course include the accounting cycle, adjustments, reporting, merchandising, controls, bad debts, notes, inventories, payroll, fixed assets, bonds, and much more. The course is designed to help students develop an understanding of and ability to work with accounting principles. It also will help them to develop an understanding of the accounting cycle and to analyze transactions and apply them to the cycle and to compile financial statements and interpret from the contents. It provides the foundations for advanced work in accounting and business related subjects and a working vocabulary of accounting terminology. It will also help students to gain exposure to the ethical responsibility of accountants and to emphasize writing and interactive skills for accountants.

Homework: The course revolves around the preparation, completion, and explanation of homework. Homework problems are assigned to help students learn and experience the principles covered in the course. Although some memorization is essential, accounting usually has to be experienced before a thorough understanding can be achieved. Be prepared to spend many hours in solving homework problems. Time and effort are just as important as the correct solution.

The exercises and problems that are assigned as homework are found at the end of each chapter in the text. The problems are always from the A set. Homework assignments should be completed prior to the day that they are discussed in class based on the tentative schedule included in this course outline. The homework is due on the day of the quiz given over each chapter. There are 16 homework assignments and 5 points possible for each assignment, regardless of the number of exercises and problems assigned. The lowest homework score will be dropped for grading purposes.

The homework assignments are to be done in pencil using the working papers and should be done very neatly (both form and content are important). The homework will normally be corrected by the students in class. Any corrections made to homework should be done in pen in order to give the instructor a good impression of where mistakes are being made. The homework will not be graded based on accuracy. It will be graded on being complete and timely. Any sloppy, negligent, or incomplete work will receive at least a 1 point deduction.

Quizzes: Quizzes are used as stepping stones in helping prepare students for the exams. There will generally be a quiz after each chapter. Quizzes will be taken and corrected in class. There will be 14 quizzes worth 5 points each. The lowest quiz score will be dropped for grading purposes.

Special Projects: Two of the most common complaints against recent accounting and business graduates is their inability to express themselves in writing and their inability to work in groups or teams. Accountants and other business people are frequently called upon to express opinions and findings in an accurate, clear, thorough, and concise manner. They are also regularly expected to work with a variety of individuals in groups or teams. The special projects in this class are designed to address these areas of concern.

During the first few days of the semester, the instructor will divide you into groups of three or four to work on these special projects. It is expected that all group members will actively participate in their group. The projects will consist of ethics cases, internet activities, research projects, and other assignments. The specific topics, instructions, and due dates of each project will be discussed in class.

The projects will normally require a written paper. These are formal papers and should be treated as such. Logical thought development along with good grammar and spelling are important. You will be given considerable freedom in your approach to the projects. Be creative!!

Late Assignments: Any homework or quiz submitted after the assigned class period will receive a 1 point per-school-day deduction. Special projects will not be accepted if they are late. No homework will be accepted and no quiz can be taken after the test for that material has been administered. Tests may be taken late under extreme extenuating circumstances and with prior permission from the instructor and will receive a 5 point per-school-day deduction
