RICKS ANNOUNCES RECORD SECOND TERM ENROLLMENT

Ricks College has a record 2,282 students attending its second five-week term of summer school. This is a 288-student increase from the 1,994 students who attended last year at the same time. Statistics for the second term show students are taking an average load of 4.6 credits. There are 1,016 freshmen and 1,266 sophomores; 905 males and 1,377 females; 965 from Idaho, 263 from Utah, 180 from California and 145 from Washington.

The numbers reflect the college goal of better utilization of the campus resources year round to enable more students to attend. Three academic calendar tracks and a Fast Track program are currently being offered at Ricks College and will continue to be a part of the new BYU-Idaho. The calendar year will revolve around a three-semester system: Fall, Winter, and Summer.

This year Ricks is offering an eight-week summer session that began June 25 and a third five-week term that will begin July 16. This will be the last summer to include both eight-week sessions and five-week terms. Under the BYU-Idaho program, the summer semester will be divided into two eight-week terms.



RICKS HIRES DIRECTOR FOR NEW INTERNSHIP PROGRAM

Guy Hollingsworth has been named the internship director at Ricks College, a new position reflective of its growing emphasis on internships as it becomes BYU-Idaho. The internship program will be a vital piece of the identity of the four-year institution. The program is designed to give students valuable experience that will complement their studies and increase their marketability.

Most recently Hollingsworth worked as director of recruitment services at Idaho State University in Pocatello after working as assistant director of admissions and scholarships at Ricks College for seven years. Hollingsworth is developing parameters for internships to create a unified program, but he will still leave enough room for departments to find suitable opportunities that are specific to a given discipline. A faculty member from each department will act as internship coordinator who will remain in contact with the interns. Academic criteria will be set so students may receive credit whenever possible.

The internship program will benefit the students, the employer and the school. For the students, it affords an opportunity to get real-life experience in an area of vocational interest. Internships allow employers to hire someone with relatively few strings attached and get viable work done by a student who is eager and excited to make a difference. "The internship program allows our students to be successful, and it allows the reputation of BYU-Idaho to go forth and make a positive mark on society and on the work force," Hollingsworth said.

The internships are also intended to bolster and enhance integrated bachelor's degrees by giving them a broader educational experience. The integrated degrees will be customized to fit students' interests and will become the primary academic offerings at BYU-Idaho. "An internship enriches the integrated majors," Robert Wilkes, vice president of student life, said. "It prepares them for the world of work in a way that is quite helpful to them and to their employers."

"The internship becomes an extended job interview and it's very effective," Wilkes said. One statistic shows that 60 percent of interns are offered a job within the same company.

Internships will be located abroad, nationally, locally and within BYU-Idaho. The internships will generally last for an equivalency of a semester. Hollingsworth will have access to BYU's database of hundreds of companies already participating in their nationally recognized internship program. Businesses interested in getting information specifically about the BYU-Idaho internship program may contact Hollingsworth at HollingsworthG@ricks.edu.



RICKS ORGANIST TO PERFORM SUMMER RECITAL

Rexburg organist Darwin Wolford will perform the first of three free summer organ recitals at Ricks College Thursday, June 28, at 7:30 p.m. in the chapel of the Taylor Building. Wolford, who is director of organ studies at the college, will play three works having to do with water, including the "Water Music Suite" by Handel, "By the Waters of Babylon" by Karg-Elert and "Water Nymphs" by Vierne. He will also will play "Bach Prelude in B minor" and pieces by French composers Widor, Gigout and Franck.

Wolford, who has taught at Ricks since 1967, is a well-known composer. He holds a doctorate from the University of Utah, where he studied organ with Alexander Schreiner and Robert Cundick.

Organist Marsha Bjornn will perform July 27, and adjunct faculty member Rebecca Parkinson of Idaho Falls will perform August 10.
