Ring of Kerry

Iveragh Peninsula, Eire

The "Ring of Kerry" is perhaps the most travelled touring drive in all of Ireland and it's easy to see why. This exquisite country scene taken on the road to Cahersiveen welcomes one to stay awhile and breathe in the Irish experience.
 
 
 

The scenery is spectacular all along the route. This 112 mile ( 180 km approx. ) route around the Iveragh Peninsula, just to the south of the Dingle Peninsula, is certainly well worth journeying along. With such spectacular scenery it is no wonder it is a long-established route.
 

If one begins his/her journey in Killarney he/she will travel along the banks of the river Laune until Killorglin, the home of Puck Fair, and then take the coast road and see the magnificent views of the beach at Glenbeigh and the Blasket Islands at the mouth of Dingle Bay. In Caherciveen it's possible to take the elevator to the top of the former Barracks and see the country side for miles around.
 

Any one of the many stopping points along this magnificent tour makes an idyllic holiday destination. Killorglin, the home of the legendary Puck Fair, is on the banks of the Laune, an excellent salmon-fishing river.
 

When the sun shines there is nothing quite like a walk along Rossbeigh Beach. At Caherciveen you can visit the birthplace of the great justice-seeker and parliamentarian Daniel O'Connell (1775-1847). He eventually settled near the beautiful secluded beaches of Derrynane.

If you have time Valentia Island is well worth while. Waterville and Derrynane are of course known worldwide for having miles of beaches, walks and angling possibilities, as well a wealth of archaeological sites. From here your route takes you to Ireland's most picturesque village, Sneem, and follows the coast road to the beautiful town of Kenmare. Our journey has a grand finale in the drive from Moll's Gap to Killarney, overlooking the entire Killarney valley. (1)
