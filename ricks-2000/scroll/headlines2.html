Tabernacle Choir makes historic visit to BYU-I

by Patricia Selman
sel00001@byui.edu
Scroll Staff

Members of choir perform Saturday night in the Hart Auditorium. .
As part of the celebration of the transition to BYU-Idaho, the Mormon Tabernacle choir performed Saturday and Sunday in the Hart Auditorium. The 154-year-old choir consists of 360 members. It took the choir over five minutes for every member to get in place on the stands.

Lloyd D. Newell, speaker for the choir, welcomed the audience to the choir’s performance.

“We are making history,” Newell said. “[BYU-Idaho] is truly a unique and wonderful institution. We are thrilled to be here.”

It was the first time the choir had sung in Rexburg in over 17 years, Newell said.

Saturday night’s concert began with a fanfare of sparkling bells and voices singing “Praise to the Lord, the Almighty.”

Bells placed sporadically throughout the choir — combined with the full strength of the orchestra — caught the attention of the audience.

“[‘Praise to the Lord, the Almighty’] was my favorite,” Lynn Elsworth, a sophomore from Nyssa, Ore., said. “It was an awesome experience listening to the choir.”

The next songs, labeled as hymns of praise, included “All Creatures of Our God and King.”

“[The Hallelujah Chorus from The Mount of Olives] was my favorite,” Alison Smith, a freshman from San Antonio, Texas, said. “I liked it because of the chords and arpeggios.”

The choir sang two pieces from American composers. “O Magnum Mysterium” was composed by Morten Lauridsen of Los Angeles, and Robert Cundick of Salt Lake City composed “How Beautiful Upon the Mountains,” a song from a larger work titled The Redeemer.

Next the choir performed patriotic pieces.

“When we sing of America, we sing with one voice and one heart,” Newell said in his narration.

Saturday’s program then turned to hymns of faith. Prior to the choir singing “Come, Come Ye Saints,” Newell said, “This song speaks of our gratitude toward our God, our country, and to the pioneers. This beloved LDS hymn represents our struggles and our joy.”

The choir ended the performance by singing “Come Thou Fount of Every Blessing” with BYU-Idaho’s Men’s Choir, Women’s Choir and Collegiate Choir. The crowd erupted into a standing ovation and the choir gave an encore performance including “The Battle Hymn of the Republic,” for which they won a Grammy in 1953.

Sunday’s performance included the choir’s weekly broadcast of Music and the Spoken Word, as well as a post-broadcast mini-concert similar to Saturday night’s.

“This is the best concert I’ve been to on campus,” Rodney Nehring, a freshman from Columbus, Ind., said. “If I had the chance, I would go again.”
