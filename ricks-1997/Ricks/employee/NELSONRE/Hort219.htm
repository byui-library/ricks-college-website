Purpose:  Learn how to think like a landscape manager.  Learn landscape management principles and practices.  Maintenance of established landscapes with emphasis on turfgrass, tress, shrubs, and flowers.

 

Results Which You Can Expect:  When you complete this course, you will be able to successfully determine the maintenance requirements of landscapes and be able to provide quality care practices such as mowing, trimming, pruning, irrigation, fertilization, renovation, and pest control.  You will be able to relate these principles and practices to a commercial landscape maintenance business.  You will be able to relate landscape management principles to your other classes and trade publications, so you can continue to expand you knowledge and skills.

 

Text Recommended:  Turfgrass Management, 5th edition by A.J. Turgeon; Arbiriculture, 3rd edition by Richard Harris.

SUGGESTIONS FOR SHORT REPORTS

1.      Select a magazine, newsletter, or other source suggested by the instructor which pertains to the assigned subject.

2.      Select an article or selection which looks interesting and appropriate.

3.      Scan the material.  Pay particular attention to summaries, synopses, and conclusions.  Read the first and last paragraphs carefully.  Read headings and the first sentence under headings.  This will help you determine if you want to read the entire article carefully or select something else.

4.      Take notes as you read.  If you write something down word for word, put quotes around it.  Be sure to write down the magazine name, article title, author’s name and page numbers.

5.      When writing your report, include as many as possible of the areas listed below.  Even though the author may not specifically include some of them, in most cases you should be able to figure them out.  For example, the author’s purpose should always be apparent, even though he may not state it specifically.  This report should be much more than a summary of the author’s ideas (that is only one of the seven areas).  It is important to include your own reactions, ideas, and applications.  In short, your ideas are an essential part of this report.  The underlined words below would make good headings for your report.

Purpose of the material.  Why did the author write this article?

Source of information.  Author’s personal observation or knowledge, experimentation, reading, etc.?

Summary or Synopsis.  What is it about?  Boil it down to three or four

sentences    or subdivide it into logical parts.  What main ideas or principle is the author trying to convey?

                    New Information.  What did you learn that is significant,  or interesting to you?

helpful to      Conclusions:

              Author’s.  Did he draw conclusions?  What were they?  Do they follow logically

               from his other material?

Your own.  What conclusions can you draw from this material plus your other knowledge and experience?

Applications.  May be the same as conclusions, but not necessarily.  How can you put this into practical use?  How will it help you in your future studies, work, etc.?

References.  What other sources of information on the subject did the author suggest or imply?  If you wanted to obtain more information on this subject, where would you look? 

6.      Short reports should always include the following:

Your name and course title

Identify reading material.  Include author’s name, article name, magazine name, date of publication, page numbers.   

Use headings.  Those listed above or other natural ones suggested from the reading material.

Use quotation marks on any word for word excerpt of more than six words.  If the quotation is from another publication, it should also be identified.  If you rewrite an idea in you own words, do not use quotation marks.  However, you will probably want to give credit to the source of you idea.

Use a typewriter or word processor(computer) for your report.  Check for spelling, punctuation, and grammar errors.  Use a dictionary or the article as reference on spelling, etc.  Use a dictionary or the article as reference on spelling, etc.  Reread final copy before handing in to catch any errors.
