Meningococcal
Infection/Immunization

Because of recent publicity on the incidence and severity of meningococcal infection, many students and their parents have been concerned about this disease on college campuses. We wanted you to know that to date there have been no such infections at BYU-Idaho.

Meningococcus is not common (1.7/100,000 students), but it is more common among students who live in dormitories (3.2/100,000). It can cause meningitis, which is an infection of the membranes of the brain. The consequences of the infection can be serious if not treated. However, there is a vaccine, which is very effective in preventing this infection, and it is available at the BYU-Idaho Student Health Center for those who desire to receive it. The side effects are minimal and the cost is $75.00. Immunization appointments can be made at the Student Health Center by calling (208) 496-1300.

The CDC Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) recommends that immunization should be provided or made easily available to those who wish to reduce their risk of meningococcal disease and that undergraduate college students, particularly freshmen who live in or plan to live in dormitories or resident halls, should consider getting the vaccine. In addition, the American College Health Association and the Federal Advisory Committee on Immunization have recommended that all college students be current on their immunizations, including tetanus, diphtheria, polio, measles, mumps, and rubella (also, hepatitis A for students in medical or food services and those caring for the aged of infirm).

If you would like further information on meningococcal disease and/or prevention, we suggest you refer to the internet at www.cdc.gov or www.acha.org.
