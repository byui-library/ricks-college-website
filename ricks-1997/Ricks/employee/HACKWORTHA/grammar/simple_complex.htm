Simple Sentence
Clauses can be combined in various ways.  But, if a sentence contains only an independent clause -- with no other clause -- it will be a simple sentence.  A simple sentence is an independent clause.  IC = Independent Clause (simple sentence)  
 
The rains washed the dirty grass.   This is a simple sentence.
 
Conjunctions
You have been introduced to seven coordinating conjunctions (and, or, but, for, nor, yet, so).  These will be used in a compound sentence.
 
Compound Sentence
A compound sentence is one containing two or more independent clauses.  These clauses will be joined by a semi-colon (;) or by a coordinating conjunction (and, or, but, for, nor, yet, so).  IC and IC = Compound Sentence
 
Hot mist clouded our view, and thick smoke burned our noses.  This is a compound sentence.
 
Complex Sentence
A complex sentence is a sentence with one independent clause and one or more dependent clauses.  DC, IC = Complex Sentence
 
After the earth became pregnant with spring, she gave birth to a million multi-colored children.  
This is a complex sentence.
 
Sentence Fragment
Sentence fragments are not to be written in freshmen papers.  Although fragments do appear in some professional writings, never use them here.  A fragment is an incomplete thought.  If you write a phrase, and that is all, it will be a fragment.  If you write a dependent clause, and that is all, it will be a fragment.  DC = Sentence Fragment.  Phrase = Sentence Fragment
 
When the stars winked at me.  This is a fragment.
 
The use of the subordinating conjunction, when, makes the above sentence a fragment.  Here the dependent clause needs to be connected to an independent clause in order to form a complete sentence.
 
The sentence can be corrected in two ways:  (1) The stars winked at me.  (2) When the stars winked at me, I felt all would be well.
 
Compound Complex
A compound-complex sentence contains two or more independent clauses and one or more dependent clauses.  DC, IC and IC = Compound-complex sentence
 
Because my wife is kind, intelligent, and beautiful, I love her and I enjoy her happy company.
This is a compound-complex sentence.
