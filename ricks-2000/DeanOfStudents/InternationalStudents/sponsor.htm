SPONSOR INFORMATION

The Affidavit

Each international student is required to provide an affidavit of support. The purpose of the affidavit is:

1. To prove that the applicant has financial resources adequate to provide for his or her expenses.

2. To comply with U.S. Immigration and Naturalization Services standards (INS), that the school be satisfied that funds are guaranteed at least for the student=s first year of study and that, barring unforeseen circumstances, adequate funding will be available from the same or equally dependable sources for subsequent years.

3. To identify the specific sources of financial resource, and define the exact nature and duration of the donations.

Each sponsor indicates the amount of money (U.S. dollars) that they will be contributing and the method of distribution. An attached certified bank statement is required to demonstrate evidence of available resources. If more than one sponsor is being used for a student, each sponsor will complete a separate affidavit of support.

The affidavit must be submitted to the Admissions Office prior to the issuance of the I-20. Newly admitted students will likely be required to present documentary evidence of financial support at the time they apply for a visa, and again when they apply for entry into the United States.

Note to potential Sponsors:   Please understand that by completing the affidavit, you have sworn to provide the support as indicated.  Failure to provide support may lead to the student being forced to stop schooling and return to their home country.

Projected Expenses

Brigham Young University-Idaho defines a reasonable estimate of all expenses B including tuition, fees, books, supplies, maintenance (lodging and food), health insurance, and miscellaneous expenditures (clothing, local travel, recreation, toiletries, telephone, etc. This amount is reviewed annually. Please be aware that the amount is projected for an academic school year (two semester equaling eight months). Additional expenses should be considered for students who:

1. Attend summer school

2. Have classes/programs that require above average fees.

3. Experience unforeseen circumstances.

Books, supplies, & fees: 820                    Books, supplies, & fees: 820
Personal Expenses: 1250                          Personal Expenses: 1250

Total LDS: $8,200                                   Total Non-LDS: $9,440

Married students must also provide evidence of an extra $4000 for their spouse and $2000 for each child who is not a student. The provision for the funding of dependents must also be shown on the affidavit of support.

Although the initial application only requires proof of one year, the sponsor needs to plan for the student=s entire academic studies in the United States. It is unwise to plan on scholarships and employment to provide for funding after the first year. Jobs are scarce, and are usually part-time in nature. International students are limited to on-campus employment only.  Following the first year, off-campus paid internships are available on a limited basis depending on department requirements.  Scholarships are awarded only after completing at least 24 credit hours.

Signing the Affidavit of Support is not just providing a signature to enable a student to enter the United States, assuming the school will take care of them once they arrive. Your signature obligates you to the financial commitments as indicated on the affidavit.  Students whose sponsor cannot meet the financial obligations agreed to may be required to leave school and return to their home country.

The Deposit

All international students, excluding those from Canada are required to pay a financial deposit of $1200 prior to the issuance of an I-20.  The money belongs to the student and can be used by the student to pay for expenses. Should a student decide not to attend  BYU-Idaho after paying the deposit, it the student=s responsibility to contact the Accounting Office to have the deposit returned.

Managing Money

Many of our students are experiencing for their first time the management of money, particularly of a large sum.  There have been unfortunate experiences of a few students who spent their annual account very quickly, leaving little money for the remainder of the year.  This places hardship on the sponsor, as well as the school. Students with unpaid accounts are restricted from registering for further classes, so it is essential to pay for BYU-Idaho related expenses promptly.

Some sponsors request that we assist students in managing their finances. Money can be designated to be placed in an account with specific amounts disbursed per defined periods by the Accounting Office. The International Office gladly assists in counseling students or monitoring expenses if the parent or sponsor request. Students may also place their money in a local bank if desired.

Sponsors must realize that their commitment on the Affidavit of Support is critical to the student=s education in the United States. Failure to provide the student financial assistance as committed does not shift the burden to the College, and the student may need to return home.
