FIRESTONE ENDS ITS 95-YEAR RELATIONSHIP WITH FORD OVER RECENT TIRE RECALL

Ford Motor Co. president and CEO Jacques Nasser, center, discusses the company's plans to recall Firestone tires on their vehicles as Chairman William Clay Ford Jr., left, and Vice President John Rintamaki, right, listen during a news conference at Ford World Headquarters in Dearborn, Mich., Tuesday, May 22, 2001. The company will spend $2.1 billion to replace all remaining 13 million Firestone Wilderness AT tires on its vehicles, twice the number of tires in last summer's massive recall by Bridgestone/Firestone Inc. AP photo archive

by Andrea Scott
scroll staff


Ford Motor Co. is expected to announce that it will replace up to 13 million tires after Firestone said Monday it would end its 95-year business relationship with Ford - the latest developments in the battle over tire-related rollovers that have killed more than 100 people.

Some 6.5 million Firestone tires were recalled last year from Ford's Explorers and Mercury Mountaineers because tread separations and blowouts were linked to 174 U.S. deaths. News reports in recent days have said Ford planned to demand that Firestone conduct another tire replacement program, according to CNN.

Firestone chose to end its nearly business relationship with Ford Motor Co. Monday, insisting Ford is trying to use it as a scapegoat for an unsafe vehicle. Firestone's chief executive John Lampe publicly questioned again the safety of Ford's Explorer vehicle to which most of the tires were fitted, according to Reuters.

Lampe told ABC's Good Morning America program that Ford Motor Co. had "steadfastly refused" to work with Firestone to understand the role of Ford's Explorer vehicles in rollover crashes.

Ford insists the problem is with the tires made by Firestone, a unit of Japan's Bridgestone Corp, according
to Reuters. Little Rock, Ark., attorney C. Tab Turner, the lawyer in 30 lawsuits against Firestone and Ford over the alleged defect, said he had been informed by Firestone that Ford plans to call for the  replacement of 10 million to 13 million Firestone Wilderness AT tires from the company's Wilson, N.C., and Joliet, Quebec, plants.

"This is good news for the consumer," Turner said.

Ford presented Firestone officials with the results of its "review of the latest Firestone real-world data, competitive tire data and exhaustive laboratory and vehicle testing" regarding the Wilderness AT tire Monday, Jacques Nasser, Ford president and chief executive officer, said.

"We are deeply disappointed that upon hearing and seeing this analysis ... Firestone decided not to work together for the safety of our shared customers, which is the only issue that matters," Nasser said.

With the announcement of more tire recalls, auto industry watchers said Ford Motor Co. has taken a major hit in confidence and prestige, according to CNN.
