SKIMMING AND SCANNING

SKIMMING

SCANNING

DEFINITION

 Reading for the general idea or overview.

Reading key parts at normal speed and skipping the rest.

NOT reading everything.

 Selective skipping.

Looking for one thing in particular.

HOW TO

 1. Read the introduction carefully, at normal speed.

2. Read the first sentence of each paragraph until reaching the conclusion.

3. Read the conclusion thoroughly at normal speed.

When reading difficult material for the first time:

1. Skim the chapter to get the main idea.

2. Attend the lecture and listen for important details.

3. Actively read the entire chapter carefully (marking and taking notes).

 1. Clearly fix in your mind the information for which you are scanning.

2. Use headings or subheadings to find quickly the part of the page (or chapter or book) that probably contains your information.

3. Run through material at high speed until the answer is located. Look for synonyms, or alternate ways your information might be worded ("whales" might be listed under "marine life," "oceanography," or "mammals").

4. Stop and fixate.

5. Read only what is needed.
