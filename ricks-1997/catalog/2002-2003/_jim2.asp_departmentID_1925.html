    Computer  Science  is  "the  study  of  computers,  including  their  design,  operation,  and  use  in  processing  information.  Computer  Science  combines  both  theoretical  and  practical  aspects  of  engineering,  electronics,  information  theory,  mathematics,  logic,  and  human  behavior.  Aspects  of  Computer  Science  range  from  programming  and  computer  architecture  to  artificial  intelligence  and  robotics."  (Microsoft  Press  Computer  and  Internet  Dictionary)  The  Computer  Science  curriculum  teaches  students  algorithms  and  problem  solving,  software  development,  aspects  of  computer  hardware,  the  theory  underlying  computing,  and  ethics. 

    Students  interested  in  creating  computer-based  solutions  to  problems  are  attracted  to  Computer  Science.  Students  with  an  aptitude  for  mathematics  are  usually  successful  in  Computer  Science. 

    Students  graduating  with  a  bachelors  degree  in  Computer  Science  are  prepared  to  enter  the  work  force  and  develop  software  applications  in  any  industry.  Currently  there  is  a  significant  shortage  of  software  developers;  the  quantity  of  unfilled  positions  for  developers  is  expected  to  continue  to  increase  over  time.  Students  may  also  choose  to  continue  their  education  in  graduate  school  and  research  some  of  the  many  facets  of  Computer  Science.  Regardless,  Computer  Science  majors  will  have  a  challenging  and  rewarding  career.

    Course Descriptions

    CS 100 Computer Basics (1-2:0:0)
    A pass/fail course that requires passing a two-part exam. The exam includes objective-type questions and performance section where the student uses computer applications to produce a final product. The topics in the exam will include the basic computer operating system, word processing, spreadsheet, and Internet skills. If desired, a student can prepare for the exam by completing self-paced tutorials that will be provided over the Internet. No class time is required, and the exam may be taken at any time during the semester. This course fills the online learning requirement.
     
    CS 102 Introduction to Computer Science (1:1:0)
    Prerequisite: High School Algebra
    Provides information to help a student determine if he/she would like to major in Computer Science. Basic concepts of Computer Science, software engineering, and software development.
     
    CS 104 Ethics I (0.5:1:0)
    Survey of issues regarding ethics in Computer Science. This is the first in a series of four Computer Science ethics courses.
     
    CS 124 Introduction to Computer Systems (3:3:0)
    How a computer works from hardware to high-level programming. Logic circuits, computer instructions, assembly language, binary arithmetic, C++ programming.
     
    CS 144 Introduction to Software Development (3:3:0)
    Prerequisite: Math 110 - College Algebra (may be taken concurrently)
    Algorithm formulation, problem solving, fundamental data types. Procedural paradigm of software development. Structured programming. Introduction to objects.
     
    CS 145 Software Design and Development (3:3:0)
    Prerequisite: CS 144 - Introduction to Software Development
    Introduction to software engineering principles with emphasis on design. Object oriented paradigm of design and development.
     
    CS 148 Computer Science Review (1:1:0)
    Prerequisite: CS 144(or CS 165, or CS 170, or CS 205, or CS 206) and CS 145 (or CS 171, or CS 246)
    Review of CS 144 and CS 145 concepts for those who have not been taking Computer Science classes for an extended period of time.
     
    CS 165 Object Oriented Software Development (3:3:0)
    Prerequisite: CS 144 (Introduction to Programming), or CS 124, Math 110
    Software design and development using an object-oriented language. Algorithm formulation, an introduction to software engineering, and object-oriented programming.
     
    CS 204 Ethics II (0.5:0:0)
    Prerequisite: CS 104
    Overview of intellectual property rights relative to Computer Science.
     
    CS 224 FUNDAMENTALS OF DIGITAL SYSTEMS (3:3:0)
    Prerequisite: CS 124
    See CompE 224
     
    CS 233 INTRO TO COMPUTER ORGANIZATION (3:3:0)
    Prerequisite: CS 144
    Fundamentals of computer organization and operation. Memory structure, registers, arithmetic and logical function, instruction formats, addressing modes, machine and assembly languages, and internal-external data representation.
     
    CS 235 Data Structure (3:3:0)
    Prerequisite: CS 165
    Abstraction, recursion, introduction to algorithm analysis, list structures, binary trees, sorting and graphs.
     
    CS 236 Discrete Mathematics (3:3:0)
    Prerequisite: CS 165 and Math 112
    Discrete mathematics for computer science. Introduces the mathematical topics related to computer science focusing on providing a solid foundation for further courses.
     
    CS 273 Web Engineering (3:3:0)
    Prerequisite: CS 144 (Introduction to Doftware Development) or CS 165 (Object Oriented Programming) or CS 170 (Introduction to Software Development) Familiarity with the World Wide Web, Web browsers, and e-mail is assumed. Familiarity with Linux is assumed.
    Internet and Web overview. Introduction to human-computer interaction. Information architectures. Client-server computing with client-side and server-side programming.
     
    CS 290 Special Topics (1-3:0:0)
    Prerequisite: Consent of Instructor.
    Faculty/student consultation will determine an area of study/research that will give an advanced student greater appreciation and experience in this field. Terms of enrollment, credit, etc. will be determined by the instructor.
     
    CS 304 Ethics III (0.5:0:0)
    Prerequisite: CS 204
    Overview of privacy and confidentiality issues relative to Computer Science.
     
    CS 306 Algorithms and Complexity (3:3:0)
    Prerequisite: CS 236
    Theory and application of languages and algorithms. Topics include relational data model, graph theory, trees, automata, languages and grammars, parsing technques.
     
    CS 324 Computer Architecture (3:3:0)
    Prerequisite: CompE 224
    See CompE 324
     
    CS 330 Programming Languages (3:3:0)
    Major features of common programming languages; various types of languages including procedural, functional, and object-oriented; langauge translation including scanning and parsing techniques.
     
    CS 345 Operating Systems (3:3:0)
    Prerequisite: CS 235 CS 324
    Analysis and design of methods used by operating systems to perform typical system services; design and implementation of file and directory systems; I/O methods, including prorammed, interrupt-driven, and DMA; CPU scheduling; memory management techniques and implementations; concurrent programming; deadlocks; protection mechanisms; distributed systems.
     
    CS 346 Software Design and Development (3:3:0)
    Advance object oriented design and software development.
     
    CS 371 Human-Computer Interaction (3:3:0)
    Prerequisite: CS 273
    Overview of human-comptuer interaction theories and methods. Graphical user interface design, user-centered design, usability engineering, user pyschology, and the nature of human-computer interaction.
     
    CS 404 Ethics IV (0.5:0:0)
    Prerequisite: CS 304
    Overview of "harm to other" issues relative to Computer Science
     
    CS 410 Compilers (3:3:0)
    Prerequisite: CS 306
    Principles and practices in the design and development of programming languge compilers.
     
    CS 426 Software Engineering I (3:3:0)
    Prerequisite: CS 346
    Software requirements and design engineering.
     
    CS 427 Software Engineering II (3:3:0)
    Prerequisite: CS 426
    Software quality engineering and metrics.
     
    CS 428 Software Engineering III (3:3:0)
    Prerequisite: CS 426
    Software process management. Software project management.
     
    CS 452 Database Systems (3:3:0)
    Prerequisite: CS 306
    Study of database design and implementation; comparison of basic models (entity-relationship, hierarchical, network, relational); study of query languages; discussion of issues of integrity, security, dependencies, and normal forms.
     
    CS 460 Computer Communication and Networks (3:3:0)
    Prerequisite: CS 306 and CS 345
    Introduction to data communications and networking. Communications fundamentals, computer networks, software, architecture, telecommunications, regulations, standards.
     
    CS 490 Special Topics (3:3:0)
    Prerequisite: Consent of Instructor
    Current topics in Computer Science
     
    CS 498 Internship (1-4:0:0)
    Planned and supervised practical experience in vocational or educational settings. Interns acquire practical skills while applying classroom theory and principles.
     
    CS 499 Senior Project (3:1:2)
    Prerequisite: CS 427
    Directed research and study of a topic in Computer Science not covered by the curriculum. The topic shall be such that the student shall apply material covered by the curriculum to understand a new topic.
