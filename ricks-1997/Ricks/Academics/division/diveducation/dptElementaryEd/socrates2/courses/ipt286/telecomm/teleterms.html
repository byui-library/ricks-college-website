Telecommunications Basic Terms

Appletalk - A software protocol (communications rules) that allow Macintosh computers to share date over a network. Appletalk is built into every Macintosh computer.

Bandwidth - The capacity of a network medium (cable) to carry data. Usually the data is conveyed using differing frequencies or channels over the cable. The broader the bandwidth the greater the capacity to convey data.

Baud - A term used to refer to the speed at which data is sent from one device to another in bits. This term is being replaced with "BPS" (bits per second). Usually baud or bps are used when referring to a modem's speed.

Binary - The type of mathematics (base 2) used by computers to process all data, comprised of only two digits, 0 and 1.

Digital - Refers to the type of data that computers process. It is comprised of binary digits, 0 and 1 called "bits".

Download - To move data from a remote computer to a local computer work station.

Electronic Mail - A messaging system that permits users to send and receive electronic mail over a network. Key elements of the "e-mail" are the senders address, receivers address, subject line, and message.

Ethertalk - The protocols used on Macintosh computers that allow them to communicate on an Ethernet network.

Fax - Abbreviation for Facsimile Terminal. A device that transmits a picture copy of a document over telephone lines to another fax.

Fiber Optics - A data transmission cable made of specially processed glass. Data is transmitted via light pulses over fiber optic cable at very large bandwidths (14 million bps).

Hardware - Refers to the physical devices used that make up a computer work station and network.

Hypermedia - Software programs that allow for user directed, non-linear, exploration and use.

Interactive media - Software programs that respond to the user. Good examples of such software would be new generation computer games,and artificial intelligence programs.

Internet - A network of networks. The internet is comprised of many servers (estimates range up to 40+ million) that can be accessed by users via modem or direct connect. One of the most popular elements of the internet is the World Wide Web.

Local Area Network - More commonly known as a LAN. It is a computer network, usually contained in a limited geographical area that allows computer work stations to share data and resources.

Modem - Literally, a modulator / demodulator that translates a digital signal from the computer to an analog signal that can be transmitted via telephone lines and back again. Basic hardware to a telecommunications system.

National Information Infrastructure - The U.S. Government's name for the internet as it operates within the United States.

Network - A system of computers, servers, and other devices that are connected via cables, and use a common protocol to communicate and share resources.
On-line - Refers to establishing a maintaining a network connection between a local work station and a remote work station. Most commonly used to a person who is accessing the internet.

Open-network architecture - An allowance by a computer manufacturer to share the process of how their hardware and software operates, with the intent to allow others to build devices that will work with the original.

Telecommuting - Through the use of a computer and modem, a system whereby an employee is connected to a company's network and can perform his or her work at home, without having to travel to work.

Teleconference - A means whereby individuals located in different physical locations can communicate simultaneously. The media that allow teleconferencing may include computers, microwave video transmissions, or telephone conference calls.

Twisted Pair - A wire comprised of two smaller wires twisted around one another - each with its own protective cover - then encased in another protective cover. Commonly used as telephone wire and in LAN's. Twisted pair is also commonly called 10base-T wire.

Upload - A term used to indicate the transfer of data from a local work station to a remote server or work station.

Wide-area Network - Similar to a LAN; however, WAN's are usually spread over a wide geographical area, and may include microwave or dedicated telephone trunk lines.

World Wide Web - A popular element of the internet. The WWW is comprised of servers that distribute documents known as "Home Pages" via HTTP (Hyper Text Transfer Protocols). This is the fastest growing element on the internet.
