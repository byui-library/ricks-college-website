Thesis   Support
 
Writing is composed of two parts: thought and structure.  The thought is what you say and the structure is how you say it, how you relate one idea to another.  
 
If writing is good, it contains both worthwhile ideas and a worthwhile, conscious, supportive structure.  Structure in writing adds clarity and focus to the ideas.  It helps guide a writer, keeping his or her task within certain bounds and conditions.  Structure, therefore, helps the writer gain control.
 
With writing, an outline provides structure.  An outline is to writing what daily planning is to our lives.  An outline (structure) is to the paper what our skeleton is to our body. Once a basic structure is defined, the writer should flesh out his paper with meaty examples, concepts, anecdotes, or facts.
 
The writing structure you will practice for some of your papers is thesis support.  The thesis is the main point of your paper, a summary sentence, and the support includes the reasons you believe this main point.  For example, here are some possible thesis statements:
 
1. My father is one of the most honest men I know.
 
2. My mother, Dorothy South, inspires me for several reasons.
 
3. I conquered several challenges when I
a. worked in South Texas.
b. lived with my step father last summer.
c. participated in high school basketball.
 
4. Working at McDonald's last summer, I learned the value of dependability.
 
5. Of all the creeps I know, Willie Hotdog offends me the most.
 
Once you have written a thesis, ask, "Why do I believe this thesis?"  List the reasons.  For example, why does your mother inspire you?  The reasons become your topic sentences.
 
Here is an example:
 
Thesis: For several reason, I am continually inspired by my mother, Dorothy South.
 
1. because of the way she treats my father.
 
2. because of the way she supports me in my school activities.
 
3. because of her success as a real estate broker.
You have now observed what is meant by thesis-support structure.  Once you have an outline, use free writing to complete the introduction, details, examples, and conclusion of your paper.

The Miracle of the Mind
 
Someone has said that to understand the atom is child's play compared to understanding the play of a child. This is true if we mean that to understand the play of a child one must understand the functioning of a child's brain. Today, more than at any time in history, the matter of the mind really matters. The human brain is a subject of research by many universities throughout the world. This research centers around three areas.
 
The first area deals with the relationship of brain chemistry to memory. It is believed that the chemistry of learning partly relates to RNA, that is, ribonucleic acid. It is theorized that learning alters the structure of some molecules. If RNA from the brain of a trained animal is transferred to an untrained animal, the untrained animal quickly learns what the trained animal already knows. Also, drugs which block the RNA molecule in the brain cause fish to suffer from a loss of memory. Additionally, in experiments performed by Ed Ewing Cameron, the injection of one drug, magnesium panalien, caused a man to retain powers of memory. This man was given a set of geometric figures and asked to reproduce them. Between his pre and post drug period, there was marked memory improve-ment. It is hoped that such research can eventually help restore memory for the aged. It would be good to couple the wisdom of age with the metal flexibility of youth.
 
A second area of research deals with control of the mind through electronic stimulation. For example, Dr. Degatto of Yale University implanted electrodes into the brain of a bull. By manipulation of the electrical stimulation sent electronically from Dr. Degatto to the brain of the bull, the doctor was able to control the bull's behavior. The same technique was used with monkeys. Their aggressiveness, passivity and appetite were all controlled electronically by external experimenters. Among other things it is hoped that such research will lead to an effective treatment of epilepsy.
 
A third area of research relates to helping man understand, alter, and/or control his own mind. Dr. Joe Kammiya used a EKG machine to help people produce their own alpha brain waves at will. Through feedback techniques, people are able to do this within one to twenty sessions. Eventually it is hoped that man can learn to control his own moods and creativity.
 
Because of the research being done in the above areas, some scientists think that learning will be supplemented and augmented by a pill, that one will eventually strengthen one's IQ with chemicals, and that our attention span will eventually be expanded or contracted by a drug. Scientist are continually discovering more about the chemical-electrical makeup of the brain. Perhaps someday we will take a pill to learn Spanish 101.
 
Grizzly
 
Because we live in a tame, cultivated community, we sometimes forget or are unaware of the primitive, natural beauties that rest only a few miles to the west, east, north, and southeast of Rexburg.  Particularly to the north is the land of the Yellowstone, a world virgin, rugged, and beautiful. This land of the evergreen, full of granite, towering mountains and plunging mammoth canyons, is home to the deer, elk, big horn sheep, moose, and buffalo.
 
It is also a home for the great North American grizzly. This animal, feared and worshiped by earlier Indians, has provided data for an eight year study conducted by two ecologist, John and Frank Craighead. Because of this study, the Craigheads have learned some interesting facts about the grizzly.
 
By mounting a collar containing an electronic transmitter onto the necks of selected grizzlies, the researchers were able to determine information about the bear's summer and winter behaviors. During the summer each adult grizzly uses approximately twenty square miles of range. This range must be able to provide adequate wild game and water for these voracious carnivores.
 
The bears' winter range, their site for hibernation, is usually miles away from their summer range. The instinct to hibernate is triggered by the cold temperature. However, the bears will only go into their dens just before or during a snowstorm. This way their tracks are always covered and the location of their dens always remains a secret. The entrance to the den faces north, perhaps to insure that the snow will adequately seal the doorway. During hibernation the new cub or cubs will be born.
 
The new cubs will grow to have impressive characteristics. Obtaining approximately 100 pounds the first year, the full grown adult may weight as much as half a ton. Yet in spite of the grizzly's great bulk, this unique animal is as fast as a horse, as quick as a cat, and as strong as an ox. The cubs will nurse for a year. During this time the mother will not accept another male. Although the father will have nothing to do with raising the cub, the mother remains protective, doting, even tender.
 
As we live quietly in our warm, well-lighted homes, we do forget the beauty and magnificence of the natural world that borders our valley. Within this world lives the grizzly, a particularly interesting animal.
 
Thesis Support Practice
 
Your are now to write a thesis-support paper. Pick any topic but make sure the topic is one worthy of your time.  Hopefully, write a paper that will be included in your life story.  Write something personal, something honest.  Evaluate your paper based on the following checklist:
 
Thesis Support Checklist
 
1.  Thesis: location is the last sentence of the first paragraph, simple or complex  (not compound), worthwhile subject.
 
2.  First paragraph flows from general to specific and has AT LEAST five sentences.
 
3.  Each topic sentence directly supports the thesis.
 
4.  The paragraphs of the body have concrete, specific examples. You give the reader a "once."
 
5.  The paper is accurate (spelling, punctuation, grammar).
 
6.  Transitions occur between paragraphs.
 
7.  The last paragraph contains a conclusion. This conclusion is a re-statement of the thesis, only in different words.
 
8.  The paper is at least 600 words.
