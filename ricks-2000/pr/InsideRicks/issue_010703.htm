RICKS HOSTS THE 25TH ANNUAL NO- TILL OR DIRECT SEED FIELD DAY

Ricks College will host its 25th Annual No- Till or Direct Seed Field Day Thursday, July 12, from 9 a.m. to 12:30 p.m. The day will begin at the Ag Engineering Building on the south end of the Ricks campus for area farmers and other interested parties. The tour will feature college demonstration plots as well as cover other topics including wild oat and broadleaf weed control, wild rye suppression and control, fall and spring wheat varieties, triticale and barley varieties, drip irrigation and the frustration of ground hogs, and the herbicides Discover, Everest and Roundup.

Hosting the field day will be John D. Walker, Greg Blaser, Blake Willis and Larry Stephens. Walker was hired in 1976 to create a crops and soils program modeled after a successful forage and farm crop department he helped initiate at Ohio State University 's Ag Technical Institute. Walker says, "We have developed this program to offer something for everyone. We will cater information for both dry land as well as irrigation farms in the region. "

A free luncheon will be served at the conclusion of the field day compliments of the Ricks Agriculture Engineering Department.


SECOND SUMMER TERM ENROLLMENT SETS NEW RECORD

Ricks College has a record 2,282 students attending its second five- week term of summer school. This is a 288- student increase from the 1,994 students who attended last year at the same time. Statistics for the second term show students are taking an average load of 4.6 credits. There are 1,016 freshmen and 1,266 sophomores; 905 males and 1,377 females; 965 from Idaho, 263 from Utah, 180 from California and 145 from Washington.

The numbers reflect the college goal of better utilization of the campus resources year round to enable more students to attend. Three academic calendar tracks and a Fast Track program are currently being offered at Ricks College and will continue to be a part of the new BYU- Idaho. The calendar year will revolve around a three- semester system: Fall, Winter, and Summer.

This year Ricks is offering an eight- week summer session that began June 25 and a third five- week term that will begin July 16. This will be the last summer to include both eight- week sessions and five- week terms. Under the BYU- Idaho program, the summer semester will be divided into two eight- week terms.


LONGTIME EMPLOYEE DAN BLACK NAMED DIRECTOR OF FOOD SERVICES

The high quality of cuisine offered on campus is a Ricks College tradition. Now longtime employee Dan Black will maintain and enhance that legacy at BYU- Idaho having been named director of Food Services. Black, who has been serving as interim director since April, has worked for Food Services for 35 years. He began his career working part time at Quality Bakery in downtown Burley while in high school. At age 23, he came to Ricks to be the baker when the Manwaring Center first opened in 1966. At the time, it was the only bakery in Rexburg. "I was planning on getting my associate degree and going on," he says of coming to Ricks. "I liked it well enough that I'm still here."

He worked as the baker for about 15 years before moving into management. He served as assistant director for several years, working both with past directors Sherrel Davis and John Soules. Food Services employs about 45 full- time people and approximately 250 students each semester. During the school year, it serves about 2,500 meals a day. It operates a student cafeteria called The Galley, Nordic Landing, Viking Place, Thor's Court, Traditions Catering and vending services.


RICKS PLAY IS NEW TWIST ON STORY ABOUT CINDERELLA

Ricks College presents "This Castle Needs a Good Scouring," a new twist on the Cinderella story. Running July 5- 7 and 12- 14 in the Snow Drama Theater , the parody was written by drama faculty member Omar Hansen in 1991, with the music written by his brother John Hansen, a former Ricks faculty member.

The play won the Great Platte River Playwright Festival in 1992. It was then produced by the University of Nebraska for the following two years. It has been produced several times since elsewhere. This will be the first time the play has been presented at Ricks. Tickets are $6 for the general public from the Ticket Office (208) 356- 2230 or www.ricks.edu/tickets or at the door.

The production offers a new perspective of the traditional tale. The story follows the manipulation of trying to get the characters married. The musical is set in late medieval times with a Shakespearean faced set. Hansen praises the costumes and the actors. "I have a wonderful cast . . . people who have fleshed out their characters and that are fun to watch." Hansen says.


MONTANA GROUP TO RETURNS FOR ENCORE SHOW

The Montana singing group Opus IV returns for an encore performance Thursday, July 12, in the Barrus Concert Hall at 7:30 p.m. The program features music, comedy, and Broadway interwoven with classical selections by the world's great composers.

Tickets are $3 for general admission and available from the Ticket Office or at the door. Call 356- 2230 or order online at www.ricks.edu/tickets .

The two married couples met while singing with the Montana Chorale and now are Montana's only professional vocal quartet. James Aiken and Nita Hamilton live in Missoula while Robert Worobec and Linda Terry live in Livingston.

They are joined by accompanist Kenneth Christensen, a piano instructor at Montana State University in Bozeman. He has served for many years as pianist for the Intermountain Opera Association. He is an accomplished ragtime pianist and for eight years has appeared as pianist and musical director of the Virginia City Players production of The Brewery Follies.
