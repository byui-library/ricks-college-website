ELDER BEDNAR SPEAKS AT DEVOTIONAL

"Why are we here on the earth?" is a question we all need to ponder according to Elder David A. Bednar in his address to the students and faculty at the opening devotional assembly of the winter semester. Like many gospel concepts, the answer can be both profound and simple. Elder Bednar said a basic answer would be "to receive a physical body, to live by faith rather than sight, and to be tested." He then focused closely on the importance of obtaining and reverencing the physical body. The body "affects everything we do - what we think, how we act, where we go, what we eat, what we drink, and what we wear and how we look."

Elder Bednar explained that "obtaining a tabernacle of flesh is an essential step in the process of becoming like our Heavenly Father." The ability to create life and establish an eternal family is a sacred power. "An essential part of the test of mortality is having and properly using a physical body"

A knowledge and understanding of principles is essential. Using scriptures and quotations from latter-day prophets and apostles, Elder Bednar taught that "our bodies are not our own." Identifying our physical bodies as temples of God leads to an understanding of the importance of "what we take into our temple," "what we put on our temple," "what we do to our temple," and "what we do with our temple." "Our physical body is a marvelous blessing and a timeless trust. The most sacred of all our divine powers is to become a co-creator with Heavenly Father in providing physical bodies for His spirit sons and daughters and in establishing a righteous and Christ-centered family. Nothing is more holy; nothing deserves more reverence; nothing is more central to the plan of happiness. And our very souls are at stake," said Bednar. (The complete text of Elder Bednar's devotional message is available at http://www.byui.edu/Ricks/devotionals.)

SACRED MUSIC SERIES IMPACT CONTINUES

Every other year, Ricks College commissions a significant composition for its Sacred Music Series. Student vocalists and instrumentalists are able to work with masterful composers and share in the excitement of presenting new music that inspires audiences with its Christ-centered messages. These works are premiered and recorded on the Ricks College campus. Tapes and videos of presentation in 1997, 1995, 1993, and 1991 are available through the Ricks College Music Department. For the first time, the 1999 oratorio is more readily available through Deseret Book in video and CD formats. This special arrangement is due in part to Deseret Book's ongoing working relationship with Merrill Jenson, the commissioned composer who wrote "Come Unto Christ." As the composer, Jenson maintains the copyrights and has several options on what to do next with his composition. As he promotes his work, he also shares the unique experience of creating the oratorio and working with the Ricks College musicians and faculty. Deseret News recently reported on the impact of "Come Unto Christ" and Jenson's ongoing creativity. (See http://deseretnews.com/dn/view/0%2C1249%2C145011980%2C00.html? )

Ricks College has commissioned Daniel Gawthrop to write a composition for presentation in 2001. Gawthrop is a well-known composer who for years was an announcer/producer for public radio station WETA in Washington, D.C. He has served as a music critic for the Washington Post. He is a resident of Virginia and is known for his musical lyricism and poetic texts. He has twice served on NEA grant panels and is currently teaching at the Shenandoah Conservatory in Winchester, Virginia.

Those involved with the Sacred Music Series are grateful to the composers who give birth to new creations, to dedicated faculty who add direction and expression to the compositions, to talented students who reach to new heights of ability, and to the many generous donors who make it financially possible to share the joy of inspired sacred music well performed.

"HOLD TO THE IRON ROD"

One by one, students are gaining new insights into developing stronger commitment to gospel principles through "Hold to the Iron Rod." Student Life Vice President Bob Wilkes sees a "healthy and growing" initiative. From its basic beginnings in the fall of 1999, over 400 students have volunteered to be trained as teachers. They, in turn, have passed along the messages to their peers.

Students are encouraged to act on their own initiative, at their own discretion. They experiment with the words of Elder Boyd K. Packer who taught, "True doctrine, understood, changes attitudes and behavior." By learning why the doctrine is important and how the principles apply to their personal situations, they come to discover how they can implement gospel teachings to their daily lives.

The office of Special Assistant Vice President Gerald Price serves as the resource center for presentation materials. Price is mentor to student Jeremy Jackson as they develop additional student leaders. Jackson coordinates weekly training sessions. Any student interested is welcome to attend. Jackson finds the experience of teaching others invigorating. He said, "One thing I have noticed, that each time I teach it seems like the discussion is swayed to a different area depending on the people. It is exciting because the spirit is so strong and the message gives hope and strength." "The 'Hold to the Iron Rod' initiative is student driven and student taught," said Jackson. "We focus first on the need to know for ourselves and then to go teach others." They apply the guiding principle given by President David A. Bednar that "everyone at Ricks College is a teacher."

After attending a training session, those interested may check out presentation material to share with others. Students sign up on the waiting list for their opportunity to take home one of the limited number of packets available. The initiative's goal is to have a ripple effect that will impact the whole campus. Price promises, "We will respond to the student requests and needs."

THOR SCORES HIGH HIMSELF

As the Ricks College mascot, Thor is expert at generating cheers for the athletic teams. This week is time for a grand applause for Thor himself who won the respect of the judges and crowds at the national Mascot Championships in Florida this last weekend. Thor was invited to attend and was the only junior college representative. He was among the youngest mascots there. The seventeen universities in the competition came from throughout the United States. Thor came away with seventh place. The University of Tennessee took first place. Most people attending the championships had never heard of Ricks College. Thor did what he does so well - draw attention and build enthusiasm, thus spreading the goodwill of Ricks College. He gained the affection of the crowd and captured the interest of ESPN and Disney recruiters. He returned to Rexburg with a large trophy for his efforts. Even though the real identity behind the mascot image will remain a mystery until the end of the athletic season, we extend a mighty three cheers to Thor! Hip- hip- hooray!
