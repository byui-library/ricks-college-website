BYU-Idaho Internship Philosophy

BYU-Idaho is rethinking education....We realized that a key to the success of our business program is to stay in close contact with the professional environment, and to require our students to prove themselves on the real business playing field of battle prior to graduation.

Professional work experience is not an option in our program, but a requirement for all graduating business management students. It is the ultimate application of classroom learning before graduation.

We invite the business world to join with us as partners in education. Through on the job applications and supervision, students will become refined and primed for graduation. With company evaluations and feedback, our business program can stay abreast of current business recruiting expectations and become more aware of how to improve our classroom preparation for graduating, qualified business managers. Businesses can screen future employees, take advantage of low cost government subsidized internship wages, and use educational facilities for research and problem solving. All parties can become business partners in a more beneficial way for students, industry, and education.

As we teamwork in refining the educational process, the university, the workplace, and most of all the students will all be winners in the process. Internship work experience is definitely a WIN-WIN-WIN partnership proposition.

Beginning Work Experience Internship - B298

Questions & Answers

Question: What is a beginning work experience internship? (B298)

Answer: If you are working in any business related job while going to school or during any
summer prior to the completion of your junior year, you can earn from one to five
credits depending upon how many hours you work. (One credit = 90 hours of
professional work)

Question: What type of credit?

Answer: Generally this type of credit is elective credit. (For the two-year program in business
this credit can fulfill the three-credit specialization class requirement.)

Question: What are the requirements of the class?

Answer: There are no formal classes scheduled, all work is done on-the-job and at home.
The requirements are:

a. 90 hours of work per credit earned with back-up time-cards or work logs
required.
b. Two work evaluations per semester by supervisors.
c. One personal work self-evaluation.
d. One evaluation done by the BYU-I Internship Director. (on-site, or by phone)
e. Two written professional learning reports (2-3 pages double spaced) per credit
earned.
f. A BYU-I work contract, completed and signed by you, your employer, and
the BYU-I Internship Director

Question: Why would I want to take this elective class? What is the value?

Answer: Internships have become a "prized" accomplishment to show on your transcript.
College recruiters have made them valuable by showing preference to students who have completed internships and even offering them higher beginning pay upon graduation. In a recent survey done by the University of Indiana the results showed that students who completed internships are recruited at a salary offering 8.9% higher than those who have not. Many college counselors encourage students to complete as many internships as they can prior to graduation. If you are working in a related business area, it makes sense to validate your experience by earning credits/grade to enhance your recruiting strategies.

Question: What must I do to enroll? Are there any prerequisites?

Answer: No prerequisites to sign up on the internet, but you must visit with the Internship
Director in Smith 261B and pick up an "Internship Packet" and a "BYU-I Internship Contract" to be completed as part of the class assignments. The packet will give you all of your assignments, and the contract must be signed by all parties involved and placed on file in our offices.

Questions: Who do I see to find out if my job qualifies or to ask more questions?

Answer: Brother Kenton Anderson, Smith 231B, 496-1427, E-mail: andersonkc@byui.edu

 

Advanced Internship Requirement - B398

Questions & Answers

Question: Who is required to complete the Advanced Professional Internship (B398)?

Answer: All four-year majors in the Business Management Program.

Question: Why am I required to complete an internship? My parents never had to complete this type of class.

Answer: There are three major reasons why the Business Management Department require students to have an internship work experience prior to graduation:

1. For the student's benefit: Internships have become a "prized" accomplishment to show on your transcript. College recruiters have made them valuable by showing preference to students who have completed internships and even offering them higher beginning pay upon graduation. In a recent survey done by the University of Indiana the results showed that students who completed internships are recruited at a salary offering 8.9% higher than those who have not. Many college counselors encourage students to complete as many internships as they can prior to graduation. As a student gains experience on the job, he/she will internalize those business concepts taught in the classroom and gain greater confidence, vision, direction, and professional maturity that will help them start a successful career.

2. Business Demand: Businesses are using internships as a screening device to save time and money. Businesses feel that if a student has proven him/herself on the internship field of battle, their chances of becoming successful in a real job are greatly enhanced. Since many companies spend from 50%-100% of a first year salary in recruiting new management employees, the internship has emerged as a primary cost-saving screening tool that will ensure a much higher rate of success. Another aspect of this is the company who only hires beginning full-time management from the pool of past company interns screened on the job.

3. University Benefit: By working with a multitude of internship sponsoring companies, the business department can stay abreast of the on-going current demands of business. This will benefit each student by incorporating in our classroom instruction the current skills and business training expectations of the business world.

Question: When can I complete my Advanced Internship (B398)?

Answer: Only students who have completed at least four classes in the junior core, and the internship pre-class orientation sessions/assignments can enroll for the Advanced Internship B398 (Exceptions must be approved by the Business Management Department Chair). It is strongly suggested that students complete the advanced internship during the summer between their junior and senior year. This will give them power/experience in their senior year to use the internship to maximum classroom advantage.

Question: What are the internship "pre-class orientation sessions and assignments all about?

Answer: Part of the class advanced internship requirement starts in your junior year, prior to the internship class enrollment. Four orientation sessions will be announced (usually held at 2 p.m. on one Thursday per month.), related to job-getting skills and networking. Assignments will be given at these sessions pertaining to the preparation of a personal "Job Portfolio," and job interview training. You must competently pass a mock job interview with the Internship Director to qualify for an advance internship.

Question: How do I register for the class?

Answer: You will register for the class during the last pre-class orientation session or in the Internship Director's office.

Question: What materials are required?

Answer: An internship work packet including the required company agreement contracts etc., will be given to you during the orientation sessions, or can be picked up at the internship office in Smith 231B.

Question: How many credits must I take, and is the class given a letter grade?

Answer: The Advanced Internship class is 3.0 credits and each student will earn a letter grade.

Question: What are the basic requirements of the class? Who finds me the job?

Answer: The three basic requirements can be summed up in three words: FIND, WORK, REPORT.

#1. FIND an internship job with a reputable approved business organization of your choice, and directly related to your business management major area of study. Although the BYU-I Internship Placement Office will help you find a job, it is ultimately your responsibility to secure appropriate employment. Begin networking now-85% of internship jobs in the U.S. are not advertised, and are most often acquired through personal networking with family and friends.

#2. WORK in your job for a minimum of eight weeks, completing a minimum of 270 hours of professional, evaluated business management work experience. You must journalize your work experience, log your hours, and summarize your accomplishments. You will be evaluated by your supervisor, yourself, a co-worker, and your internship director.

#3. REPORT in the following three areas:

a. A Company Report showing your interest, knowledge, and research of your company, products, services, and the typical customers you will be dealing with.
b. A Specialized Report based on your area of interest and responsibilities.
c. A Learning Summary Report based on what you have learned from your Internship experience.

Question: How will the BYU-I Placement Center help me find a job?

Answer: BYU-I has hired a full-time Internship Director who will be working with the Department Director to help in job placement. The following tools are used:

a. A BYU-I Internship database with all companies and contacts that have recruited interns at job fairs in many of the western states in the current year will be available. This database will also include all companies that have had placements of BYU-I students.

b. The BYU-Provo database of past internship openings and opportunities will also be available to our business students at BYU-I.

c. Posting of all current business job openings that have been reported/researched by the BYU-I placement and department offices will be available. (Bulletin Boards are located in the Kimball Building and in Smith 230.)

d. Job fairs with recruiting companies will be scheduled on the Rexburg and Provo Campuses in the fall and spring. A Job Fair specifically for Internship placement will be provided on the Provo Campus, with travel arrangements made (at a cost) for BYU-I business students to attend. (Dates of all these job fairs will be posted on the bulletin board in Smith 230 and announced in the Junior Core business classes.)

e. Lists of internship internet sites and other job seeking materials will be available at the BYU-I Placement Center.

f. Job-seeking skills will be reinforced in the orientation preparation meetings. Students will be given information and instruction in the semester prior to enrolling in the internship.

g. Student resumes will be posted on a special web site so that all internship sponsoring companies have an opportunity to view the resumes of potential candidates for hire.

Question:  Must I get my internship job approved before I register for the class?

Answer: Although you can register for the class prior to finding a job, ultimately you must find placement in an approved (approved by the Internship Director) business related job.

Question: Is it really fair to expect me to find a job while going to school-shouldn't BYU-I find me the job?

Answer: In almost all internship programs found in the U.S., companies will not hire until they interview and pick the students of their choice. Students, therefore, must be successful in resume writing, networking and job interviewing in order to secure a job. For example at the University of Texas, approximately 3500 students in business are required to find related work internships with limited help from the University. This is the norm, and is part of what makes an internship valuable to recruiters.

Question: Can I complete my internship while going to school here in Rexburg?

Answer: Yes, if you are willing to work an average of 20 hours per week, and can find a reputable-approved local job in your area of business management.

Question: What are the limitations of my internship job location?

Answer: None, you can work anywhere in the world. Your assignments are handed in via mail, FAX, or E-mail.

Question: How are most internship jobs found?

Answer: Networking with friends and family. Because most students want to work near home (for economical and social reasons), networking is the current number one source of finding a advanced internship at BYU-I. (65%)

Question: What is the difference between a beginning and advanced internship?

Answer: A beginning internship is focused on proving your work ethic, dependability, respect for authority, ability to learn fast, and lastly your limited business skills. An advanced internship focuses first on applying your business skills, solving problems, showing leadership, management and teamwork, and articulating what you have learned in the classroom.
