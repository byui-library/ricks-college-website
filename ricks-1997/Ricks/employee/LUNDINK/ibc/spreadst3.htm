Individual Spreadsheet Exercise #3—Promotional Budget Analysis

Use Excel to complete the following 

As a community service, former basketball players for Rick's College agreed to play a basketball game against the current team.  The proceeds from the game will go to a local not-for-profit organization.  This is the sixth year of charity basketball game, and ticket sales for the last to years have been down--averaging about 300 per game.  The price of a ticket is $7.00.  A committee member, Jill--who also happens to be a marketing student, suggests using a sales promotion to increase ticket sales.  She suggest that we give away T-shirts to the first 500 ticket buyers.  The committee is concerned about the risk and wants to feel confident that they will be better off by running the sales promotion.

She believes that the T-shirt give-away will increase interest and certainly double sales to 600.  It may even sell 900 tickets (surpassing all years).  A local firm can supply the shirts and do the printing for $3.60 per shirt if the committee agrees to order at least 400 shirts.

The committee is interested but would like to do a little "what if" analysis first.  Here is what they want from you

  1. How much more gain will their be if they do the promotion and give T-shirts to the first 500 ticket purchasers assuming 600 people purchase tickets.  How many tickets would they have to sell to breakeven with no sales promotion?

  2. If they decided to only order 400 T-shirts, what would their gain and breakeven be?

  3. What would the gain be if the best case, 900 tickets sold, was reached (assuming they ordered 500 T-shirts)?  What is the breakeven (in number of tickets)?

  4. What if the committee chairperson suggested just lowering the price to $3.40?  What would the gain be if they sold 600 Tickets.  What is the breakeven compared to the regular price with no sales promotion?
