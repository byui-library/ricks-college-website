IS 230 - Page Layout and Graphics
Fall 2002 Syllabus

Index | Materials | Objectives | Labs | Assignments |  Grades | Policies

Credits: Three (Semester)

Course Description:

Word processing and desktop publishing software are used to create business documents suitable for print or electronic presentation. Emphasis is given to design and layout principles and includes strong graphic usage.

Materials:

  • Graham, L. (2002). Basics of design. Delmar: Albany, New York.
  • Andrews, P. (2002). Adobe Photoshop Elements: A visual introduction to digital imaging. Focal Press: Oxford, England.
  • Active BYU-Idaho student email account (check it daily!)

Raw data files for each of the Photoshop Elements projects can be copied from J:\\Jupiter\IS\IS230Projects on the BYU-Idaho network or downloaded from the Photoshop Elements Projects web page.. On campus these should be stored on your H: drive.

Objectives:

Students will:

1. Demonstrate and apply layout and design principles in a variety of media.
2. Demonstrate proper use of emphasis, contrast, balance, alignment, repetition, flow, images, and color in all work.
3. Demonstrate appropriate use of typography concepts and principles in a variety of media.
4. Implement appropriate file formats for graphics and documents and practice integration of media types.
5. Develop and implement business documents with developed branding symbols.
6. Demonstrate proficiency using a variety of applications including: Draw, Chart, TextArt, WordArt, Photoshop, PowerPoint, Publisher and word processing software.
7. Demonstrate proficiency using layering, slicing, shadowing, graphic mapping, and additional enhancements using PhotoShop and other applications.
8. Identify and use other resources that can be implemented in a business environment.

Assignments

There are two main topics of the course: Design and Graphics. A number of applications will be used, including Photoshop Elements, Word and WordPerfect. Use of these tools for this course involve class demonstrations, projects and other assignments as homework. The specific assignments for each area are:

Portfolio

Each student will maintain a portfolio of their assignments in electronic format (this may require the scanning of some paper media). Multiple copies must be maintained to protect against erasure or loss. These portfolios will be demonstrated at the conclusion of the semester.

Design Assignments

For each of the Design Elements (Design Ch's 2-7) the student will search professional sources (print and electronic) and find four examples of the element under discussion. Two of the examples will be positive (proper use) and two will be negative (poor use). Each example must be accompanied by a written paragraph of explanation of why is was selected and the criteria used to make the determination of positive or negative status.

Stationary Assignment

In lieu of a final exam, each student will develop a Stationary set for her or him self and for a company (real or ficticious). The sets must demonstrate all of the design concepts covered in the course and use of the software. For demonstration purposes, these sets will be in both electronic and print formats. The sets must include:

  • Standard size business card (orientation doesn't matter)
  • #10 Envelope
  • Letterhead for 8 1/2 x 11" paper
  • Thank you or other special purpose card (you determine the size)
  • Web page logo for main web page
  • Web page logo for secondary web pages

Class Policies

Attendance
Attendance is required. Materials, processes and techniques will be covered in class that may not be found in class resources. Therefore, it is to your benefit to attend consistently. Remember it is your responsibility to fulfill all assignments and other coursework, regardless of attendance, by the date and time specified. If you will be missing class for any reason other than emergency sickness or school excused absence, work must be submitted in advance.

Children In Class
Children are not allowed to be present in BYU-Idaho classes due to space restrictions, fire code compliance, and the need to limit distractions. Students have the responsibility to arrange for childcare away from classrooms and study areas so as to preserve and enhance learning opportunities for all students. In one-time emergency situations, the teacher of a class may allow a student to bring a child to class based on terms set by the teacher. Teachers are neither required nor encouraged to consent to a student's request to bring a child to class, but should use discretion based on the best interest of all students in the class.

Computer Ethics
The use of the school's network, systems and data impose responsibilities and obligations upon the students to use these computer components in an ethical manner and in accordance with the guidelines found in the Honor Code. Students are to access only data and files that are owned by them, or the instructor for their use has given explicit authorization. Copyrighted material must be used in accordance with copyright law and applicable license agreements followed. Attempting to circumvent or subvert system security measures or engaging in any activity that causes harm to systems or to any information stored thereon, such as creating or propagating viruses, disrupting services, or damaging files are considered inappropriate use. Violators are subject to disciplinary actions as prescribed by school policy and may also be prosecuted under applicable local, state, or federal civil or criminal law.

Computer Use
During class presentations you are to use only those applications being studied as part of the specified curriculum. E-mail, Internet and Instant Messaging use are prohibited unless being used for class assignments. Violators of this policy will be asked to close the application or leave class. Please do not disrupt class by using these other applications during class time.
