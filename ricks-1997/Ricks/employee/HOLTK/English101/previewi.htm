Previewing Exercises

Textbook Preview

Directions: Preview a textbook from one of your college courses by answering the following questions (scriptures, study guides and math books don't work well for this exercise).

1. What is the full title of your book? (Remember to underline this title and all other book titles when you write them.)

2. Name the author or authors of your book.

3. Who is the publisher of the book?

4. In what city is the main office of the publisher?

5. When was your book published (copyrighted)?

6. Does the book have a preface, introduction, or foreword? Which ones?

On which page(s) is each?

7. What are the titles of each unit in this book?

8. How many chapters does this book have?

9. What can you tell about this book by reading the table of contents?

10. Does this book have a glossary? If "yes," what are the page number(s)?

11. Does this book have an appendix or appendixes? If "yes," list the contents.

12. Does this book have an index? If "yes," on which page(s) is it printed?

13. Use the space below to comment on what you noticed about the textbook as you looked through it. Or comment about your feelings as you looked through this book. Or write down any questions about this textbook that you would like to ask your instructor.

 

Chapter Preview

1. Choose a chapter in one of your college texts.

What is the title?

How many pages does it have?

2. Flip through the chapter pages, reading each heading as you go. Can you distinguish major headings from sub headings by size, color and location?

What are the distinquishing characteristics of each?

3. Go through the chapter a second time, reading all the captions under pictures, charts and graphs. List some of the major topics you have found in the chapter.

4. Does the chapter have a list of objectives or an introduction?

Is there a summary?

How long is it?

Read the beginning and end material in the chapter.

5. Does the chapter have study questions at the end?

Does it have a list of key words or terms?

Read these.

6. What study aids does the chapter offer for vocabulary, definitions, and key people you need to know?

Are important words bolded or in a different color?

What kind of marginal notes are provided?

What other kinds of study aids are provided?

Once you have completed a five minute chapter preview, you have built a framework for the material you will read. This framework is like the straight edges and corners of a puzzle; the outside edge tells you the size, shape and colors of the puzzle will. A preview introduces the chapter topics, concepts and key information. When assembling a puzzle, once the framework is connected you are ready to fill in the rest of the pieces; once you have done a chapter preview you are reading to begin reading and fill in the details. Remember, reading a text is an active process. Read with a pencil in your hand so you can mark, write marginal notes, make written notes, write an outline or create a map.
