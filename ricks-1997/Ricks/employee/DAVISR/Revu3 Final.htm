The medium of literature is language.

sonnet - has 14 lines of iambic pentameter (5 iambic feet per line), and may be English or Italian, based on rhyme scheme

blank verse - unrhymed iambic pentameter (all of Shakespeare's heroes speak this way)

alliteration - repeated consonants, usually at start of the word

Peter Piper picked a peck . . .

rhyme scheme - last sound of the line

Mary had a little lamb A

Its fleece was white as snow B

And everywhere that Mary went C

The lamb was sure to go B

simile - comparison of two objects using like or as/He fought like a tiger.

metaphor - a brief, implied comparison/He was a tiger in battle.

denotation - what a word refers to/Red is a color

connotation - ideas associated with a word/A red is a communist.

allusion - a reference to an item or event which is assumed knowledge in behalf of your audience/ "I'll meet you at 2PM in the Hart." "Remember the Alamo." "Merrill Lynch is bullish on America."

idiom - an expression which does not mean literally what it says

"I'm going out of my mind." "Don’t put all your eggs in one basket."

dialect - an accent/language from a certain part of a country

provincialism/colloquialism - a figure of speech from a certain part of the country/local slang/bag vs. sack, soda vs. pop

meter iambic -/ (unaccented, accented)across, enjoy, among, today, around

trochaic /- kitchen, human, snowflake, center, frozen

anapestic --/ tribulation, geriatrics

dactylic /-- yesterday, telephone, library

Photography Chapter 8

ASA is "film speed"/grain increases (pictures get fuzzy) with film speed, so lower ASA is better

composition: Rule of thirds/vertical vs. horizontal

wide angle lens (20-40mm); normal is 50mm; telephoto lens (100-500mm)

best light is morning or evening (from the side), not mid-day (overhead)

f/stop = opening = aperture (i.e., f/4, f/8, f/16)

depth of field: (depth of focus) amount of photo in focus, varies with f/stop (the higher the number the more is in focus; more at f/16 than at f/4)

Architecture/Periods Chap 11-14 and Appendix

Greek - post and lintel/three Greek orders: Doric, Ionic, Corinthian; defined by capital

frieze - a decorated band around the exterior of a building, etc.

flutes (grooves on column)

Roman arch is round; Gothic arch is pointed, but not stronger. Arch stronger than post and lintel. It permits larger construction/bridges/aqueducts etc.

Romans give us concrete (gravel+sand+water+cement).

Romanesque - Late Middle Ages/castles/knights/heavy/small windows

Gothic - church in form of cross/nave (body)/transept(arms)/center of cross (the heart of the church) is for the alter; has pointed arches/flying buttresses/stained glass/age of cathedrals/named for Mary (Notre Dame="Our Lady")/took a century to build one, starts with St. Denis church in Paris

Renaissance - Man is the measure of all things, Reformation, discovery, exploration

Baroque - very busy, highly ornamented, "bigger is better," Louis XIV, wigs, Rembrandt, Bach

neo-Classic - logical (Spock), use your head, white columns, again return to the Greek/Roman outlook, Washington, Jefferson, Mozart, Haydn

Romantic - emotional (Kirk), return to Nature, the Unknown, Poe, Dickens, Delacroix, Goya, Chopin

Impressionism - all French, Renoir, Monet, Debussy, fuzzy, soft images, show the instant

Modern - skeleton construction/steel/cantilever/ "home sweet box," since WWI, gloomy outlook at first, Picasso, Stravinsky, then Gershwin (jazz), 60's and later - Beatles (rock)
