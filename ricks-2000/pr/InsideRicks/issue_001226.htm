YEAR IN REVIEW

In the year 2000, Ricks College went from chasing Y2K bugs to seeking new cheese! A glance backwards at the top stories reveals traditions being kept alive while the future begins to take shape.

January: 

  • ROTC finalizes partnership with ISU and BYU allowing Ricks students to take the first two years of training in Rexburg.
  • Elder Richard G. Scott speaks at devotional telling students, "The greatest motivation in life for me comes from knowing you have a Father in Heaven who loves you very much and wants your eternal happiness. It comes from knowing the Savior and believing that He will help you."

February: 

  • Men's Choir sing for the Idaho Legislature and Governor Dirk Kempthorne.
  • Elder L. Aldin Porter is honored with the Man of Excellence Award at Men's Week Fireside.

March:

  • Elder David B. Haight speaks at the devotional and encourages introspection "to be what we ought to be.
  • President Hinckley and other members of the Board of Trustees meet with President's Club in Salt Lake City where President David A. Bednar reports the completion of a fund drive which raised $19.3 million. Philanthropic priority goals for the years 2000-2005 are set at $27.5 million. Funds are used to expand the doors of educational opportunity, enhance the learning environment, support selected building and beautification projects, and extend the influence of Ricks College, and prepare for future opportunities.

April:  

  • President Bednar speaks to Rexburg business leaders and says, "You not only supply goods and services, you help teach these young people. It takes the entire community to create the spirit of Ricks."
  • The class of 2000 has 3,012 graduates receiving a total of 3,188 degrees. Elder Neal A Maxwell tells graduates, "This mortal experience . . . is not like a college course which we can passively audit. Instead, we are taking life's course for credit and there are no summers off - not even semester breaks." While in Rexburg, Elder Maxwell attends the premier of "Apotheosis," a composition by Gaylen Hatton with text selected from past expressions by Elder Maxwell.

May: 

  • Construction projects include renovation of the old Administration Building to become the east wing of the McKay Library; upgrades and renovations in the Smith Building, Manwaring Center, and Hart Building, construction of the Radio Graphics Services Building; and roof repairs on various campus roofs.

June: 

  • Education Week brings nearly 3,000 to campus to participate in a diverse selection of educational discussions and entertainment.
  • The First Presidency and Board of Trustees announces that Ricks College will become a baccalaureate-granting university to be known as Brigham Young University-Idaho. President's Council begins a series of question-and-answer sessions and information meetings specific to the transition.

July: 

  • American Folk Dancers and Bluegrass Band presents programs for stakes in Portugal and Spain, participates in international folk festivals, and appears on national television programs. Combined with successful tours by Extravadance, Showtime Company, and the Music Department's "Expressions of Faith," Ricks students expanded the influence to an estimated combined audience of over 46,000 people.

August: 

  • Ricks College experiences record enrollment of 8,949.
  • New Student Orientation expands to a weekend of events to get students involved. Early indoctrination into the Spirit of Ricks through service as students begin year with a project of assembling 8,800 personal hygiene kits and tying 100 quilts for the LDS Church Humanitarian Service Center.

September: 

  • Ricks alumnus Rulon Gardner amazes the world by winning gold in Greco-Roman wrestling in an upset victory over three-time Olympic champion, Aleksandr Karelin of Russia.
  • Proposals for the transition are presented to the Executive Committee as they meet on campus. Chairman of the committee, Elder M. Russell Ballard, says, "What is happening at Ricks is a result of revelation."

October: 

  • Homecoming is also a Spori Celebration with a nostalgic look at the past while focusing on the future. The replacement facility will maintain the name of the Jacob Spori Building.

November: 

  • Framework of transition takes shape with announcement of estimated time line and anticipated numbers of baccalaureate programs, enrollment, and faculty hires.
  • The Spori Building's planned nighttime demolition sparks a final flaming goodbye. The building catches fire at approximately 3 a.m.
  • Both men's and women's cross country teams take the NJCAA titles in an unprecedented repeat of their previous year's success.

December: 

  • Ricks Vikings has a sweet 45-14 victory over Snow College in the 14th annual Real Dairy Bowl.
  • Open house is held for new Radio Graphic Services Building which will house KRIC, KWBH, and Graphic Services.
  • Ricks quarterback Marc Dunn is named as NJCAA Offensive Player of the Year.
