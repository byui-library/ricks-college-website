Suggestions about scripture marking:

SMALL

The Standard Works should be a personal handbook of instructions. Annotations (notes you write in themargins) serve to clarify, explain, and bring attention to passages of scripture. Cross-references serve to relate passages of scripture for verification of a principle or principles. In order to allow for future marking and additions, writing should be small. The use of fine point ball point pen, a mechanical pencil (point five [0.5] with HB lead), or a draftsman's ink pen (like Mars, Rotring, etc.) will help in keeping the writing small. If you use a red pencil or the LDS Scripture Marker, keep the point sharp. Ink? What if you make a mistake?

NEAT

Others may look into your Standard Works. What kind of an example will those scriptures be? Marking neatly shows consideration for valuable property. It also helps you to organize and to find things better than a sloppy, mixed-up bunch of marking does.

SIMPLE

There are many complicated systems of marking; some use many colors and many tools. Sometimes these complicated systems take more time than they are worth and require more tools than are easy to carry. Keep your system uncomplicated and useful.

The circling (or underlining) of a word or a short phrase may make your marking more practical, more useful, than just boxing in or shading an entire verse. Your marking should indicate more than just a passage has been marked: it should also tell why it has been marked. Highlighted key words can help in doing that.

Examine how others mark their scriptures. As you compare and contrast, pick what you like and incorporate it into your system. There probably isn't a one best system that will suit everyone.
