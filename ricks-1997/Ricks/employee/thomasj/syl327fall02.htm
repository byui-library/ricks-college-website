Welcome to our class! I hope you will enjoy learning and teaching this semester.

 

Aims

                A. To become more familiar with the Pearl of Great Price and through it to gain a better understanding of (1) the plan of salvation and the meaning of the atonement; (2) the power of symbolism in scripture; (3) and the value of the restoration of doctrines, ordinances, and covenants.

                B. To deepen your enthusiasm and competence for lifelong study and teaching of the scriptures.

                C. To better understand the purpose and meaning of temple ordinances and covenants.

                Please download and study “Principles and Promises to Guide Our Learning” from my faculty web page. As you study this document, identify and record the promises and principles that will help you most as a student and teacher of the scriptures.

 

Course activities

1. Preparatory study: The course calendar specifies a block of scripture to study before each class meeting—when we spend more than one class meeting on a block, you should always study the block at least twice. Much of your most valuable learning and growth can take place as you study on your own. I strongly recommend that you purchase and use the CES Student Manual. Study Guides, along with a few supplementary readings are posted on my faculty web page. Please report the quality of your preparation for each study assignment. I may help assess the quality of your preparation as needed. Basic expectation: read and mark the whole block of scripture before coming to class, use the study helps in the scriptures or another study resource, and take some notes.

2. Attendance and participation: Class presentations and discussions should complement your personal study of the scriptures. I hope to help highlight important principles, clarify complex issues, and promote understanding consistent with the course objectives. I invite you to participate in that process. Please report attendance and the quality of your participation for each class meeting. I may help assess the quality of your participation as needed. Basic expectation: be in class, on time, with scriptures, notebook, and intent to listen, write, and speak meaningfully.

3. Essay question: “What does the Pearl of Great Price teach me about the purpose and power of the atonement of Jesus Christ?” Your essay should provide a clear summary of the atonement as revealed in the pages of the Pearl of Great Price, highlighting the distinctive insights of this book of scripture.  It should clearly and completely communicate what you learn about the atonement through your study of this book this term.  Every time you study, you should ponder the question, and write down thoughts and impressions that come–these will be the raw material for your final polished essay. Although you may use other scriptures or prophetic statements, you should make the Pearl of Great Price your primary text. Though you will quote some scriptural passages, avoid lengthy quotations–strive to communicate what you understand in your own words, indicating the relevant verses. 

Your grade will be determined by your assessment of the quality of the process of preparing the essay, your assessment of the quality of the finished typewritten product, and my assessment of the finished product.  Consider these criteria: Am I recognizing and recording relevant references and impressions consistently? Can I summarize my doctrinal understanding in my own words? Can I recognize passages that relate to the question in all parts of the book? Can I draw connections between these passages? How is my understanding growing at this time through this process? Am I building an organized discussion?

4. Memorization/Scripture teaching: You should either memorize the Articles of Faith or  develop a lesson plan for teaching one of the scriptural blocks on the course calendar. If you memorize, it will be assessed in writing in the Testing Center in early November. Otherwise turn in a typewritten outline of a lesson plan on the same date. The outline should specify intended audience; lesson purpose(s);  sources for teacher preparation; and suggestions for lesson development (including introduction, discussion and application, and conclusion). The outline should be similar to curriculum outlines you can find on the Church website (see me if you need further advice).

5. Mastery assessments: Two exams will be administered in the Testing Center on the dates indicated below–late exams are penalized.  The exams use a variety of question types and you can use your scriptures. Study Guides 1 and 2 can help you in all phases of class preparation, including preparation for the exams.

6. Temple Compilation:  Find and copy at least 25 articles that focus on doctrines and principles related to temple work, especially the endowment and sealing ordinances.  At least 15 of the articles should be by current or former members of the First Presidency or Council of the Twelve.  You may use the Church’s database of publications on CD-ROM or its website: www.lds.org. When you have copied and compiled the articles, type two copies of a detailed table of contents, which gives full publication information for all the articles (i.e., author, titles, date, pages). You can arrange the articles and table of contents chronologically, alphabetically or topically.  Put both copies of the table of contents in a 3-ring binder with the articles and bring it to my office on or before Tuesday, November 19.  I will check the collection, keep one copy of the table of contents, and return the binder to you for future use.  If you follow the guidelines with exactness and honor, you receive full credit.
