Computer Aided Design (CAD) Courses

 

CAD 112 Intermediate Engineering Graphics

Credit Hours:  3 credits (three 1-hour lectures per week)
Prerequisites: CAD 172 or consent of Instructor

The purpose of this course is to instruct additional design skills to students using CAD as the design tool. A review of basic design practices with CAD will be conducted. Topics include engineering graphics, precision dimensioning and tolerancing (GD & T), pictorial layouts, introduction to manufacturing materials and forming processes, numerical control drawings, threads and fasteners, welding drawings and design concepts. Use of 3D CAD design tools will be heavily stressed. Portfolio developments will be emphasized.

CAD 172 Engineering Graphics: Principles and Applications

Credit Hours:  3 credits (three 1-hour lectures per week)
Prerequisites: none

Review of engineering graphics principles. Fundamentals of CAD engineering drawing principles, descriptive geometry, and geometric dimensioning and tolerancing.

CAD 211 Advanced Engineering Graphics

Credit Hours:  3 credits (three 1-hour lectures per week)
Prerequisites:  CAD 172 and CAD 213 (CAD 213 can be concurrent)

This course teaches advanced design skills using 3D CAD and rendering tools. Subject matter includes working drawings (detailed and assembly), introduction to belts, gears, and cams, sheet metal drawings, structural steel, electrical manufacturing tooling, presentation quality output. Emphasis on industrial quality output will be stressed. All design work will be done on 3D CAD tools.

CAD 212 Descriptive Geometry

Credit Hours:  3 credits (three 1-hour lectures per week)
Prerequisites: none

Fundamentals of CAD engineering drawing principles. Special relationships and design principles relating to point, line, plane, curved lines, and surfaces, vectors, intersections, and developments with application to practical problems.

CAD 213 Introduction to Computer Rendering and Animation

Credit Hours:  3 credits (three 1-hour lectures per week)
Prerequisites: none

An introductory course covering fundamental principles of computer generated illustrations, still renderings, and full motion animations using 3D Studio Max software. Topics include understanding 3D work environment, object creation and modification, materials, lighting, cameras, rendering, animation, lofting, morphing, boolean operations, space warps, hierarchal links, inverse kinematics, and dynamic simulations.

CAD 234 AutoCAD

Credit Hours: 2 credits (two 2-hour classes per week)
Prerequisites: none

Computer aided drafting design AutoCAD software. Class includes 2D and 3D drawing generation, solids modeling, and other software applications.

CAD 238 Advanced AutoCAD

Credit Hours:  3 credits (three 1-hour lectures per week)
Prerequisites: CAD 172 or CAD 234, and CAD 213 (CAD 213 may be concurrent)

A study of advanced Computer Aided Design applications using AutoCAD and 3D Studio Max software. Topics include advanced solids modeling, drawing layout, menu & toolbar creation, slides, script files, linetype & crosshatching creating, attributes, system variables, macros, automation, dialog boxes, AutoLisp programming, advanced animation and creation of special effects.

Manufacturing Engineering Technology (MT) Courses

 

MT 131 Machine Tool Operation

Credit Hours:  3 credits (five instruction hours per week)
Prerequisites:  Math 101 or equivalent

Operating principles of basic machine tools. Major emphasis is on metal lathe operation and techniques and acquaintance with precision measuring instruments.

MT 132 Manufacturing Processes

Credit Hours:  2 (two instruction hours per week)
Prerequisites:   None

Introduction of modern manufacturing, methods and processes; shows possibilities and limitations of these processes and their application to fabrication of industrial products.

MT 132L Manufacturing Processes Lab

Credit Hours:  1 (one 3-hour lab   per week)
Prerequisites: Concurrent enrollment in MT 132

Enrolling for this optional lab will make it possible for Engineering majors to transfer the class for MFE 232 at BYU. MFE is required for all Mechanical and Manufacturing majors at BYU.

MT 134 Machine Tool Operation II

Credit Hours:  3 (six instruction hours per week)
Prerequisites:  MT 131

Planning and machining of metal parts to print dimensions, cutting feeds and speeds, surface ifnish, tool geometry, horsepower requirements and tool materials.

MT 222 Engineering Materials

Credit Hours:  3 (five instruction hours per week)
Prerequisites:  Chem 105

Physical properties, composition and constituents of metals and metal alloys.   Laboratory work consists of preparation of metallographic samples for microscopic examination and also the effects of heat treatment on steels. (Fall)

MT 231  Tool Design

Credit Hours:  3 (five instruction hours per week)
Prerequisites:  CAD 172, MT 131 and MT 132

Design and evaluation of jigs and fixtures for manufacturing processess.   Introduction to design concepts of pressworking tools. (Fall)

MT 234  Computer Numerical Control (CNC)

Credit Hours:  3 (six instruction hours per week)
Prerequisites:  MT 131, MT 134, Math 110, and Math 111

Automated machine control through Computer-Aided Manufacturing (CAM) and Computer Numerical control (CNC) will be the main emphasis of the course, with a brief introduction to Automated Program Tools (APT).  In the laboratory, students gain practical experience on CAM and CNC software and equipment. (Winter)

MT 236  Hydraulics and Pneumatics

Credit Hours:  3 (five instruction hours per week)
Prerequisites:  Math 101 or equivalent

A study of the fundamental principles governing the transmission and control of fluid power (hydraulic and pneumatic).  The course includes a study of the basic components of hydraulic and pneumatic systems and how they are combined to build up various circuits.   (Winter)

MT 252  Quality Control

Credit Hours:  3 (three 1-hour lectures per week)
Prerequisites:  Math 101 or equivalent

Fundamental coverage of quality control concepts.  Teaches the students the quality concepts and problem solving techniques associated with statistical process control.  Emphasis will be placed on statistical methods.  computer will be sued for analyzing data and plotting charts.

MT 260  Industrial Plastics

Credit Hours:  3 (six instruction hours per week)
Prerequisites:  Chem 105 or consent of instructor

The course emphasis is in selection, use and processing of thermoplastic and thermoset plastics.  Experience will be gained in extruding, blow molding, laminating, compression molding, injection molding, and thermoforming.

MT 291R  Professional Seminar

Credit Hours:  0

Attendance required for all manufacturing majors.  Held three times each semester to discuss technical developments, career opportunities, advisement, etc.  Pass-fail determined by attendance.

Welding Engineering Technology (Weld) Courses

 

Weld 105 Essentials of Welding 3-4

Credit Hours:  3-4 (2 lecture hours and 6 lab hours per week)
Prerequisites:  none
Lab Fees:       $25

A general Welding course which includes Oxyacetylene welding, Oxyfuel Cutting (OFC), Shielded Metal Arc Welding (SMAW), Gas Metal Arc Welding (GMAW), Flux Core Arc Welding (FCAW), Gas Tungsten Arc Welding (GTAW), in the flat and horizontal positions. Includes the safety procedures, electrode identification and focuses on theory and practice.  Welding majors should take the four credit class (Fall and Winter semesters).

Weld 117 Repair and Maintenance Welding

Credit Hours:  2 (one lecture hour and two lab hours per week)
Prerequisites: Weld 105 or consent of instructor

Welding techniques, principles, theory, and practices used in the repair and maintenance of equipment. Includes brazing, cast-iron , aluminum welding, carbon arc gouging, hard surfacing and other process. The study of the physical and mechanical properties, as well as chemical composition of the materials to be repaired will be included. (Winter)

Weld 123 Advanced Weld Processes

Credit Hours:  3 (two lecture hours and four lab hours per week)
Prerequisites: Weld 105 or concurrent enrollment
Lab Fee:         $25

Advanced pre-employment training which emphasizes developing manipulative proficiency in the use of SMAW, FCAW, GMAW, and GTAW in the vertical and overhead positions in light and heavy gauge plate for the welding industry. (Winter)

Weld 210 Blueprint Reading and Sketching for Welders

Credit Hours:  2 (two lecture hours and one lab hour per week)
Prerequisites:  none

Sketching, blueprint and welding symbol interpretation, plate and pipe layout measurement, bills of materials, dimensioning, notes and specifications, and abbreviations used in fabrication.

Weld 218 Welding Fabrication

Credit Hours:  2-3 (two lecture hours and four lab hours per week)
Prerequisites: Weld 105

The use and application of equipment, tools, fasteners, and processes used in fabrication practices in the welding industry. Interpretation of drawings for fabrication of small projects. Techniques for layout, fitting, squaring, tacking, and fabricating will be developed. May be repeated for credits. (Fall and Winter)

Weld 225 Pipe and Structural Weld

Credit Hours:  4 (two lecture hours and six lab hours per week)
Prerequisites: Weld 105, 123, or concurrent enrollment
Lab Fee:        $20

A variety of material and configurations using SMAW, GTAW, FCAW and GMAW processes on pipe and structural shapes. The welding will be done in all positions on carbon, stainless steel and aluminum. (Fall)

Weld 229 Certification and Code Welding

Credit Hours:  3 (two lecture hours and four lab hours per week)
Prerequisites: Weld 105, Weld 123, Weld 225 or concurrent enrollment

Interpreting and writing welding procedures and welder qualifications and specifications. Welding to AWS, API, and ASME codes to meet the entry level employment standards for Code work. Training in the procedures using SMAW, STAW, GMAW, and FCAW processes for qualification of welders in all positions on plate, pipe and rebar. May be taken more than once. A qualification test will be given at end of course by a qualified Certified Welding Testing Lab. (Winter)

Weld 250 Welding Equipment Maintenance and Service

Credit Hours:  2 (two lecture hours and one lab hour per week)
Prerequisites:  none

A study of the theory, application, and practices used in maintenance of welding equipment used in the Welding industry.

Weld 256 Automated Welding Processes

Credit Hours:  3 (two lecture hours and two lab hours per week)
Prerequisites: Weld 105, 123, or current enrollment

A survey of the automated welding processes and their applications used within today’s welding industry including Panograph, Robotics, Sub-Arc, etc.  Field trips and hands-on learning will be used.

 Weld 258 Non-Destructive Testing and Inspection

Credit Hours:  3 (two lecture hours and two lab hours per week)
Prerequisites: Weld 105, 123, 225 or current enrollment.

Theory and application of non-destructive testing systems. Techniques for quality control. Includes magnetic particle, liquid penetrant, leak, visual and ultrasonic testing.

Weld 290 Special Projects

Credit Hours:  1-3
Prerequisites:  consent of the Department Chair

Weld 298 Welding Technology Work Experience

Credit Hours:  1-3
Prerequisites:  consent of the Department Chair

Work experience with selected welding firms. Work period may be arranged during regular semesters. An acceptable written report on the organization and the work done will be required.

General Studies Courses (GS)

 

GS.SS 201 Technology in our Modern Society

Credit Hours:  3 (three 1-hour classes per week)
Prerequisites: Sophomore standing or 21 credits completed

This course examines the effect that technology has had and continues to have on our modern society. Issues explored in this class include: history of significant technological advances; catastrophic technological failures; case studies; changing job skills in the work place; product design cycle; artificial intelligence; group technology; the change and development of manufacturing systems and automated production systems; principles of numeric control and computer integrated manufacturing systems. To help internalize the issues presented in this class and gain a better understanding and awareness of the effect of technology, students will be formed into small, mock companies, multi-disciplinary by design. Each company will be required to participate in the product cycle through the development of a product. This cycle includes gathering background and research information, designing a product, production of a prototype, and presentation of oral and written design reviews. (Fulfills General Studies Social Science Requirement)
