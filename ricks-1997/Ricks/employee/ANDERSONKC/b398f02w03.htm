Mission Statement: Students will be more prepared to enter the business work force upon graduation from college, by completing an evaluated work experience program.

Students will become more attractive to potential college recruiters by

supplementing related work experience with their regular classroom college curriculum.



Objectives: Review and learn new job-getting skills in resume writing, job portfolio preparation, networking, and interviewing.

Find a professional business management internship/job in an approved business and complete 270 hours of work over a minimum time length of eight weeks.

Apply the principles and skills learned in the classroom to on-the-job practices and procedures in business management.

Through on the job experience, gain a greater vision of what it means to be a

business management professional.

Establish professional career networking links with the business you are

employed in and other exposed businesses and businessmen you come into

contact with during your work experience.

Receive timely consultation from inside and outside sources to help direct your learning progress in your various job responsibilities.

Receive compensation and recognition by receiving wages from the employer, and letters of recommendation from both the employer and BYU-Idaho.

Some guidelines as you prepare to secure your internship job:



1. Register with the BYU-I Placement Center by submitting your resume (244 Kimball Building). All internship job openings that come to BYU-I are funneled through the Placement Center. Get to know the counselors at the center as well as all the job-getting publications, tools, data bases, etc. found there. This is the central internship job-finding hub on campus.



2. For your convenience, an "Internship Bulletin Board" with current business management listings from the Placement Center and internship information is located at the Business Management Internship Office in Smith Building 231.



3. Eighty percent of all internship jobs are not advertized, but are found through networking. Use your times spent at home (weekends, vacations, holidays) to talk to your family, Church leaders, and friends about possible job openings in your area. Remember: Do not ask your family and friends to find you a job, but instead ask them for advice on how you can find a job. This is your quest, not theirs.



4. You must complete the "Advanced Internship Application," and schedule a formal interview with the business management Internship Director during the semester prior to your internship. Your job with related job duties, (or your job possibilities if you have not been hired) will be discussed in detail during the interview and a formal determination will be made as to whether or not your job qualifies as an advanced internship. Remember, that your internship job must be approved in advance. Do not wait until you have worked for several weeks and hope that it will be approved.

5. Some students will not be able to secure an internship job prior to leaving BYU-I for the summer. Remember that you are required to work 270 hours within an eight-week or more time period. This will give you plenty of time to follow up on your networking and job search after you go home (or to the destination where you are seeking employment). All BYU-I semesters have sixteen weeks, twice the amount of time your are required to work on your internship. (Eight weeks X 40 hours = 320 hours.)



6. You should plan on working for a reputable company with at least five or more employees. If you work for a family business, you must have major management responsibilities and be evaluated by a non-biased supervisor.



7. Private business up-starts for the advanced internship must be accompanied by a strong professional business plan, and be approved by the Business Management Director.

8. Your work assignment must give you the opportunity to apply and learn a variety of business management skills and concepts as you work on the job. Door to door salesmanship would not qualify unless you split your salesmanship duty with office/financial/marketing management time, and working closely with a managing supervisor.

9. Your employer must be willing to spend time with you in management training, evaluation, and consultation.

10. It is strongly suggested that you work full-time during your internship so that you can understand the pressures and responsibilities of a full-time management employee. Your internship sponsoring company should have full-time career possibilities.

11. Your business must be willing to sign the BYU-I work contract agreement, and allow visits by the Internship Director to view your work. (A copy of the contract is attached to this packet.)



12. The Business Management Director is willing to call your potential business and give a telephone recommendation or answer any questions that your business may have. Use this resource if helpful.

4. Requirement #4. Company Cultural Report: (10%)



The purpose of this report is to become a better employee by learning more about your company and the customers they serve. This report should be typed in "report format" in either Word Perfect or Microsoft Word, and sent via E-mail attachment to the Business Management Internship Director. Make it neat and creative.



Page #1: Research and compile a one-page minimum historical sketch of your company.

Page #2: Give a detailed description of the location of your company - country - state- city - county -

street. Include some of the main roads and landmarks you would need to know in giving

customers directions on how to get to your company. Why is your company located where it is?

What location advantages does it have? Why is it to your interest to know the details about location? Give the address, web site, and phone number of your company at the top of this page. Page #3: Give as detailed description as you can on one page of the products and services your

company provides to the world. Include as additional pages if you can any brochures provided by your company of their products and services.

Page #4: Give as detailed description as you can of a typical customer for your business and the basic

wants and needs that are satisfied by your company.

Page #5: Give a detailed one-page description of your job.

Page #6: Marketing Majors: Give a one-page general description of your company's marketing plan.

Finance Majors: Give a one-page over-view of the financial operations in your company.

General Majors: Give a one-page over-view of a major management area in your business.

Page #7: Give a company organization chart for your company, or if it is a large national company, the

branch or location in which you work. Highlight your position.

Page #8: Paragraph #1: Give a description of your supervisor, his/her main duties and goals related to

your work and how you can contribute to his/her success.

Paragraph #2: Give a detailed description of one of your co-workers. (Same items as your supervisor.)



Note: This report will be given a letter grade based on content, grammar, and form.



Requirement #5. Specialized Work Project Report: (10%)



This four-page typed report is focused on your major career mission. It should cover the topic of how you adding value to your company by preforming your duties/assignment in your area of specialty. Most interns will be assigned special projects such as web development, special reports, research, on-going specialized duties such as selling, keeping books, creating minor publications such as pamphlets, organizing inventories or displays, going to conventions or trade shows, etc. The challenge of this report to not only report in detail of your specific assignment and the results, but to show how your efforts added value to your company. This report should reflect your business writing communication skills. It should be a high quality report with grafts, charts, statistical data organized in a simple yet highly communicative manner for either internal or external interested parties. Your report should be bound with a title, some kind of visual display, a body, and a summary of data found. Reports should all be arranged in business report writing format style. The report will represent your company's as well as your professionalism in written communications.



Note: This report will be given a letter grade based on accuracy, knowledge, content, grammar, and form.

(Report format should be reviewed from your business communication classes.)





Requirement #6. Work Summary Report: (10%)





This report will not be evaluated critically for format or content, but should be typed in good form showing a spirit of common professional business sense. It should be a summary of your journal and what you have learned and experienced during your work experience. Please use the following organization:



1. First Section: Include at the beginning, in priority order, a list of the five most important things your have learned on the job. Give each of the five items a title phrase or sentence, and then a short paragraph of commentary on why you ranked this item as you did.



2. Second Section: What did you enjoy most about your job? Why?



3. Third Section: What was your overall greatest challenge? Why?



4. Fourth: Self evaluation of how you did and what grade you feel you earned and why.



5. Fifth: Final evaluation of your company and supervisor. How were you treated, do you

have a future with this company, etc?



6. Sixth: Evaluation of the Internship Program addressing three items.

A. The value of the program in your career/life.

B. What needs to be changed?

C. Suggestions for other students entering the program.



Note: This assignment will receive a pass+/pass or fail grade.

This final evaluation will be a part of the final letter of recommendation BYU-Idaho will give you upon the completion of your internship.
