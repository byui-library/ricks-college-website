Religion 343 Study Guide 2 (Thomas)

 

Chapter 42--Growth into a Worldwide Church

1. How was General Conference of April 1951 distinctive?

2. What was the background and personality of David O. McKay, ninth president of the Church? How did that background shape his ministry as president? How did the Church change during his 19-year presidency?

3. In the 1950s and 1960s, what were the main social challenges facing Church members in (a) Europe, (b) Polynesia, (c) Latin America, and (d) North America?

4. How did the Korean war impact the work of the Church?

5. Where did President McKay dedicate temples? What were other landmarks of his worldwide ministry?

6. What happened that facilitated President McKay’s visit to Fiji?

7. What missionary slogan did President McKay make famous? What other measures were taken to improve the sharing of the gospel?

8. What happened in Nauvoo and New York in the 1960s that had lasting impact on the Church’s public image?

9. How did Church leaders expand educational opportunities (a) in North America, and (b) overseas?

10. How did the seminary program change in these years?

 

Chapter 43--An Era of Correlation and Consolidation

1. What led to the formation of the all-Church coordinating council and what was it designed to do?

2. How was correlation intended to affect (a) councils (b) teaching and (b) other Church activities?

3. Name the three major objectives of the Church identified in the 1964 Melchizedek Priesthood handbook.

4. How was family home evening strengthened in 1965 and 1970?

5. What were the ages of Joseph Fielding Smith, Jr. and Harold B. Lee when each man became Church president? How long did each man serve as president? What aspects of each man’s background best prepared him for his service as president?

6. What “tangible” evidence of the consolidation of Church programs opened in 1972?

7. What Church magazines were introduced in 1970-1971? How did they differ from their predecessors?

8. How did the Sunday School curriculum change in 1972? How is it similar to and different from the present?

9. What were the major initiatives in temple and family history work in this era?

10. Who was called as commissioner of education? What did his office identify as the main principles to guide Church education?

11. What kind of programs were governed by the Welfare Service department when it was created?

12. What initiatives were taken to meet the needs of ethnic minorities and single Church members?

13. What was the purpose of Area Conferences? Where was the first one held? What message about gathering was enunciated at these conferences?

14. What were the purposes of (a) regional representatives and (b) the International Mission?

*Supplementary reading: In his 1977 address, what did Elder McConkie say about: (1) continuing revelation; (2) three historical phases of the latter-day work; (3) the meaning and purposes of Zion and how it is built; (4) the meaning and purposes of the gathering of Israel and how it is accomplished?

 

Chapter 44--The Church Lengthens Its Stride

1. How had Spencer Kimball reacted to his call to the Quorum of the Twelve? What background and personality did he bring to his service as the twelfth president of the Church?

2. What motto did President Kimball make famous in a 1974 address? What was the reaction to his address?

3. What measures were taken to expand missionary work in this era?

4. Based on the chapter and Official Declaration 2, what was the context for the revelation on priesthood?

5. What were the revelation’s main effects on the work of the Church?

6. Why did the First Presidency oppose passage of the ERA?

7. What was President Kimball teaching about marriage, family, and women’s roles?

8. How did the standard works change in 1976, 1978, 1979, and 1981?

*Supplementary reading: In his article, what did Elder McConkie say about: (1) the process by which the revelation on priesthood was received by President Kimball and his brethren; (2) the feelings of those who were present and of other members of the Church; (3) the significance of the revelation for the work of the Church; (4) the significance of continuing revelation in the Church.

 

Chapter 45--Meeting the Needs of a Worldwide Church

3. How did General Conference and auxiliary conferences change at this time?

4. What was the “basic unit” program and how did it affect the Church worldwide?

5. When and why was the three-hour block of Sunday meetings introduced?

6. What were the major initiatives in temple building in the 1970s and early 1980s?

7. What technological changes and new program helped members in temple districts to provide their own supply of names for vicarious ordinances?

8. What are identified as the most important legacies of the ministry of Spencer W. Kimball?

 

Chapter 46--A Period of Challenge and Growth

2. How did President Benson apply D&C 84:54-57 to his day?

3. How did his message about the Book of Mormon affect the Church?

4. What were the main themes of his messages to young men, young women, mothers, and fathers?

5. How did the First Presidency respond to the issue of AIDS?

6. What were the most notable forgeries of Mark Hofmann? How did his crimes affect the work of the Church?

7. How were Seventies quorums at the general and local levels reorganized in 1984, 1986, and 1989?

8. What initiatives were taken in family history in these years?

9. What anniversaries were celebrated in 1987?

10. What were the major steps in the Church’s entrance into countries behind the “Iron Curtain”?

*Supplementary reading: In his 1987 address, what did Elder Oaks say about: (1) the charge that the Church suppresses historical documents; (2) the way Church leaders deal with people; (3) examples of “Mormon-bashing” in news media coverage of the Hofmann forgeries; (4) Joseph Smith’s relationship to folk magic; and (5) the lessons of the Hoffman tragedy for the study and teaching of Church history?

 

Chapter 47–Continued Growth During the Early 1990s

3. What opposition faced the Church in Latin America? What difference did it make for the Church to be registered by the Mexican government?

4. What is the significance of the Guide to the Scriptures?

5. What did US Church members do to provide famine relief for Africa in 1985? How much humanitarian aid was distributed from 1985 to 1995? What was the purpose of Latter-day Saint Charities?

6. How was the Joseph Smith Memorial Building developed? What are its purposes?

7. How were mission costs and ward budgets reorganized at this time? How did the changes help members in  developing areas?

8. What was the background and personality of Howard W. Hunter, fourteenth president of the Church? What were the two focuses of his brief prophetic ministry?

9. What were the main developments in family history and temple work in this period? What made the temples at Hong Kong and Vernal distinctive?

10. What milestones in the numbers of wards and stakes were reached in the 1990s?

3. In addition to the 1995 Proclamation, what did President Hinckley do to try to strengthen families?

4. Why did President Hinckley agree to be interviewed by 60 Minutes? What other efforts did he make to meet with news media?

5. How much travel did President Hinckley do in his first three years? What did he do on his various visits?

6. How did Church leaders and members celebrate the pioneer sesquicentennial?

7. How were Areas and the Seventy reorganized at this time?

8. What prior apostles had foreseen the day of a new conference center? What were the intended purposes of the new center?

9. What were President Hinckley’s main contributions in regards to temple work?

10. What changes in Sunday teaching and Church education were initiated in these years?

11. What changes were made in the Church’s musical groups?

What testimony did both these apostles bear about Jesus Christ?
