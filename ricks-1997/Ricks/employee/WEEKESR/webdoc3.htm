Class Overview:

This class is a broadcasting majors only course.  Although non-majors are allowed into
the class, preference to seats available in the class will be given to broadcasting majors.

This course is a prerequisite to other broadcasting classes such as Communication 259 (Beginning TV Production) and Communication 265R (Radio Station Operation).

The basic premise of this class is to help you understand the possibilities of a career in
radio or television broadcasting.  This goal will be achieved in two ways.  First, we will read and discuss in class broadcasting as a career opportunity.  Secondly, you will learn the basics of audio and video production techniques as professionals utilize it in the radio and television
broadcasting industries today.  Through this class I hope to help you determine early in your
collegiate studies if you are willing to make the sacrifices necessary to work in broadcasting.
 Please read the remainder of this syllabus.  It will give you vital information that will
help you in the class throughout the semester.  If you have any questions about what we are
discussing throughout the semester, please talk to me after class or make an appointment to see
me in my office.

Broadcasting as a career can be rewarding!  The more you put into this class, the more
you will get out of it and the better your chances will be of working in the extremely competitive
career of broadcasting.

Lab Projects:
Each audio lab assignment should begin with the standard preopen (10 sec. of
silence, 10 sec. of test tone @ 100 % [0 dB] modulation, voice slate, 10 sec. of silence).

(1) During the first lab session, we will go into the audio production lab.  There you will be
introduced to the various pieces of audio production equipment and how they operate.  During the first lab session, we will go into the audio production lab.  There you will be introduced to the various pieces of audio production equipment and how they operate.  You will need to write a two page double-spaced typed summary of this equipment and what it does.  Both content,
spelling and grammar will be considered in your grade.

(2)  On your cassette you will record three musical selections.  The end of the first will cross
fade (blend) into the beginning of the second.  The end of the second will cross fade into the
beginning of the third.  The final selection should fade out at the appropriate time.  You will
need to provide your own musical selections.  Pick whatever type of music you want to play.
All selections should come from compact discs.  You will have to use the stand alone compact
disc player as well as the CD-ROM drive in the computer to play back these discs.  This project
(and all lab projects)  should be done in the lab in the Spori Building.  The recorded volume of
all three selections should be similar.  You will be graded on how smooth your cross fades are
and if you have an audio level consistent between the three audio selections and the 100% tone
at the beginning of your project.  Each song should be played from 60 to 90 seconds each.

(3) You will produce (using your own voice) one 30-second Public Service Announcement.  It
shouldn't be shorter than 28 seconds nor longer than 30 seconds.  There should be narration with
an appropriate music bed.   The script for your narration will be assigned to you. One of the
purposes of this project is for you to work on varying the four elements of your voice as
discussed in class.  Additionally, you will need to create an appropriate audio mix between your voice and the music selected.  The music should have a clean start and a clean or smooth ending.  You will be graded on the length of your spot, your vocal quality, and appropriate audio levels.

(4) For this lab you will use the computer to record and edit a very short news report.  Besides
recording your own voice onto the computer using the provided script, you will need to check
out the news actuality compact disc and use sound bites from the disc.  Make sure you edit out
any mistakes that you or the sound bite might make.  Once you have completed the news report,
dub the file onto your cassette and remove your file from the computer.  If the file is smaller than
1.4 MB, you might want to store it on a floppy disc.

(5) This will be your sole video project for the semester.  You will check out one of the video
camcorders from the instructor and shoot various video assignments on your tape.  More details
on this assignment will be given to you in class.  You can expect that the scenes you shoot with
the camcorder will give you an opportunity to show proper/improper white balance, good focus,
stable shots that show good pans, tilts, zooms as well as wide shots, medium shots, close-up
shots and extreme close-up shots.  Two of you can work together on this project, but you each
must turn in your own tape showing different shots.  A majority of the shots on the tape should
be done outside, regardless of the weather.

(6) On a date and time that you will be assigned, you and a lab partner will listen to a specific
shift on KWBH-FM.  Using the Radio Format Report guidelines, you both will answer the
questions and then turn in a typed double-spaced two-page paper of what you determined.  Only
one paper is required between the both of you.  This is a group project.

(7) After completing Lab Assignment #6, you and your partner from Lab Assignment #5 will
spend an entire air shift (two hours) will a qualified KWBH-FM on-air personality. The first
hour is time for you to find out more about what happens during a typical air shift.  During the
second hour the you will be able chat on air with the B91 D.J.  Before you leave, you should be
able to play a short segment on-air with the B91 D.J. assisting you.

If you turn in any lab or written assignment after the deadline, you will be penalized a full
letter grade on your assignment.  Example:  If you received a 95 for a project and it is
turned in late, your grade will become an 85.
