ETHICS CASE To properly develop the following ethics case, the student should identify any accounting or ethical issues involved, evaluate alternative courses of action, and make a decision as to the best course of action. In doing so, the facts of the case need not be rehashed but may be referenced to support a conclusion.

Good grammar and logical development are important to promote one's credibility. The length should be approximately one type-written page.
 
 

IMS Inc

You are a CPA who was recently hired as the financial director for a manufacturer called IMS Inc. As a condition for receiving a bank loan to finance the company's rapid growth, the company has promised to upgrade its financial reporting to the extent that the company can be audited this year. One of your assignments is to create the accounting systems and records necessary for this upcoming financial audit for the year ending December 31, 1998.

In reviewing 1998's financial statements with the president, the president questions accruals for property taxes, salaries payable, and interest on the bank loan. These accruals are material and convert a net income into a net loss. He is emphatic that since these expenses will be paid next January, they should be recorded then. Furthermore, since similar accruals last year were not booked then but recorded in January at the beginning of this year, the effect of the proposed accruals would be to insert 13 months of expenses into the current year and make this year's net income not comparable with last year's or the next. You respond that generally accepted accounting principles require accrual accounting and that the auditors will not give a clean opinion to statements prepared on a cash basis. At this point the president becomes angry and shouts "You don't get it! You don't even know which side of the bread the butter is on! You are no longer in public accounting. You work for me! Leave the auditors to me!" As you leave his office, your head is spinning and you wonder what you should do.
