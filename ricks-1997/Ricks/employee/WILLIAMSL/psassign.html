Your First Assignment:

Think of a funny, clean joke of about two minutes in length that you can share with us. We will take a few minutes to get acquainted with one person in class and that person with you. We will start at the beginning of the alphabet. In order, we will do two-minute introductions of the person you became acquainted with. That person will then get up and share the joke.

This two-part assignment is not graded, except that you must do it.

Please make your joke appropriate for Ricks . . . that includes avoiding ethnic jokes.

Good luck and have fun.

Speech Assignment #2
Personal story

The purpose of this speech is to help you with story-telling technique. Pick a story from your life. It must be true and must have happened to you. Make it your most __________ moment (pick the adjective to fill the blank . . . things like dramatic, funny, embarrassing, spiritual, amazing . . . you get my drift.) This story must be between 3 and 5 minutes in length. You will be timed. Expect to lose points for being over or under the time limit. Expect to lose relatively more being further from the limit. An important preparation tip: The underprepared person is most often the person who goes over the time limit.

You will be graded on your story-telling ability, your confidence in delivery, and your eye contact and paralanguage.

This will count about 8 percent of your final grade, assuming we complete all the assignments as outlined on the syllabus. If we don't, of course, expect it to count more.

We will do this one from the end of the alphabet.

Good luck.

Speech #3--The mini-informative speech.

Pick a single-sentence topic, make a simple outline of no more than one page and two supporting points. You must show your outline to the class, so bring it on overhead/transparency.

Give us an informative speech of no more than 4 minutes in duration and no fewer than 2 minutes. The only criteria you will be judged on will be the length and on whether you did it or not. The main difference on this speech is that the class and the teacher will evaluate your performance immediately after you complete the assignment before the entire class. This makes it a particularly useful, but potentially painful, class period, so be prepared.

Speech #4:
Informative or Demonstration speech.

You are to deliver an informative or demonstration speech to the rest of the class.

  • You must use at least two sources from the library or from expert interviews. (An expert interview would almost always be a scholar, but if it is about a subject that affects a person -- a person affected by your subject could be a good source. I would prefer, however, if you use a source that is a friend or family member, you have two sources in addition to that person.) More sources are probably better.
  • You get to choose the topic.
  • The speech must be between six and eight minutes in length.
  • You will be timed, but you will receive no cues.
  • The speech must be extemporaneous, that is either memorized or from notes.
  • Visual aids are required.
  • You must submit a typed, stapled outline as you begin your speech.
  • The outline must use complete sentences.
  • The outline must include a list of at least two sources you used for this report. More sources help you deliver a better speech and, therefore, improve your grade.
  • Above the outline and beneath your title, on the outline you submit, include three things: Your specific purpose, your central idea written in a complete sentence, and the kind of structure you are employing (See chapter 6 for an example in this..)
  • You may evaluate each other.
  • This, together with the persuasive speech, is the most important speech you will deliver.
  • This speech is worth roughly 15 to 20 percent of your final grade.
  • You may actively demonstrate in class with class involvement, but you must still keep it within the time limit.
  • We will begin this at the middle of the alphabet and go up the alphabet to A, jump down to the end of the alphabet and progress backward to the beginning of the alphabet.
  • You will be graded on your structure, the support of your information, the power of the information, your paralanguage, the timed length and its effectiveness in informing. Also, of course, the paralanguage, gestures, confidence and eye contact will also be graded.

Suggestions:

  • Dress neatly.
  • Rehearse a lot.
  • Make your subject narrow enough to cover easily and completely.
  • Pick a subject that interests you and one you have a good deal of knowledge about.
  • Review chapter 6 in preparation for this speech, even though you won't be quizzed on it for a while yet.
  • Remember your purpose is to inform. While it is good to be entertaining, informing is the key. Most people in the class should learn something.

 

Speech assignment #5:
Persuasive speech.

You will be required to give a persuasive speech. You pick a statement you wish to argue and whether you support or oppose the point of view. The statement is your central idea.

1. Your talk must be six to eight minutes long. You will be timed without cues

2. Your talk must come from a typed outline that you will turn in at the beginning of the speech. At the top of the outline, YOU MUST HAVE A SINGLE SENTENCE CENTRAL IDEA THAT IS NOT PHRASED AS A QUESTION. IT SHOULD ARGUE IN FAVOR OF SOMETHING OR PERSUADE US TO TAKE A PARTICULAR COURSE OF ACTION.

3. You must have a central idea with supporting points organized in a systematic manner. These must be in your typed outline.

4. The talk must be delivered extemporaneously. Notes are acceptable, but do not read a full speech.

5. You will be judged on structure, delivery, and the presentation and logic of your arguments. I will also look to see the amount of research you have done to support your argument and whether the argument shows an understanding of the issue. Hence, include a sourse list with your typed outline is required, though no specific number of sources are required. Whether your views match mine or the class's will not matter.

6. Although it is acceptable to use scripture and religion in your persuasive speeches, use caution because this exercise is not meant to be a contentious battle about scriptures and their meaning.

7. Be courteous to those with whom you disagree. You may not mention another speech that disagreed with yours nor one that agreed with yours.

8. We have planned for this to take four sessions.

9. I have inlcuded a list of possible topics, though you are not required to select one from the list.

10. Remember, these are speeches to convince us to a point of view, not to actuate or reinforce.

 

Some Sample topic sentences. Each of these sentences could be argued for or against and sentences reworked accordingly. You are NOT required to use any of these sentences:

1. The best solution to juvenile crime is to try young people as adults and put them in adult prisons when convicted.

2. The President of the United States should be immune from frivilous lawsuits for sexual harrassment.

3. Marijuana should be legalized to help sick patients.

4. Chronically ill people in pain should be allowed to commit suicide.

5. Americans should stay out of the war in Kosovo.

6. We are building too many prisons and not spending enough on rehabilitation.

7. America is a racist country and still needs affirmative action.

8. The O.J. Simpson acquittal in his criminal trial was a good thing for this country.

9. The culture at Ricks College puts too much emphasis on dating. Less emphasis should be placed on dating.

10. FA 100 is a class that wastes our time and should be eliminated.

11. We should have a Constitutional Amendment making it illegal to burn the U.S. flag.

12. The United States should pass a law banning all pornography on the World Wide Web and other parts of the Internet.

13. The United States government should stop giving so much aid to the poor because people are wasting what they receive.

14. Football is too violent and should be banned.

15. All students at Ricks College should be required to pass a standardized examination in order to get a diploma from this school.

16. The United States should spend less money on cancer research and give the balance to AIDS research.

17. The college G.E. courses I must take are irrelevant to my coming life; therefore, G.E. credit should be overhauled to reflect modern society by including more technical courses about computers and business and less about art and science.

18. Cheerleading is not a sport, and students in high school should not receive credit for it as a sport.

Speech #6. Teach a chapter

This may come earlier in the semester so heads up.

In class, we will sign up for a chapter. Three or four students will work together. You must have a visual aid. You must have a coherent presentation among all members of your group. Use typical attention-getting and excitement as if it were a regular informative speech. You are not required to teach the entire chapter, but a minimum of 15 minutes total for your group is expected here.Though each participant will be graded separately, the best grades usually will come from the best overall group performances. Good luck.

The Religious Speech . . .
Speech Assignment #7

Write a religious speech to occupy three to five minutes of time (should be about 1 and a half to two pages in length.). You will be graded on this as a paper and a delivered speech. The speech will be judged on standard delivery including paralanguage, structure, eye-contact and gestures. The paper will be judged on writing. Some of it will be mechanics. The rest will be on the beauty of language (that is, how well do you follow the principles of writing on The Guide to Cleaner Writing.) and the success of the speech. This will be a manuscript speech. This is short in length of time, but my intent is for you to write a speech that has ten minutes worth of content packed into three to five minutes. You should learn something new and thought-provoking and inspirational in your preparation.

The manuscript speech must include:

  • At least one story derived from personal experience, well-told.
  • If you use another anecdote, it may be only one and occupy no more than 5 percent of the speech.
  • Effective parallel construction.
  • Evocative detail.
  • It must utilize some search structure from the scriptures to give new insight into the subject. (As per class discussion. You may talk with me early about this, if you wish to begin early.)
  • It must have a central idea, effective attention-getter, classical structure.
  • You must use scripture in some form.
  • A metaphor or example or self-derived parable could be useful.
  • If you so choose, you may opt, instead, for a more inspirational, non-religious talk (ex. "I Have a Dream.") If you do, some systematic research (with bibliography) is required.
  • No sources other than scriptures are required, though certainly allowed . . . (Maybe try Bro. Nibley's thinking or some scholarly work, since this is college, after all.)

 

Writing Assignment #1

Please observe the speech of a "professional" speaker, or a speaker in an official setting. This might be a forum or devotional, a recording (on video) of general conference, a speech on C-SPAN.

Please include the following:

  • Tell me what the speaker's main point was. That is, if you were to put the central idea of the speech on a small napkin, what would that idea be?
  • Tell me how well the speaker did. Did he or she appear confident? Did the speaker use gestures effectively, or were the gestures forced? Did the speaker have effective eye contact, insofar as that was possible? Did the speaker hold your attention? Were the stories the speaker used effective?
  • Please include things the speaker did well -- approximately one page -- and what things you noticed that weren't done so well -- also approximately one page. (This can be tough to do comfortably with a general authority's talk. Hence, I would encourage you to look at other speeches.)
  • I have asked the library to record two speeches on C-SPAN for our use on video. This will be available in the audio/visual services of the library's third floor.

This paper must be two pages, double-spaced in length -- specifically, at least 500 words. You will graded heavily for mistakes in grammar, spelling and the like -- 50 percent. You will also be graded on your analysis of the speech, which counts about 50 percent of your grade.

 

Video Assessment Assignment -- Writing #2.

The assignment: Video Assessment .

You will be required to anaylze your informational speech.

Specifically:
1. Complete an assessment form I will provide similar to the one included, which I will provide at speech time.

2. Then type a list of 20 specific comments about your speech -- 10 good things and 10 bad things.

3. Staple your list to your evaluation form.

4. No feedback on your informative speech will be provided until you turn this in.

5. This will count relatively less than other papers.

6. You will be graded on following instructions, on how cleanly you analyze yourself. Basically, if I agree with your assessment, you will be OK. So, it will be to your benefit in your list to be as specific as possible citing examples. Also, I will punish your paper if I perceive that you have been too hard on yourself . . .

The final (3rd) paper.


This paper is to analyze what you learned and what you improved on.
1. Two pages, i.e., no less than 500 words double-spaced.
2. Type and staple the paper.
3. Make half of the paper, roughly, things you learned from the class that you will try to put into practice in the future. Be specific.
4. Make the other half of the paper, roughly, things you improved on. Be generous with yourself. Be specific.
5. If you say you improved on nothing, then you will receive an "F" on the paper.
6. As always, I will look for grammar, spelling and mechanical errors in grading. These will count about 50 percent of the final grade on this paper.
7. I will also look for understanding of the material presented in the class. Hence, specificity will help you.
8. You will be graded, in part, by how well you follow instructions. For example, expect to lose points if it is not stapled.
