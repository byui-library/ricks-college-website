Student Employment & Career Placement

1. Job Interview - Physical & Mental Preparation

A great interview starts with the preparation before the actual interview. Following are some things that you can do to prepare to succeed in the interview.

Physical
When in doubt . . . dress conservatively.
Conservative haircut and style.
Don't overdo the make-up or cologne.
Shake the interviewer's hand (firm handshake; not fishy, not crushing).
Look in a mirror before the interview.

Mental
Analyze your skills and qualities prior to the interview.
Be prepared to express your skills and abilities at every opportunity.
Know what skills and qualifications the employer is looking for.
Do your company research - know CEO, locations, strengths, size, etc.
Know interviewing procedures.
Be prepared for common questions.
Be prepared for "stress" questions.
Be on time for the interview!
Use clear voice, diction, and correct grammar.
Act confident (but not overbearing) - first impressions are important.
Show enthusiasm and be positive.
Be cordial, polite, and friendly.
Maintain good eye contact.
Be aware of body language; don't slouch or fidget.
Look for opportunities to express your skills; use specific examples.
Think of questions to ask when you are given the opportunity to ask.
Don't disclose personal information that may be used to discriminate.
Don't lie.
Thank the interviewer. Always follow up with a thank you letter.

 

2. Interview Essentials

Make a positive first impression.
Communicate your skills.
Anticipate and answer problem questions.
Answer the question: "Why should I hire You?"
Follow-up with a thank you letter.

4. Common Questions Asked In Employment Interviews

The Three Most Frequently Asked Questions
Tell me about yourself.
Why do you want this job?
What makes you think you are qualified for this job?

Specific Questions
In what school activities have you participated? Why?
How do you spend your spare time? What are your hobbies?
In what type of position are you most interested?
What jobs have you held? How were they obtained and why did you leave?
What courses did you like best? Least? Why?
Why did you choose your particular field of work?
What percentage of your college expenses did you earn? How?
What do you know about our company?
Can you work in a culturally diverse environment?
Do you prefer any specific geographic location? Why?
What do you think determines a person's progress in a good company?
Why do you think you might like to work for our company?
Do you prefer working with others or by yourself?
How did previous employers treat you?
What have you learned from some of the jobs you have held?
What interests you about our product or service?
How long do you expect to work?
Have you ever had difficulty getting along with fellow students or faculty?
What size city do you prefer?
What is your major weakness?
What do you do to keep in good physical condition?
Are you willing to go where the company sends you?
What job in our company would you choose if you were free to do so?
What types of books have your read?
What types of people seem to "rub you the wrong way"?
What jobs have you enjoyed the most? The least? Why?
Would you prefer a large or small company? Why?
What kind of work interests you?
What have you done which shows initiative and willingness to work?

 

5. Questions Asked by Students During an Interview

What would my duties and responsibilities be?
What kind of support would I have to continue my education?
What methods do you use to evaluate your employees?
Describe your ideal employee for me.
How would I be supervised?
Who would I be working with?
What are the opportunities for advancement with your company?
What kind of training program would I go through?
What kinds of benefits does your company provide?
What is the normal salary range for someone with my background?
These are just a sample of the questions you could ask. It is advisable to think of additional question during the course of the interview. This will show that you have been attentive and are sincerely interested in the position.

Be careful not to ask questions that could easily be answered by doing your company research. In addition, questions relating to salary and benefits should be asked either at the end of the first interview, or most likely during the second interview. Use your best judgment when asking salary and benefit questions.

 

6. Unlawful Interview Questions

(Asked by the Interviewer)
Age.
Whether you are married, divorced, separated, widowed, or single.
Children and their ages.
Whether you are now, or plan to become pregnant.
Your race or questions relating to your color or national origin.
Your height or weight - where it is not relevant to the job requirements.
Religious affiliations.
Whether you have ever been arrested.
Political or social affiliations.

The way you respond to an inappropriate question could play a vital role in whether or not you get a job offer. Most interviewers ask illegal questions out of ignorance or lack of understanding of employment law. Discrimination, although illegal, is very difficult to prove in court. The best way to deal with an illegal question is to identify the real concern of the employer and answer that concern.

For example, if a woman is asked if she has children, the real concern would probably be that of reliability on the job. She should assure the interviewer that child care is not a problem and will not interfere with her ability to do her work. Likewise, the question pertaining to marital status shows concern for whether you will be staying in the area very long. Prospective employees should assure the interviewer that they are seeking permanent employment and they have no plans of moving at this time.

Remember, It is important to look past the initial question, recognize the real concern, and answer that concern.
