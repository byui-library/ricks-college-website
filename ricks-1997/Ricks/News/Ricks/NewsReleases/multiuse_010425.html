Ricks College News Release
April 25, 2001 
Ricks College breaks ground
on new multi-use building

REXBURG, Idaho — There are "new beginnings springing forth from the foundations of the old," said Elder F. Melvin Hammond of the Quorum of the Seventy as he dedicated the site for a new multi-use building at what will soon be known as Brigham Young University-Idaho.

Reflecting on his days spent at Ricks College as a student and a teacher, Elder Hammond said he had witnessed nearly every significant occasion at Ricks College of the last half century. "The wonder of this campus is built into the lives of each of us," he said.

The audience first gathered in the Taylor Chapel and then walked to the building site south of the parking lot adjacent to the Kimball Student and Administrative Services Building for the formal turning of the soil. Key individuals handling the shovels included Elder Hammond, President David A. Bednar, college stake presidents, the contractor and architect, representatives from the Church facility management organization and the Church Educational System, members of the President’s Council, student body officers, two state legislators, and Rexburg Mayor Bruce Sutherland.

The 54,000 square foot, two-story building, which has not been named at this time, will serve a campus stake and eight wards, as well as general academic purposes on school days. Presently there are 57 wards and six stakes on campus. It will contain approximately 15 classrooms and 25 offices and a permanent chapel. Coupled with an adjoining cultural hall, the building will seat about 1,600 people for such needs as stake conferences and overflow for devotionals.

The building plan is similar to those used by other Institutes of Religion of the Church. Some adaptations to the standard plan are being done by the architect for the project, Jensen Haslem of Logan, Utah. Jacobsen Construction of Salt Lake City, Utah, is the contractor.

The area around the new building will be landscaped, and parking will be placed east of the building adjacent to South Second East Street.

Completion date for the building is projected for August 2002 when the expected enrollment will be about 9,800 students, some 800 above the current enrollment.
