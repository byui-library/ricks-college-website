Introduction

Welcome to the Semester Online version of H.S. 131

H.S.131 consists of several components that must be completed to satisfy the requirements of this general education course.

  1. Begin a program of regular physical activity and keep a journal of your experience for 5 consecutive weeks.
  2. Complete all thirteen lessons and submit the required assignments. These lessons will each have an objective format.
  3. Complete the comprehensive final examination, which consists of 100 objective questions.

Optional Course Activities: Complete the Health and Fitness Appraisal twice; once when you begin the course and once as you complete the course.

Course Objectives

This course takes a practical and personal approach to the needs and interests of each student. At the conclusion of the course you should have learned to:

  1. Discuss objective means for detecting health and safety needs in oneself, thus reducing disease and disability.
  2. Apply information provided in the course to fit one's personal health and fitness needs and interests.
  3. Accumulate knowledge and understanding of health principles and practices that will enable you to modify your health behavior
  4. Design and follow your own personalized fitness and lifestyle management program, preferably in lifetime physical activities
  5. Recognize that this course will not only improve the quality of your life immediately, it will also provide personal, family and community benefits throughout your life.

The Blood Test and the Health and Fitness Appraisal

While this portion of the course is optional you are strongly encouraged to participate. This is an opportunity for you to learn valuable information about your current state of health and wellness and identify potential risk factors for many chronic diseases. If you were to have these very same tests done in the private sector you could easily spend up to $300. As a H.S. student you can participate in this screening process and receive personal results at a nominal charge. It is not everyday that you are presented with a opportunity like this, so take advantage of it now.

The second appraisal is critical in order to determine the benefit of the lifestyle changes that you have made. The first appraisal is a baseline measure and the second appraisal will show you the results of all your hard work. Additionally, you will be able to determine what areas of your personal health may need more of your attention. Having this information is one of the keys to personally taking charge of your own good health.

First Health and Fitness Appraisal Guidelines (Click on the highlighted text for more information.)

Step 1.

Perform the 1.5-mile walk, jog, or run test on or before your first scheduled appointment -- and bring your time to your appointment. You should complete the 1.5-mile walk, jog, or run test on your own. If you need a physician's release, obtain the release note before you perform the test. Follow all instructions provided. Be sure to time yourself as you perform the test and bring that time with you to your Health and Fitness Appraisal appointment.

Step 2.

Complete the Blood Test. We highly encourage you to participate in the blood testing. However, if you have medical or personal reasons for not participating, you will need to complete the "No Blood Test Form" (available in the H.S. Office, Clarke 116. The results of this test will give you significant insights regarding possible health risks. The blood test will cover the following areas: glucose and serum cholesterol levels, including total cholesterol, LDL, HDL, VLDL, and triglycerides. There will be no make-ups for missed blood testing appointments.

*You must fast for twelve hours before the blood test (water is okay). Most students choose to eat a normal supper, then do not eat again until after they have had their blood drawn the next morning.

Step 3.

Complete the first health and fitness appraisal by Sept. 28th). The health and fitness appraisal will evaluate your: General Well-being, Resting Blood Pressure, Blood Profile, Cardiorespiratory Fitness, Body Composition, Muscular Fitness, and Flexibility. You are encouraged to use your appraisal results in order to help you throughout the course and throughout your life to develop individualized programs to enhance your health and fitness.

Physical Activity Log/Journal

You will be required to keep a journal of your regular physical activity (PA) program for 5 consecutive weeks. The activity that you do should be written in the journal on a regular basis, or the days that you have decided to participate in regular PA.

Make sure that you record the details of your PA by including the type of activity performed, the duration of the activity and the intensity of the activity. You can even record what are called lifetime physical activities. These activities might include the number of flights of stairs you climb, the estimated miles or time that you walk each day, mowing the lawn, or gardening, etc.

At the beginning of each week you should identify two or three goals that you intend to achieve, and then address each of these goals during the week in your journal entries. As you learn about the principles of fitness and regular physical activity from the lessons you complete, you will be able to incorporate new components of activity, such as muscular strength training, into your regular activity schedule.

If you find that your goals are too easy or hard then you can make adjustments to your activity program in subsequent weeks and make new goals.

The important thing to remember is that something is better than nothing. Additionally, you should know that significant health benefits are seen with only minimal amounts of regular PA. Balance, variety, and moderation are the keys to a successful PA program. Remember, your PA program is personal and customized to your individual needs. As you learn from this course you will become empowered to take control of your own good health.

When you have completed eight consecutive weeks of regular PA and recorded the appropriate information in your journal, you may submit your journal to me either as an e-mail attachment in WordPerfect, Microsoft Word, or any other format (ie. Excel, etc.) or you may hand deliver a paper hard-copy to my office in Clarke 116f.

To see examples of activity journals that have been submitted in previous semesters, click on the following link. You may format your journal any way you choose; just make sure to include the required information--type of activity, duration of activity, intensity of activity, and weekly goals.
