About Communication 102

Public Speaking

Public Speaking is a class designed to help the student improve and sharpen his or her skill in organizing, preparing and delivering a speech to an audience. The class is a careful blend of traditional rhetoric and the results of modern social science research.

The origins of this field of study extend back to the time of the Romans and Greeks and beyond, but many of the practices are enlightened by twentieth-century social science research.

This course is based on the assumption that human beings learn incrementally; that is, they learn in small bites, not big gulps, especially when they are learning a complex skill such as public speaking. For that reason this course is designed to move from simple to complex, to learn public speaking one small lesson at a time.

This course is also based on a second assumption that it is not possible to become an effective public speaker without actual practice in the process. Therefore, the course is designed to allow the student the maximum amount of time to prepare and deliver speeches. The idea being that through the process of watching, evaluating, and delivering speeches, the student will get the maximum amount of skills enhancement.

The actual outline of the course will allow the student to present seven speeches. These start with the very simple and progress up through the more complex. The first speech is one of introduction of the student. The second speech is a one point speech to make a point using an experience from the students own life. The third speech is an oral reading where the student finds, prepares and delivers a story with extemporized introduction. The fourth speech is an informative speech making use of visual aids. In the fifth speech the student will be giving a persuasive speech with the object changing audience beliefs, attitudes, values, or behavior. The sixth speech is a debate, in which you will be asked to defend a position against opposing arguments. Finally, speech number seven is an impromptu speech.

Each speech is preceded with a lecture on the type of speech and techniques use in its preparation and delivery.

The student is required to read the text book and is tested over the material in the text with four tests.

There are five written assignments which require the student to analyze various types of speeches as well as his own ability to speak. These evaluations are based on a list of evaluation techniques which are handed out to the student.

One of the students speeches is video taped and the student then watches the tape and writes an extensive evaluation of his speech.

Grading of the speeches is done by the instructor and three of the other students in the class picked at random. The four grades are added and the average is the final grade on the speech. This has two distinct advantages. One: It forces the student to carefully analyze his audience since three fourths of his grade comes from them. Two: It helps the student see the positive and negative points of a speaker which he will want to incorporate into his own speaking.

One of the biggest problems college age students seem to have is stage fright. Therefore, one of the first areas worked on is the overcoming and controlling of stage fright.

Other areas covered are Listening, Audience analysis, Topic selection, Research, Organization of the speech, use of supporting material, Introductions and conclusions, language used in the speech, delivery techniques, presentational aids, informative speaking, and persuasive speaking.

The student is subjected to each of these areas in three ways. First, the students reads about the area in the text. Second, the instructor goes over the points. Third, the student actually uses the points to prepare, organized and deliver six different speeches.

This course is advantageous to everyone, but especially anyone going into a profession where they will be dealing with the public and have a need to communicate with them.
