ENROLLMENT RECORD

Ricks College has broken its own enrollment record with 8,840 students enrolled for the winter semester. This is over the target enrollment established in 1998 of 8,600 and higher than the fall semester's enrollment of 8,628. "It is difficult to know exactly how many students are going to show up, but we have not changed our cap of 8,600 students," says Jim Gee, assistant academic vice president. Attending Ricks this winter semester are 805 new students. The remaining number includes 7,606 students continuing from the prior semester and 429 students returning after an absence, such as returned missionaries who re-enter Ricks following their missions for the LDS Church. Winter enrollment statistics show that Ricks College is attended by mostly 18-22 year-old students. The average credit hours per student are 15, showing that most students are attending on a full-time basis. In a breakdown of students from the United States, statistics show 2,931 from Idaho, 1,312 from Utah, 865 from California, 739 from Washington, and in fifth place, Oregon, with 387 students. There are 272 international students representing 51 countries. Fifty-eight percent of the students are women (5,111). LDS returned missionaries number 2,575. Marital status shows 542 Ricks students are married.

SYMPOSIUM ON THE SCRIPTURES

Students of all ages will explore the Book of Mormon in the 14th annual Symposium on the Scriptures on Saturday, January 29. The free symposium will be held in the John Taylor Building on the Ricks College campus from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. It is offered to anyone who is interested in getting better acquainted with the Book of Mormon. Registration is not required. Presentations are given by the members of the Ricks College religion faculty, members of the Brigham Young University religion faculty, and local seminary teachers. The symposium generally draws more than 500 participants from across Eastern Idaho and Western Wyoming. The symposium is centered around this year's course of study in the Sunday School gospel doctrine class of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. Elder David A. Bednar, president of Ricks College, will begin the symposium at 9 a.m. with his keynote address entitled "Coming Unto Christ: Preparatory Conditions, Ongoing Requirements, and Promised Blessings." Various one-hour classes will be taught at 10:30 a.m., 11:30 a.m., 2 p.m. and 3 p.m. For further information see http://www.byui.edu/Ricks/Academics/division/divReligion/dptReligion/symposium.htm.

FORENSICS WIN REGIONAL FIRST PLACE SWEEPSTAKES

The Ricks College forensics team took the Northwest Region by storm. The team returned from the largest tournament of the year in the Northwest Region which was hosted by Pacific Lutheran University. Participating teams represented 27 schools, mostly universities. Ricks took First Place Sweepstakes with only five students. It is believed to be the first time a junior college has won the Sweepstakes award at this tournament.

In Impromptu Speaking, Hillary Clemens took first place and Dave Blunk took second. Hillary also won first place in Extemporaneous Speaking, first in Poetry Interpretation, and second in Communication Analysis. Chalise Ludlow won second in Poetry Interpretation, second in Dramatic Interpretation, and second in Program Interpretation. Angie Newman won first place in Dramatic Interpretation.

LAST NIGHT OF BALLYHOO

"It's a beautifully warm play and story that will touch people," says John Bidwell, director of The Last Night of Ballyhoo that opens Thursday at Ricks College. The play will be presented in the Kirkham Arena Theater Jan. 20-22 and 26- 29 and Feb. 2-5 at 7:30 p.m. Tickets for all performances are available at the Ricks College Ticket Office for $6 for the general public. For more information, call the Ticket Office at (208) 356-2230. The comedy was written by Alfred Uhry, who also wrote Driving Miss Daisy. The play involves a Jewish family in a predominantly Christian society. Bidwell explains the ironic mind-set the Freitag family has as they try to assimilate into the Christian culture. "They do all they can to fit into the society without necessarily converting to Christianity" and denying their faith. "It's a very sweet story," Bidwell says. The audience will leave Ballyhoo with a "better appreciation of who they are, where they fit in, and what they need to do to be better members of their faith."

NATIONALLY ACCLAIMED YING QUARTET TO PERFORM AT RICKS

The Ying Quartet, a nationally acclaimed string group featuring four siblings, will perform Thursday and Friday, Jan. 20 and 21, at 7:30 p.m. in the Barrus Concert Hall at the Snow Center for the Performing Arts. Tickets for this Center Stage event are $6 for the general public and are available through the Ticket Office at (208) 356-2230.

The Ying Quartet has won nationwide critical acclaim, and has become one of America's most exciting and compelling young ensembles. The members of the Ying Quartet were the first recipients of a National Endowment for the Arts grant to support chamber music in rural America. The quartet's success in making artistic and creative expression an essential part of the everyday life of the Jesup community was widely chronicled in both the national and international media. The Ying Quartet won the Naumburg Chamber Music Award and has since established an international reputation for excellence in performance. The Ying Quartet's passion for performing in diverse settings has led to appearances in Carnegie Hall, the White House, hospitals and juvenile prisons. These four siblings play together with high energy, emotional power and technical brilliance. While at Ricks, the quartet will be conducting a three-day residency with orchestra students.
