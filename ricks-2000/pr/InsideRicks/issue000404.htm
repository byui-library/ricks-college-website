IN MEMORY OF THOMAS E. RICKS

"Everyone was sent to earth to make a difference," said Eleanor Ricks Colton of her great-grandfather, Thomas E. Ricks, colonizer in eastern Idaho and founder of what is today's Ricks College. "I am sure he had no idea what was ahead (for him) when he crossed the frozen Mississippi River (in 1846)." Sister Colton spoke at the unveiling of three large-sized oil paintings depicting events from the life of Ricks. The paintings, commissioned by the college, were donated by Sister Colton and her husband Sterling. The couple serves as president and matron of the Washington, D.C., Temple. The paintings are now on permanent public display in the lobby of the Manwaring Center on campus. Glen Hopkinson, a noted LDS artist, created the paintings based on three occurrences in Ricks' life: as a teenage teamster crossing the frozen Mississippi River with one of the earliest wagon trains to leave Nauvoo, Ill.; helping with the rescue of a stranded handcart company in Wyoming in an early-winter storm of 1856; and introducing Jacob Spori as principal of Bannock Stake Academy on Nov. 12, 1888. The academy was opened that day by Ricks who was president of the stake. The academy eventually became Ricks College.

AWARD FOR OUTSTANDING YOUNG ALUMNI

This year's Outstanding Young Alumni Award has been given to Michael W. "Mike" Mosman, Assistant United States Attorney. As a federal prosecutor, he handles a wide variety of cases from international drug smuggling and money laundering to felony crimes on the three major Indian reservations in Oregon including cases of murder, rape, and child abuse. His current assignment involves prosecuting organized crime cases. He says, "I consider it a tremendous honor to serve as an Assistant United States Attorney. I have no illusion that this job alone can keep the streets safe, but I believe that it is a very important part of that effort."

Mosman attended Ricks prior to and after a Church mission to Spain. He left Ricks in1979. He is a graduate from Utah State University, and obtained his law degree from BYU in 1984. He is a native of West Linn, Ore., where he now lives, but was raised in Lewiston and Moscow, Idaho.

RICKS COLLEGE HORSE AUCTION

The Ricks College Livestock Center will hold a horse auction Thursday, April 6, at 6 p.m. at the Livestock Center west of Rexburg. The sale is part of an ongoing process that starts in October. Livestock Center Chairman Lynn Stewart and instructors Nick Baldwin and Jack Muir buy about 15 horses from locations ranging from Idaho to Washington and bring them to Ricks. Once here, students of the college participate in training and breaking of the animals, preparing for the auction in the beginning of April. "This program provides a lot. There are satisfied horse owners and the students benefit," Stewart said. He added that horses in the sale usually sell for around $2,000. Stewart said the whole process is a lot of work, but well worth it. "It teaches economics and experience in training, but it's just great to see a student take a horse, break it, and be successful. It is a good confidence builder."

RICKS COLLEGE RODEO

The Ricks College rodeo team competes against 13 other two-year and four-year institutions in 10 rodeos a year plus the College National Finals Rodeo. As part of our participation in the National Intercollegiate Rodeo Association (NIRA), the Ricks rodeo team sponsors a competition in Rexburg each spring. The rodeo is scheduled for the weekend of April 14-15 in the indoor arena at the Madison County Fairgrounds. There will be a Friday evening performance at 7:30 and two Saturday performances at 1:30 and 7:30 p.m. For evening performances, tickets are $4 purchased in advance from rodeo team members or at the Ticket Office, or $5 at the door.
