GENERAL INFORMATION

What is Elderhostel?

(The following  was taken from some Elderhostel publications)


Elderhostel is a unique non-profit organization that combines the best traditions of education and hosteling to create a stimulating academic environment for older adults. Elderhostel is based on the belief that retirement does not have to mean withdrawal from an active life; instead, it provides the perfect opportunity to enjoy new adventures and activities. 

Elderhostel is a short-term educational program for older adults who want to continue to expand their horizons and to develop new interests and enthusiasms. We're for elder citizens on the move, not just in terms of travel, but in terms of intellectual activity as well. Our commitment is to the belief that retirement does not represent an end to significant activity for older adults but a new beginning filled with opportunities and challenges. 

Eldershotel schools of Scandinavia. Founder Marty Knowlton, a social activist and educator, was impressed by the way in which the availability of a network of modest accommodations encouraged and nurtured an adventuresome hosteling spirit in European youth. He also observed the very positive impact a residential setting had on adult education programs offered by the folk high schools. In 1975, five colleges and universities in New Hampshire offered the first Elderhostel to 200 pioneer participants. 

We've grown enormously since those early years. Elderhostel is a network of more than 1800 colleges, universities, conference centers, environmental education schools, museums, state and national parks, and other academic and cultural institutions throughout the United States, Canada, and in 45 countries overseas. In 1996, these institutions opened their doors and made their unique resources available to nearly 1/4 million Elderhostel participants. 

 
Who is eligible to attend?

Individuals 55 years of age and older are eligible. A spouse, or an adult companion, may also attend with an age-eligible participant. Elderhostel encourages diversity and welcomes people of all races, colors, and religions. 

 
How much does it cost and what does a typical program consist of?

The cost of attending Elderhostel is intentionally held to a modest level. The typical charge for a six-night program in the U.S. is $375. Institutions in Alaska and Hawaii as well as specialty sites may charge somewhat more.

The typical program consists of:

 
Registration fee; 
A six-night program beginning Sunday afternoon and ending Saturday morning;
Three courses, each meeting 1 ½ hours per day for five days (7 ½ contact hours each, or 22 ½ hours per week total instructional time);
Housing in typical dormitory accommodations (double occupancy, bathroom-down-the-hall or private bath);
17 meals provided (Sunday dinner through Saturday breakfast); 
Maximum enrollment of 50 (including commuters); 
Minimum enrollment of 20 (unless program can operate with fewer than 20 participants);
A variety of extracurricular activities; For all programs in the United States and Canada, the Hostelers will be responsible for arranging their own travel to and from the campuses they have selected.
Hostelships (scholarships) are available to help those people who cannot afford the cost of attending ELDERHOSTEL. Hostelships are not intended as travel grants, and are normally used "close to home."

 
What about housing and meals?

 
Simple but adequate housing accommodations are available usually in the  campus dormitories of a host institution. Participants may room with a spouse or traveling companion or come alone and form some lasting friendships. Rooms are usually double occupancy with a bathroom down the hall. Single rooms may be available at an additional charge. At programs not located on a college campus, hostelers may stay in hotels, motels, inns or RVs (recreational vehicles).

 
Meals are served in campus cafeterias. While Elderhostelers are usually favorably impressed by the generally high quality of institutional food, it is intended to be wholesome and nutritious but not fancy or up to gourmet standards. At off campus programs, hostelers may eat at a restaurant or cafe, or have nutritious "portable" meals.

 
What may be expected during the week?

An intellectual smorgasbord of courses in the liberal arts and sciences. Each institution selects the courses it wishes to offer. There is something of interest to everyone.

 
Programs are one week in length, beginning on Sunday afternoon and ending Saturday morning. The program wraps up on Friday, often with a "farewell" gathering. After breakfast on Saturday, Elderhostelers are on their way home, to visit family or friends, or to attend their next Elderhostel program.

 
Participants will be offered these courses which meet for 60-90 minutes each weekday. Generally speaking, enrollees are only required to attend one course, but they are scheduled so all these can be taken.

 
Courses are not for credit, there are no exams, grades or required homework. Whether a person has a Ph.D. or never graduated from high school, all they need to enjoy a program is a lifetime of experience and an inquiring mind.

 
Extracurricular activities are unique and distinctive at each Elderhostel program. Although our programs operate on a limited budget, in many instances, institutions already have a regular schedule of cultural, educational and recreational events. Occasionally, the campus staff will organize programs of particular interest to Elderhostelers. Also, each campus will try to schedule sufficient free time for you to get acquainted with your fellow hostelers or to see some of the local area.

 
At a typical program, you will find 20-50 participants from all walks of life and from all corners of the U.S.

 
What if someone has a physical disabiltiy?

 
Participants with physical disabilities are welcome. However, as the physical limitations of individuals can vary, so do the physical characteristics of each campus. Generally, we are notified well in advance and have already made arrangements for these individuals. Most accommodations are approved for disabled people.

 
Who are Elderhostlers?

 
Elderhostel is a great way to exchange ideas, sample college life and make new friends with active, interesting people with whom you can share the joys of a new experience. It's a unique opportunity to combine study with the adventure of exploring someplace new in a friendly and supportive academic environment.

 
The national office has periodically surveyed hostelers to find out who these active, educationally curious folks are. Based on our latest survey, here are some key facts about the people we serve:

 
Sixty-one percent are female; thirty - nine percent are male. 
Fifty percent are 70 years or older; Thirty - one percent are between the ages of 65 and 69; Nineteen percent are 64 years or younger.
Seventy percent are married; Twenty percent are widowed; 
Sixty-nine percent are retired completely. 
Forty-six percent have a four-year college degree or more; twenty percent have a Masters Degree.
Sixty-seven percent attended their most recent Elderhostel program with a spouse or relative; Twenty-seven percent with a friend.
Seventy-eight percent were completely satisfied with the last Elderhostel they attended; only two percent were somewhat dissatisfied.
Courses offered and location were a major factor in selecting programs for over seventy percent of those surveyed; sleeping and bathroom arrangements were also given a lot of consideration by over fifty percent of those surveyed.
Twenty-seven percent traveled between 200 and 500 miles to their last program; twenty-five percent traveled between 500 and 1000 miles; thirty-two percent traveled more than 1000 miles.
