Elk in Southwestern Colorado
by Stephanie Lanier
 
Elk are amazing animals. As  they travel many miles during a year, their bodies adapt to the climate, weather, and food availability. People  enjoy watching, studying, hunting, and eating them. Elk are a part of our lives. This paper tells facts about elk in the Southwestern Colorado.

I live in this region; my family owns land where elk live for most of the winter.  We don’t own the elk, but we have an arrangement with the Colorado Division of Wildlife which allows us each fall to lease the land to hunters. The hunters come during the hunting season and hope to find the perfect elk for them to shoot.

Elk are beautiful.  It is such a thrill to see a large herd in the open. The body of an elk is usually a light brown, the neck and legs are dark brown, and the rump is a light beige.  A cow might weigh 500 pounds, grow to be 4-1/2 feet at the shoulder, and 6-1/2 feet from nose to tail.   Bulls will usually weigh around 700 pounds, grow to be about 5 feet at the shoulders, and 8 feet from nose to tail. Their antlers often weigh up to 40 pounds.   A newborn calf might weigh just 35 pounds.

In late June or early July, when the days get warmer and longer, elk slowly make their way to the mountains where food is plentiful and humans are not. Bulls move faster and go to higher elevations than cows and calves. Cows usually move up the mountain until they are ready to give birth. Then they stop and wait until the calf is a couple of weeks old.

For most of the year cows and calves will stay together in groups called a harem. Bulls either go together in small bachelor groups or stay alone. Then when the summer is over and breading season starts, bulls and cows will get together and start heading off the mountain to warmer areas. This season is called the rut.

The gestation of a cow elk is 8-1/2 months. In the spring when a cow is ready to give birth, she will leave the rest of the herd to find a place to have her baby. This way, predators are not as likely to find the calf because it is not with the rest of the group.  Usually, she will find a place in the thick trees or brush.

When the calf is born, the mother eliminates any birth signs that might let a predator know where the mother and calf are.   The cow licks the calf’s whole body, devours the placenta and any soil or plants that might have gotten soaked. While the calf is growing, the mother is very protective and will do anything to protect the new calf.

Each day, on average, elk will eat about three pounds of food for every 100 pounds of body weight. During the summer they eat as much as they can so their bodies will store fat, and they will be better off for the coming winter season. The plants during the winter are usually scarce. 

In this area the elk eat mostly bitter brush and service berry, and once in a while they eat oak brush. Elk are usually better off than deer because they can reach higher vegetation, are able to eat a wider variety of plants, and they can move through the deep snow a lot easier.

Elk shed every hair on their body twice a year. They have a winter coat and a summer coat. The winter coat is thick, and the hair is long. The summer coat is thin and helps them stay cool. During the winter elk eat less and rest more. They save the energy they have for things they must do.  Elk stay warm in the winter by making their hair stand on end, trapping more air and creating a thicker coat.

The habitat of elk and all other animals is very important.  Elk have evolved over thousands of years to live in a specific habitat.  When their food, water, shelter or space is suddenly altered or destroyed, many animals cannot adapt quickly enough to survive.

Biologists have learned many things about elk over the past many years from different studies. Last winter the game warden and his group came on our land to set up traps. The traps were about six feet tall and eight feet long. They were made of pipes and net. Inside the trap and just out side of it, the men laid hay on the ground, hoping the elk would eat the hay and step inside the trap.

In order to capture the elk, the biologists have to know where the elk are likely to be at that time of year and what routes they will most likely take. Then the game officers can set up the traps along the trails.

Last year when they did this on our land, they set up about six traps and caught eight elk. When an elk walks into the trap, it stays there until the wildlife people get there. Then, suddenly about four men go inside, grab its neck and tackle the elk. When the elk is down, the men put a radio collar on around the neck.

Having men tackle the elk causes less stress than if the workers were to use a tranquilizer.  If a tranquilizer is used, the animal may not wake up for a number of hours. Tranquilizers sometimes hurt or kill the animal. The drugs use are not healthy.

About six times a year they try to fly around the area in an airplane. They are able to pick up signals from the elk with collars on and see how far they travel throughout the year. This year, we know that they have traveled throughout at least a 50-mile area. They have gone up in the mountains during the summer, then back down to lower elevations, our land, during the winter.

Some of the other things they do to learn about elk are taking blood samples, body measurements, and other kinds of samples from them. They have people watch and study the animals to learn about their behavior.

When wildlife managers learn about the habitat of different areas that elk are living in, they sometimes manipulate it so it will benefit the elk. For example, last spring we planted some grass in our pasture for the elk and other wildlife. Sometimes people will fertilize the grass that is already there.

In one small area here in Colorado, there could be herds of at least one thousand. When that many elk are together, they can damage the land or fences, they can eat the grass that was intended for domestic cows to eat, and more. They also might get diseases from each other or somewhere else. Sometimes they literally eat themselves out of house and home. In these circumstances, it is good for hunters to come and reduce the population. Hunting has been used as an elk management technique to keep the number of elk in balance with their habitat. Wolves, bears, mountain lions, and coyotes also help keep the numbers down.

If there are not too many, elk can be of help to the land. As they travel along, they break up the soil with their hooves. The indention of the hoof print holds water and other nutrients. It also provides sheltered places for seeds to germinate.

About 200 years ago there were many more thousands of elk around the world than there are now. Today there is about one million in North America. For quite a while wildlife managers have tried to increase the number of elk. They say that the numbers will probably never reach as high as they used to be. The elk can not compete with humans and the things we have done. When we build houses in the forests, or cut down trees to build houses, we are ruining their habitat. The chances of humans giving up their land or habits for the elk are very slim.

"It might seem that the western United States has enough land for people and wildlife, but essential wildlife habitat is being lost every year to human use. In Colorado alone, wildlife habitat is lost at the rate of about four acres every hour, which adds up to 35,000 acres of habitat every year" (Where).
