Ricks College News Release, Feb. 19, 1998

President of LDS Church to preside
at inauguration of new Ricks president


The president of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints will preside at the inauguration of the new Ricks College president Friday, Feb. 27.

Following a processional of faculty members from Ricks and other educational institutions, Church President Gordon B. Hinckley will give the charge to David A. Bednar, who will be officially installed as 14th president of the church-owned junior college with 8,350 students.

The inauguration will be held at 11 a.m. in the Hart Auditorium at Ricks College. Because of limited seating, tickets are required in not only the Hart, but overflow rooms in the Kirkham and Taylor buildings and the Manwaring Center. The inauguration also will be carried over the Ricks College owned radio station KRIC, 100.5 FM.

Events will begin with the Inaugural Showcase Thursday, Feb. 26, at 7:30 p.m. in the Hart Auditorium. The free showcase will highlight several Ricks College performing and music groups. The fast-paced show will feature folk dancers from the American Folk Ensemble, Ballroom Dance Team, Symphonic Band, Men's Chorus, Percussion Ensemble, Bluegrass Band, Showtime Company and Chelsea Bagley, this year's homecoming queen.

A public reception to meet the new president will be held following the inauguration from 3 to 5 p.m. in the Special Events Room of the Manwaring Center. That evening a combined inaugural ball and preference dance will be held.

Bednar assumed his duties as president July 1. He replaced Steven D. Bennion, who served as president for eight years before accepting the position of president of Southern Utah University in Cedar City, Utah.

He says he never aspired to become a college president. "I loved teaching," says the former business management professor from the University of Arkansas in Fayetteville.

"The best job in the world is to be a full professor," he says. "But I left the best job in the world for an even better job."

He didn't come up through the usual ranks as most college presidents. It was his work in the LDS Church that brought him to the attention of church officials who made the appointment. The college professor had served twice as a stake president, then as a regional representative and most recently as a member for the LDS Church's Fifth Quorum of the Seventy.

Bednar became interested in business management and organizational behavior following his church mission in Germany during the early 1970s where he experienced leadership and organizational challenges. Besides teaching, in recent years he also ran a private consulting business where he worked with such corporations as Wal-Mart, which is headquartered in Arkansas.

He was raised in San Leandro, Calif., near San Francisco. He met his wife, Susan, a native of Afton, Wyo., at BYU following his two-year church mission in Germany.

He earned a master's degree in organizational communication at BYU in 1977 and completed his doctorate at Purdue in 1980.

Since 1980, he was on the business school faculty at the University of Arkansas except for 1984-86 when he taught at Texas Tech University. He recently has been teaching courses in organizational behavior, team management and managerial communication.
