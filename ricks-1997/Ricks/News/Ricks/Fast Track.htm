Ricks College News Release, November 3, 1998

Students find first lap around
Fast Track to be promising

By Doug Anderson
Ricks College News Bureau

REXBURG, Idaho--Forget the old adage "Slow and steady wins the race." These students come off the starting blocks fast and hard. They want just one thing: to finish fast.

After last year's initial run, Fast Track, a program allowing students to complete their associate's degree in about a year, has officially run out of the starting gates and students in the program who have been on the academic go since summer are poised to finish by summer of 1999.

Midway through fall semester, those involved in Fast Track seem to agree: so far, so good.

Freshman Emily Alsop from Salt Lake City found out about Fast Track through the Ricks College web page on the Internet. When she began attending Ricks during the 1998 second summer term, she immediately signed up to participate in the program.

"It's nice knowing that by next August I'll be done and heading to a different school," Alsop said, who plans to become a child-advocate lawyer.

Fast Track students give up time during the summer to begin their studies at Ricks. The usual Fast Track student comes with Advanced Placement or college credit earned while still high school students to give them a quicker start and easier academic load to complete the program. After taking a normal number of credits during the fall and winter semesters, Fast Track students take more classes the following summer to graduate.

The program is designed to increase the "through put" of the school, says Kip Harris, assistant director of admissions and coordinator of the program, enabling more students to attend Ricks on a yearly basis. According to Harris, some students have been able to attend Ricks in a year's time with careful planning in the past, but this program now gives students a guide to accomplish the task as well as offers guaranteed scholarships for their summer attendance.

Helping others to have a Ricks experience by completing her associate's degree early was not something Alsop even thought about when she began Fast Track. But after spending some summer terms and half of fall semester on campus, she believes Ricks has an atmosphere everyone should experience.

"(Ricks College) is good for anyone," Alsop says. "It's good that I'm only here a year and a half so that someone else can get in next fall."

David Olsen of Irvin, Texas, first came to Ricks last May as a 16-year-old following his junior year of high school.

"I wanted to get as much schooling out of the way as possible before my mission," he said. The business management major adds, "It's exciting to me to get one step closer to getting a real job."

Because mistakes in scheduling the right classes at the proper times could be detrimental to Fast Track students, special attention is given by the academic advisers to ensure that students are taking the right courses. Advisers especially focus on the summer terms to make sure the classes they should take are available.

"If they don't take the classes they need in the summer, they're stuck," says Sharon Hansen, a Fast Track adviser for the elementary education department.

She says it is important to give Fast Track students options of classes they could take so they aren't left credits short of graduating in the summer term without the necessary classes available. The tight scheduling and constant workload are two reasons why Hansen doesn't recommend Fast Track to everyone.

"We tell (those involved in Fast Track), 'There is a good chance that there is going to be burnout and you are welcome to stop Fast Track at any point,'" she says.

Alsop says burnout is possible, but she tries to make time for friends and fun activities on the weekend to create balance in her life. "Anyone can have burnout, but the social aspect is a priority too," she said.

Olsen doesn't believe his academic demands affects his social life. "I don't feel like I'm missing anything at all," he says.
