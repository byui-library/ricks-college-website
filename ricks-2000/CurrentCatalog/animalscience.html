The Department of Animal Science has two programs that prepare students for the agricultural/livestock work or for transfer to a four-year university.

Beef Production Management is a technical program designed to prepare students for management positions in the livestock industry. Animal Science offers an associate degree to prepare for university transfer programs. Degrees are offered in the following areas:

Associate Degree in Beef Production Management

This technical program allows students to apply practical, state-of-the-art management techniques as ranch managers. Skills in practical veterinary medicine, feedlot operation, nutrition, farming, animal reproduction and the business aspects of cattle ranching are stressed in the courses. The program is designed to prepare students to meet the required needs of the industry. Beef Production Management extends over two academic years, plus one summer term. An eight-week internship is required.

Associate Degree in Arts and Sciences with Major in Animal Science
This option is designed for students who plan to transfer to a four-year university to pursue studies in Animal Science or Ag Business. See Biology Department for Pre-Veterinary information.

Internship
Beef Production and Animal Science Programs offer eight weeks of occupational internship to give students on-the-job training. Students must maintain a GPA of 2.0 or better to qualify for internship. The students are taught management concepts and techniques by participating ranch owners and managers. Beef Production students must complete an 8-week internship. Animal Science students are encouraged to complete a 4 to 8 week internship.

Facilities|
The Department of Animal Science is housed at the Ricks College Livestock Center located five miles west of Rexburg. Facilities include indoor and outdoor arenas, a feedlot, a horse barn, corrals, meat processing lab, teaching stations, and a 140 -acre farm.
Travel to and from this facility is the responsibility of the student.

Career Outlook
The employment opportunities for students graduating in Beef Production are good. Graduates primarily find jobs as ranch managers, herdsmen, marketing specialists, feedlot managers, or store managers.

Transfer
Animal Science graduates experience a smooth transfer to most four year universities.

COURSE DESCRIPTIONS
   Credits
AS 100 Orientation

 1

One hour class per week ( open to all students)

This course will acquaint students with the challenges and adjustments needed to be successful in the college setting, use of available facilities and services, and discussion on the future possibilities in Animal Agriculture. (Fall)

 
 AS 120 Horsemanship

 1

Two hour lab per week Lab Fee: $25
Prerequisite: Consent of instructor

A course designed to help the student and his horse in working with cattle. Roping emphasized. Field trip required.
(Students must provide a horse).

 
AS 130 Basic Equitation

 1

Travel/Equipment Fee: $60

Western riding skills emphasizing establishment of a balanced seat and proper use of aids. Instruction in handling, grooming, saddling, and supplying the horse.

 
AS 150 Trails and Trailriding

 1

Travel/Equipment Fee: $40
Prerequisite: Consent of instructor

Provides student with background knowledge and skill of basic trailriding horsemanship. The use, care, and maintenance of trail stock in the trailriding environment.

 
AS 151 Horse Packing and Outfitting

 2

Travel/Equipment Fee: $60
Prerequisite: AS 130 or consent of instructor
Field trips are required

Techniques of horse packing in wilder-ness areas including equipment, stock management, route planning, camp set up, cooking, and first aid. (Summer)

 
AS 165 Judging

 1

Prerequisite: Consent of instructor
4-6 hours of lab per week
Travel Fee: $10

Basics of judging techniques in evaluating cattle. (1st block, Fall)

 
AS 210 Livestock Production

 3

Three hours of lecture per week
Field trip required

Study of beef and dairy cattle, swine, sheep, horses, poultry, and alternative livestock. Basic principles in breeding, nutrition, health, and management practices.

 
 AS 220 Feed and Nutrition

 3

Three hours of lecture and/or lab per week

Digestion, absorption, metabolism, identification, and composition of feeds. Introduction to feed analysis and selection of rations.

 
AS 221 Anatomy & Physiology

 3

Three hours of lecture per week

A systems approach to the study of animal anatomy and physiology. Includes structure and function of the cell, skeletal, muscular, nervous, digestive, and reproductive systems. Practical applications of anatomy and physiology and their relation to diseases and disorders.

 
AS 222 Animal Reproduction

 3

Three hours of lecture and/or lab per week Lab Fee: $20

A study of reproduction in cattle and applied practices to maintain reproductive efficiency. Subject matter will include artificial insemination, breeding problems, genetics and other related topics.

 
AS 223 Practical Veterinary Skills

 3

Two hour lecture and two hours of lab per week Lab Fee: $10

Applied use of skills needed by livestock producers and ranchers to maintain healthy cattle and correct health problems when needed. Animals will be used in labs where applicable.

 
AS 225 Beef Production

 3

Three hours of lecture per week

Topics include: Management of winter maintenance, pre-calving, calving, post-calving, breeding season, weaning, marketing options, bull selection, feedlot, and facilities.

 
 AS 230 Principles of Meat Science

 3

 Two hours of lecture and a three hour lab per week Lab Fee: $15

Live animal evaluation through processing of beef, hog, and lamb carcasses. Slaughter, cutting, curing, smoking, and cooking of meat.

 
AS 231 Intermediate Equitation

 1

Travel/Equipment Fee: $60
Prerequisite: Consent of Instructor

Instructor level skills in horsemanship. Horsemanship skills in western riding, barrel racing, pole bending, team penning, team roping, and trailriding.

 
AS 232 Colt Breaking

 2

Travel/Equipment Fee: $40
Prerequisite: consent of Instructor

Techniques of training a colt. Extra time required out of class. School colts are trained by the students and sold at a sale in the spring. Enrollment in Fall and Winter Semesters required.

 
AS 235 Timed Events

 1-2

Travel/Equipment Fee: $30
Prerequisite: Consent of Instructor

Emphasis on team roping. Some other roping skills covered. Students must provide a horse.

 
AS 240 Horse Production

 3

Three hours of lecture per week

A study of horse production; including feeding, breeding, health, conformation, and business aspects of the horse industry.

 
AS 265 Fitting and Showing

 1

Prerequisite: Consent of instructor
One three hour lab per week

Preparation of cattle for exhibition; applied use of breaking to lead, washing, clipping and showing of beef cattle. (2nd block Fall)

 
AS 275 Grazing Management

 2

Five hours of lecture and one lab per week; an overnight field trip required. Travel Fee: $20

Identification, distribution, adaptation, and management of selected forage plants. Principles and practices of forage and grazing management.

 
AS 290 Individual Studies

 1-3
Independent study, special assignment and/or advanced inquiry in an area of special interest; approved after consultation with instructor in charge. (Upon request)  
AS 298 Occupational Internship

  2-4
This internship program is to provide the student with direct practical experience on a ranch, farm, dairy or an ag business. Under the direction of the ranch manager and Animal Science faculty, each student will spend four to eight weeks at an assigned intern station.
