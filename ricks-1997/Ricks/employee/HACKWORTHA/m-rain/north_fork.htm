The Snake River
audio
for R. K. Messer

 

 

 
Meandering Snake, unaware of your beauty,
Mixing melting snow and mountain rain,
You create a life of your own.

Pressing the clouds, the old Indian Sawtell
Watches your never-ending birth
As you spill out of Henrys Lake.

As lightly as a goose on the wing,
You slide over the mountain floor
And slip against the cold, wet shore.

Antelope graze in the meadow;
Aspen whiten the ridge
As you wander under Osborne Bridge
Whispering tales of ancient fishermen.

Dripping from a thousand springs,
You gather strength
From the Buffalo, Warm, and Fall Rivers.

O your stroll, you refresh we hurried men
Who have forgetten the fragrance of a fir.

Yet your soft motions are deceptive.
Coming of age, you show a mind of your own
And move pine and stone to follow your destiny.

Near Bear Gulch at Upper Mesa Falls,
Where cliff wall hang high,
You stand upright and roar like a grizzly.

Black waters tumble white into prisms of light
As you fall 114 feet onto yourself
And create a new, wet wind.

Clean fountain, quivering and dancing in the sun,
You spread green joy to everyone
Who receives life for their farms.

At South Butte near Menan you join your royal sister.
What a relationship! The genealogy
Of this branch is as rich as your own.

Then, stretching your wet arm west,
You help the Locksa, Clearwater, Salmon, and Selway
Cut the Columbia River Gorge.

Magnificent River, beautifier of the plain,
Restricted yet free, wild yet tame,
You turn darkness into light.

God spoke a truth when he pondered his stream
And a land flavored with potatoes and cream,
And proclaimed, "It is good."

(c) Allen Hackworth 1998
