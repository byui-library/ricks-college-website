GARDNER INDUCTED TO ATHLETIC HALL OF FAME

"I am honored to come back. This is home, and there is no other place I would rather come," alumnus Rulon Gardner said as he was inducted into the Ricks College Athletic Hall of Fame at a special luncheon on Jan. 25. The college acknowledged his distinction of garnering the highest honor ever achieved by a former Ricks athlete: Olympic gold!

While in Rexburg, Gardner and his wife Stacey attended a Viking wrestling match, meet friends and students, speak at an assembly on campus, and to be a part of the kick-off for Special Olympics. He credits faith with taking him to the heights of wrestling glory. "Believe in God," Gardner said, "and He'll give you everything you need."

Gardner has a learning disability and from an early age often faced obstacles in academics. The youngest of nine children growing up on a dairy farm in Star Valley, Wyo., he was nevertheless expected to pull his own share of work assignments. He said his past was instrumental to the evolution of becoming who he is. "I believe in hard work, in my personal ability, and I have the confidence to get the job done to the best of my ability," said Gardner. "We walk through life one step at a time. Life for me has been step-by-step progression."

Gardner complimented his Ricks College coaches, Bob Christensen and Fenton Broadhead, in encouraging him both on the mat and scholastically. He said he had asked Coach Christensen why he was recruited to wrestle at Ricks when the one match the coach had observed was the only match that he had lost as a high school wrestler. Coach Christensen's reply, "You had heart and you never gave up."

Gardner reflected, "I believed my coaches. They put responsibility on me and those qualities become instilled upon me. If my coaches believed in me, I would give 100 percent." Gardner's personal motto became, "Never give up. Never quit trying."

He now hopes to be a positive influence for other, "In my life, I have been blessed with so many things. If I can influence someone's life and let them know what kind of difficulties I have overcome, that is what I want to do." Gardner keeps things in prospective as he said, "My desire in life is to do my best. If I had never won this medal but knew I did my best, I would be successful." FOSSIL COLLECTION DONATED TO RICKS

A prestigious fossil collection has been donated to Ricks by a renowned scientist who recently retired from BYU. Dr. William D. Tidwell of Provo recently donated a portion of his private fossil collection that he acquired during his distinguished career. The collection of plant fossils, collectively known as a "flora," is the most diverse and complete known from the Carboniferous Period of North America. The flora dates from approximately 285-345 million years ago and contains representative species from forests that predated the dinosaurs.

Tidwell has discovered and named more than 100 fossil plant species from around the world. Upon retirement as a professor of botany at BYU, a number of museums throughout the world, including the Smithsonian, were interested in housing and studying Tidwell's collection. The collection is important because of its completeness and exceptional preservation. It will help demonstrate to students how plants have changed over the ages. Much of the collection came from the Manning Canyon Shale Formation in central Utah while other pieces came from throughout the United States. After the more than 150 pieces have been catalogued, part of the collection will be displayed at the Natural Sciences Museum in the Romney Building. The museum is generally open from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. on school days. (www.byui.edu/Ricks/News/Ricks/NewsReleases/fossil_010126.htm)

 

HISTORIC TEICHERT PAINTINGS ON DISPLAY IN TAYLOR BUILDING

Four historic painting by Latter-day Saint artist Minerva Teichert are now hanging in the John Taylor Building . The paintings are on loan from the Cokeville, Wyo., LDS church while it is being renovated. They will remain on display at Ricks until sometime toward the end of 2001, says Ricks art faculty member Gerald Griffin. The paintings are "The Song of Quetzalquatal" (a large painting depicting Christ's visit to the Americas), "Relief Society Sisters Quilting," "Christ on the Sea of Galilee," and "Pioneer Trek."

Teichert's art work reveals her love of service - women spinning, quilting, laundering, driving oxen and caring for children. She also loved to illustrate stories from the scriptures. Her work is in the collection of Brigham Young University in Provo and the Church Museum in Salt Lake City. She has had many illustrations from her Book of Mormon series appear in the LDS Church's Ensign magazine. In 1988 a major exhibition of her work was presented at the Church Museum of History and Art in Salt Lake City.

RICKS UNDERTAKES SURVEY ON BYU-IDAHO TRANSITION

Ricks College officials are asking area residents, alumni and students to tell them how they feel about the school's transition to Brigham Young University - Idaho.

The results of the survey will help administrators know what questions and concerns people have about the changes that will occur as the junior college becomes a baccalaureate-granting institution, says Brent Kinghorn, community services vice president. The survey is designed to see how much people understand about the transition to BYU-Idaho, what benefits they foresee with the proposed changes, and what concerns they have. To take the survey, log onto the college home page on the Internet at www.byui.edu and find the option "Transition Survey." (http://www.byui.edu/byuisurvey/ ) If you do not have access to the Internet, call the Public Relations Office at (208) 356-1150 and a survey will be mailed.
