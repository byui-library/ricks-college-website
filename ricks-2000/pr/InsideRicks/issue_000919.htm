TRANSITION UPDATE

President's Council communicates openly in regular question-and-answer sessions with students and employees. Recent sessions focused on the progress of the transition for Ricks College to become BYU-Idaho. President David A. Bednar said, "President's Council and Academic Council have met with the academic deans and department heads. The deans and department heads are working with their faculty and staff to develop proposals. Ideas are being generated. The transition is in progress."

Ricks is working with other campuses within the Church Educational System. "The preliminary discussions were mostly with BYU-Provo. We went through every program and talked about which ones might be offered here and which ones might not," said Academic Vice President Don Bird. "We will duplicate a lot. There are some programs in high demand, and it would be impractical to expect one campus to admit all the students desiring to be in the program. At the same time, there are some programs consistently under enrolled; it does not make sense to duplicate those programs."

The prospects for increased opportunities at the Rexburg campus is exciting for those seeking a bachelor's degree. Even as students become optimistic about staying for four years, President Bednar stresses caution. "Every student should have two plans of action. The first plan should be to obtain an associate degree and transfer to another four-year university. The second plan should be to stay at Ricks if in a timely manner a desired four-year major comes on line," said President Bednar. "Do not put all your eggs in one basket."

Be involved in helping the transition. President Bednar said, "At Ricks College, everyone is a teacher. We have nearly 9,000 student teachers. (1) Become a teacher. Do not feed rumor mills. Help people know when they are disseminating inaccurate information. (2) Help other people become equipped to be teachers. We will be open and provide information when we have it."

Updates and information are posted on the college's home page at www.byui.edu.

OLYMPIC DREAM COME TRUE

"When I came here, I had a dream of seeing somebody that I had coached march into the Olympic Stadium. It has been a long time," said Ferron Sonderegger, who began coaching track and field at Ricks College in 1987. As he sat with his family watching the opening ceremonies in Sydney, Australia, his dream came true.

When Marsha Mark first came to Ricks College as a scholarship student in 1994 from her native Trinidad, she was met at the airport by Coach Sonderegger. He said, "It was like she was one of our family members from day one." She proved to be an expert javelin thrower and was gently coached to expand her skills to include the heptathlon events: 100 meter high hurdles, 200 meter dash, 800 meter run, javelin, shot put, long jump, and high jump. Coach Sonderegger was impressed with her determination and desire to succeed. And succeed she did. She is the Ricks school record holder in the heptathlon and one-mile relay. She was the National Junior College Athletic Association champion in javelin in 1994. In the heptathlon, she nationally placed second in 1994 and third in 1995.

After graduating from Ricks, Marsha transferred to BYU where she recently completed her master's degree in social work. Coach Sonderegger kept in touch and noted her incredibly high values, her success in track and field, her baptism, and her temple marriage. Then came September 16, 2000, when Marsha Mark Beard marched into the Olympic Stadium as a member of the Trinidad Olympic team. She was ready for the Olympic heptathlon.

Coach Sonderegger sat at home with his family watching the opening ceremony on television. "I was near to tears as we watched all the different people, the different costumes, the different races," said Sonderegger. "I realized that these are all Heavenly Father's children. He loves every single one of them. LDS athletes there have such a great opportunity." Alphabetically the teams continued with the processional. Then came Trinidad and Marsha Mark Beard. "Our whole family instantly exploded with clapping to actually see her walk into the stadium. Maybe the response was because I coached her, but more importantly it was because of the kind of person that she is. I know that she will represent her country but also represent the Church in such a wonderful way. In this day and age, we think win, win all the time. But what an incredible opportunity it is to participate in the Olympics. It is a cultural experience, a celebration of life and youth. To be there with all the other people of the earth would be really something," said Sonderegger.

Also competing in the Olympics is former Ricks College student Rulon Gardner representing the United States in Greco-Roman wrestling 286-pound division.
