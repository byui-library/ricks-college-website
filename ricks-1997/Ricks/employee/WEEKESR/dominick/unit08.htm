Broadcast & Cable TV Today:  Unit 8

 

There are over 1,500 TV stations in operation today
 

Types of Television Stations
Commercial TV - 77% of stations
–Sell time to advertisers

Noncommercial TV - 23% of stations
–Not allowed to advertise
– Survive through donations from individuals, businesses, and the government

Types of Television Stations
VHF = Very High Frequency
–Channels 2 to 13

UHF = Ultra High Frequency
–Channels 14 and above

Network Television
Dominated by the Big Three
–ABC, CBS, NBC
– Strongest in the 1960s & 1970s

The New Networks
Fox Broadcasting Company: 1986
United Paramount Network (UPN): 1995
WB: 1995
Pax TV: 1998

Local Television
Television's Cash Cows: Network Owned & Operated (O & Os) stations
O & Os are generally in large markets like New York and Los Angeles
–WABC, KABC, WNBC, KNBC, WCBS, KCBS

Public Television
Some 400 hundred stations are classified as noncoms
Originally called Educational TV
PBS is a service not a network

Cable Television
Providing early subscribers with only 12 channels to over 100 channels in areas with
fiber optics and digital compression

Packaging Cable TV
Homes Passed (HP): All households that could subscribe to a cable system if they
wanted to
Basic Subscribers: Percentage of HP that actually subscribe
–Called Basic Penetration
Expanded Basic: Additional non-premium channels
Pay Households: Subscribers who pay for one premium channel like HBO or Showtime
Multipay Households: Subscribers who pay for more than one premium channel
Pay-Per-View (PPV): A special event like WWF, boxing, rock concert, etc.

TV Station Organization
General Manager (GM) or Station Manager is overall supervisor
Sales: Account Execs or Sales Reps sell time to clients (accounts)
Engineering: Operate, maintain, and repair equipment needed for local programs
Programming: Decides what network, syndicated and local programming to broadcast
News: Most local programming revolves around the local news team. Most visible
department of all

The Job Outlook
Keep an open mind about your career choice. The job you seek today may not even exist
when you graduate.
Despite the power and influence of broadcast and cable TV, the industries are in fact
quite small.
Less than 250,000 people work in these industries.
Be willing to start at small market stations, with a low salary, and relocating to get a
better job/salary.
Don't limit yourself
Explore allied fields
