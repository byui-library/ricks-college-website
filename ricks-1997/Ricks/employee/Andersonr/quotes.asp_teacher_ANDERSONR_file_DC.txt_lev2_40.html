Doctrine & Covenants Section 51
The Law of Consecration
1. Terminology
Although we speak of "the law of consecration and stewardship," the revelations in the D&C do not label it such. In the revelations it
is simply designated as "my law" (44:6),"the law" (58:36), "this law"(70:10), "laws of consecration"(105:29).
The "united order" (92:1) was a business partnership of about a dozen leaders of the Church organizedd March 1832 (D&C 78) and
disorganized April 1834 (D&C 104). The "order," also known as the "United Firm" (the early manuscripts of the revelations have the
word "firm" instead of "order"), or the Order of Enoch, operated upon the principles of consecration, commiting any surplus from their
endeavors to the benifit of the Church. They acquired more debts than surplus, and in the spring of 1834 divided up their holdings into
individual stewardships (D&C 104:19-46), forgave each members debts to the order, and appealed to the saints for donations to help
meet their indebtedness to their eastern lenders.
The "Literary Firm" (not so called in the revelations) consisted of those appointed by the Lord to be "stewards over the revelations"
(70:3). Organized Nov. 1831 the firm was concerned with the revelations , the New Translation of the Bible, the Church hymnal,
children's literature, Church newspapers, and a Church almanac (see Lyndon W. Cook, Joseph Smith and the Law of Consecration
[Provo, Utah:Grandin Book Co., 1985], p.44). The literary Firm also sought to operate according to the principles of consecration until
its demise in Aug. 1838 (ibid.,p.51).
2. Purposes, principles and implemation of consecration should be distinguished.
The Lord revealed several purposes for the law of consecration. The principles are constant and required for celestial living. The
implemation may take variouss formss, and has done so historically, depending on the circumstances and the spiritual maturity of the
saints.
a.Purposes-
*"That the Church may stand independent above all other creatures beneath the celestiiial world" (D&C 78:14).
*To prepare the saints for a place in the celestial kingdom (D&C 78:7).
*To stregthen Zion; to adorn her in beautiful garments, as a bride prepared and worthy of the Bridegroom (D&C
82:18;90:36;109:73-74;;33:17;58:11;65:3;133:10).
b.The principles include the following:
*ALL things belong to God--we are stewards (104:11-14).
*"Equality" (38:24-27;51:3,9;70:14;78:6;82:17).
*Agency (104:17).
*Accountabilty (72:3;104:13-18).
*Consecration of all--time, talents, possessions--with heart, mind and eye single to the glory of God, "every man seeking the intrest of
his neighbor" (82:19;64:34;88:67-68;98:12-14).
c. Implemation concepts for the financial part of the law of consecration revealed in the early 1830's.
*Consecration of everything to the Church by legal deed (42:30).
*Recieving stewardship by legal deed, according to circumstances, family, wants (inasmuch ass the wants are jusst--82:17), and needs
(42:32;51:3-4).
*If there is a surplus from the operaation of the stewardship, the SURPLUS GOES TO THE STOREHOUSE (42:33-34;82:17-19;83:4-6).
*If there is a deficit fromthe operation of the stewardship, the DEFICIT IS MADE UP FROM THE
STOREHOUSE (42:34;82:17-19;83:4-6)
*If a person leaves the Church or is excommunicated, he takes with him his stewardship, that which was legally deeded to him (51:5).
Efforts to implement the financial elements of consecration in the Church went through a number of phases or adjustments during the
Prophet Joseph Smith's day (see charts in the following pages [Cook, pp. 19, 32, 78, 91]). Later, President Brigham Young established a
variety of the "United Order" systems in many Mormon settlements, attempting to help the saints apply the principles of consecration.
3. Early implemation system suspended; principles remain
The Lord suspended the implemation system of the law of consecration in 1834 (see 105:34). The following commentary is instructive:
"President Joseph Smith, Jun., addressed the Council on various subjects, and in particular the consecration law ....The law of
consecration could not be kept here, and that it was the will of the Lord that we should desist from trying to keep it; and if persisted in,
it wouldd produce a perfect defeat of its object, and that he assumed the whole responsibility of not keeping it until proposed by
himself." (Extracts from the Minutes of the Iowa High Council , Friday,6 March 1840. History of the Church 4:93.)
Although the Church-wide implemation system as revealed inthe early 1830''s was suspended (also by revelation), it is intresting to
consider that the principles themselves have not been suspended. They are principles that have always been part of the gospel of
Jesus Christ, and charecteristic of the Lord's people who have applied the fulness of the gospel (i.e. the city of Enoch [Moses 7:17-18];
the Nephite golden era [4 Nephi 1:2-3,15-17]; and the early Christians [Acts 4:322-37]). The Lord has made clear that "Zion cannot be
built up unless it is by the principles of the law of the celestial kingdom; otherwise i cannot recieve her unto myself. And my people
must needs be chastened until they learn obedience, it must needs be, by the things which they suffer." (D&C 105:5-6.) "Zion--THE
PURE IN HEART" (D&C 97:21)is made up of people who implement the principles of consecration in their own lives under whatever
financial system is operative Church-wide. Such people consider all their earthly blessings as a stewardship and are pleased to share
the surplus with others.
Today, worthy saints make sacred covenants of consecration, accepting the fact that they are stewards in all areas of living. Through
the current system of "tithes and offerings," one can indeed (and many do) give their "surplus" for the benifit of the Kingdom of God,
in addition to "one-tenth of heir intrest annually." It is simply a matter of personal commitment rather tahn an institutional expectation
of everyone.
4. What of the future?
Ultimatly, Zion can only be redeemed (whether individually or collectively; whether place or spiritual condition) by obiedience to the
Lord's law of consecration. Just how the financial part of the law of consecration will be implemented when Zion, the New Jeruselem, is
fully established will be made known by the Lord to His duly appointed Church leaders at the proper time. Whether it will be similar to
the system revealed in the 1830's, or like the current procedure of tithes and offerings, or some mew approach that is not clear. What is
clear iss that whatever system is used, those involved will be fully and happily subscribe to the principles of stewardship, equality,
agency, and accountablity. And the purposes of the law will be fulfilled-- the Church will stand "independent above all creatures
beneath the celestial kingdom" (D&C 82:14), and the saints will be prepared for an eternal inheritance in the "celestial world" (D&C
28:7).
