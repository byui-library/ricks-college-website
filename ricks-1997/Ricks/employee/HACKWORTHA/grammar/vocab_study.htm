First read the definition.  Then learn the word.  The MLG software is the best way to learn these words and meanings.  These terms come mostly from literature and business.
 
1. a group of words
--  phrase
 
2. a group of words with a subject and a verb
--  clause
 
3. an implied comparison
--  metaphor
 
4. a written statement made under oath
--  affidavit
 
5. insincere language calculated to give the impression of piety or religious fervor
--  cant
 
6. descriptive writing which exaggerates or distorts certain qualities of an individual (could be a drawing or cartoon)
--  caricature
 
7. a person who holds something in trust for another, someone who may be a guardian of a  minor
--  fiduciary
 
8. "seize the day"  "eat and drink for tomorrow we die"  A common theme of 16th and 17th century English love poetry (two words)
--  carpe diem
 
9. the number of members required to be present in order to constitute a formal meeting
--  quorum
 
10. the purging or cleansing of one's emotion.  The term comes from Aristotle's Poetics
--  catharsis
 
11. "a type of rhetorical balance in which the second part is syntactically balanced against the first but with the parts  reversed."  For example, "Old King Cole was a merry old soul; a merry old soul was he."
--  chiasmas
 
12. a piece of literature that by common consent has been recognized for its superior quality. This term also applies to music.
--  classic
 
13. No two words have exactly the same meaning,  but these two words have similar meanings.  
--  synonyms
 
14. any expression that has been used so often that the expression has lost its freshness.  From a French word meaning "stereotype"
--  cliche
 
15. a quality that good writing should have which allows it to "stick together."
--  coherence
 
16. a familiar proverb
--  adage
 
17. something stated in exactly the same form as the original, word for word.
--  verbatim
 
18. having a harsh sound
--  cacophony
 
19. property offered as security for the repayment of a loan
--  collateral
 
20. the repetition of consonant and vowel sounds.  For example, "The fair breeze blew; the white foam flew."
--  alliteration
 
21. the person designated to receive the benefits from an insurance policy or will
--  beneficiary
 
22. "A rhetorical term applied to that figure of speech making casual reference to a famous historical or literary figure or event."  For example, "A Daniel come to judgment."
--  allusion
 
23.  "A comparison of two things, alike in certain respects;  particularly a method of exposition when an unfamiliar object is compared to a familiar one."  For example, consider Christ's  "Parable of the Sowers."
--  analogy
 
24. a temporary, conditional pledge OF PROPERTY to a creditor as security against a debt
--  mortgage
 
25. a metrical foot in verse consisting of three syllables, two unaccented and one accented  "Like a child  /  from the womb /,  like a ghost / from the tomb"  Shelley
--  anapest
 
26. in name only, not in actuality
--  nominal
 
27. the opposite: surplus of money
--  deficit
 
28. a duty on imported goods
--  tariff
 
29. false name
--  pseudonym
 
30. love for mankind often shown by making financial donations that help other people
--  philanthropy
 
31. comparing the bank's statement with the depositor's own records
--  reconciliation
 
32. the opposite: output
--  input
 
33. the opposite: assets
--  liabilities
 
34. "a figure of speech in which someone, some abstract quality, or a non-existent personage is addressed as though present."  For example, "Hail, Holy Light, offspring of Heaven firstborn!"
--  apostrophe
 
35. "the prevailing tone or mood of a literary work" -
-  atmosphere
 
36. a story of one's life written by himself
--  autobiography
 
37. a word of opposite meaning
--  antonym
 
38. the study of God and His relationship to the universe -
-  theology
 
39. the appearance of God to man
--  theophany
 
40. the opposite: insolvent
--  solvent  (Solvent means "able to pay all legal debts." )
 
41. a form of narrative verse intended for singing
--  ballad
 
42. the opposite: plaintiff (The plaintiff is the one who commences the lawsuit to obtain a remedy for an injury to his or her rights.) -
-  defendant
 
43. the opposite: depreciation  (Depreciation means the lowering the price or estimated value.)
--  appreciation
 
44. another name for "poet"
--  bard
 
45. the dictionary meaning of a word
--  denotation
 
46. open, not secret
--  overt
 
47. covered, hidden
--  covert
 
48. the opposite: deficit
--  surplus
 
49. the use of words in oral and written discourse
--  diction
 
50. the meaning of a word according to your experience with the word
--  connotation
 
51. a curse
--  malediction
 
52. a blessing -
-  benediction
 
53. to renounce or give up the title to something
--  abdicate
 
54. quaint, ordinary, familiar
--  colloquial
 
55. in a play the actor talks out loud to himself
--  soliloquy
 
56. the final outcome of the main dramatic complication in a work.  Solution of the mystery.-
-  denouement  ( pronounced day new maw)
 
57. a word processing or data processing term which describes the materials or information that has been produced
--  output
 
58. a written travel plan which shows arrivals, departures, and reservations
--  itinerary
 
59. an informal conference or group discussion
--  colloquium
 
60. a determination of property value by an expert
--  appraisal
 
61. a public officer who administers oaths and witnesses signatures (two words)
--  notary public
 
62. obsessed with one's own greatness
--  megalomania
 
63. the amount of money left after all the bills have been paid
--  net
 
64. The (effect/affect) of her extra study paid off.
--  effect   (Connect the e's to help remember   thE Effect.)
 
65. to plead in favor of someone or something
--  advocate
 
66. hobby
--  avocation
 
67. poetry or prose which is intended to teach a lesson.  Such writing is said to be
--  didactic
 
68. using ambiguous terms in order to deceive
--  equivocate
 
69. a calling together or assembling by summons
--  convocation
 
70. jerky, rude, poorly written poetry
--  doggerel
 
71. plant eating
--  herbivorous
 
72. meat eating
--  carnivorous
 
73. eating both plants and animals
--  omnivorous
 
74. an inscription on a tombstone
--  epitaph
 
75. we finally (past/passed) the needed bill
--  passed   (Remember that "past" is an adjective;  "passed"
is a verb.
 
76. the student (council/counsel) voted  to  have  more  dances
--  council    ("Counsel" is advice.)
 
77. a three-line form of Japanese poetry having five, seven, and five syllables
--  haiku
 
78. an expression which cannot be translated literally into another language.  For example, "kick  the bucket."
--  idiom
 
79. extravagant exaggeration
--  hyperbole
 
80. The plane leaves for the (capitol / capital) of Montana in 15 minutes. (the city)
- --capital    ("Capitol" is the building.  Think of the "o" as the dome.)
 
81. Will this wet weather (affect / effect) your cold?
--  affect    (Use "affect" as a verb, "effect" as a noun.)
 
82. Will you (accept / except) responsibility for your own life?
--  accept    (They were all here "except" Harry.  Please "accept" my answer.)
 
83. Moral (principals / principles) help glue our society together.
--  principles
 
84. an exact copy
--  facsimile
 
85. a word you should NEVER use in place of "regardless" -- irregardless    (The word "regardless" means "without regard.")
 
86. Your payment is applied to both the interest and (principle / principal).
--- principal  (The "pal" ending applies both to the leader of the school and money.)
 
87. a word picture which appeals to the senses
--  image
 
88. It looks like (your / you're) going to need a new suit soon.
--  you're
 
89. done by hand
--  manual
 
90. Would you please (site / cite) an example from the story?
---  cite     ("site" is a location.)
 
91. What was the result of (their/there/they're) survey?
- -  their    (Remember that "they're" means "they are.")
 
92. We say one thing but mean the opposite (two words)
--  rhetorical irony
 
93. Please (proceed / precede) to the back of the room.
- -  proceed
 
94. the science of the culture and origin of man
--  anthropology
 
95. the audience knows something the actors do not and so the actor's words have a double meaning for the audience (two  words)
- -  dramatic irony
 
96. something written after the main body of a work (noun)
--  postscript
 
97.  an income payable at stated intervals
--  annuity
 
98.  I (could of / could have) gone too.
--  could have     ("Could of" is improper.)
 
99.  not clear; multiple meanings
--  ambiguous
 
100   twice a year.
--  biannual or semiannual
 
101.  once every two years
--  biennial
 
102.  once every three years
--  triennial
 
103.  resembling man or in the shape of a man
--  anthropoid
 
104.  I borrowed some money  (off  of / from / off) Martha
--  from
 
105.  mean, cutting irony
--  sarcasm
 
106.  the word to which the pronoun refers
--  antecedent
 
107.  an apparent contradiction.  For example, "The pen is mightier than the sword."
--  paradox
 
108.  The year between one balancing of accounts and another is what kind of year?
--  fiscal
 
109.  a humorous imitation of another (usually serious) piece of writing
--  parody
 
110.  wholesale dealer who buys from the manufacturer and sells to the retailer
--  jobber  
 
111. The rise and fall of prices is known as
--  fluctuation
 
112.  the obligations or debts of a business or person
--  liabilities
 
113.  evidence of property ownership that is held by a third party until certain conditions are met
--  escrow
 
114.  an explanatory remark thrown into the body of a statement, set apart with these marks ( )
--  parenthesis
 
115.  a verb that means "to suggest; to counsel." The "s" sounds like a "z."
--  advise
 
116.  the deliberate and systematic killing of an entire national or racial group
--  genocide
 
117.  to restate something in your own words but not to change the meaning of the original
--  paraphrase
 
118.  a collection of information stored on a disk or in the computer's memory
--  database
 
119.  a noun that refers to the suggestions received when someone advises
--  advice
 
120.  a merger or joining of two or more businesses into a single company
--  amalgamation
 
121.  all the property owned by an individual or company
--  assets
 
122.  origin, creation, beginning
--  genesis
 
123.  existing at or from a person's birth
--  congenital
 
124.  Which of the twins has the best grades?  There is an error in this above sentence.
--  better
 
125.  well-know and famous
--  eminent     (Don't confuse this word with "imminent" (about to happen).
 
126.  well-born, refined, elegant
--  genteel
 
127.  Smoking is the least of the two evils.  An error is in the this sentence.
--  less    (Use "least" if  you compared three or more.)
 
128.  that which is applicable to all members of a class or group
-  generic

--  should have    ("Should of" is non standard English
 
135.  one bound by the terms of a signed document -
-  signatory
 
136.  a flag, banner, or emblem
--  ensign
 
137.  Working hard in a class actually improves your (morale / moral).
