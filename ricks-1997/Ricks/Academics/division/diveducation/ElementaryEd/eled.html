Department Mission Statement

We believe that students are best served by teachers that:

1. Possess and demonstrate comprehension, communication, and creativity.

2. Value and implement an integrated liberal arts curriculum.

3. Conceive and structure experiences that allow students to become self-directed learners.

4. Use and adapt technology as a tool to meet student needs and further self-directed learning.

5. Develop and maintain balance through self-renewal.

Matriculating within the Elementary Education Dept.

Currently Ricks College serves approximately 7,500 full time students. Over 700 of those students are interested in graduating from the Elementary Education Department. Because of limited resources it is necessary to govern entry into the department.

Initial Major

When incoming Freshmen apply to Ricks College they are asked to declare a major. Students interested in the Elementary Education Department, must first enter as a Pre-Education major. The major code to be entered on the application is 300.

Incoming freshmen may not enter as any other major within the Elementary Education program.

Matriculating

Freshmen must complete at least one semester at Ricks before being admitted to the Elementary Education Dept. To matriculate, the student must:

  • have finished 1 semester of college course work,
  • have completed ElEd 100 / 150 (Introduction to Education) with a "B" or better,
  • have earned a cumulative 2.75 or higher grade point average for all college work, and
  • complete a matriculation form, obtain her or his advisor's signature, and submit the matriculation form, along with an unofficial transcript, to the ElEd. Dept. secretary.

The matriculation form may be picked up in Smith 350, the Elementary Education Dept. Office. The unofficial transcript may be obtained from the Registrars' office in the Administration building, there is no cost for an unofficial transcript.

Notification

Students who are accepted into the Elementary Education department within one of the majors will be notified by phone or by mail. As e-mail becomes more readily available to all students on campus, students may also be notified by e-mail.

Following Through

Once a student has been admitted to a major within the ElEd. Dept., the student should make an appointment with her or his new advisor immediately. With the help of the advisor, the student should plan out the course work and the time frame in which the course work will be taken in order to graduate successfully.

It is the student's responsibility to complete all course work, and to track progress. It is the advisor's responsibility to assist the student by answering questions concerning which course work is most appropriate and what course work is best to facilitate a smooth transfer to the university or college of the student's choice after Ricks.

Brigham Young University Courses offered at Ricks

Art 326 - Art for Elementary Teachers

Children's conceptual and expressive development in art. A balanced program emphasizing content from the art disciplines: (1) studio production, (2) art criticism, (3) art history, and (4) aesthetics.

DA 326 - Rhythm and Dance

Methodology course for elementary education majors; creative dance and structured rhythmic dance forms.

ElEd 310R - Introduction to Learning and Teaching

Course consists of two major components: (1) teaching for learning, and (2) cognitive understanding of effective teaching and learning. Demonstrated competence in both components is required for admittance to the professional program.

ElEd 340 - Children's Literature

Trends and titles in children's and adolescent literature. Reading and critiquing as well as practical uses of children's literature in both school and home. Must be taken concurrently with ElEd 240.

ElEd 351 - Multicultural Education

Cultural issues related to public education that promote constructive interaction among people of differing economic, social, racial, ethnic, and religious backgrounds.

ElEd 493R - Independent Projects

Independent readings and / or projects within education.

EdLdr 452 - Professional Teacher

The professional, historical, philosophical, sociological and financial aspects of education in the United States.

EdPsy 400 - Exceptional Child

Basic principles and legislative issues in effectively communicating and collaborating with professionals, parents, and other service providers in meeting the needs of individuals with disabilities in inclusive, educational environments. Must be taken concurrently with ElEd 205.

Eng 313 - Advanced Writing for Elementary Teachers


Improving writing proficiency by focusing on the writing process, collaborative writing, and techniques for teaching writing. Library research paper. Carries GE advanced writing credit.

Fam315 - Language Development


Philosophical, social, intellectual, and emotional contexts of language acquisition and usage.

HS 361 - Health for Elementary Teachers

This course emphasizes the teacher's role as health educator; appraisal of typical elementary student health problems.

IS 286 - Instructional Technology in Teaching


Using instructional design, visual design, and differentiated staffing principles along with multimedia authoring systems, telecommunication and other computer-based tools in the development of education applications.

IS 301 - Learning

Basic principles of Educational Psychology applied to teaching including learning theory, instructional strategies, assessment of learning outcomes, and classroom inquiry.

Music 378

Establishing a classroom environment for musical development, emphasis on appropriate songs, activities, resources, and teaching strategies. Concurrent enrollment with Music 278 required.

PE 169 - Skill Acquisition and Analysis

Introduction and drill in basic skills.

ThF 372 - Drama in the Elementary Classroom


Creative dramatics/improvisation, puppetry, storytelling, reader's theater/choral speaking, theater production with children.

Graduating with an A.A. degree in Elementary Education


All pertinent information regarding graduation requirements may be found in the Ricks College catalog. The information provided below is general in nature and may not pertain to every student in every circumstance. Refer to an advisor and the catalog for answers to questions that may affect you.

Students in Elementary Education must realize that not only do they have to meet Ricks' graduation criteria, but they must also be concerned with transfer institution and state teacher certification requirements. Fulfilling all of these programs can be a challenge, but it can be done successfully with careful planning, thorough reading of catalogs, and frequent meetings with an advisor. Occasionally, a phone call to an advisor at the transfer institution may also be required. Don't be afraid to ask questions!

Finally, because transfer institutions differ in their requirements it is critical that you decide early-on as to your transfer school of choice. Then, develop a study plan to meet the transfer school's requirements while at Ricks College. Problems most frequently arise when you don't know where you want to go.

Faculty and Staff of the Elementary Education Department

The Elementary Education Dept. at Ricks College was established in 1989. At that time only three faculty members constituted the department. Since 1989 the department has expanded to six faculty members, a full-time secretary, and over 700 students. The current faculty and staff members include:

Joseph Alldredge, Ed.D.

Joseph Alldredge has taught at Ricks College for the past 17 years. His preferred areas of expertise are Children's Literature and reading. Joe began working with youth as an executive with the Boy Scouts of America. His public school experience includes teaching third and fifth grades and serving as an elementary principal. Dr. Alldredge has degrees from BYU, Southwest Missouri State, and an Ed.D. From BYU. Joe and his wife have eight children.

Sharon Hansen, M.Ed.

Sharon Hansen taught for 13 years in the public schools, kindergarten through eighth grade. She has degrees from BYU and USU. Her fields of specialization are Gifted and Talented education. Sharon married Steve Hansen in 1993; combined they have eight children . Sharon is the chairman of the Teacher Education Department at Ricks.

Blaine Robertson, Ed.S.

Blaine Robertson holds degrees from Ricks College, Idaho State University, and Utah State University. His teaching specialties include basic computer applications, computer applications in the classroom, and technology's impact on instruction. Blaine has been a secondary teacher, as well as principal at the Elementary, Jr. High, and High School levels. He is married and has six children. His hobbies include reading, woodworking, learning new computer uses, browsing the web, and talking with people.

Linda Lloyd, Ph.D.

Linda Lloyd recently completed a Ph.D. from the University of Idaho. Previously she has earned degrees from Utah State University and Idaho State University. Linda has taught in the elementary schools in Rexburg, and been an elementary principal and Jr. High assistant principal. Dr. Lloyd has been a single parent for a number of years, but has worked hard to balance the responsibilities of mother and educator.

Kendell Nielsen, M.S.

Kendell Nielsen received his B.A. Degree from Southern Utah University, and an M.S. degree in music education. He played the French Horn professionally, including a stint with the Utah Symphony Orchestra. He served an LDS mission to Japan. His professional experience includes teaching and conducting high school bands, orchestras, and teaching history and student government. Kendell was honored as Utah's "Best Junior High Music Educator" in 1984 by the Utah Holiday magazine. Mr. Nielsen came to Ricks College in 1987 as Director of Wind Studies. He joined the Teacher Education faculty in 1993. His interests include developing music literacy as part of the "core" curriculum, K-12. Kendell is married with five children.

Rhonda Seamons, Ed.D.

Rhonda Seamons received degrees from Brigham Young University and Utah State University. Prior to coming to teach at Ricks College, Rhonda worked in several elementary schools in the state of Utah - where her specialty was education of the gifted and talented. Rhonda served an LDS mission in Madrid, Spain. Rhonda loves to travel and has an extensive toilet paper collection from around the world. She also breeds miniature doves.

Mrs. Jackie Mitchell

Jackie Mitchell is the department secretary. She is an Idaho Native who graduated from Ricks College. She attended Idaho State University and Brigham Young University prior to serving an LDS mission to England. Prior to assuming her position at Ricks College she pursued a career in Banking and was employed with several local banks for over 15 years. She is married to David Mitchell and they have four outstanding children. Jackie's hobbies include writing, painting and stamping and just about any kind or craft. She also enjoys cooking.
