Communication 250

Organizational Communication

The purpose of this course is to provide a theoretical and practical understanding of how communication operates within an organization, and to aid you in the development of communication skills that will be of value in today's information oriented workplace. The theory will be presented in both the class lectures and the readings. The analysis will come from class discussions, activities and written assignments. Wherever possible, assignments will allow you to apply what you have learned and provide hands-on practical experience.

Attendance Policy: As what occurs in class is unique and can never be relived you will be expected to attend class. Short quizzes on the reading will be given daily. If you are not present when a quiz is given, you lose the points.

Grading: Points will be given for the completion of assignments. The following activities will be included:

  • Quizzes and Class Participation........100pts.
  • Case Studies (4 x 25 pts. each)........100pts.
  • In Class Interview.............................50pts.
  • Small Group Activity.........................50pts.
  • Mid Term Exam...............................100pts.
  • Presentation......................................50pts.
  • Final Project.....................................150pts.
Extra Credit: For a suitable bribe (baked goods work best), I can be persuaded to offer extra credit assignments, but you should bear in mind two things: first, you will have to give a very good reason why you should be given the opportunity to make up lost points, and secondly, any extra credit assignment will be significantly more difficult and require more work than normal, I.E. you will have to work twice as hard to earn the same amount of points as you could have earned through the regular assignments.

Test: The mid-term will be short answer, multiple choice, matching, short essay and long essay. There will be no final, as your final project will show how well you have learned to apply the principles taught in the class.

Interview: Each of you will engage in two mock interviews before the class, one in which you are the interviewer, and one in which you are the interviewee.

Group Project: You will each be a member of a group with 3-4 members. The group will be given an in-class assignment in which all members will participate. There will be an oral report and a written report on the activity due for each group, which you will prepare together.

Case Studies: Case studies will be assigned at the end of three of the sections. These case studies can be analyzed in several ways, therefore no two students will approach all of them in the same way. The idea behind this assignment is to give you a chance to apply the principles discussed in the book and lectures to real problems. I would like to see some creative thought put into your analysis...which means asking some good questions of your own.

The next key to successful analysis is consciously using concepts and terms from the class. Ask yourself which ideas discussed might apply to the situation...and explain how.

Please do not spend much space in your analysis re-telling the case study. Simply analyze as well as possible. There will be no required length for each case study analysis, but it will be difficult to do very much in less than two double spaced pages for each case study. In some cases they will be significantly longer.

Presentations: You will each give one oral presentation in which you will briefly preview for the class the results of your final project. The time frame for each presentation will be from eight to ten minutes. Visual aids may be used. We will discuss these presentations at length in class, but a glance at the point value should make it clear that a great deal of time and preparation will need to be put into this presentation.

Individual Project: In the same style as the case studies, you will be assigned a final paper which will focus on a real organization with which you are familiar. You should present a description of the organization, as well as a discussion of real problems existing within it, and your analysis and diagnosis.

-OR-

Alternately, you may create (on paper) an organization of your own. If you take this option, you will have to be very thorough, think it through and discuss everything from structure to organizational values to leadership style.

The key to successfully completing this final project will be to identify the organization early in the semester - at least by midterm. Successful projects will show a great depth of analysis and completeness. Feel free to use pictures, diagrams, etc. Please use your imagination and have fun, but REMEMBER - this project takes the place of your final exam! I will use it to determine the depth of your understanding of and ability to use the principles discussed in class, so please use them in your paper.

One student's project actually ran more than 100 pages long. Most typically are 10-15 pages. We will discuss the project more later in the semester - but start thinking about it now and save yourself much grief later!

Requirements for Papers: Each paper must be typed and double spaced. I am not an English teacher, but this is college. Pretend this is a trial run for your future career and ask yourself, "would my boss accept this?" Papers will be graded on content, and mechanics.

All assignments submitted after a deadline are late. I feel very strongly about students handing in papers on time. Late papers will be accepted within a week of the original deadline, but no grade higher than a "C" will be given, even for otherwise excellent work. If you are late, you should still try to get the assignment in as soon as possible. Materials not submitted will receive a "zero" which is far worse than a "C".
