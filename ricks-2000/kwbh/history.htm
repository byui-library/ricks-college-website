A little bit about KWBH
KWBH provides an opportunity for students to learn how to be radio broadcasters by operating an actual working radio station.  The stress and pressure found in a commercial radio station does not apply to KWBH.  KWBH gives supervised, hands-on experience in the radio broadcasting.  Students have the opportunity to do production recording, news reporting, sports broadcasting, and all the other elements of radio operations.  KWBH broadcasts 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, on 91.5 FM.   The class and the program continue throughout the year.



KWBH History
About 1950 A group of students wanting to begin a novice radio station started working on a carrier current transmitter system. Various forms of this type of transmission were implemented.  KVIK, a non-licensed station, was born. VIK cames from the Ricks College Vikings.
1973 KRIC (another Ricks College radio station) was granted a 10-watt FM license. KVIK moved its studios next to KRIC.  (KRIC is now 100,000-watts @ 100.5 FM.)
1993 KWBH was granted a broadcasting license with 100 watts at 91.5 FM. Programming was LDS Contemporary music. KVIK carrier current transmitting was dropped.  Kay Wilkins, Alan Bossard, and John Haeberle were the three individuals in the Communications Department most responsible for up-grading the station to what it is today.  Hence, the call letters KWBH stand for Wilkins, Bossard, and Haeberle.  KWBH will always be indebted to these gentlemen.
1997 Reintroduction of current hit music (CHR), to go along with the LDS music format to establish "Your Best Mix of Music." (60% LDS music and 40% CHR)
