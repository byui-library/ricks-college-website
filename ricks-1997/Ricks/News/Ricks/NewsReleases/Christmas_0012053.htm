Ricks College News Release
December 5, 2000

True faces of Christmas season

REXBURG, Idaho — The many faces of the Christmas season were addressed by Randall L. Hall, an LDS Church Educational System zone administrator at Tuesday’s weekly devotional at Ricks College.

"Just as there are many wonderful sites and sounds, smells and tastes associated with this time of the year, Christmas also wears many faces," Hall said.

He elaborated on several different scenarios of various people and what each one might experience during the season from a young 7-year-old girl to parents to a young couple preparing to marry. He shifted to the face of baby Jesus being born in the arms of Mary and Joseph.

"A face full of compassion reaching out . . . a face full of serene power that stood on the ship in the Sea of Galilee during the storm, a face filled with love, . . . it is the same face 33 years after his birth that expressed the agony on the cross at Golgatha," he said of Jesus.

"As we talk about the true meaning of Christmas, trying to understand and then live according to our understanding of what that means. Will his face become more clearly defined in our hearts? Will we sense his countenance more accurately? If our understanding and love for the Savior were represented in an artistic rendition, would it be a simple pen and ink drawing faintly outlined or would it be a full color portrait?"

Hall said God’s greatest gift to the world was his son Jesus Christ.

"And just as we enjoy in giving gifts to those we love, the Savior likes to give us gifts," he said. "His gifts to us are truly extraordinary. . . We come to understand that the Savior has taken upon himself our pains and sicknesses, our sins, infirmities and death, and replaced them with gifts of well being, healing, forgiveness, strength and life everlasting. His gifts outlast the short days of the holiday season."

He returned to the scenes of people’s lives and added the gifts of the Savior and how they were given, unwrapped and appreciated by needy souls.
