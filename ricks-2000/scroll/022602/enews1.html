CEO of Edge Wireless speaks at Forum

by Seth Moncur
MON98005@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Edge Wireless CEO Wayne Perry spoke to students and faculty Thursday at the Forum in the Taylor Chapel.

Perry started off by telling the attendee's that he wouldn't mind if their cell phones went off during the forum. "I'll be thinking about the additional revenue I will be getting."

Perry has had a "blessed" career in telecommunications. aside from being CEO of Edge Wireless, he has served as CEO and V.P of AT&T wireless, V.P and CEO of Nexlink Co, and as V.P and President of McCaw communications.

In the United States there are130.8 million cell subscriptions. That is more than there were regular phones in1972. 43% of Americans get cell-phones which is low compared to 93% in Taiwan. Perry said. "Last year alone the world spent 492,770 years on cell phones."

Perry said that in 1886 the first communication between New York and London took place, and it cost $10 a word. new fiber optic technology allows 55.68 trillion conversations a minute. 111 times more than is needed, soon it will be 1,000 times more than is currently needed.

As for the future of telecommunications many new developments are coming very soon Perry told of tiny cell phones that just fit in your ear. He also told how cell phones will soon be outfitted with tiny cameras that will take photos you can send on-line. Perry gave an example that a realtor can take pictures of a house and instantly send them to interested buyers.

2/3 of all cell calls are to 911. Most people do not know how to explain to authorities where they are. Soon Cell Phones will send a GPS signal so they can easily be found.

Perry also told of a simultaneous ear piece that allows instant translation. A practical use could be found in the mission field Perry said, If someone goes to a country with several languages, with the ear piece, the missionary will be able to understand.

The last new item is micro vision glasses where images are put on your glasses, "Missionaries won't bother memorizing discussions after that, they can just read it off their glasses." Perry said. "The uses of telecommunications are endless."
