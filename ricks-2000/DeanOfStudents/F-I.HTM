Financial Aid
The Financial Aid Office, located in Kimball 100, can advise students on when and how to apply for Financial Aid. The BYU-Idaho Title IV school code which is required on the FAFSA is: 001625. For more detailed information about financial aid, deadlines and other general information. See www.byui.edu/Ricks/FinancialAid.

Most students are eligible for some form of financial aid. Financial aid at BYU-Idaho primarily consists of Federal Pell Grants, Stafford Loans, and a limited number of other types of aid. BYU-Idaho does not participate in Perkins loans, SEOG grants, work study, or any other campus-based programs. Students applying for Federal Student Financial Aid must first complete the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA). This form is available from high school counselors, a college or university near students’ homes, or the BYU-Idaho Financial Aid Office. Students may also complete the FAFSA on the Web at www.byui.edu/Ricks/Financial Aid.

Students planning to get married within the year should contact the Financial Aid Office. Getting married may affect students’ eligibility. The Financial Aid Office can advise students planning on getting married as to when they should apply for financial aid.

1. Federal Pell Grants

Under the Federal Pell Grant Program students may be eligible for a non-repayable government grant. Grants are awarded on the basis of financial need.

2. Federal Family Education Loans

Federal Subsidized, Unsubsidized and Parent Loans for Undergraduate Students (PLUS) are available. First year students may borrow up to $2,625 per school year. Students who have earned 32 credits or more may borrow up to $3,500 per school year.

3. Federal Subsidized Stafford Loan

Principal and interest payments are postponed while students are enrolled in school at least half time and during their six month grace period. The federal government pays the interest for students during this time.

4. Federal Unsubsidized Stafford Loan

Monthly principal payments are postponed while students are enrolled in school at least half-time and during their six month grace period. Students are responsible for paying the interest that accrues on their loans. Students can either make interest payments or request that the lender adds the accrued interest to the principal balance. This is called capitalization and increases the total amount students will pay.

5. Parent Loans For Undergraduate Students (PLUS)

Parents of dependent undergraduate students may borrow up to the student’s annual cost of attendance.

6. Financial Holds

Financial holds will be placed on student records when any debt owed to the university is past due. This will stop any registration and the release of transcripts until the debt is paid. Make payments at the Cashiers Office. Questions should be directed to the Accounting Office.

Fire
Proper conduct during fire evacuations is the most important contribution students can make toward their own safety and that of their friends.

If fire or smoke is discovered:
1. Activate the nearest fire alarm
2. Leave the building immediately, staying close to the floor where smoke is less dense and
3. Follow staff, police, and fire personnel directions.
4. If time permits in the residence hall, students should:
a. Turn on the light in their rooms.
b. Put on a coat and shoes during inclement weather.
c. Open the drapes and/or the blinds.
d. Close the windows.
e. Pick up a towel to cover face in case smoke is encountered.
f. Close the door leaving the lights on.
g. Exit the building quickly.

TAMPERING WITH FIRE EXTINGUISHERS AND/OR FIRE ALARMS IS STRICTLY PROHIBITED. ANY VIOLATION WILL BE DEALT WITH CRIMINALLY AND WITH SCHOOL DISCIPLINARY ACTION.

Firearms
BYU-Idaho prohibits the possession or use of firearms and weapons on property owned or controlled by the university, including residence halls, (see Idaho Code 18-3302C), and at programs sponsored by BYU-Idaho which occur off campus property (i.e. Discovery, travel related to academic studies, ASBYUI activities, etc.). For the purpose of this policy, firearms or weapons includes any device which can expel a projectile, and/or other dangerous weapons, including knives, explosives, or other items which, in their intended use, are capable of inflicting serious injury. Because these items pose a clear risk to the persons and property on BYU-Idaho’s campus, violation of this policy will result in appropriate disciplinary sanctions.

No one (personnel, students, or visitors) except law enforcement officers of municipal, county, State of Idaho or Federal Law Enforcement Agency, shall be permitted to possess or carry firearms or other weapons, concealed or not concealed, with or without a concealed weapon permit, while upon the properties owned or controlled by the university without specific written permission from the Director of Campus Police.

Members of the campus ROTC units may possess and/or carry firearms or weapons as part of official ROTC functions, when authorized by the ROTC administrator.

Health Services
The Student Health Center (SHC) is located in Clarke 120. Call ext. 1300 to make an appointment. For more information see www.byui.edu/healthcenter/default.htm

1. Hours
The Health Center is open when classes are in session as follows:
a. Fall and Winter Semesters (full services): Monday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday 8 a.m. to 11:45 a.m. and 1 p.m. to 4:45 p.m. Tuesday 8 a.m. to 12:45 p.m. and 3 p.m. to 4:45 p.m
b. Summer Terms (no lab, x-ray, or pharmacy): Monday through Friday 9 a.m. to 2:45 p.m.
c. Holidays/Breaks: The SHC is closed on holidays, during semester breaks, and any time school is not in session.

2. Staff
The Health Center professional staff consists of: family practice physicians, a family-nurse practitioner, two registered nurses, pharmacist; lab technologist; and x-ray technologist.

3. Pharmacy
The Health Center pharmacy carries prescriptions and over-the-counter medicines. The Pharmacy accepts most prescription cards. A Xerox copy of both sides of the card is sufficient.

4. Payments
Most services and medications can be charged to the student’s BYU-Idaho account. Mission physicals, pre-marital exams, pap smears, acne medication, and birth control pills must be paid for at time of service. Students do not need BYU-Idaho insurance to use the Health Center. Students will receive a copy of their bill at the time of service and are responsible for filing their own private insurance.

5. BYU-Idaho Insurance
Students having BYU-Idaho insurance need to be seen at the Health Center before they can be referred to an outside doctor during Health Center office hours. Referrals are made according to need. BYU-Idaho insurance does not cover office calls or prescriptions. 80% of most lab and x-ray charges are deducted at the time of service.

6. Injections
Tetanus shots are given for injuries as needed. Two flu shot clinics are held during October. Students should get their flu shots during these clinics as they will not be given at the Health Center. Allergy shots are given for students who bring their serum and instruction forms from a home doctor.

Immunizations for visas, missions, trips, or health reasons are available at the District 7 Health Department in Rexburg. Call 356-3239 for more information.

Honor Code Council
BYU-Idaho is committed to the preservation of student rights and to fair treatment of all students. If the Dean of Students office determines that students have violated the Honor Code and the appropriate sanction appears to be suspension, students have the right to request a review before the Honor Code Council to determine if they should be suspended.

The council is comprised of a chairperson, three faculty, staff or administrator members, three students, a secretary (nonvoting, and the Dean of Students or his/her representative as an ex-officio member of the council. The ex-officio member will serve as a nonvoting advisor to the council.

The students are selected by the ASBYUI president and approved by the Student Life Vice President. Decisions of the council are by a simple majority, and the council may impose any sanction which it deems appropriate.

Students who have received a major sanction may, within two school days of receiving oral or written notification of that sanction, petition the Dean of Students to review the decision of the Honor Code Council to determine if there should be mitigation of the sanction imposed. The Dean of Students will then review the case with the Student Life Vice President. Consideration for mitigation by the Dean of Students or Vice President will be based on the following: (a) students establish to the satisfaction of the Dean and Vice President that they were denied a fair council meeting; or (b) students establish to the satisfaction of the Dean and Vice President that since the council meeting, new and material evidence has been discovered which, had it been introduced at the council meeting, would probably have changed the decision.

Identification Cards
Student ID cards are issued at the ID Center, next to the Ticket Office in Manwaring Center 162. The ID Center is open from 9:30 a.m. to 3 p.m. Monday through Friday and closes at 2 p.m. on Tuesdays for devotionals. See www.byui.edu/IDCenter.

1. ID Card Policies
ID cards are for the exclusive use of students to whom they are issued and is invalid if students are not in compliance with BYU-Idaho standards. It is against the Honor Code to be in possession of more than one ID card. Possession or efforts to acquire more than one ID card is grounds for disciplinary action. ID Cards remain the property of BYU-Idaho and must be surrendered upon request. Please Carry Your BYU-Idaho ID With You.

2. Lost/Stolen ID Cards
Lost ID's are returned to the ID Center. There is a $10 non-refundable fee to replace lost ID cards. It is important that students report lost or stolen ID cards so that their card cannot be used without permission.

3. Proper Care of the ID Card
Students should treat their ID card with care, as they would their driver’s license, credit cards, or cash.

a. Do not bend or wash the ID card. Card readers cannot read bent or cracked cards reliably.
b. Keep the back of the card away from the back of other cards with magnetic stripes, the magnetic fields erase each other. Also, keep the card away from any magnetic fields such as VCR’s or stereo speakers.
c. Do not use the card as an ice scraper to clean car windows. They will break.

Incomplete Grades
An incomplete is a conditional grade and is given only in exceptional cases--never to extend time to obtain a better grade. It is not a substitute for a failing grade and is given only when students can complete the prescribed coursework without further attendance in the classroom. To receive an incomplete grade in a course, students must meet with their instructors and request an incomplete. Instructors and students determine what work needs to be completed and the time given in which to do it. Students and instructors must both sign the contract. A $5 fee will be charged at the time students pick up the Incomplete Contract at the Registrar’s Office. A copy of the contract is given to the Registrar's Office by instructors at the time final grades are submitted. To remove the incomplete grade, students must complete the work required. Instructors will notify the Registrar's Office, who will change the grade on the transcript. If the work has not been completed by the end of the next major semester, the "I" will be changed to "F."

Insurance
BYU-Idaho requires that students must be covered in the Rexburg area by health and accident insurance. Students who register as a half-time or more are automatically charged for BYU-Idaho Student Insurance, this includes Continuing Education students. Student with insurance will then automatically be charged for insurance for each subsequent semester or term as long as they have continuing student status, including breaks from school up to four months. A Student Health Plan booklet will be given to students to provide information regarding insurance coverage. This booklet can be obtained at any time in the Accounting Office. Insurance information is also available over the web. See www.byui.edu/FinServices/rmcontacts.htm.

1. Insurance Waiver
Students may have BYU-Idaho Insurance waived if: (1) they are covered in the Rexburg area by another health and accident insurance plan, and (2) a completed Waiver Form has been submitted to the BYU-Idaho Accounting Office before the deadline (first week of a Semester or block or term). Once a Waiver Form has been submitted, students will not be charged for insurance in subsequent semesters or terms unless they notify the Accounting Office that they no longer have private insurance. Failure to do this is grounds for disciplinary action. Waiver Forms are mailed to new and former students with the registration packets. A waiver form will also be mailed prior to Fall Semester to students who have been charged. Students may also pick up a waiver form in the Accounting Office. Students who have still been charged when they arrive at school should bring their private insurance information (insurance company, address, policy number, group number, and policy holder’s name) to the Accounting Office and fill out the waiver card before the deadline (first week).

2. Continued Insurance After Leaving BYU-Idaho
For those who have student insurance, Extended Insurance is available for up to nine months after leaving school. This can be obtained by picking up an enrollment form from the Accounting Office and submitting it to the insurance company (DMBA) before the end of the last semester or term at BYU-Idaho.

3. Waiver During Break From School
If students are covered by private insurance during their break from school, then student insurance can be waived for the time they are away from Rexburg. Otherwise, students will be charged for the insurance during the break. A Temporary Insurance Waiver Form can be obtained at the Accounting Office and turned in before leaving for the break. This information may also be entered over the web. The insurance will automatically be reinstated upon their return to school.

4. Dependent Insurance
Students may purchase insurance for their dependents during the first week of Fall Semester or the first term of attendance during the school year. A newly acquired dependent can be added within 60 days after the new dependent is acquired (marriage, birth, etc.). Dependent insurance is not available unless said students are also covered by student insurance. The insurance coverage will automatically continue for students and/or their dependents as long as they have continuing student status, including breaks from school up to four months.
