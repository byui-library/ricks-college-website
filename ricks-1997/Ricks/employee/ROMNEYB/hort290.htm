 The purpose of the special problems course is to provide opportunities for students to enrich their learning experience with areas of study that are not generally covered in the regular curriculum.  There are also at times opportunities outside the classroom for experiences that further enhance the education that is provided in the horticulture programs.  It should be noted that this class is not intended as a substitute for other courses in the department.  All enrollments in this course are subject to approval of the instructor.

General Guidelines:
 Although each of you will have your own specific requirements, the following general guidelines will apply.

1.  There is a time requirement of 2 hours per week, per credit hour.  This will mean about 30 hours of time commitment for one credit, 60 for two credits, and so on.  You must turn in a log of time spent on this course at the end of the semester.  You should include the date, time spent, and the nature of activity every time that you are involved with study for this class.

2.  You should have a clear idea in mind of a particular subject or area of interest.  You must submit a written proposal to me by the end of the first week of the semester indicating your projected use of the time in the course.  Include a brief description or list of activities, some goals or objectives to achieve, and the general purpose of the time you plan to spend.

3.  You are required to submit a final report, and a time log, prior to the last week of the semester.  This report should summarize all activities, and evaluate how your goals and objectives were met.

4.  If you are working with another member of the Landscape Horticulture Department Faculty, it is your responsibility to make certain that the faculty member that you are working with gives me an evaluation of your effort prior to exam week, so that you can receive proper credit for your study.  If you fail to do this, I will have no information to use for your grade, and you will not receive a passing mark.

“Floral Retailing & Display”
Course objective: To provide opportunity and experience in presentation of flowers for sales in: display windows and coolers, large banquet halls, auditoriums, custom design in residential and commercial settings.

This class is structured to provide experience and group cooperative interaction.  We will meet together as needed to coordinate what we are doing for sales and for devotionals.  Please check the bulletin board for scheduled times for you to work, as well as notices of times that I may want to meet with you.
Most of the time, even though we are scheduled to meet once a week, we will not have formal class time, but will use those hours for individual and small group training.  Each student will be responsible for signing up for times during the weekday afternoons to help run the flower shop.

The retail cooler, and keeping its unpredictable appetite satisfied, has been our major focus.  We have learned  that we can sell more product if we keep it full more than twice each week.  This will require a great deal of coordination to provide everyone with equal opportunities yet still be flexible enough to fit into all of your schedules.  Please be willing to cooperate when you are asked to help.  We will plan on having the shop open for specific hours each afternoon, probably 3 - 5:30.

Devotionals and forums, have gone very well in the past.  We may have a conflict with seminar on the day that you are assigned to help.  This will need to be coordinated with Brother Wilson so that you can prepare in advance to be late for, or leave early from the seminar presentation, or to watch the video of it afterward.  Forums will conflict slightly with practicum, so we will have to make sure that flowers are delivered even if someone has to miss the last few minutes of a class

 We have had some other limited opportunities to provide service on and around campus, such as the President’s Club Reception and banquets.  We will be hosting the Idaho State FFA floral contest in march, and will expect those in this class to help with that event.
We have to be careful not to conflict with our local florist friends.  We also have to be compensated for most of these opportunities for budgetary reasons.  I will pursue opportunities that will meet those limitations.  There may be opportunities for many of you to practice additional skills.

The primary purpose of this special class is to help to prepare you to go into an internship situation with at least a minimal skill level.  You will be much more useful to an employer if you already have the confidence to perform basic design and processing skills.

Your grade is entirely dependent upon your participation.  You will need to log in a minimum of thirty hours working in the shop throughout the semester, usually two to three hours per week.   The lab assistants will be responsible to make up a weekly schedule, which you will, in turn, be responsible to follow.  Should you need to change your assigned time, you will need to make sure that you are covered, and that you have made the lab assistant aware.  If you fail to participate, you will not pass the class.  If you do participate willingly and regularly, you will be given an “a.”
