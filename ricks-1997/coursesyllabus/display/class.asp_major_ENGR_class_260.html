ENGINEER MATLS
CLASS CODE: ENGR 260 CREDITS:  3
DIVISION: PHYSICAL SCIENCE & ENGINEERING
DEPARTMENT: MECHANICAL ENGINEERING & TECHNOLOGY
GENERAL EDUCATION: This course does not fulfill a General Education requirement.
CATALOG DESCRIPTION: Microstructure of engineering materials, including metals, polymers, ceramics, composites. Factors influencing the selection of materials in engineering analysis and design. For all engineering majors.
DESCRIPTION: Atomic and microstructure of engineering materials, including metals, ceramics, polymers, and composites. Factors influencing the fabrication, processing, and selection of materials in engineering analysis and design. Case studies of engineering material failures.
TOPICS: Introduction to Engineering Materials; Review of Atomic Structures and Bonds; Atomic Structures and Bonds; Models of Crystal Structure: Metals and Ceramics; Models of Crystal Structure: Polymers; Models of Crystal Structure: Polymers; Imperfections and Dislocations in Solids; Solid-State Diffusion
Solid-State Diffussion; Mechanical Properties of Materials; Deformations and Strengthening; Equilibrium Phase Diagrams; Phase Transformations and Thermal
Treatment of Material; Material Types and Applications
OBJECTIVES: •Develop classification criteria for metals, ceramics, polymers, and composites.
•Review principles of covalent, ionic, and metallic strong bonding. Review weak interatomic bonding models. Review classical and quantum concepts of atomic structure.
•Review models of unit cell structures. Develop conventions for descriptions of unit cell locations, directions, and planes. Compute maximum linear and planar atomic densities [slip planes and directions].
•Develop quantitative descriptions for crystal imperfections.
•Develop and correctly apply mathematical model equations for steady state and transient solid diffusion.
•Review macroscopic definitions for properties of elastic materials.
•Understand concepts of material strengthening due to dislocations.
•Develop models for quantitative description of material failures, including fatigue, creep, and inelastic deformation.
•Develop and utilize conventions for equilibrium phase diagrams for binary systems of metals and ceramics.
•Emphasize iron-cementite binary phase diagrams [steel].
•Develop structure and nomenclature for time-temperature-transformation binary component diagrams.
•Develop classical descriptors and quantitative measures of hot- and cold-work alloy transformations.
•Review ferrous and non-ferrous material descriptors and property values.
•Apply micro- and macroscopic material description skills previously developed to ceramics.
•Review polymerization, polymer chain structures and bonding, and structures and types of polymers.
•Develop relationships and descriptors for composite materials, including modulus of elasticity and volume fraction.
•Select engineering component which was suffered material failure and perform forensic analysis. Propose alterations to design of component to prevent failure under similar service conditions.
•Write design analysis report in standard professional engineering format for forensic analysis and design improvements of fractured engineering component.

OTHER: "Design and Forensic Reports". Each student who chooses this option will locate an engineering component or system which has failed [go out and find something which is broken!]. The failure will be investigated to determine: (a) what is the material from which the part was manufactured; (b) what were the likely causes for the failure; (c) what changes in design or material would prevent the failure from occurring under normal operating conditions. A summary report of findings will be written. A one-page handout will be prepared for class members. A multi-media presentation [using either PowerPoint or Presentations] will be prepared and presented to the class for each project. This will be a great opportunity to actually "do some engineering", learn some new skills, perform materials research, and develop skills in presentation.


"Topic Presentation Project." A student who chooses this option will research the selected topic [using the text and other resources] and produce a coherent multi-media presentation on a topic of relevance in this introductory materials science and engineering course. The presentation will usually be limited to one 50-minute class period. Two students can share one topic on a two-day presentation. The intent is for the student to teach the rest of the class about the material characteristic, to provide for the students a summarized handout of the presentation, and to gain experience and confidence in making oral presentations to a group of engineering peers. This is an opportunity for us to teach and to learn from each other.
