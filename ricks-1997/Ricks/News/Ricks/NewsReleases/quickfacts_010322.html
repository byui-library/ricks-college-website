March 22, 2001 

Quick Facts about Ricks College

Ricks College was established as Bannock Stake Academy in 1888 and today is the largest private two-year college in the United States.

In 1903, the school was renamed Ricks Academy in honor of Thomas E. Ricks, the Bannock Stake president at the time it was founded and the chairman of the school’s first Board of Education. In 1923, the school became known as Ricks College. 

On June 21, 2000, the chairman of the Board of Trustees, President Gordon B. Hinckley, announced the college will become known as Brigham Young University-Idaho. Using the new name will begin later this year when the school begins offering bachelor’s degrees.

The college is directed by the President’s Council consisting of:

David A. Bednar, president

R. Brent Kinghorn, community services vice president

James R. Smyth, administrative vice president

Donald C. Bird, academics vice president

Robert Wilkes, student life vice president

The residential college currently has 8,955 students who come from all 50 states and more than 40 foreign countries.

Ricks College is located on a 255-acre campus in Rexburg, Idaho. The population of Rexburg is nearly 16,000.

Ricks College has a comprehensive liberal arts, vocational, and scientific curriculum, consisting of 1,000 courses, 105 majors, and 40 career programs.

The college is affiliated with The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.

The mission of Ricks emphasizes building testimonies of the restored gospel of Jesus Christ and encouraging living its principles; providing a quality education for students of diverse interests and abilities; preparing students for further education and employment, and for their roles as citizens and parents; and maintaining a wholesome academic, cultural, social and spiritual environment.
