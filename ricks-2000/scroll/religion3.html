Sander Larson asks students to ‘refuel’ their missionary lights

Katie McMurray
mcm00001@byui.edu
scroll staff

Sander Larson and his wife, Merlyn Larson, greet a student at the devotional Oct. 23. Larson, a former mission president of the Washington Seattle Mission, spoke on the impact of example on missionary work.
Members of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints must refuel their lights in order to shine forth among their fellowmen, Sander Larson, former Ricks College administrator and president of the Washington Seattle Mission, said at last week’s devotional.

“If your light is weak, fix it. Re-fuel it. Let your light so shine,” Larson said.

Larson returned “home” Tuesday to give a devotional at BYU-Idaho. He served as the associate director of public relations and also dean of student life at Ricks College from August 1975 to June 1998.

Larson shared the motto of the Washington Seattle Mission he created while serving as mission president. Ten lighthouses line the Seattle mission, Larson said.

All ships were led by the lighthouses. The passage of scripture found in Doctrine and Covenants 115:5, “Verily I say unto you all: Arise and shine forth, that thy light may be a standard for all nations,” became the mission’s motto, Larson said.

Larson compared this concept to student life. Righteous people radiate positive signals to others, Larson said.

He also gave five steps to happiness: Keep the commandments, learn from the scriptures, always be an example, be prayerful and let your light so shine.

“We must be a light at all times, we never know who is watching,” Larson said.

Larson related stories of his past to illustrate each thought, one of which was about his own conversion to The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.

During his high school years, Larson had noticed little things about certain young adults. He observed them and was “drawn to them like a magnet.”

He told of one boy who befriended him by driving Larson to church every week. Over time Larson joined the LDS Church and thus started a domino effect; his parents joined two years later and many others joined as a result of his friend’s influence.

“All of this happened because of some young single adults that came into my life,” Larson said. “Through the life of a few Latter-day Saints that befriended me, ...we were knit together.”

“How does your light shine?” Larson asked the students. “Don’t be afraid to share your testimony.”

He reminded students about the “pass-along” cards and to be an example within each apartment or at the grocery store.

In order to have a bright light, Larson emphasized the importance of personal prayer and scripture study. He then quoted Elder Boyd K. Packer, saying that we miss everything and gain nothing when we do not study the scriptures.

By following Elder Packer’s counsel, Larson advised the students to “stay close to the Lord.”

Larson told students about three different gardens. The garden of Eden represents choice and free agency.

The Garden of Gethsemane represents mercy and the atonement. The Garden Tomb represents the love and sacrifice of Jesus Christ and the restoration of all things.

“We must live worthily,” Larson said. “It takes time to prepare for eternity and sin wastes time,” he said.

Larson reminded students that Lucifer led away one-third of the people while in the presence of god.

Lucifer is able to lead Latter-day Saints into darkness in this generation, even if they are attending a Church school, or who are surrounded by the Church members.

“Put away childish things,” Larson said.

“We cannot live with one foot in Babylon and one in the Kingdom. ... There is a difference between light and dark,” he said.

“As Latter-day Saints, we need to make sure our lights are bright and not be afraid to share our testimonies,” Larson said. “Missionary work is an essential part of this Church and everyone can be a part of it, as long as we let our lights so shine.”
