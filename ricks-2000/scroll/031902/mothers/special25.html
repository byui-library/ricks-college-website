Composer's wife makes music

by Tammy Walquist
WAL01018@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Clara Schumann was talented in piano, voice, and instrumentation. photo courtesy www.geneva.edu
It can be said that beside every good man is a good wife.

However, in the case of Robert and Clara Schumann, it could be said that beside a great composer stood a great wife who was also a composer.

Robert and Clara Schumann were both pianists and composers. Even after their marriage, Clara continued to compose and perform as a concert pianist.

Clara Josephine Wieck Schumann was born Sept. 13, 1819, in Leipzig, Germany. Her father, Friedrich Wieck, had already decided before Clara’s birth that she would be a child prodigy and musician.

Clara’s first public appearance was in 1828, and her first complete recital was in 1830.

Clara did extensive of piano, voice, violin, instrumentation, score reading, counterpoint and composition performances, in addition to publishing several pieces for solo piano.

Robert Schumann came to live and study with Wieck in 1830. He asked for permission to marry Clara in 1837, but her father objected and tried to prevent it before Clara’s 21st birthday. Robert and Clara filed a lawsuit, won, and out of spite, married one day before Clara’s birthday.

The Schumanns had eight children.

After Robert was sent to an institution in 1854, Clara moved to Berlin in 1857 where she performed, taught and edited Robert’s works and letters.

Clara’s music was characterized by technical mastery, thoughtful interpretation, poetic spirit, depth of feeling, a singing tone and strict observance of the composer’s markings.

Clara traveled on 38 concert tours outside of Germany. Her greatest contribution to the musical world can be seen because of these tours, Darwin Wolford, director of organ studies at BYU-Idaho, said.

“As a concert pianist, [Clara] performed a lot of [Robert’s] compositions after his death around Europe,” Wolford said. “She is responsible for bringing his music to the attention of the European public.”

All of her compositions date from 1853 or before. They include 29 songs, three partsongs, four pieces for piano and orchestra, 20 pieces for solo piano and cadenzas for three piano concertos by Beethoven and Mozart.

Clara died May 20, 1896 in Frankfurt, Germany.
