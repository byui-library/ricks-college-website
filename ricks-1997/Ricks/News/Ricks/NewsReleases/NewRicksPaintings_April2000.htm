Ricks College News Release
April 3, 2000

New paintings at Ricks celebrate life of namesake Thomas E. Ricks

REXBURG, Idaho — Three large-sized oil paintings, commissioned by Ricks College and depicting events from the life of Thomas E. Ricks, are now on permanent display in the college’s Manwaring Student Center.

The paintings, which are accompanies by information about Ricks’ life, are located in the stairwell outside the ballrooms on the second floor. The building is open daily, except for major holidays, from 7 a.m. to midnight.

Ricks colonized eastern Idaho in the early 1880s at the request of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. He founded Rexburg in 1883 and opened Bannock Stake Academy, the forerunner of Ricks College, in 1888. The academy was named in Ricks’ honor in 1902. Ricks, who died in 1901, was considered the ecclesiastical leader of eastern Idaho and, as such, was chairman of the area’s Church Board of Education.

The three paintings, measuring five to six-and-a-half feet in height, were painted by Glen Hopkinson of Mesa, Ariz., an artist renowned for his western-themed and pioneer-era paintings. Many of his paintings have been published in LDS Church publications.

Funding for the paintings was provided by Sterling D. and Eleanor Ricks Colton. Mrs. Colton is a direct descendant of Ricks and grew up in Idaho Falls. The couple are presently serving as president and matron of the LDS Church’s Washington D.C. Temple.

The largest of the three paintings depicts Ricks as a teen-ager crossing the frozen Mississippi River as a teamster with one of the earliest wagon trains to leave Nauvoo, Ill., in February of 1846. To its left is a painting of Ricks and others coming to the rescue of a stranded pioneer handcart company in Wyoming in an early winter storm of 1856. The third painting is of Ricks introducing Jacob Spori as principal of Bannock Stake Academy in Rexburg in November of 1888.
