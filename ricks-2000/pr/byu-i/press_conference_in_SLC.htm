The Transition: Press Conference-SLC, June 21, 2000
The announcement regarding the transition was included in a press conference held in Salt Lake City with President Gordon B. Hinckley and Elder Henry B. Eyring, Commissioner of the Church Educational System, on June 21, 2000.  Among the questions asked by the press were the following:
President Hinckley: We should note that Dr. Bednar, the president of Ricks College, is on the phone in Rexburg; and he will conduct a press conference there at 11:30 this morning. And President Merrill Bateman of BYU is on the phone in Provo. Any questions?

Does this mean that the competitive football team will be phased out and, if so, when will that happen?
President Hinckley: It means that it will be phased out. I don't know when it will happen.

How many more students will be served by this?
President Hinckley: We don't know exactly. Elder Eyring, do you want to venture a guess on that?

Elder Eyring: This campus is so innovative in terms of changing their schedule to allow more students to come that it's hard to forecast. There is a real possibility that even with a four-year status they might be able to serve more students without adding appreciably to the facilities. They've been very creative, looking at ways of using the Internet and also in changing the calendar to allow people to come in through the full year, that it is very hard to predict. But it looks as if it will be at least not any reduction and maybe an expansion, even as they go to the four-year status.

When will this actually take effect?
President Hinckley: Well, it's being announced today, and the plans will move forward from this time on.

Elder Eyring: Back to the athletic question though. Obviously, for a year it will go on pretty much operating as it has been as a two-year school. So it's hard to say beyond that one year; but for the next year, it will be essentially operating as it has been operating.

Regarding the comment about faculty rank not being part of the academic structure, is that a reference to tenure or can you explain that?
President Hinckley: Go ahead.

Elder Eyring: Thank you, President. Ricks College has never had the distinctions of assistant professor, associate professor, and full professor. It simply will continue that practice. It's an indication that the character of the school will remain very much the teaching-oriented, student-oriented institution it has always been. So that's not a change, and that has to do with simply the titles of faculty. Everyone is a teacher and a good one at Ricks College.

BYU-Provo has traditionally been pretty difficult to get in to. With no disrespect to Ricks, it has been perhaps a little easier to get in there. Will the academic standards and things be raised so that it may be like BYU in that regard?
President Hinckley: It will be just as good a teaching institution as we can make it.

Elder Eyring: I think that's exactly true.

Will students still be going to Ricks or BYU-Idaho for a few years and then trying to come down to Provo like they have been doing for years and years and years?
Elder Eyring: President?

President Hinckley: Go ahead.

Elder Eyring: I would think that one of the things that will remain is some of the distinctive features of Ricks College which have included some people going there for a brief time to then transfer to other places. That surely will continue. So there will be, my guess is, just an addition of the possibility of young people staying for the four years if they choose. But I would assume that much of what has gone on in the past would continue with a nice addition.

Would this suggest a greater emphasis on graduate programs at BYU-Provo?
Elder Eyring: I see no related change there. I think they are on their course, and this would not change that at all. It's an outstanding institution doing wonderful work as it has done. I don't see a related change at Brigham Young's campus in Provo.

Why do away with athletics?
President Hinckley: It takes too much money. It takes a great amount of time and energy. We think that, in this instance, we would like to change the emphasis. In the first place, Ricks is a long ways from everybody. You have to travel great distances to play. That's a factor in it. It's a very costly program. We would hope that with an intramural program of athletics and sports that the need could be met and that the great emphasis would be on academics.

President, the Ricks athletic program has always been considered a feeder program especially for the football team down to BYU. What was Lavell Edwards' response to this?
President Hinckley: We haven't talked to him.

Are there any plans to start another two-year college?
President Hinckley: No. We have none in mind. No.

How many bachelor degree programs do you envision being offered at BYU-Idaho?
Elder Eyring: It's too soon to tell. What we will do is gradually begin to look at the possibilities and move in such a way to be of the most benefit to the young people who want to come.

President Hinckley: This man [Elder Eyring] is a former president of Ricks, so he has a little love in his heart for what goes on up there.

I am sorry. I would like to go back to a question that was asked before. Virtually every four-year school in the country has promotion and tenure as we know it. Will it not exist?
Elder Eyring: No. I think I answered the question saying that it had to do with titles. The question of whether or not a person has continuing status would remain the same as it has always been. There is no change there.

Dr. Eyring, as a former Ricks president, how would you categorize - particularly for the people who aren't that familiar out there in the country with BYU and Ricks - how would you categorize what makes Ricks unique?
Elder Eyring: I would think Ricks College, because of its history of having been a two-year school, has always been seen as a place where students could come with a little more expectation of nurturing and personal attention than they might at some other institution - although it's excellent in all of our campuses. But I would think that if there is a slight distinction, it has come out of that two-year history and a little bit (as was suggested earlier) as a place where people went sometimes and were surprised at how well they did because they didn't see themselves as having quite the potential they found at Ricks College. That spirit, I think, has been there; and, surely, that will continue - the feeling of a student finding themselves and doing even better than they thought they could do and finding a faculty who are eager to help them.

President Hinckley: May I say that if you have not been to Ricks recently, you have a surprise awaiting you. That campus has been renewed - new buildings, modified buildings, remodeled buildings - it is a beautiful campus with wonderful facilities. It is not a little rundown country college. It is a great institution with a wonderful campus, great facilities, a very caring faculty, a very strong and innovative president, and everything that we feel is needed now to make a program of this kind succeed.

When will the name change be effective?
President Hinckley: Oh, it won't be today or tomorrow. We don't know. We'll phase into this. We'll phase into it.

Can you speak to whether tuition will increase, what you see in terms of costs, and how this speaks into BYU's stated mission to provide wider access to a BYU education through the Internet and technology?
President Hinckley: All those things will be worked on as we move forward with this program. There are many, many details to be worked out. It isn't going to happen in a day. It will take some time. But it is a most significant thing to have happen - the transformation from this good two-year college to an even better four-year institution.

President Hinckley and Dr. Eyring, what brought about this and what was the catalyst?
President Hinckley: Elder Eyring, would you like to respond?

Elder Eyring: I think President Hinckley has already suggested that we realized there was an opportunity here, largely because of the wonderful strengths that have been developed already to increase the blessing to the young people of the Church and to those who would want to come. It was [that] the opportunity became obvious.

You mentioned that Thomas Ricks will still be remembered and honored despite the name change. How will that happen?
President Hinckley: That will be worked out very carefully.

Elder Eyring: Could I make a comment, President? Thomas E. Ricks is the name of the stake president of the Bannock Stake at the time that whole area was being pioneered. The story, the tradition, is that the people of the town wanted to name it not Rexburg but Ricksburg. And he declined saying that he thought it would be better if they honored the king. So it is the city of the king. I think he had that feeling toward this academy as well. And my guess is he is very pleased today.

President Hinckley: All right. Anything else? Thank you very, very much ladies and gentlemen. Thank you.
