Former BYU student charged with sexual crimes

by Melanie Bridge
The Daily Universe (Brigham Young U.)

(U-WIRE) PROVO, Utah — As one female BYU student discovered, sometimes “Happy Valley” isn’t the perfect place it can appear to be.

On Nov. 8 Joshua Matthew Meyer was charged with the rape, sodomy and forcible sexual assault of an 18-year-old female BYU student, according to the University and Provo police departments.

Meyer, 25, from Denver, Colo., was also a BYU student at the time, said Carri Jenkins, assistant to the president for university communications.

According to Provo Police Detective DeVon Jensen, Meyer said the act was consensual.

On or about Oct. 20, during the day, an incident took place in the Knight Mangum Building, according to the Utah County Fourth District Provo court records and University Police Lt. Arnold Lemmon.

Later that day, “He picked her up on campus, they went for a drive to talk and ended up at his apartment,” Jensen said. The other incident happened in his apartment.

The victim and Meyer knew each other; they had classes together, Jensen said.

“She didn’t perceive him as a danger or a threat,” Jensen said.

Within 24 hours of the incident the victim reported it to the University and Provo police, Lemmon said.

“The victim was unsure what she wanted to do as far as criminal charges,” Jensen said.

After talking to the victim and convincing her to press charges, the police began to investigate the case, Jensen said.

“The safety of our students is always of concern, which is why University Police worked closely with Provo police for the arrest of the suspect,” Jenkins said.

Provo police picked him up at his apartment off-campus for questioning Nov. 8. He was arrested and charged with the incident, Jensen said.

Rape and sodomy are both first-degree felonies, and forcible sexual assault is a second-degree felony, according to the court records.

Meyer was put in the Utah County jail Nov. 8, and was released on $50,000 bail Nov. 9, according to the court records.

As soon as the suspect was identified, he was closely monitored, and shortly thereafter he was banned from campus, which includes that he cannot enroll in classes, Jenkins said.

BYU was told that after he was released on bail he left the state, Jenkins said.

“We deemed him an imminent threat to our population,” Lemmon said.
