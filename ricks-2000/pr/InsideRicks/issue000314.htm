EXEMPLARY EMPLOYEES

Three Ricks College employees have been honored as the year 2000 Winter Exemplary Employees for giving excellent service. Kelly Hymas, the college's registrar; Jo Lynn Davis, manager for the bakery in Food Services; and Eva Sommer, secretary in Public Relations, were acknowledged for their contributions by Elder David B. Haight and President David A. Bednar Tuesday at the weekly devotional. "Each semester the college recognizes one administrator and two staff members who are serving the college, as well as the community, and making extraordinary contributions in their various areas," said Carla Ricks, director of Personnel Services." President's Council evaluates to see if the nominee is exemplifying the seven guiding principles: teaching, attitude, friendliness, neat personal appearance, job performance, service, and overall improvement."

A Ricks alumnus, Hymas began working at the college 14 years ago in computer services. His background has enabled him to make an excellent contribution to the position of Registrar which he began in 1997. One of his biggest highlights was helping install Internet registration.

Davis graduated from Ricks College in 1970 and started working in Food Services in 1971. She now coordinates the schedules of 17 "great people who give their support" in the bakery that serves a growing college and surrounding community. Davis says "I am encouraged to learn new things and new trades everyday."

Sommer started working at Ricks College in 1991. Since 1999 she has been the secretary for Public Relations. Reflecting on previous employment at the Church Office Building, Sommers said, "Working with the high-caliber students and employees to me is like I'm still working with General Authorities."

HORTICULTURE STUDENTS REAP ACCLAMATIONS

For three days a team of fourteen Ricks horticulture students participated in the twenty-fourth annual ALCA Student Career Days at Mississippi State University. The team placed third out of 43 schools, the majority of which were four-year universities. The event is staged much like a horticulture Olympics with twenty-five events and challenges the depth of understanding and variety abilities. "It is the best we have ever done," said Byron John, chairman of the Department of Landscape/Horticulture. "We have been told that this was the highest placement of any two-year school in the history of the competition. We received many positive comments about the clean-cut appearance of our students. They were highly sought after for employment by industry representatives." John says, "We are proud to represent Ricks College as the professional and ethical future of horticulture."

FORENSICS TALK AWAY WITH REGIONAL CHAMPIONSHIP

For the seventh time in eight years, the Ricks Forensics team has won the Regional Championship. The Regional tournament was held this year at Dixie College in St. George, Utah. The region covers five states, and includes Casper College, Northwest College, Sheridan College, The College of Eastern Utah, Snow College, Dixie College, Glendale Community College, Mesa College, The College of Southern Idaho, and Ricks. Categories included debate, readers theater, poetry and prose interpretation, program interpretation, persuasive speaking, informative speaking, extemporaneous speaking, and communication analysis. The Ricks College forensics team qualified for Nationals and will be competing in Portland the first week in April.

WOMEN'S WEEK DESIGNED TO ENHANCE PARENT-CHILD RELATIONSHIPS

An estimated 2,000 visitors are expected to attend the annual Women's Week at Ricks College from Thursday to Sunday, March 16-19. While at Ricks, the visitors, who are mainly mothers of current students, will have the opportunity to spend time with their children and attend some activities especially planned for visitors and students. "The purpose of Women's week isn't to have an educational week, but to build relationships between mothers and their students," says Kris Fillmore, organizer of the event that is sponsored by the Associated Women Students.

Regular classes will continue Thursday and Friday and some instructors will allow mothers to attend class with their students. There will also be a wide variety activities during the four-day event. Please refer to the "Upcoming Events" listed below for details of events. For ticket information, contact the Ricks College Ticket Office at 356-2230 or online at www.byui.edu/tickets.

WITTY ENGLISH COMEDY HITS RICKS

"The Importance of Being Ernest," considered an English classic, will be performed March 17-18, 21-25 and 28-29 in the Kirkham Auditorium, including Saturday matinees. The play was written be Oscar Wilde in 1895 and is set in London of that year. "It's a farce, considered a classic in English and has been performed all over the world," says Hyrum Conrad, Ricks College instructor and director of the play. The play is centered around two young couples and their interactions with each other in love and courtship. "It's a comedy, witty, articulate, clever and fun,"Conrad says. "I know the audience will enjoy it." For tickets, call (208) 356-2230 or look online at www.byui.edu/tickets.
