Russians unveil space shuttle for tourists

by Allison Jenks
REE00013@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

The C-21 spacecraft is now only a protocol model, but in a few years may be much more.

Russian Aerospace Company, the Sub-orbital Corporation, presented a full size model of the Cosmopolis XXI, or the C-21, which seats a pilot and two tourist passengers last Thursday, according to ABC News.

The trip will cost $100,000 and require four days of training for the hour long flight, not the six months required for orbital flights, according to CNN.

Nearly 100 people have already paid the deposit for their trips on the spacecraft, which are scheduled to begin in 2004 or 2005, according to CNN.

The 3.5-ton spacecraft is the size of a mini-van. It was developed by Russian engineers and funded by private investors and would be launched on top of a Russian M-55 in high altitude. Approximately12.4 miles into the flight, the C-21 would fire its own engine and climb to 62 miles — the limit of outer space — then begin its space journey, according to CNN.

Passengers — required to wear spacesuits — would be weightless for three to five minutes during the hour-long flight, according to ABC News.

“[Passengers are] from all walks of life, from all countries, people from Japan, Denmark, the U.S., men and women, young and old. Space is something which is a common theme. It is something people have a passion for,” Eric Anderson, president and CEO of Space Adventures, said.

Space Adventures, an American-based company working with the Sub-orbital Corporation, is working with U.S. Airways to offer frequent flier miles that can be applied to space trips, according to ABC News.

NASA estimates a 1-in-250 probability that one of its space shuttle crews won't make it back to Earth. By comparison, passengers on commercial airlines face odds of about one in 2 million of crashing, according to ABC News.
