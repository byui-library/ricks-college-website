ENROLLMENT

The official count is a record with 8,949 students enrolled at Ricks College for fall semester 2000. "We are pleased that this many students are getting an opportunity to come to Ricks College," said Jim Gee, assistant academic vice president. The total compares with 8,628 enrolled last fall and 8,840 students enrolled for the winter semester.

Gee remains cautious about the future. He said "We are trying to stay within our 8,600 ceiling and will be working towards staying within that guideline for future semesters."

Attending Ricks this fall are 2,089 new students and 6,204 continuing students. The remaining 656 are returning after an absence of one or more semesters such as those who are re-entering after serving missions for the LDS Church. All in all, 2,498 of those now attending Ricks are returned missionaries.

Fall enrollment statistics show that the youngest students are 16 and the oldest 65. Most fall in the 18-22 years-old range. Married students total 546.

Students come from all 50 of the states in the United States. In a breakdown of students by state, there are more than 200 students coming from seven different states. 2,883 are from Idaho, 1,433 from Utah, 864 from California, 717 from Washington, 378 from Oregon, 278 from Texas, and 263 from Colorado.

There are 247 international students representing 48 countries. Of these, 141 are from Canada.

There are 5,407 freshman with less than 32 credit hours and 3,542 sophomores with 32 or more credit hours. Women represent 58 percent of the student body with 5,195 enrolled. There are 3,754 men enrolled at Ricks College. Full-time students tally 8,487 with 462 going part time. Statistics are based on enrollment after the first ten days of class.

HOMECOMING

Ricks College is inviting alumni and friends to a millennial homecoming celebration like no other on Oct. 13 and 14. Alumni Association director Devin Shaum says Homecoming is being tied into a commemorate the historic Jacob Spori Building that will be torn down later in the fall and replaced with a new classroom building. He says, "The Spori Building represents a solid foundation upon which to build the future as the college transitions to a four-year university." He encourages people to come and see how the campus has changed and feel the "spirit of Ricks" that has been part of campus life ever since the school was established more than 100 years ago.

The two days of celebration will begin Friday evening with a "Hallelujah Homecoming" concert followed by a light parade, victory celebration and fireworks. It will be followed on Saturday by the annual Emeritus Breakfast, parade, Rexburg Harvest Celebration, Spori commemoration, football game, alumni concert and homecoming dance. (See list of events below.)

Because of the many events associated with this one-of-a-kind homecoming, those planning to attend should purchase tickets as early as possible. For ticket information, call the Ricks College Ticket Office at (208) 356-2230 or 1-800-71RICKS. Tickets may be ordered over the Internet at www.byui.edu/tickets.

DONATED TRUCK HELPS HUMANITARIAN PROJECT

The college expresses appreciation to Andrus Trucking of Idaho Falls for transporting the recently assembled humanitarian project. Some 2,000 Ricks College students recently compiled 8,800 hygiene kits, tied 100 quilts, and cut out fleece toy bunnies and fleece jackets. Box after box were loaded onto the truck for the 250 mile journey from Rexburg to Salt Lake City. The trucking company gladly accepted the opportunity to donate their services when approached by the Development Office. Since nearly all materials were donated by the Latter-day Saint Humanitarian Center and the labor was donated by the students, the donation of transportation was the final element that made the orientation project feasible without cutting into appropriated money. Andrus Trucking took the boxes to the center in Salt Lake where the items will be distributed throughout the world in times of disaster or as special needs become known. Through the efforts and donated services of many, love can stretch love beyond the borders of our college campus, across political boundaries, and into the outstretched arms of those in need.
