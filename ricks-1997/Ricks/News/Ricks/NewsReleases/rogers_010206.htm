Ricks College News Release
February 6, 2001

Devotional: Sandra Rogers

By Trent Toone
Ricks College News Bureau

REXBURG, Idaho — Measuring oneself from an eternal perspectives was the topic of Sandra Rogers’ devotional talk Tuesday at Ricks College.

Rogers, an associate academic vice president at Brigham Young University in Provo, Utah, spoke on the importance of measuring oneself on an eternal level instead of on a worldly level.

"The importance of accurate measures (in our lives) is something we can all appreciate," she said. "In my profession as a nurse I am constantly relying on instruments to give me an accurate picture of a patient’s condition. Yet for all the scientific and practical concerns we have about accurate measurements, we often seem to be inaccurate in how we measure ourselves with others."

"The important thing to remember, as we examine and choose what we want to build, to accomplish, to learn, and to do with our lives, is that when we select the measurements, or standards, or the balances by which we want to be weighted, we are also selecting the master of the scales," she said. "We cannot choose the measurements of one master and hope they will be sufficient for the other -- plumb lines will never tell us how fast someone runs the 100-meter dash."

Rogers shared some suggestions on how one might stay focused on the Lord’s measures.

"Honoring God more (than giving in to the world’s temptations). Regardless of the issue – imagine the strength we could have in simply saying, ‘I honor God more’," she said.

In the second suggestion, Rogers warned against choosing Satan’s tools in our lives.

"Satan may momentarily convince us to use the wrong measuring tools for our lives," she said. "But, what would be even worse for us would be if he were able to deceive us about the finality of those measurements and our inability to change to new ones. The atonement makes it possible to trade measuring sticks as it were; to turn away from false Gods and return to the one and true living God."

Her third suggestion was to be worthy of the companionship of Holy Ghost.

"One of the most important blessings the Lord has given us to keep us on his scales is the gift of the Holy Ghost," Rogers said. "We can test every doctrine and instruction read in the scriptures, or heard from the pulpit in general conference, or spoken by any of those we sustain as prophets and revelators. We can receive answers to our deepest prayers."

Rogers concluded, "I pray that we might choose the correct and eternal measurements of our worth and our direction; that the importance of the Lord’s perspective will distill on our minds and hearts, and that we will truly be worthy of the Holy Ghost to help evaluate ourselves with our instruments," she said.

Next Tuesday’s speaker will be Elder W. Craig Zwick, a member of the LDS Church’s First Quorum of the Seventy. He will speak at 2 p.m. in the Hart Auditorium. Devotionals are broadcast live and repeated at 9 p.m. on KRIC-FM.
