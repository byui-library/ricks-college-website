A YEAR LATER: PROGRESS REPORT ON THE BYU-IDAHO TRANSITION

Since June 21, 2000, it seems as though the once unthinkable has become deeply ingrained into every mind on the Ricks College campus. The school truly is becoming BYU-Idaho and will begin to offer upper level courses this fall. This week is the one-year anniversary of the announcement that Ricks College will become a bachelor's degree-granting university.

President David A. Bednar is planning to meet with area media Thursday to review the past year's accomplishments. He is expected to publicly unveil the university's new system of logos and word marks. Aug. 10 has been selected to be the official day the college becomes known as BYU-Idaho.

During the past year, college employees worked to make it possible to start offering 17 bachelor's degree programs in the fall. A total of 51 baccalaureate programs are expected by 2004, along with 19 associate degrees. Other milestones during the past year have been the hiring of 38 new faculty members (25 due to the changes), groundbreaking for the new Spori and multi-use buildings, announcement of the Thomas E. Ricks Building and Gardens, and candidacy status for accreditation of the proposed bachelor's degree programs.

A multi-day celebration is being planned for Sept. 18-22 during homecoming week that will include fireworks, a concert with Gladys Knight and the last homecoming football game.

Beginning in the fall, BYU-Idaho will offer upper-division classes leading to integrated bachelor's degrees and secondary education programs. In addition to bachelor's degrees, BYU-Idaho will offer specialized associate degrees that prepare students to be employable after completing two years of schooling.



NEW HEAD BASEBALL COACH NAMED

Don Schiess has been named the new head baseball coach. He will take over for the departing Jerry Schlegelmilch. Schiess will coach baseball as Ricks College becomes BYU-Idaho and ends intercollegiate athletics at the end of the 2001-2002 school year. This will be the third time around in the dugout for Coach Schiess. His first stint as head coach at Ricks covered eight seasons from 1970 to 1977. His teams finished first in their division three times, in 1971, 1973, and 1974. After sitting out for nine seasons, Schiess returned to the dugout for three years, 1988 to 1990. His 1989 team won another division championship.

Schiess recently retired from Ricks where he had been serving as director of intramurals. "We're grateful that Don would come back to help us through this transition," said Garth Hall, athletic director and vice president of advancement. "He'll help us be competitive in our final season."

Schlegelmilch will continue as a teacher at BYU-Idaho through the summer and fall semesters, then coach baseball in the spring at Southern Virginia University. 'Schleg' coached the Vikings for eleven seasons, winning 290 games while losing 259. His teams won division championships in 1994 and 1995 and finished second in Region 18 in 1995.



FASHION CLASS UNDERTAKES HUMANITARIAN SERVICE PROJECT

Fashion students in Joleen Smith's clothing manufacturing class at Ricks College have been mass producing items to be given to needy people around the world. Instead of just listening to lectures and reading a textbook, students in the first summer term class made about 1,000 children's school bags that are being distributed through LDS Humanitarian Services. "Rather than just teaching out of the manual, I felt it would be more beneficial if they had a hands-on experience," Smith says. "It was a wonderful project knowing the need for these items. I would do it again in an instant."

The 10 students spent about 300 hours on the project that involved designing, cutting out, sewing and assembling the school bags, as well as making layettes (baby clothing), booties and receiving blankets. Smith says the heavy fabric for the bags was donated by Delta Airlines to LDS Humanitarian Services, and in turn was given to the class to use. The students spent two days at Pioneer Plus in Ashton where they were able to cut out multiple layers of material at one time using industrial cutting knives. They then returned to the college where they sewed and assembled the bags and other items



SWINGSET JAZZ SEPTET TO PERFORM AT RICKS BEFORE ITALY TOUR

Swingset, a Ricks College faculty jazz septet, will perform its tour-launching concert Friday, June 22, as part of the Summer in the Snow entertainment series at Ricks College. Swingset will travel later this summer to jazz festivals in Italy. Their pre-tour show will be at 7:30 p.m. in the Kirkham Auditorium. Tickets are $3 for the general public and available from the Ricks College Ticket Office. Call 356-2230 for more information.

The concert will include the group's own arrangements of traditional standards such as "You'd Be So Nice to Come Home to," "My Favorite Things" and "On the Sunny Side of the Street" as well as a few jazz standards and originals. The group includes Ricks employees Mark Watkins and Mat Miles on saxophone, Brian Pyper on trombone, Wilson Brown on piano, David Taylor on percussion, vocalist Missy Babcock and Ricks student Adam Pyper on bass.
