Welcome to our class! I hope you will enjoy learning and teaching this semester.

 

Aims

--to enhance understanding of and appreciation for the words and work of the living prophets.

--to develop the discipline of recording “thoughts” and “feelings” (D&C 8:2) that come when studying prophetic teachings.

--to improve capacity and readiness to learn from General Conference and Conference Reports.

--to recognize and learn from distinctive attributes of the living apostles and prophets.

--to prepare for lifelong study, teaching, and application of the words of the living prophets.

 

Please download and study “Principles and Promises to Guide Our Learning” from my faculty web page. As you study this document, identify and record the promises and principles that will help you most as a student and teacher.

 

Course Activities

1. Preparation: Before coming to class, please study the assigned materials and record insights and impressions that come from their study. Students should also come prepared to learn and teach by the Spirit. Much of your most valuable learning and growth can take place as you read, ponder, and write on your own. I will ask you to assess the quantity and quality of your preparation regularly. I may help assess your preparation as needed. Basic expectation: read the whole assignment before class and record some thoughts and impressions.

2. Attendance and participation: Class presentations and discussions should complement your personal study, as they highlight important principles, answer questions, and promote understanding in harmony with the Spirit and the course aims. Please participate in that process. I will ask you to assess the quantity and quality of your participation regularly.  I may help assess your participation as needed. Basic expectation: be in class, on time, with scriptures, notebook, and intent to listen, write, and speak meaningfully.

3. Conference Report review: Using the Ensign, May 2002, please read all talks by members of the First Presidency and Quorum of the Twelve at the April 2002 General Conference. As you read, note the teachings that impress you most and record thoughts and impressions that apply to you. Chapter 13 in the CES Manual may help you organize your study and recording. Please complete your study of First Presidency talks by 1 October and your study of Quorum of the Twelve talks by 3 October —we will discuss and assess what you learn in class on those days. Please type a one-page single space summary of your insights and the teachings that impress you most (due in class October 3).

4. Conference participation:

a.        Please participate in the October 2002 General Conference and record your observations and impressions about the proceedings and the teachings of the prophets. We will discuss what you learn in class on October 8.

b.       Please read as many talks from the November 2002 Ensign as you can this semester. Using one or more talks by members of the First Presidency or Quorum of the Twelve, compare your insights and impressions when reading the talk to those you had when hearing the talk at General Conference. Please type a one-page single space summary of what you learn from the process (due in class December 17).

5. Report on apostolic attributes: Using published sources by or about the living prophets, find a story from the life of one of the living prophets that illustrates one or more of his distinctive attributes. Type a brief report that introduces and relates the story, identifies the attributes it illustrates, and reports the source accurately. If you submit your report early enough, it may be possible to share it in class. Reports are due anytime on or before class on November 7.

6. Living prophets update the CES Manual: Please choose a principle in the CES Student Manual (e.g, “5-4 Doctrinal Interpretation is the Province of the First Presidency”) and find two or more statements from living prophets that teach that principle. Please type the statements and the principle they teach (due anytime on or before class on November 26).

7. Content mastery assessment: There will be at least one exam to assess your mastery of principles taught in selected chapters of the CES Manual. The exam will be scheduled in the Testing Center in late October or November and will use a variety of question types. There may also be some assessment of your familiarity with the identity of the living prophets.

8. Special project: Please take advantage of the unique opportunity this university course provides you to learn, understand, teach, and apply the teachings of the living prophets. Choose a research/teaching project that will deepen your understanding of what one or more of the living prophets is teaching and how he teaches. You will have occasion to report on your project and to describe what you have gained from it in an essay during the final examination period. I hope your project will benefit you beyond this class. If you need help with ideas for a project, talk to me, and then start.

I believe that the Board of Trustees has good reasons for requiring graded academic work in Religious Education courses at this university. I believe these courses are like no other gospel study experience you can have, and so my expectations for you and for me are high. Please do your best to meet my basic expectations and to go beyond them by constantly striving to enhance the quality of your work. Quality refers to the excellence of a process and a product. Although quality requires time, it also comes from intelligent design, creativity, consistent refinement, and righteous risk-taking.  I believe that the highest grades should be reserved for truly excellent work, rather than work that meets basic expectations. There are opportunities for both you and me to review the excellence of your work (meaning both the process and products). I hope that we all will enhance our ability to recognize the quality of our work.  In this course, when basic expectations are specified, satisfying those basic expectations earns a score of 80%.
