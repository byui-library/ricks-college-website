Computer Information Systems (CIS) involves the application of computers in information systems and problem solving. CIS teaches computer programming, systems analysis, systems design, networking, and Internet skills necessary to prepare a student for employment in a business environment.

The Associate in Arts and Sciences allows a student to enter the workforce as an information professional upon graduation and/or transfer to a four-year college or university. This means that students completing the requirements for this degree can work full or part time as an information professional and still pursue a four-year degree in business. The program is designed to meet the requirements for the American Assembly of Collegiate Schools of Business (AACSB). Transfer students should check the program requirements of the school to which they are transferring and with a CIS advisor to assure that core requirements for transfer are met.
No D credit is allowed in computer courses for a CIS major.

A keyboarding skill of at least 35 words a minute is strongly suggested for CIS major.

COURSE DESCRIPTIONS

Credits

CIS 140* Microcomputer Applications 3
Introduction to the use of microcomputers in business including Windows, introductory presentation programs, spreadsheets, database management systems, and the integration of these programs with other Windows programs and the World Wide Web.

CIS 201 Management Information Systems 3
Prerequisite: CIS 140, or Consent of Instructor

Introduction to information systems; use of information technology to solve business problems. Hands-on use of spreadsheet, database, HTML, and telecommunications software in a business context. (BYU transfer)

CIS 202 Visual Programming Languages 4
Application of event driven and structured programming techniques to the development of information system software. Emphasis is on program logic and user interface design. Using a visual language, students will design, code, test, and debug programs.

CIS 235 Internet Techniques 3
Prerequisite: CIS 140 or consent of instructor

Study of concepts, strategies, tools and ethical issues involved in the use of the Internet. Focuses on design and construction of Home Pages including HTML. Includes Web Server management concepts and development of Internet and Intranet web sites and servers.

CIS 240 Networking 3
Principles of networking designed to provide an understanding of data communication protocols, transmission systems, media, software and hardware interfacing. NT Network operating system covered.

CIS 256 Systems Analysis and Design 3
Examination of systems development life cycle emphasizing tools and methods of analysis and general design including investigation, study, definition, systems specification/selection, modeling, and normalization.

CIS 257 AS/400 Concepts 2
Introduction to the following IBM AS/400 computer topics: Control Language, files, DFU, and Query.

CIS 258 RPG IV Programming Language 4
Introduction to the RPG IV programming language, including programming concepts, design, data structures, report programming, screen design, subfiles, and interactive programming.

CIS 265 CASE Tools 3
An introduction to the use of computer-aided systems engineering tools (CASE). CASE tools are used in the analysis and design of computer information systems. CASE tools will be used to develop several small introductory information systems.

CIS 286 Job Search 1
A career planning, job search and interview preparation course.

This course should be taken during the Sophomore year.
