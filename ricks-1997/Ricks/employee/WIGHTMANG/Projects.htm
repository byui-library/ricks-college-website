Guidelines for Application Projects
Purpose
    It has often been said the teacher usually learns the most.  This is because the teacher usually spends the most time and effort in preparation.  Therefore, the purpose of this project is to give you an opportunity to apply doctrines in your life as if you are the teacher.  President Ezra Taft Benson once said:
    I add my voice to these wise and inspired brethren and say to you that one of the most important things you can do . . . is to immerse yourselves in the scriptures.  Search them diligently.  Feast upon the words of Christ.  Learn the doctrine.  Master the principles that are found therein.  There are few other efforts that will bring greater dividends . . . .  There are few other ways to gain greater inspiration.
    But that alone, as valuable as it is, is not enough.  You must also bend your efforts and your activities to stimulating meaningful scripture study among the members of the Church.  ("The Power of the Word" Ensign, May 1986, p. 81) (emphasis added).
    This project will seek to give you the opportunity to search, feast, learn, and master the content outlined in the blocks of scripture given by preparing lesson outlines as if you were the teacher.  Then you will have an opportunity to bend your efforts in stimulating meaningful scripture study among other members of the church by actually teaching what you learned to a group of friends or roommates, etc.  Using this form of application will hopefully cause you to "master" the scriptures as President Benson suggested.

Outline
    The intent of this project is to create a manual (of sorts) to teach the Book of Ether.  You are to create lesson outlines for the following three blocks of scripture:  #1) Ether 1-6 ,  #2) Ether 7-11, and #3) Ether 12-15.  See Due Dates On Your Syllabus For Each Section!  A quality document will include in-depth research.  Feel free to refer to commentaries and talks that might give you greater insight and anything you might find on the church web sight.  It is anticipated that you will use about 8 hours outside of class per section.  That should allow for some quality study and research!!  Each lesson outline should contain three main sections.

1.  Introduction to the lesson -   Treat this section as sort of thesis statement, even though you won't formally write it as one.  Begin your outline by dividing up the material into logical blocks of scripture to be discussed.  Make a list of possible doctrines or principles that could be discussed from these blocks of scripture.  Introduce possible audience and objectives.
2.  Main Body -  In this section you will outline the doctrines and principles to be discussed and how you will go about teaching them.  Create an outline for the scripture blocks you have chosen and show me how you intend to stimulate meaningful scripture study using the results of your study and research.  Just remember what President Benson said . . . Search . . . Feast . . . Learn . . . Master!  All research/quotes (preferably from the Brethren), and scriptures, etc. should be completely typed out and properly cited.  Use whatever format you would like but type this in a way that a complete stranger could take your outline and use it as a manual to teach the Book of Ether.
3.  Conclusion - What will you use to emphasize the important points discussed.
Teaching
    Once you have completed the three lesson outlines for the Book of Ether, you will need to teach one of the three lessons to someone else.  Some ideas may be in a Family Home Evening lesson, an apartment discussion with roommates, a talk in church, or a lesson in your ward.  Whatever you choose, you need to have at least five people in the group you teach.  You could also choose to miss out on the opportunity of  teaching for the loss of  10 points.  Teaching time should be no less than 20 - 30 minutes, unless you are giving a talk in church and are assigned less time to speak.  You obviously won't be able to present all your material in 30 minutes.  Choose whichever lesson or portions of lesson that will best suit what you are doing.  However, if you can completely teach one of the three lessons you prepared, all the better.  When you include your teaching score at the end of lesson #3, please do so by adding an additional page that contains not only your score of 1 - 10, but also an explanation of why you gave yourself the score you did. What went well? . . . what did not? . . . what would you change in your lesson plan? . . . etc.

Scoring
    This project is worth a total of 100 points: 30 points for each lesson, and 10 points for teaching. The 30 point lesson preparation score and the teaching score (again, included with lesson #3 only) will come from you.  When you turn in your project, I would like you to write your lesson preparation score at the top of the project.  Score yourself for each lesson according to the following scale:

Lesson Preparation Score
    (0) did not complete the assignment
    (1-10)  completed the assignment in a hurried or rushed manner with very little time or thought given.
    (11-20)  completed the assignment but lacked quality research, insight and depth.  Used minimal time required.
    (21-30)   spent maximum time amount required by putting together a quality document for future use.  Incorporates quality research, insight, and depth.  Gained pertinent insights by doing the project.

    *** I  reserve the right to lower or raise your Lesson Preparation score according to my discretion!! ***

Teaching Score (with LESSON #3 ONLY!)
    (1-10) You will score yourself on a scale of 1 - 10 based upon how well you stimulated meaningful scripture study among the members of the church you taught.  The score should also reflect the quality of material you compiled.  Was it really useful and did you present it in a useful way?  Be sure to explain.

End Notes
    1.  Again, you are to use a approximately 24 hours (8 hours per lesson) of out-of-class time to produce a quality, well thought out document!
    2.  Use a cover sheet to put your Name, Class, Hour, and your Lesson Preparation Score.  DO NOT turn this project in with a binder, folder, or protective cover!  They only get in the way!  A good staple is all I want.
