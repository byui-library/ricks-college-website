COMMUNICATION 110

Course Portfolio Guidelines



Due: April 17 at 5 p.m. Late portfolios will not be accepted.



Assignment objectives: To provide incentive for extra, personalized study of the mass media in areas that particularly interest you. To give you something useful and valuable to carry away from this course that can serve your academic and personal needs in the future.



Required contents:

*A significant set of media observations with regular (not necessarily daily) entries. These can range over any of the following: your feelings about the media's role in today's society, personal reflections when the media has had impact on your life, reactions to readings for class or class discussions, analysis of your own media consumption habits and how you can improve them, commentary on specific media you are exposed to, insights from introspection or discussions with others, noting relationships between what you learn about the mass media and gospel principles, etc.

*Your personal projects from the semester.



Other possible contents: No other single element is required, but more is expected to be included in your final portfolio. Feel free to consider any of the following, or use these as a springboard to your own ideas:



• Listing, summary or copies of outside reading or other research

• Listing of current events in the mass media you've collected over the semester.

• Copies of articles you like (or disagree with)

• Listing of books about the mass media you've either read this semester or would like to read someday

• Listing of reference materials (including on the internet) that can help you learn more about the media in your area of interest

• Outline of a "career path" of steps you'll need to take in study and experience if you want to eventually work in the mass media

• Interviews with people in the mass media, media critics, parents and family members, friends, church leaders, those who have experienced life without many of the modern mass media, etc.

• Personal research on media messages or content, opinion polls, observational studies, etc.

• Summaries of discussion groups, internet chat sessions, media-related speakers or presentations

• Analysis of media coverage of national affairs or politics

• Ideas you develop along the way -- to improve the media, forge a unique career for yourself, make money, protect yourself or your family, better balance an active media life with membership in the church, etc.



How portfolio will be evaluated: Consistency and overall quantity and quality of study and work; scope and breadth of efforts; organization and packaging of portfolio; potential usefulness to you in future studies and life. This will be viewed as an overall reflection of your work in this course for the semester.
