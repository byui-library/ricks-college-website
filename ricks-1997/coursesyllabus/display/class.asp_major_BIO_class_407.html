Medical Microbiology and Immunology
MED MICRO/IMMU
CLASS CODE: BIO 407 CREDITS:  4
DIVISION: AGRICULTURAL AND LIFE SCIENCES
DEPARTMENT: BIOLOGY
GENERAL EDUCATION: This course does not fulfill a General Education requirement.
CATALOG DESCRIPTION: Fundamental and clinical principles of microbiology and immunology as they relate to bacteria, viruses, fungi, and parasites.
DESCRIPTION: Fundamental and clinical principles of microbiology and immunology as they relate to bacteria, viruses, fungi, and parasites. Major areas of study will include (1) the immune system on the organismic, cellular, and molecular levels; (2) infectious agents and their morphology, biology, epidemiology; and (3) the resulting host-parasite interactions between the two. Includes both lectures and laboratories.
TOPICS: Innate (non-specific) immunity,
Complement system
Response to antigens
Immunoglobulins
Indiotypes
Specific Immunity
Major Histocompatibility Complex
Immuno Regulation
Vaccines
Bacteria structure
Virus Replication Strategies
Classification and Identification of Infectious Agents
Antibiotics and mechanisms of resistance virulence factors
Experimental approaches to investigating host-microbe interaction
Various paradigms of microbial-host interactions
OBJECTIVES: The student should be able to demonstrate a knowledge of the fundamental life processes in microorganisms at the molecular level; to differentiate the various disease causing microorganisms: to be able to list both harmful and helpful attribute of microorganisms; to understand how animals develop resistance and immunity to diseases; and to be familiar with the manner in which microorganisms gain entry into the body, establish themselves, and produce disease.
REQUIREMENTS: Enrollment in lab and attendance in class and completion of all assignments, labs, and exams.
