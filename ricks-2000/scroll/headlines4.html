Mormon Tabernacle Choir

by Emily Toy
Post Register

Barrus Concert Hall at BYU-Idaho is a somewhat familiar place for the Mormon Tabernacle Choir. They last sang there in November 1983 for the dedication of the Keith Martindal Stefan Memorial Organ.

As the choir returns to Rexburg to perform other members are especially excited to visit their old stomping grounds. The choir of more than 300 voices includes many from the upper-Snake River Valley.

“Ricks College was a fabulous time for me. With less than 4,000 students there when I attended, it was then that I met some of my closest friends,” said Ron Gunnel, 1975 Ricks Graduate and choir member.

Jeff and Jenny Pederson, both members of the choir and 1977 Ricks College graduates, look forward to coming to Rexburg so family members who still reside in Rexburg, and haven’t seen the couple perform as part of the choir, can watch.

The Mormon Tabernacle Choir first sang more than a century and a half ago, only 29 days after the pioneers entered the Salt Lake Valley in 1847. Their first major concert tour was in 1893 when the choir sang at the Colombian Exposition in Chicago, being awarded a second prize of $1000. Since then, the choir has made various international and national tours, released more than 150 albums, held a 72 year spot doing the weekly national radio broadcast Music and the Spoken Word and is scheduled to perform at various times during the 2002 Olympic Winter Games. The choir is becoming more renowned through video satellite broadcasts and Internet broadcasts and as they agree to do other special performances.

Their program is based on family, country and God. In reflection of the events of September 11, there was no need to modify the show for the circumstances. The choirs sings songs faith and hope, choir director Craig Jessop said.

The program the choir will present is the program that they did on their two week tour of the Southern States in June.

Becky Strong, a 1993 Sugar Salem High School graduate and a Mormon Tabernacle Choir member, is excited to be performing at the University. She believes that performing now rather than before the events of September 11, has brought more meaning to coming back to Rexburg. The death of Brady Howell, also a 1993 Sugar Salem High School graduate, at the Pentagon has reminded her of the tender feelings she has for her friends in this area. Strong served on student council with Howell in high school.

“With only 103 people in our graduating class we became very close. [Howell’s death] really hit home,” Strong said. “Home is still fresh for me; my friends are still there. I am definitely excited to return.”

Strong is the youngest female choir member.

Members of the choir, though the auditions are selective, are called as special missionaries for the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.

“The interesting thing is, we don’t [often] see the people we touch,” Jeff Pederson said.

Diane Homer, a 1971 Idaho Falls High School graduate who attended Ricks College for two and one half years, said when they perform she tries to focus on someone who looks as though they won’t enjoy the concert at any cost. As the concert persists she watches the facial expressions of the individual and sees them change from boredom to peace as they feel something different.

Homer, who has been a member of the choir for nearly 15 years, is looking forward to returning to Rexburg to perform in front of people she knows. She is also excited to walk through the familiar halls and practice rooms, where she spent much of her time as a student.

“I loved Ricks. I had exciting experiences there. That is where I fell in love with my husband. I know it isn’t Ricks anymore, but always will be to me,” Homer said.

Jessop said, “It’s wonderful being involved in entertainment that brings joy, peace and hope to it’s audience and working with wonderful individuals while singing praises to God.”
