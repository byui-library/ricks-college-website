Scope and Content

The massive project that generated the documents in this collection ran from 1975 until 1978. The project, entitled "The Musical Heritage of the Upper Snake River Valley and its effect on human lives" was an in-depth evaluative study of the region's musical traditions. The collection comprises the records created in the course of the project. It includes correspondence, research files, biographical sketches of local musicians, music history of communities, and texts of presentations. Photographs, slides and video tapes have been separated.

The originator and leading spirit behind the project was Ricks College music instructor Ruth Barrus. The Barrus concert hall in the Snow building is named after her.

The primary goal of the project was to make the public aware of the significance of the valley's musical traditions and in Barrus's own words, "motivate a new cultural thrust, a resurgence of fine cultural activities." Secondarily, she hoped to write a history from the findings, one that would provide data to help hard pressed administrators support costly music programs in valley schools and communities. Unfortunately, Ruth Barrus died before she was able to complete this task.

In order to carry her message to the people Barrus prepared a slide show on the musical history of schools, communities, clubs and special individuals. These presentations were delivered in each community and reflect each community's own unique musical culture. The collection includes all of these presentations, which taken together, provide an unrivaled history of the musical heritage of the area. As the years pass the historical documentation provided by the collection grows ever more significant.

The project enjoyed widespread public support. A grant from the Association for the Humanities in Idaho provided funding. The planning committee of the project consisted of former Ricks College president John L. Clarke, Ricks professors Norman Ricks and Dr. Alan C. Clark, community worker Mrs. Lenore Passey and Dwain Silvester from KID-TV. Various community organizations from throughout the upper Snake River valley sponsored presentations.
