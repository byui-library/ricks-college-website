The Formal Research Report

    The purpose of this document is to provide English 216 students with the guidelines and standards necessary to prepare a formal research report. The three main sections of the report include front material, main body, and end material.

 

Front Material

1. Cover
2. Title page
3. Transmittal correspondence
4. Summary or abstract
5. Table of contents
6. List of illustrations

Body

7. Introduction
8. Discussion--Body Sections
9. Conclusions/Recommendations

End Material

10. References
11. Glossary
12. Appendix

 

 

Front Material

1. Cover

- The title of the document
- The name of the writer
- The name or logo writer=s organization

2. Title Page

- Center all the elements.
- Name the contents of the report in the title.
- Use a 1-1/2 inch left-hand margin.
- Use either all caps or initial caps and lower-case letters; use bold-face when appropriate. Do not use Aglitzy@ typefaces, such as outlined or cursive fonts.
- Include the writer=s name and title or department, the date, the recipient=s name and title or department.

In very long reports, use small Roman numerals (i, ii, iii) for all the pages before the main body. In this case count the title page as page i, but do not put the i on the page. The next page is the transmittal correspondence which is page ii, but since it is correspondence, count but do not number it. The first page you actually number is the Table of Contents which becomes page iii.

3. Transmittal Correspondence

Transmittal correspondence is a memo or letter that directs the report to someone. The correspondence contains:

- the title of the report.
- a statement of when it was requested.
- a very general statement of the report=s purpose and scope.
- an explanation of problems encountered (for example, some unavailable data).
- acknowledgment of those who were particularly helpful in assembling the report.

4. Summary or Abstract

After reading a summary, the reader should know

- the report=s purpose and the problem it addresses.
- the conclusions.
- the major facts on which the conclusions are based.
- the recommendations.

Follow these guidelines to summarize your formal report.

- Concentrate this information into as few words as possible--one page at most.
- Write the summary after you have written the rest of the report.
- Avoid technical terminology.

5. Table of Contents

- Title the page Table of Contents.
- Present the name of each section in the same wording and format as it appears in the text. If a section title is all caps in the text, place it in all caps in the table of contents.
- Do not underline in the table of contents; the lines are so powerful that they overwhelm the words.
- Do not use Apage@ or Ap.@ before the page numbers.
- Use only the page number on which the section starts.
- Set margins so that page numbers align on the right.
- Present no more than two levels of heads.
- Use a leader, a series of dots, to connect words to page numbers.

6. List of Illustrations

- Use the title List of Illustrations if it contains both figures and tables; list figures first, then tables.
- If the list contains only figures or only tables, call it List of Figures or List of Tables.
- List the number, title, and page of each visual aid.

Main Body

7. Introduction

A formal introduction includes statements of purpose, scope, procedure, and problem and background.

Purpose

State the purpose in one or two sentences. Follow these guidelines:

- State the purpose clearly. Use one of two forms: AThe purpose of this report is. . .@or AThis report presents. . . .@
- Use the present tense.

Scope

A scope statement reveals the topics covered in a report. Follow these guidelines:

- Identify the main sections, or topics, of the report.
- Specify the boundaries or limits of your investigation.

Procedure

The procedure statement--also called the methodology statement--names the process followed in investigating the topic of the report. This statement establishes a writer=s credibility by showing that he or she took all the proper steps. Follow these guidelines:

- Explain all actions you took: the people you interviewed, the research you performed, the sources you consulted.
- Write this statement in past tense.

Brief Problem and Background Statements

You must explain the problem. Your goal is to help the readers understand--and agree with--your solution because they view the problem as you do. You may also need to provide background, especially for secondary or distant readers. Follow these guidelines:

- Give basic facts about the problem.
- Specify the causes or origin of the problem.
- Explain the significance of the problem (short-term and long term).
- Name the source of your involvement.

8. Discussion--Body Sections

Paginating

- Assign a page number to each piece of paper in the report.
- Place the numbers in the upper right-hand corner or bottom middle of the page, with no punctuation.
- Use Arabic numerals in the upper right-hand corner (may use a running head).
- Begin numbering the main body at the introduction as page 1.
- Suppress numbering on the first page of the main body (count but don=t number).

Indicating Chapter Divisions

To make the report more Aformal,@ begin each new major section at the top of a page.

Headings

- Make each level look different to express your visual logic.
- Make higher levels more prominent than smaller.
- Left is more prominent than indented.
- The center of the page is the highest position. But use it only if you have three levels or more.
- Boldface print is more prominent than nonboldface print. Use italics for heads only if you boldface them.
- Make the wording parallel with each level. Use all noun phrases, all questions, or all -ing words.
- Use wording specific to your content, not generic wording. For instance, use Factors in Total Cost rather than Cost, or use a question such as What Factors Affect Total Cost?
- Indicating two levels of heads. Most documents need only one or two levels of heads. Most writers use a side left head as the level 1 head and a paragraph head as the level 2 head.

Graphics

- Number figures consecutively throughout the report, using arabic numerals.
- Use a brief, clear title to specify the content of the graph. Do not punctuate after the title.
- Put the caption below the figure.
- Place graphics within a box border.

9. Conclusion/Recommendations

- Relate each conclusion to specific data. Don=t write conclusions about material you have not discussed in text.
- Keep commentary brief.
- Number each recommendation

Make the solution to the problem the first recommendation

 

End Material

10. References

- Title the reference page References.
- Present information for all entries following exact APA format.
- Use hanging indentation (indent the second and succeeding lines 5 spaces).
- Alphabetize sources by author=s last name.

11. Glossary (optional)

- Place each term at the left margin, and start the definition at an indent farther to the right. Start all lines of the definition at this indent.
- Alphabetize the terms.

12. Appendix (optional)

The appendix contains information of a subordinate, supplementary, or highly technical nature that you do not want to place in the body of the report.
