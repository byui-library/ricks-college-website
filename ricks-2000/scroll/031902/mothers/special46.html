Basic women’s rights overlooked

by Elizabeth Hatchett
HAT01004@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

A woman in burqa awaits medical treatment with her child. Pakistani clerics are accusing aid organizations of vulgarity by demanding rights for women and of trying to replace their Islamic fervor with Christianity. AP Photo Archive
In Pakistan, a 16-year-old, mentally handicapped girl was raped repeatedly by a junior clerk who worked in the local government department of agriculture, according to Amnesty International.

Lal Jamilla Madokhel’s uncle reported the crime to local authorities who arrested the man responsible. It was later reported that he was in jail because he needed protection from the girl’s family not because he committed a crime. He received no punishment for this crime.

Madokhel, on the other hand, was turned over to her tribe, the Mazuzai, in the Kurram Agency.

A Jirga, a group of Pashtun tribesmen, decided the girl had brought shame to her tribe, and the only way to restore honor was to kill the girl, according to Amnesty International.

She was shot and killed in front of the tribe.

This is only one example of the violations women endure daily across the world. Despite the fact treaties have been passed, signed and ratified by countries to prevent it, the countless women who are suffering are still ignored, according to Amnesty International.

The Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women, which was ratified by 168 counties as of May 2001, gives the definition of a women’s right violation:

“Any distinction, exclusion or restriction made on the basis of sex which has the effect or purpose of impairing of nullifying the recognition, enjoyment or exercise by women, irrespective of their marital status, on a basis of equality of men and women, of human rights and fundamental freedoms in the political, economic, social, cultural, civil or any other field,” according to United Nations CEDAW document.

These things are not happening, and it can be seen around the world in violence that still haunts women, according to Amnesty International.

Although the Islamic religion specifically said women should be given respect from men, these ideas have changed over time to fit political agendas. Organizations of Muslim women are attempting to fix the problems that face women in Islamic countries, according to mwlusa.org.

A common form of violence against women is abuse from their husbands.

The Muslim nations are not the only countries where this happens and goes unnoticed or where no punishment is given.

In Peru, although it was one of the first South American countries to make legislation against domestic violence, women are abused to the point of serious injury or death. They are not allowed to even mention what has happened to them, according to www.hrw.org.

This also happens in Russia, Jordan, the United States and many other countries around the world.

Countries such as Israel, Japan, Trinidad and Tobago, Brazil, Pakistan, Peru, Argentina, Costa Rica and Bangladesh each have less than 10 shelters for battered women.

In many countries, female genital mutilation occurs.

FGM is a term used to refer to any practice which includes the removal or alteration of the female genitalia.

Every day 6,000 girls are mutilated, that is more than 200,000 per year, according to www.sigi.org.

“Female genital mutilation, another form of violence against women, has no basis in Islam. Rather, it is a cultural practice which must be eliminated through education and the empowerment of women.”

In the United States, it is estimated that one in five women will be victims of rape in their lifetime. A women is raped every three minutes, and 55 percent of American women report having experienced rape and/or physical assault in their lifetime.

Many organizations, including Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch, have ways people can help to eliminate this violence.

“States should pursue by all appropriate means and without delay a policy of eliminating violence against women,” United Nations Deceleration on the Elimination of Violence against Women said.
