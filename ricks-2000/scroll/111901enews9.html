Man shot by police suspected in his ex-wife's disappearance

COEUR D’ALENE, Idaho (AP) — Michael Penson, killed more than a week ago by city police after he shot his wife in the leg, has since become suspected in his ex-wife’s disappearance 18 years ago, police in Washington state said.

In the wake of Penson’s shooting, Tacoma police investigators have reopened the probe into the disappearance of Karen Ann Marzano of Tacoma, Penson's ex-wife, department spokesman Jim Mattheis said.

“They are taking another look at the case,” Mattheis said in a copyright article in the Coeur d’Alene Press newspaper. “I guess you could call him a ‘person of interest.’”

Karen Marzano was 30 years old at the time and had been married to Michael Penson about 12 years when she was reported missing by her parents May 26, 1983, said Mattheis and Marzano's brother, Jim Marzano, 42.

“They never found any evidence or anything,” Marzano said.

After awaiting news for years, Marzano’s parents died without knowing what happened to their daughter.

“My mom just passed away in January,” Marzano said. “I’d like to see if they can figure out what happened — exactly what happened,” he said.

While Tacoma police dust off old files in the case, Idaho State Police continue to investigate the shooting of Penson by five Coeur d’Alene Police officers.

The five officers and a sixth who was on the scene are all on standard administrative leave as the investigation continues, Police Chief Tom Cronin said.

Cronin said his officers could be back on duty next week.

Penson was killed the evening of Nov. 9 after he emerged from his home behind his wife, Katrina Penson, and fired one shot at her, wounding her in the leg.

Coeur d’Alene Police officers on the scene opened fire. It is unclear how many shots were fired or how many times Michael Penson was hit.

Penson, a native of Billings, moved to Idaho 11 years ago from western Washington.
