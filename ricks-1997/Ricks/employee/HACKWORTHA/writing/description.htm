Write three practice paragraphs.  Take your time and just worry about one practice at a time.  Each practice will be based on a model.  Study the model carefully.  Then pick a different subject and write a paper based on the model.  The first two models come from Mark Twain; the third model comes from Victor Hugo.

Twain Two
Step 1.  Study the following paragraph written by Mark Twain.  Read it several times so that you feel its rhythm and meaning.
 
As I said, I spent some of part of every year at the farm until I was twelve or thirteen years old.  The life which I led there with my cousins was full of charm, and so is the memory of it yet.  I can call back the solemn twilight of the mystery of the deep woods, the earthy smells, the faint odors of the wild flowers, the sheen of rain-washed foliage, the rattling clatter of drops when the wind shook the trees, the far-off hammering of woodpeckers, and the muffled drumming of the wood pheasants in the remoteness of the forest, the snapshot glimpses of disturbed wild creatures scurrying through the grass  I can call it all back and make it as real as it ever was, and as blessed.

Now, you must make an observation about the writing models we have studied.  Go to the web pages on abstract and concrete language and read or re-read this section.  In the Orwell paragraphs, find one sentence per paragraph which summarizes the whole paragraph.  Then do this with the two Twain examples and the Hugo example.  Write these sentences on a piece of paper.  Consider the differences between a general statement and the specific, concrete images that later occur in each paragraph.  With writing, we should use both types of sentences.  We state in a general way what we are going to write; then we write it, creating wonderful verbal maps which accurately describe the territory   We create concrete images.
 
Topic Sentences and Restricting our Topic
 
When we write, we must limit our scope.  This concept is called, "restricting the topic."  When a writer restricts his or her topic, the writer gains some control over the paper and subject.  Although you will not use the method described next in every paper, for your first longer paper you should restrict your topic to a certain time, an exact place, and a particular dominant impression.  Your goal will be to focus on the moment.  By doing this, you will be able to expand the moment with many sensory details.  Include the time, place, and dominant impression all in one sentence.  This sentence then acts as a summary for the whole paper.  
 
Following are examples summary sentences.  Notice with each example the restrictions of time, place, and dominant impression.
 
1.  While working at McDonald's (place) last Saturday night (time), I found that my boss, Clem Burton, has a quick temper (dominant impression).  You would then tell about events that support your impression that Clem has a quick temper.
 
2.  While camping at Redfish Lake (place) last summer (time), I found what a joker Dad really is. (dominant impression).  You would then describe your father, showing him as a joker.
 
3.  While camping with Father at Henrys Lake last summer, I once again realized how much he enjoys fishing.
 
Audience
 
When we write, we must always consider, "For whom am I writing?  The answer to this question  determines your approach, summary sentence (thesis), and word choices.  To help understand the concept of audience, consider this.  How would you talk to a crew at a potato warehouse as compared to people listening to you deliver a graduation speech at your high school commencement?  Your approach, thesis, and word choice would be different based on these two different audiences.   When you write a description paper, consider the following:
 
Description Paper Assignment
 
You are now ready for an application of the things you have learned. Write a description. Pick one of the following ideas.
 
1. Describe a person you either like or dislike doing something.
 
2. Describe your most frightening or embarrassing experience.
 
3. Describe your favorite place. When you dream of a place where you have been happy in the past, where is it?
 
Next is the checklist which can help evaluate the results of your paper.  Each item on the checklist is worth 10 points.
 
Description Checklist
 
1. The paper is free from any spelling or grammar errors. (-2 points for each error up to five errors).
 
2. Early in the paper is a single, summary sentence restricted to time, place, and dominant impression.  This summary sentence is simple or complex, but not compound.  Underline your summary sentence.
 
3. All the additional sentences in the paragraph directly relate to the summary sentence.
 
4. The paper is typed and double spaced.
 
5. The paper contains at least 600 words.
 
6. The writing is clear.
 
7. Extra words have been stripped from the paper.  (-2 points per extra word up to 5 words).
 
8. The paper is filled with sensory details.  
 
9.  The paper is filled with sensory details.
