“.usa” Web scam shut down

by Allison Jenks
REE00013@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

A Web site that capitalized on post-Sept. 11 patriotism by selling fake Internet domain names was shut down Monday, according to the Federal Trade Commission.

The fake Internet domain names included suffixes “.usa” and “.bet” and “.brit” that are not recognized by the standard-setting Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers (ICANN), meaning that .usa and .brit Web sites and e-mail addresses will not function.

The fake Internet domain names were advertised through junk e-mail or spam. The messages linked to a Web site (http://www.dotusa.com) that urged users to reserve addresses like www.surfin.usa that would not work, FTC officials said.

Registration costs were $59, and the Web site probably took in at least $1 million, FTC officials said.

“These spam scammers conned consumers in two ways. They sent deceptive span, and they sold worthless Web addresses from their Web sites,” J. Howard Beales, head of the FTC’s consumer-protection division, said in a statement. He added that the commission would not tolerate deceptive spam.

The FTC plans to work with the U.K.’s Office of Fair Trading to permanently bar the operation and force it to give back the money it collected, according to ABC News.

The .usa domain is similar to the United States’ .us domain, which is a legitimate “country code” managed by the Washington-based NeuStar Inc., according to CNN.
