SPRING FAMILY HISTORY CONFERENCE SCHEDULED FOR SATURDAY

Beginning and advanced genealogists are invited to participate in the Ricks College Spring Family History Conference Saturday, March 10, from 8 to 5 p.m. "New this conference are four classes that will be hands-on computer classes that deal with the Internet," says Gloria Bradshaw, Continuing Education secretary and registrar for the event. "Some of the classes from last time were popular and are being represented. Others were adjusted after suggestions by patrons and participants."

Some topics include Getting Started, Internet Basic Tools, Personal Ancestral File 4.04, Familysearch, Using and Editing the Ancestral File, My Family Online, LDS Vital Record Indexes, Research Using CD Data Files, Pedigree Resource File, Mormon Emigration Index and Journals, Family Tree Maker, U.S. Research, English Research and Census Searches on the Net.

Class sizes are limited, so pre-registration is recommended, but not required. The pre-registration fee is $14. Registration and fees will be accepted until March 7. After March 7, participants may sign up at the morning of the conference in the Smith Building foyer beginning at 7:30 a.m. The fee will be $18 at the door. Sign-up on the day of will be on a space available basis.

Lunch will not be provided, but food will be available in the Manwaring Center. The semi-annual event draws in more than 200 participants and is co-sponsored by the Ricks College Family History Center and Continuing Education Department. For more information, contact the Continuing Education Department at (208) 356-2336 or 356-1040, or e-mail bradshawg@ricks.edu.

 

WOMEN'S WEEK VISITORS COME TO CAMPUS

An estimated 2,500 visitors are expected to attend the annual Women's Week from Thursday to Sunday, March 15-18. Mothers of Ricks College students are invited to visit the campus and experience a weekend of college life, spend time with their children, and attend some activities especially planned around the theme "For Such a Time as This." Regular classes will continue Thursday and Friday, and some instructors will allow mothers to attend class with their students.

Activities on include announcement of the Woman of the Year; concerts highlighting the performing arts, a humanitarian service project; the garden, flower and bridal open house; fashion show; and workshops. The Osmond Brothers from Branson, Mo., will perform Saturday with concerts at 3 and 7:30 p.m. in the Hart Auditorium.

Sunday will feature a fireside at 5 p.m. by Virginia H. Pearce as she gives "glimpses into the life and heart of her mother, Marjorie P. Hinckley." Tickets for the Hart Auditorium are already distributed, but overflow will be available in the Taylor and Kirkham buildings.

Tickets for activities are limited, and some are already sold out. For ticket information, please contact the Ticket Office at 356-2230 or online at www.byui.edu/tickets. For details on Women's Week activities, see http://www.byui.edu/WomensWeek/

 

CDT CONCERT OPENS THIS WEEK

The Ricks College Contemporary Dance Theatre will present a concert March 8-10 at 7:30 p.m. in the Kirkham Auditorium. Admission is $6 for the general public and $1 for Ricks students.

Ricks faculty member Wendy Bone says the annual showcase will include a variety of modern dances. Along with old favorites such as "Would You Like Butter with That?," a jazzed up version of "Popcorn Popping on the Apricot Tree" and "This Day," the company will perform some new hip-hop and modern dances.

CDT also will perform "Because of Thy Exceeding Faith," a portrayal and dedication to the women of the Book of Mormon.

 

SYMPHONY ORCHESTRA TO PERFORM IN CONJUNCTION WITH WOMEN'S WEEK

The Ricks College Symphony Orchestra will perform Wednesday and Thursday, March 14 and 15, at 7:30 p.m. in conjunction with the annual Women's Week activities. Admission for the event are $1 for Ricks students and $2 for the general public. The concert will feature the 90-piece Symphony Orchestra conducted by Ricks faculty member Kevin Call. "These concerts during Women's Week are the most traditional and formal of concerts that the Symphony Orchestra does in the year," Call said.

Call continued, "In this program we are featuring the music of the Russian composer Dmitri Shostakovich (1906-1975). Shostakovich lived almost his entire life under the Soviet system and his music was always subject to review by the Communist Party as to appropriateness and on several occasions the composer was rebuffed and reprimanded for what the party considered objectionable qualities. Yet, his music is a testimony to the power of music and its ability to communicate, in spite of opposition. Shostakovich has been recognized throughout the world as one of the truly great composers of our time. The program will also feature the six 2001 Student Concerto Competition winners.

 

ORPHEUS WINDS WILL PERFORM IN FREE AFTERNOON CONCERT

Orpheus Winds will perform a woodwind recital Friday, March 16, as part of Women's Week events at Ricks College. The five-person woodwind ensemble will perform the free concert in the Barrus Concert Hall of the Snow Center for the Performing Arts at 4 p.m. Members of the ensemble are April Clayton on flute, Gerallyn Giovannetti on oboe, David M. Randall on clarinet, Lawrence Lowe on horn and Christian Smith on bassoon. Ricks faculty member Stephen Allen will accompany on piano. The program will include "Quintet in E-Flat Major" by Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart, "Three Shanties for Wind Quintet" by Malcom Arnold and "Sextet" by Francis Poulenc.
