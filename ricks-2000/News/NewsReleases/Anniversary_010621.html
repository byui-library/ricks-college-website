Ricks College News Release
June 21, 2001

June 21 marks anniversary 

of BYU-Idaho announcement

    
REXBURG, Idaho –"They’re busy in Rexburg, Idaho: printing new letterheads and personal cards. Changing signage. Striking a new medallion and seal. Hiring help. Building new buildings. Saying goodbye to an era."

So began a story in Saturday’s edition of the Deseret News in Salt Lake City that reviewed the dramatic changes at Ricks College during the past year.

Today (June 21) marks the one-year anniversary of the announcement that Ricks College would become a bachelor’s degree-granting university known as BYU-Idaho. [See Timeline.]

During the past year, college employees worked long and hard to make it possible to start offering 17 bachelor’s degree programs in the fall. A total of 51 baccalaureate programs are expected by 2004, along with 19 associate degrees.

Other milestones during the past year have been the hiring of 38 new faculty members (25 due to the changes), groundbreaking for the new Spori and multi-use buildings, announcement of the Thomas E. Ricks Building and Gardens, and candidacy status for accreditation of the proposed bachelor’s degree programs.

Aug. 10 has been selected to be the official day the college becomes known as BYU-Idaho. A multi-day celebration is being planned for Sept. 18-22 during homecoming week that will include fireworks, a concert with Gladys Knight and the last homecoming football game.

Beginning in the fall, BYU-Idaho will offer upper-division classes leading to integrated bachelor’s degrees in accounting, English, history and recreation leadership, along with specialized degrees in business management, elementary education, interior design and nursing. It also will begin offering upper-division, secondary education programs in the areas of biology, earth science, English, family and consumer education, health, history, mathematics and social studies.

In addition to bachelor’s degrees, BYU-Idaho will offer specialized associate degrees that prepare students to be employable after completing two years of schooling. The specialized associate degrees will include agricultural management, architectural drafting technology, automotive technology, beef production, CAD technology, computer systems technology, criminal justice, culinary arts, electronics engineering technology, floral design, general studies, landscape horticulture, nursing, paramedicine, photography, piano pedagogy, pre-school education and welding.

The university expected to experience a modest increase in the size of the student body during the next few years. It will maintain our present level of approximately 8,600 freshman and sophomore students. The projected number of students on campus at any given time is roughly estimated at 9,200 for 2001-02; 9,800 for 2002-03; 9,900 for 2003-04; 10,600 for 2004-05; and 11,600 for 2005-06.

To accommodate the greatest number of students, BYU-Idaho will utilize our facilities year round and offer a complete array of courses during the summer as well as the traditional fall and winter semesters. Students will be admitted on one of four academic tracks. Besides the traditional fall-winter track, the school will offer a winter-summer track, a summer-fall track and a year-round Fast Track program. These four tracks will allow the school to accommodate more students throughout the span of a year.

This will result in more students being served during a full calendar year. We estimate that 9,600 students will be served annually during the 2001-02 year, 10,300 during the 2002-03 year, 10,700 during the 2003-04 year, 12,500 during the 2004-05 year and 14,500 during the 2005-06 year.

Intercollegiate athletics will be phased out at the end of the 2001-02 academic year. The unprecedented Student Activities Program will meet the needs of a broad and diverse student body. When fully operational, the program will have an array of year-round activities in four areas: Social and Recreational, Performing and Visual Arts, Sports and Fitness, and Service and Leadership. Each area is structured to give students numerous opportunities for active involvement at various levels of interest—including leisure, instructional, recreational, and competitive.

Although BYU–Idaho will discontinue intercollegiate athletics by the end of the school year, highly competitive athletics within the institution will involve a higher percentage of students than ever before on this or any other campus. This new program goes beyond "intramurals" through which a group of friends play together on teams. The competitive athletics program will require practicing, training, coaching, and skill development—a level formerly characteristic of only varsity athletics.
