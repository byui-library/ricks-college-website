Course overview, objectives

Communication 110 is required for students seeking an associate's degree with a major in broadcasting, journalism or interpersonal communication. Mass communication survey courses are common fare at universities. You should have no trouble with your credits for 110, regardless of where you transfer.



It is difficult to overestimate the impact mass media have on our modern society -- and on us individually. You probably spend more time with the media than with any other single activity in life except sleeping. Just try to imagine your life without sleep -- or the media!



Many of you who are enrolled in this course seek careers in the mass media and will center much of your college training in your respective disciplines. That is "micro" mass communication education.



This course, on the other hand, will teach you about mass communication from a "macro" perspective. You will learn which developments, events and people from the past influenced and shaped our media of today. You will get to know who the key players and organizations are in today's "mediagopolis." Also, you will become acquainted with how the various media:

• came to be, what their role is in society today, and where they're headed in the future

• affect and are affected by individuals and society

• interact, intersect and integrate with each other

• go about their business of informing, entertaining and provoking thought among the masses.

One overall objective is to build for you a broad background and foundation from which to provide context for and perspective to your more specialized studies. The second objective is to help you become a more informed consumer of the mass media, thus helping you and your family better capture its tremendous power and influence for good.

Class participation: The "A" standard in this category is to attend every class, to come prepared and on time, and be an alert, active participant in discussions and group activities. I invite students to speak up, share experiences, probe, challenge and tie concepts together.



I will take attendance with a short quiz at the beginning of class. Each quiz is worth four attendance points. You can earn a total of 100 attendance points for the term. Please drop me a note, call or e-mail me in advance if you can't come to class. That way I won't worry about you.

I will also deduct points from those students who come in noncompliance with Ricks College dress and grooming standards. (Yes, that includes ball caps in the classroom.)



Exams: Four exams containing short-answer essay and multiple choice questions will be given after about every four chapters of the text. All of the tests will be in the testing center. Make-up exams will not be given after the rest of the class has taken them.



Online quizzes: The Vivian text has a supporting web site at http://www.abacon.com/vivian . Each chapter has 20 multiple choice questions posted there. Complete the quiz and submit it via email for a grade. You may retake the quiz as many times as it takes to get all of the answers right before you submit it for a grade.



Group Project: This will be your accounting of the work you've done for this class over the entire semester. Early in the term I suggest you choose your group members. You may have a minimum of three and a maximum of four members in each group. You will need to produce a finished publication, (newsletter, magazine, etc), or video presentation on a single mass media topic, issue, or area of interest to your group.



Personal projects: Up to 100 points are possible for two projects to be completed at specific deadlines during the semester. Choose your projects from the assignments menu sheet (or propose your own) and complete them anytime before the deadline dates. Please include the project number from the project menu.

Extra credit: You can earn up to 30 points of extra credit. Extra credit projects are listed on the projects hand-out.



Course policies



Academic integrity: All work in this course should be your own and completed honestly -- on exams, individual projects and portfolios. It is your responsibility to know the standards for citing and crediting material to others. Students violating this standard will receive a "0" on that test or assignment. Those violating a second time will also be referred to the Dean of Students office.



Accountability: I will expect you to have read this course outline and asked questions about that which you do not understand.

You are responsible for material covered in class discussions when it differs from or expands on that found in the text. Your responsibility also extends to materials found in the text, whether or not it is covered in class discussion.



Appeals: The instructor reserves the right to play "Solomon" and make judgment call decisions as he sees fit on appeals for excused absences, make-up exams, deadline extensions, etc.



Changes in schedule, assignments: We intend to closely follow the schedule and assignments as listed in this outline but also reserve the right to make necessary changes for the betterment of the course or to respond to student interests. Any changes will be announced in class (usually in the first few minutes) and/or by electronic mail. Once changes are announced, the responsibility falls upon students to conform to them.



Code of Honor: Please obey all Ricks standards of dress, grooming and conduct while you are in my class. I will consider any willful flaunting of these rules as a serious breech of class etiquette and will reflect such in your attendance/class participation grade, as well as refer those who refuse to work with me on these inspired standards to the Dean of Students office.



Late work: I will sometimes give you a few minutes' grace time (but don't count on it!). Projects submitted up to a week late will lose 10 percent credit; more than a week late will cost you 20 percent. Exams, extra credit work and portfolios cannot be completed after deadlines.



Final Note: Now that you are overwhelmed, I guarantee an enjoyable class that will give you new insights that will be useful no matter which career path you choose. The guarantee is only valid if you take part and plunge into the course work and projects.
