ELDER PINEGAR EXPLAINS HAPPINESS-THE LORD'S PLAN

On a sunny September Tuesday afternoon Ricks College students in their Sunday best assembled in the Hart auditorium to hear the counsel of Elder Rex D. Pinegar of the Quorum of the Seventy. They were taught to pursue happiness in their journey through life.

Elder Pinegar encouraged the students to understand and pursue the Lord's plan of happiness, "the plan that He has prepared for all of us." The pathway to happiness is through Jesus Christ and His atoning sacrifice. It is a pathway of obedience to the truths. Elder Pinegar sited qualities for happiness outlined by the Prophet Joseph Smith as virtue, uprighteousness, faithfulness, holiness, and keeping the commandments of God. "We can obtain the qualities of happiness... through our personal desires," said Elder Pinegar.

Expounding on the pursuit of each quality and noting there are obstacles along the way, Elder Pinegar reassuringly said, "When you decide to do something the Lord would have you do, He finds a way to help you keep your word --commitment."

"On our pathway through life, we will not always find it easy to be happy. There are many obstacles to overcome and many hills to climb. We will find strength for the journey if we keep the commandments that will keep us from straying off our course," said Elder Pinegar. "Happiness in life comes, despite the hardships, the disappointments, our own weaknesses, and in association with our successes, when we truly understand our Heavenly Father's Plan of Happiness. . . . We who know the plan and purpose of life and have the assurance that we have a Savior, Jesus Christ, who has already completed the journey. He promised that as we follow His path in the pursuit of virtue, uprightness, faithfulness, holiness, and as we strive to keep all the commandments of God, He will continue to guide us along the path and we will be happy."

FORUM SPEAKER WILL SEND OUT A MESSAGE ON THE FAMILY

Richard G. Wilkins, the director of World Family Policy Center (formerly NGO Family Voice) will speak at the Ricks College Forum at 2 p.m. Thursday, Sept. 21, in the Taylor Chapel. The WFPC is an inter-disciplinary family policy association headquartered at BYU. Using the First Presidency's recently given "The Family: A Proclamation to the World" as a reference, Wilkins will address the challenge of "taking the proclamation to the world." The WFPC is devoted to providing world-wide democratic input on pro-family and other value-based issues within the United Nations System. It has been instrumental in gaining support of protecting the traditional family as the basis of society.

Wilkins is a professor of law at the J. Reuben Clark Law School at Brigham Young University. Wilkins graduated from BYU with a B.A. degree in journalism in 1976 and a Juris Doctor degree in 1979, summa cum laude. He has written numerous articles on constitutional issues and conducts an annual survey of Supreme Court voting trends published by Hastings Constitutional Law Quarterly. He is a former Assistant to the Solicitor General, United States Department of Justice, where he briefed and argued eight cases before the United States Supreme Court. He was a delegate to the World Congress of Families I and II held in Geneva, Switzerland in November 1999.

 

RICKS COLLEGE SPORTS STARTING UP

The Ricks College varsity athletic programs have begun play for the 2000-2001 seasons. The football team, who went 10-1 last year and beat Garden City Community College in the Real Dairy Bowl last December, started the season by losing to Glendale Community College in Arizona on Saturday, 26-14. The Vikings were ranked fourth in the National Junior College Athletic Association going into the game. Ricks will host its first home game of the year on Saturday against Mesa Community College, with the kick off set for 1 p.m.

The Lady Vikings volleyball team will also open play this week at home as they go up against Utah Valley State College on Thursday, Westminster College on Friday and Salt Lake Community College on Saturday. All matches will be played in the Hart Gymnasium and will start at 7 p.m. Currently, Ricks has a record of 17-2, with the two losses coming at the hands of the College of Southern Idaho.

The 5-time defending national champions women's and defending national champion men's cross county teams will run in their first meet on Sept. 16 at the Montana State Invitational in Bozeman, Mont.
