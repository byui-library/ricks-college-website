General Instructions for Assignments 01 through 07

  • Work in assigned groups.
  • Discuss as a group the pertinent and relevant ethical issues raised in class and in the homework assignment.
  • Develop alternate solutions and evaluate them.
  • Prepare a paper on the assigned topic. It shall be at least one page long and shall not exceed two pages.
  • The paper shall be single-spaced, have one-inch margins, and use a 12-point proportional spaced font such as Times New Roman.
  • List the name of each group member.
  • For each group member, indicate the percent contribution to the total effort.
  • Record the amount of time spent as a group working on the homework. Your group should meet at least fifty minutes.
  • Make sure your submitted paper satisfies all specified requirements. It is best to clearly identify each question you are answering. Points will be deducted if your assignment is not complete.
  • Each student shall submit notes from the group meeting. These notes shall be attached to the group paper. Points will be deducted if your notes are incomplete or if they fail to document consideration of alternatives.
  • It is best to submit the completed homework to me in person. I do not assume responsibility for homework I do not receive.
  • Homework shall not be submitted using e-mail.
  • If you miss your group meeting, you may submit a three-page research paper (formatting similar to the group paper). Your research paper shall address the issues raised in the homework assignment and shall have at least three documented sources. The research paper is due at the same time as the group paper. Standard late penalties apply to the research paper.

01 Be honest and trustworthy
10 points
     Group assignments (on-campus only).
     You have graduated with a Bachelor of Science in Computer Science (BSCS) and are employed by a startup company named VaporWare. VaporWare is paying you a better-than-competitive salary and is providing very generous benefits. This is a very good thing because you have a younger brother with serious medical problems. He has already had a couple of major surgeries and there will be many more major surgeries. You are very blessed in that VaporWare has made a special provision to cover most of his medical costs even though its health plan does not require it to. Your parents could not pay the medical costs otherwise. They have called you several time just to express gratitude for the medical coverage. They have told you repeatedly how much they depend on you to provide for your little brother. There have been a couple of episodes where your little brother would have died if not for the medical coverage you provide. Unfortunately the health plan will not cover an extremely expensive experimental procedure that would be very beneficial to your brother. Neither you nor your parents cannot pay for it yourself, nor can you and your parents borrow money to cover the costs.
     A company named RipOff has contracted VaporWare to produce a complicated software system. RipOff will use the software at one of its facilities. If the software system works as specified, RipOff will install the system in all of its facilities worldwide. If the software system works correctly, VaporWare will become exceptionally profitable, all of its employees fabulously rich, and you will be able at long last to pay for the experimental procedure for your brother. On the other hand, if the system fails, VaporWare will be forced to close its doors and you will be unemployed. While you will be able to find another job, health care for your brother will be terminated. You know you will not be able to pay current and future medical bills. It means the difference between life and death for your brother.
     It is critical to VaporWare that the software be delivered to RipOff on schedule. RipOff will cancel the contract if there are any more delays (there have already been several extensions of the delivery date). As the delivery date approaches, VaporWare is concentrating on testing the system to make sure it works correctly. Most of the system tests are going well, with the exception of the installation module. It is supposed to automatically detect hardware, the operating system, and other major applications on the host computer. It is then supposed to automatically configure the system to work in that environment. Simply put, automatic installation doesn't work. The system can only be installed manually.
     The delivery date arrives and the installation module still doesn't work. RipOff executives visit the VaporWare facility to witness a demonstration of the system. Because the system is already installed, the installation module is not demonstrated. Your manager is careful to avoid any discussion of automatic installation capabilities. The RipOff executives fail to notice this omission. Your manager informs RipOff that because it is such an important client, a VaporWare employee (you) will personally install the system at no extra charge. You know that your manager is doing this because there is no other way the system will install.
     You tell your manager that you are uncomfortable with this arrangement since it involves deceiving RipOff. Your manager tells you that if RipOff learns that the installation module doesn't work, it will cancel the contract. Your manager is confident that the automatic installation will be functional before the six-month trial period is complete. The installation module can then be shipped with the rest of the software system. He argues that since RipOff will receive everything it paid for, there is no dishonesty. (He doesn't consider his omissions to RipOff executives to be dishonest either.)
     You are now at the RipOff facility and have successfully installed the software on several host computers. Each and every computer has a different configuration and the installation process is very tedious. Even so, no one is suspicious that the installation module doesn't work. RipOff is very pleased with your work.
     One day a RipOff technician informs you that there are many host computers at the RipOff facility that you are not allowed to access for security reasons. You are to train the technician how to use the automatic installation procedure. The technician will then perform the installation on those computers you cannot access.
  1. Develop three unique and independent responses to the RipOff technician. Use the following as a fourth response: "Call my supervisor."
  2. There shall be no duplication, partial or otherwise, between any two responses.
  3. Using an honesty scale from 0 to 9, rate each of the four responses. A 0 means a total lie and a 9 means the total truth. Exactly one response shall rate as a 0 and exactly one response shall rate as a 9. The remaining responses shall rate somewhere from 1 to 8.
  4. List the short-term consequences to you, your company, and your family for each response.
  5. List the long-term consequences to you, your company, and your family for each response.
  6. As a group, select the response you feel would be best. Explain why it would be the best response.
02 Contribute to society and human well-being
10 points
     Group assignments (on-campus only).
     You have graduated with a Bachelor of Science in Computer Science (BSCS) and are looking for a job. (By the way, you are also looking for a spouse.) You have two offers (for a job, not a spouse); you must choose one.
  1. One job offer comes from a company that provides software engineering services to humanitarian organizations. Sample projects include:
    • Provide a connection between diagnostic tools to an intelligent system to help diagnose illnesses.
    • Create a monitoring system to predict famine areas thus facilitating providing aid to these areas before the famine becomes severe.
    • Engineer a computer-based training system that can be used in developing nations to reduce illiteracy and thus poverty.
    These projects could potentially benefit millions and possibly save hundreds of thousands of lives.
         However, due to the nature of the clients/customers, your salary is low, about one-quarter that of the other offer. For this low salary you will be expected to work long hours. Because of limited budgets, there most likely will be times when you will have to work for free. Their office space is decorated in early Deseret Industries (that may be an overstatement - you're not sure Deseret Industries would accept their office furniture). The office area is overcrowded; consequently they are not sure where they are going to put you, but are confident that they can squeeze you in somewhere.

  2. The other offer is from a high-tech company that works with state-of-the-art technologies. Frequently, they push beyond state-of-the-art to successfully complete their projects.
         For your job interview, you were flown with first-class seating, housed in an expensive hotel, chauffeured about in a limousine, provided with lavish meals, and given hard-to-get tickets to a concert featuring your favorite group.
         As part of the job interview, you are shown your future spacious office. You will receive a new, top of the line computer annually. You will also be given a company car every year; while you may choose the make, model, and options you are not allowed to exceed $75,000 annually. (Life is tough, isn't it?) In addition to the generous salary, there is a substantial signing bonus.
         If hired, you will contribute to an effort to create software to detect a computer's hardware and software, particularly anti-virus and other security hardware and software. The goal is to avoid detection by the installed security hardware and software. The person you are interviewing with tells you the software (that you help develop) will be used to make installation of new products easier (and without the need to disable security hardware and software). Thus secure systems will remain secure, even during the installation process.
         The person telling you about your first project with the company has a difficult time looking directly at you. Consequently, you begin to mistrust him and suspect that the software has additional uses. You imagine that the software could be used to hack into secure systems. In addition, you see customers but your interviewer is very evasive about who they are. He mutters something about it being against company policy to disclose the names of customers. You begin to wonder if they are foreign intelligence officers (spies) or Mafia.
     Use each of the following as the sole decision factor in determining which offer to accept. For each decision factor, explain why you would accept one offer and reject the other.
  1. salary
  2. work conditions (including office space and work hours)
  3. contribution to society and human well-being
Now consider all factors:
  • Which offer do you accept?
  • Why?
  • Explain the impact on this decision if you had the same family situation as described in homework 01.
03 Avoid harm to others
10 points
     Group assignments (on-campus only).
     You are working for a contractor. You were hired to work on a specific contract. At the time you were hired it was made clear to you that you might be laid off (terminated) at the end of the contract. At the time you didn't pay much attention to this condition to your employment since it was a five-year contract. You anticipated being ready for a change by then anyway.
     In nine months you will vest in the investment and retirement plans. Vesting means that you could enjoy a few extra million at retirement. This would allow you to retire early and serve a few more missions than you could otherwise. You would also like to establish a large Computer Science Scholarship Fund at Brigham Young University-Idaho. (See Jacob 2:18-19.) Failure to vest means you (and everyone else) lose out.
     The contract at the facility at which you work expires in six months and another contractor is taking over. The new contractor is bringing in its own staff. You will be out of a job.
     You are informed that you will be laid off three months short of your vestment date. You are discouraged because of the financial loss. While you don't particularly want a long term relationship with your current employer, you have a strong desire to stay with them until you vest.
     Two weeks later you are assigned a task. You are told that if you successfully complete the task, the company will take you with them when the contract terminates. You are elated! You think how good you will feel when you receive thank-you notes from BYU-Idaho scholarship recipients. You think of the blessings you will receive from sacrificing your time on a mission in Tahiti.
     Your work assignment is to create a program that will read source code files and
  1. strip all comments
  2. change all variable names to random eight-character identifiers
The source code must still compile and work as correctly as it currently does. These changes to the source code will make software maintenance virtually impossible. You are assured that this action is perfectly legal under the current contract.
     Analyze how you will respond:
  • What are the short-term consequences to you, your employer, and the new contractor if you say "yes?"
  • If you say "no?"
  • What are the long-term consequences to you, your employer, and the new contractor if you say "yes?"
  • If you say "no?"
  • What is your final decision?
  • Justify your final decision.
04 Be fair and take action not to discriminate
10 points
     Group assignments (on-campus only).
     As project manager, you have the task to hire another person to work on your project. Your company has two policies that pertain to your hiring decision:
  1. Because of costs related to recruiting new employees, training them, and integrating them into the company work force, your company typically doesn't see a return on investment (ROI) on new employees for about six years. This means that new employees need to be retained for at least six years before the company sees financial benefits from hiring them. You know of another project manager who was informally punished for hiring someone who made it clear in her job interview that she was only going to stay for four years.
  2. Your company has an affirmative action plan; hiring a female or a minority will make you look good to upper management. In fact, the company vice-president you report to has already called you in and explained the importance of supporting the company's affirmative action goals. He emphasizes that the company doesn't want another lawsuit regarding hiring discrimination. Future large contracts could be won or lost based on the company's success in diversifying its workforce.
     The following three applicants are qualified for the job; however, Julio and Jasmine have more and better technical credentials than John.
  1. John is a white male and lives in your ward. You know he is married and has six children. Because of your ward leadership position, you know John and his family are struggling financially. Your family has been preparing a meal for them about once a week for the last several months. Because of the financial difficulties, several health problems are not receiving necessary attention. This job would solve their financial problems.
  2. Julio is an older minority male with a disability covered by the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA). Making reasonable accommodation, while possible, will require modifications you have not budgeted for. From the questions he asked during the interview, you suspect that he plans an early retirement in five years, although he could stay many years beyond that. Early retirement is probably a good thing, given how much he drinks. He had four glasses of wine at lunch (although that didn't seem to impair him). A coworker wondered how much wine he would have consumed if it hadn't been a short lunch. Julio assures you that he will stop smoking if that is required by the company.
  3. Jasmine is a young female who wore a low cut dress with an uncomfortably high hemline to the interview. Her practiced walk attracted the attention of every male in the area. You observed her interacting with coworkers; you could best describe the interaction as flirting. You overheard her telling a lewd story to a group of employees in the break room. She provided another one at lunch. She tried to provide a third one during your interview, but you cut her off. She seemed to respect your feelings and did not force the issue. It seemed that she couldn't talk to anyone, male or female, without some sort of physical contact. During your interview with her, you found it necessary to move your chair several times, but with little lasting effect.
     Explain your hiring decision as if you were explaining it to the company vice-president you report to.
  • Who would you hire?
  • Why?
  • Explain why you wouldn't hire each of the other two.
  • Explain why the company won't face another hiring discrimination lawsuit for each of the two candidates who won't be hired.
05 Honor property rights including copyrights and patent
10 points
     Group assignments (on-campus only).
     The project you are managing is behind schedule (aren't they always?). Your management has agreed to provide some extra people to help you catch up. In order to take advantage of these extra people and to make people already on the project more productive, you need twelve additional copies of an expensive software package named HackIt. A package includes the software, reference material (150 pages of documentation), and the license to use HackIt to develop software. Each item in the package is clearly marked as protected by copyright and that no copies are to be made. Unfortunately, the funding for the additional copies of HackIt won't be available for another three months, near the scheduled completion date of your project. But you desperately need them now in order to finish on time.
     HackIt was the company standard for software development when you started your project. A large number of company employees were trained to use HackIt. Consequently you chose to use HackIt and a significant amount of work using HackIt has been completed, so much so that is impossible to switch to another software development package.
     When you started your project, there was a practice in your company to install HackIt on servers to make it available to a large number of employees. Unfortunately, your company had not purchased the necessary network licenses. Hence the installations were illegal. HackIt learned of this practice and a lawsuit followed. Your company lost. All illegal copies were uninstalled. In fact, any copy of the software that could not be proven to be legal was uninstalled. Legal copies with illegal upgrades were also uninstalled. As a result, there are few copies of HackIt in the company. Your project has two of them.
     And you desperately need more copies and you need them now. If you don't get them soon, your project will fail. The lawsuit with HackIt has severely hurt your company financially. If your project fails, it could take the company with it. Several employees on your project suggest that they make (illegal) copies of HackIt (including the documentation). They rationalize this by saying that the project will make those copies legal in a few months when it has the funding to purchase the additional copies of Hackit. They figure if they do this on their own and personally make the copies using equipment not owned by your company, then HackIt cannot accuse the company of pirating software.
     How do you respond to them? Explain your rationale.
  1. Are they covered by fair use?
  2. Do you forbid them to make any copies and watch your project fail?
  3. Do you forbid them to make any copies with a wink and a nod that implies your approval of their action, provides you with culpable deniability, and saves your project?
  4. Do you encourage them to proceed by telling them their course of action is perfectly legal and thus save your project?
  5. Do you tell them that you have no right to dictate their personal actions and hope they will save your project?
  6. Explain the impact on this decision if you had the same family situation as described in homework 01.
06 Give proper credit for intellectual property
10 points
     Group assignments (on-campus only).
     You are at a conference and overhear a conversation, not part of a public presentation, describing in detail an algorithm that by coincidence is relevant to a project you are working on. They have named the algorithm Ajalon. The discussion includes an evaluation of the weaknesses of Ajalon and how to correct those weaknesses. You listen, with great interest, but don't take notes. You don't know who participated in the discussion; in fact, you can't even see them. Consequently, you have no way of identifying them. You speculate that they may be from a university or from a competing firm.
     The discussion concludes with a detailed review of the corrected algorithm. It is a brilliant approach and very elegant. You only wish you could come up with something as good.
     After you return home, the project manager assigns you to work on an area that is a perfect fit for Ajalon. The project is behind schedule and is over budget. In fact, it's in deep trouble. Consequently there is substantial pressure on you to develop a brilliant solution in a very short time.
     You try to develop an algorithm, but you can think of nothing but Ajalon. You finally develop another algorithm, but it seems vastly inferior to Ajalon. It seems vastly inferior only because it is.
     The time has come to present your algorithm.
  • Discuss the pros and cons of each of the following.
  • Consider short-term and long-term consequences.
    1. Present your inferior algorithm. You suspect that your management will view you as being as inferior as the algorithm. (You really hate being laughed at in public.) You face the real possibility of becoming the scapegoat for the failure of the project.
    2. Use Ajalon but do not mention its source to anyone. You suspect that your management will view you as being as brilliant as the algorithm. This will undoubtedly lead to a big raise and a promotion. Since the company will not disclose design details, you hope no one will discover the truth.
    3. Incorporate substantial portions of Ajalon into your inferior algorithm. You do not mention Ajalon to anyone. The hybrid will neither be as good as Ajalon nor as bad as your algorithm. You won't earn any praise, but you'll probably keep your job.
  • State what you will do and discuss the reason for your decision.
07 Respect the privacy of others; Honor confidentiality
10 points
     Group assignments (on-campus only).
     You are a student employee with the Information Technology organization at BYU-Idaho. Because of your job, you have full access to the system containing all student records. You have been assigned to improve the Web-based capability to generate ward lists. You are to ensure that only authorized individuals, such as bishops, can access this capability. You are to provide options to create a variety of reports, including a list of ward members, their addresses, and telephone numbers. You enjoy your job and are excited by your current assignment. You are somewhat humbled by the trust placed in you by your supervisors. They have emphasized to you the importance of keeping private the data you have access to. You have given your personal promise that you would honor this trust.
     You are telling a friend, Gideon, about your job and current assignment. Gideon becomes very animated and declares that this is an answer to his prayers. He has been called to a position in his ward, but not yet sustained. Even still, his bishop asked him to contact a portion of the ward and remind them about a special meeting on Sunday. Gideon was supposed to stop by the bishop's home on Wednesday and pick up a list of names and telephone numbers. You have a good idea of what list Gideon is referring to because a bishop had asked you to generate something like it (it was a list of ward members currently enrolled in the apartment leadership course with their addresses and telphone numbers). You created the list and delivered it to the requesting bishop on Tuesday. Gideon forgot his appointment to get the list and then bishop left town because of a family emergency. Gideon knows the bishop was counting on him to get the word out but he was the only one who had the list. It's Saturday morning and Gideon doesn't have much time to complete his task. He has been unable to contact anyone in the ward with the authority to generate the list, not that anyone else in the ward has the authority to request such a list. He has been fervently praying for some way to complete his assignment. Every time he prays he receives a peaceful feeling, an assurance that everything will work out. And here you are, the answer to his prayers.
     Here are some of the things that you are thinking:
  • You know that Gideon does not have the authority to request the list from you.
  • You are guessing that it was his bishop that requested the list, but you can't be sure because you don't remember who it was. You fulfill so many requests like this.
  • You don't know the purpose for the requested list, if it was for the bishop's personal information or if it was to be given to someone.
  • Since Gideon hasn't been sustained, you can't verify from his fellow ward members that he has a calling that requires him to have such a list. Ward leaders who might know are unavailable.
  • You know that there is no protected data on the list and that Gideon could assemble the same information by checking the class schedule of each student in the ward (you know he has a ward list of all students in his ward). This would take time, but it is doable.
  • Gideon is a recent convert and you don't want to damage his testimony of God answering prayers.
     You have two alternatives:
  1. either you generate the list and give it to Gideon or
  2. you don't.
Discuss
  • ethical issues for each alternative,
  • short-term consequences,
  • long-term consequencesof each alternative, and
  • what you will do and why.
