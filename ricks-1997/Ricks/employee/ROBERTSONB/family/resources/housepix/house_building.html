Watch Our Home Progress!

We are building a new home. My wife and I are the general contractors and are also the sub-contractors and laborers. We receive help from our children and, at critical times, from friends and neighbors. We are indebted to them and grateful for their help and assistance.

 

 We are building in a small rural community called Burton.
What we started with - two acres of an alfalfa field.

 
A neighbor felt sorry for us eating outside all of the time and brought over his camper to use. These are the kind of people that live in this area - great neighbors! Thanks, Bruce and Jean.

 

Step one - plant some trees for future enjoyment and shelter.

At this time there are four blue spruce and three weeping willows. Later, we will plant robusta poplars and fruit trees. We would like to have around a 100 trees in all.
We had to install our septic system. Due to the number of bedrooms in the home we have a 1500 gallon septic tank and a 900 square foot drain field. Due to the ground water conditions in our area all has to be at ground level. There will be another 6 inches of gravel and then covered with dirt and planted in grass. Hopefully, you won't even know its there when we're done.

 
 Sorry for the shadow, but this is the foundation in place looking at it from the east.
This picture shows the foundation where the front porch will be (on the left in the foreground) and the living space will be (on the foreground to the right). The front porch will be a cement pad (4 inches thick) extending from the living space wall out. In total it will be 8 feet wide and 42 feet long. This should be a nice place to sit in the evenings and enjoy the evening air.
  This shows the garage area. The pole sticking up in the air to the left is the power supply pole. The electrical service will run underground and up this mast (its hollow) into the meter base. From there it will run into a 200 amp service main. The far area of this foundation space will be a mud room, laundry area and sewing room for Lori. There will be another cement wall constructed to separate the garage area from the crawlspace under the living area.
This pad is where we will construct a shop for woodworking projects and it will also house the pump system. In fact, all of the dirt to the right is where the water line has been excavated to connect the well to the shop and then back to the house.

 This is the back of the house (viewed from the south). Keith Walker's tractor is sitting by the house. It has been a lifesaver in terms of lifting people and materials into the higher elevations of the house.

In all, the house is 66 feet in length, and the main living area is 32 feet wide and 42 feet long. The garage area (the part to the right) is 24 feet long and 40 feet wide. The garage itself is 24 by 24. This makes the living area about 3000 square feet. Now we have the get the water housing (for the pressure tank and control system) built and then we can start the inside, and somewhere in there get the house sided and the roof finish trim on.
