BRIGHAM YOUNG UNIVERSITY - IDAHO STANLEY ELDERHOSTEL

 

The beauty and grandeur of Stanley Basin is apparent by being the only place in America where three scenic byways converge.  Stanley presents a picturesque backdrop of the majestic Sawtooth Mountain Range, Salmon River & an endless variety of wild flowers.  It is within the largest National Recreational Area in America (756,00 acres).  Home to historical gold mines, thermal hot springs, and abundant birds & wildlife, field trips provide an opportunity to explore Idaho's Icon for the Great Outdoors.  Lodging, meals, & classrooms are in a motel located in the heart of the Sawtooth Valley with double occupancy, private bath; limited singles, extra charge.  Use of a private enclosed natural hot spring.  Elevation: 6500'.

"GOLD FEVER!" - A HISTORICAL VIEW OF GOLD MINING IN THE SAWTOOTH MOUNTAINS
The beautiful Sawtooth Valley is home to many historical gold mines.  Retrace the history of mining for gold and enjoy field trips to explore Idaho's Great Outdoors.  Visit Custer Museum and ghost town.  From 1940-1952, the Yankee Fork Gold Dredge, weighing in at 988 tons, began a new era of gold mining on the Yankee Fork and today is the only remaining floating dredge in Idaho.

FROM BLUEBIRDS TO BLUEBELLS:  THE BIRDS AND WILD FLOWERS OF CENTRAL IDAHO
Study the unique habitat of over 200 birds and flowers in the nation's largest National Recreational Area.  Learn to identify them while exploring the beautiful valley surrounding the Salmon River & Sawtooth Mountains. 
 
 
 
GOLD STRIKE:  A HISTORICAL VIEW OF GOLD MINING IN THE SAWTOOTH MOUNTAINS
The beautiful Sawtooth Valley is home to many historical gold mines.  Trace the history of mining for gold and enjoy a field trip to a ghost town and to the only remaining dredge in Idaho (988 tons). 

GOING SOUTH FOR THE WINTER:  MIGRATING BIRDS
August through October is the Sawtooth's major migration time for songbirds, loons, and ducks (such as the hooded merganser).  Learn to identify them while visiting the country's largest national recreation area (756,000 acres). 

THE SALMON:  NATURE'S ENDURANCE CHAMP
Study the 900-mile trip of the migrating salmon.  Examine how minnow-size fish go downstream and return to the exact place two-three years later.  Learn how dams, fishing, recreation, etc. affect sensitive habitat.  Visit a fish hatchery.
