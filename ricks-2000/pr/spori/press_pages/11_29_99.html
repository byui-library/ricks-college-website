Ricks College News Release
November 29, 1999

REXBURG, Idaho — Ricks College officials believe they have found an architectural firm with the vision and capability to design a new building that will reflect the heritage and legacy of the aging Jacob Spori Building.

After a rigorous selection process, FFKR of Salt Lake City was chosen to design a new structure that will be built on the same spot as the existing Spori Building. It will begin work immediately on conceptual design of the new building with construction scheduled to begin in early 2001.

Ricks officials are impressed with FFKR’s extensive expertise in historical work. Currently, FFKR is designing a replacement of the LDS Church’s historic Nauvoo Temple in Illinois. It also did the designs for the conversion of the Vernal (Utah) Tabernacle into an LDS temple, the conversion of the Hotel Utah into the Joseph Smith Memorial Building in Salt Lake City and the restoration of the Noyes Administration Building at Snow College in Ephraim, Utah.

FFKR also designed both of Ricks College’s most recent buildings – the Spencer W. Kimball Student and Administrative Services Building and the John Taylor Religion Building.

"As a firm they bring very strong expertise in historical work," says James Smyth, chairman of the building committee.

He says the existing Spori Building cannot be restored because of serious structural and design problems. While the new building will not be a replica of the Spori Building, Smyth says, "We are interested in preserving the heritage of the Spori Building and have given the architects the charge to design a building that will tie the new building to the past. We want to extend the heritage that is rooted in the old building to the new building."

He anticipates the design process will take the full calendar year of 2000 with construction beginning as soon as the weather breaks in 2001. It is estimated that the new building will take from 20 to 24 months to complete. The Spori Building will be vacated for the fall semester of 2000 and classes will be redistributed to other locations.

The Spori Building has been a major concern to college leaders for several decades. Determining the appropriate course of action has been particularly challenging because of the historic value attached to the building, and much time and effort was expended in trying to find ways to preserve or restore the facility.

Construction of the Spori Building started in the spring of 1900 and was completed in the fall of 1903. It was the first permanent building on the Ricks College campus and is named after Jacob Spori, the first principal of the Bannock Stake Academy (the forerunner of Ricks College). The building currently houses the Communication Department and Art Department, along with KRIC and KWBH radio stations, Graphic Services and the campus newspaper, the Scroll.

An engineering study was conducted in an effort to determine the structural status of the building. While the present safety of those who teach, study and work in the building is not in jeopardy, the engineering study found a number of serious concerns that needed to be addressed in the near future.

The building is constructed of rhyolite, a soft stone that was taken from a nearby quarry. Though it was built to the standard of the early 1900s, it does not meet today’s building criteria. Any renovations done to the Spori Building would need to meet current building codes. Consequently, the entire interior of the building would need to be removed during a renovation, including the roof, floors, interior walls, and electrical, mechanical and plumbing systems. Only the outer wall of rhyolite stone, which is eight inches thick, could potentially have remained, and preserving it would have presented its own unacceptable risks and challenges.

With an enrollment of 8,600 students, Ricks College is the largest private junior college in the United States. It is owned and operated by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.
