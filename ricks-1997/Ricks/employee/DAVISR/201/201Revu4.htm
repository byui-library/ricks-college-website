Review Sheet for EXAM #4 Western Culture 201 DAVIS

Late Middle Ages
     rise of mills (industrial revolution 900-1300)/new inventions:loom, spinning wheel,
     horse collar, stirrups, horse shoes
- creates middle class (have money/goods now)
- monasteries do very well/sheep/extra wool, but too worldly/reforms
Cities begin- people have excess crops/county fairs/money/guilds begin
Crusades - 1096-1244 (4 major, but only 1st was successful, most in 1100’s)
  - for praying or preying?/savage men/ "Frankish" (early Germans and French)
  - bring home items which change European outlook/raise standard of living
  - works of Aristotle "rediscovered"/reintroduced to Europe
  - women take over while men are away
  - Eleanor of Aquitaine, Queen of France, creates "love courts"
            - now life on Earth is OK and beautiful
            - Cult of the Virgin begins/churches named "Notre Dame" (Our Lady)

Church Philosophy- scholasticism/based on Aristotle's logic
            Battle of Universals (same old question: "nature" of Christ and Man)
            - Realists/St. Augustine (church position/soul is most important)
            - Nominalists (physical world/body most important)
            - Conceptualists (Peter Abelard/tries to unite both)
  - all finally reconciled by St. Thomas Aquinas (must study bible and world)
  - body and soul must be studied together for complete happiness (because you’ll have both in heaven)

Universities start 1150-1200 - poor students/few books (no printing press till 1454)
  - students (goliards) wander from campus to campus/ lead wild life

Genghis Khan (Jen not Gen) - mid 1200’s / conquers all of Asia and most of Eastern Europe
  reinvents pony express; owns more land than any other human in history
  Marco Polo visits China 1275; returns to Venice in 1295 with fabulous stories of wealth and wonders; few believe him; but Columbus sails because of Marco’s stories.

Little Ice Age 1300 - average annual temp drops several degrees
(won’t return to these levels until 2000; global warming is OK, we’re just returning to where we were)
  - colder/fewer crops/weak health/ many starve; wide famine in 1315

Bubonic Plague (Black Death) 1347; in 3 years 1/3 of Europe dies
 - returns every decade after that for next 50 years
 - by 1450 total pop of Europe is 1/2 that of 1300

Dante (1265-1321)- Divine Comedy (1310)/written while in exile from Florence
  - written in the vernacular (Italian) not Latin/all lines grouped in 3's (new form)
  - Beatrice (Divine Love/Mary), is his love/Virgil (Human Reason), his guide/Charon, the ferryman/Minos, the judge of Hell
  - wolf (incontinence-worldly pleasures, sins of the flesh, least offensive, most natural)
  - lion (violence against self or others, unnatural)
  - leopard (fraud-only an intelligent being (Man) can deceive, completely unnatural)
  - Ask yourself why -- three divisions in Hell? the name Divine Comedy? must Dante go through Hell? must Hell exist?  

Chaucer (1340-1400) writes Canterbury Tales in 1390’s
  - well-to-do family/raised as a page/very small reading public (until 1500)
 - tales of 30 pilgrims going to see tomb of Becket, Archbishop of Canterbury
    - know characters from Prologue
 - Tales are an intimate view of life in High Middle Ages, structure of society
Pilgrimages - religious trips, usually by a group so it would be safer, to holy places to see sacred objects (relics) (pieces of wood which were part of the cross, some cloth which was supposedly part of Mary’s veil, a thorn, etc.)
  - popular from 300’s on but especially from 900-1300’s, people believed their sins were forgiven if they went on such a trip
  - reason behind of Canterbury Tales
  - most important European city is Rome (others were Mecca, Canterbury, any city with strong religious background. For Mormons it’s Salt Lake City.)

Art and Architecture
Dark Ages of Art 450-1450/little art in this entire period
            - a few new styles from barbaric tribes
                        - Celts/Germans no painting/only metalwork
                        - geometric/abstract patterns
            - only patron was church
  - Monks of Ireland were best artists of entire period, possibly because they were separated from the mainland and didn’t know that no one else was doing anything in art. Used elaborate, intertwined lines (like calligraphy of today)
            - finest is decorating art on religious works
 - Carolingian (Charlesmagne) era 800’s - spiritual, not worldly
  - religious sculpture was symbolic, not naturalistic (like it was in catacombs)
Romanesque 1000-1100’s
  - church is a fortress/holy relics/much building of fortresses
  Mont St. Michel in Western France example of fortress and church joined as one
 - first monumental sculpture since Roman times, placed on churches
 - last judgment typical and most popular (moral, like Everyman)
 - most important pilgrimage city is Rome
Gothic 1200-1450
  - churches higher/bigger/fancier/flying buttresses to support high walls
  - windows (stained glass) thanks to buttresses
  - pointed arches (Gothic arch) in churches
  - Chartres cathedral is best example of pure Gothic style
Music
 - lay (common) music starts in Burgundy (northern France) in homes of the rich merchants (thanks to wine and textile industry)
 - will gradually move from North (Burgundy) to South (Rome), the opposite of almost every other trend in Europe, which always moved from South to North.
