60 YEARS LATER:WILL THEY CONFESS AGAIN?

Ricks College wants to have a little fun with a story from the 1930s concerning a cow that was coaxed inside the Spori Building. As part of the college's Spori Celebration and homecoming activities, Alumni Director Devin Shaum is looking for alumni involved in the late-night caper in 1937. Anyone with information concerning the incident is asked to contact Shaum at (208) 356-1154. After so many years, he promises complete amnesty.

According to a story retold by retired Registrar Eldred Stephenson of Rexburg during an honors lecture in 1978, faculty member M.D. Beal's Guernsey cow developed a yearning for learning and with the help of a few students ended up in the library on the third floor of the Spori. As acting president in the absence of Hyrum Manwaring, Beal found some of the participants before the end of the day and brought them before a committee. Others voluntarily came forward to share the blame.

David Crowder, author of the book "The Spirit of Ricks," writes the ad hoc committee voted to expel the "rusty dozen." Upon being notified, the students were "subdued and apologetic, but they took the bitter pill without comment." When President Manwaring returned, he "lifted the expulsion upon payment of slender fines to defray the custodian's extraordinary labors."

Ricks officials are quick to add that currently enrolled students should not get any wild ideas, explaining that they would be dealt with in a fashion much resembling that of M.D. Beal.

HOMECOMING AND SPORI CELEBRATIONS

Homecoming 2000 activities revolve around the theme "R Century of Promise." The activities will renew a traditional torchlight parade that was held in the early days of Ricks Academy. One hundred torches are being built for the parade and will be carried by members of the Ricks Men's Association, says Russell Bice, who is producing Friday night's Hallelujah Homecoming activities. The evening will begin with a concert at 7:30 p.m. in the Hart Auditorium. It will feature Ricks music and dance groups and alumni performers and a tribute to the Spori Building. Following the concert at approximately 9:30 p.m., the light parade will wind its way through campus ending at Viking Stadium for a victory celebration and fireworks show.

Ricks College also will pay tribute to the Jacob Spori Building on Saturday. The Spori Celebration will be held jointly with the Rexburg Chamber of Commerce's Harvest Festival. The festival will be on the lawn in front of the Spori Building from 11-2 p.m. The Chamber will provide a free potato bar for the first 2,500 people. The Harvest Festival will also feature vintage vehicles and people dressed in period costumes. During the celebration, the college will open the bottom level of the Spori Building for one last look. The remainder of the nearly vacated building will be closed to the public. On display in the foyer will be several commissioned pieces of artwork focusing on the building. The historic Jacob Spori Building is scheduled to be torn down and replaced with a new classroom building which will retain the same name. Alumni Association director Devin Shaum says, "The Spori Building represents a solid foundation upon which to build the future as the college transitions to a four-year university."

The Ricks College Vikings host the Scottsdale Community College Artichokes for the football game on Saturday at 1 p.m. in Viking Stadium. MAJOR FORUM ON DEAD SEA SCROLLS

Since the Dead Sea Scrolls were discovered in 1947, they have been subject to intensive study and review. Dr. Noel Reynolds, associate academic vice president for undergraduate studies at Brigham Young University, will give insights on the Dead Sea Scrolls and their significance at a Major Forum on Thursday, October 12, at 2 p.m. in the Taylor Chapel. Major Forums are broadcast on KRIC-FM 100.5. Dr. Reynolds has served as president of the Foundation for Ancient Research and Mormon Studies (FARMS) and director of the Center for the Preservation of Ancient Religious Texts (CPART). The Dead Sea Scrolls are of particular interest to members of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints when reflection is given to the coming forth of another previously hidden record, The Book of Mormon, Another Testament of Jesus Christ. Both records were hidden as ancient civilizations tried to save their history when faced with overpowering opposition.

THEATRE DEPARTMENT TO PRESENT W;T

The Theatre Department is set to present playwright Margaret Edson's Pulitzer-prize winning, modern medical drama, W;t. The play is directed by Ricks instructor Hyrum Conrad. It will be performed on Oct. 12-13, 17-21, 25-28 in the Kirkham Arena Theater. This year's first theater production is not as lighthearted as those in the past, but it will involve students in the life of another. The conflicts of life while dealing with terminal cancer and experimental chemotherapy bring an instant connection for many. Conrad says the "play is powerful. It is a dramatic work that is intellectually challenging and emotionally compelling." The New York Times said W;t is "a brutally human and beautifully layered new play . . . you feel both enlightened and, in a strange way, enormously comforted."
