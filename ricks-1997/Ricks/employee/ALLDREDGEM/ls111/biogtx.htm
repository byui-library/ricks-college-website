The word "biography" comes from the Greek words, "bios" for life and "graphein" for to write.
A biography is the history of a person's life from birth to death revealing character, personality and philosophy.
At one time biographies were not written until after the person died.
Even today a person's place in history cannot be measured until their contributions are finished.

Several forms of biography exist.
A collective biography is written about more than one person.
An autobiography is written by the person whose life is described.
Memoirs are observations of current history by someone involved in the events, such as a diplomat or military leader.

A diary is a day by day account of life.
"Journal" does mean a daily list of news.
Journal keeping has connotations of serious writing compared to the connotations associated with diaries.
Whether called a diary or a journal an individual may witness events that are not recorded anywhere else.

Letters are the written communications revealing thoughts and philosophy.
The letters of writers or statesmen are often studied to determine what influenced their writing or decisions.
Biographers may combine
        letters of a famous person with
        interviews of the associates of the famous person
        to analyze what caused a famous person to act as he did.
 

To find biographies:

    1. Use the library catalog.
BIOGRAPHY is a subdivision for most occupations and groups of people.
See the Library of Congress' Subject Heading for examples.

HISTORY AND CRITICISM is a subject heading for materials which discuss works not just lives.
Critical standards evaluate the influence of the individual on the field of endeavor.
If an individual does not have this type of work,
    try a collective history and criticism for the national group or time period to which the individual belongs.

The library catalog may have analytical cards or contents notes for a collective biography.
Browsing the books on the shelves and using the indexes in the books may also help retrieve information.
For example, books on the Civil War may have biographies of the generals.

Books written by authors may have biographical notes.
Consult the title page of books written by the individual or contact the publisher of recent works.

Biography Index  by H.W. Wilson Company is an index to both books and periodicals with biographies.

    6. Use biography collection indexes.
Biography and Genealogy Master Index (Ref Z5303.U5B57) is an index to
collective biographies,
biographical dictionaries and encyclopedias.
An abbreviation is given for the biographical reference book which contains information about the individual.
A key to abbreviations is found in the front of the volume or the set.
This key gives complete information about the book.
Libraries often write call numbers next to this list.
If no call number appears next to the entry, check the library catalog to be sure that the book has not been acquired.

        7. Use biographical dictionaries.

  • provide clues to more information

Universal biographical sources for the living

 Current Biography (Ref CT100.C8)

  • Essay length articles are the usual entry.
  • A section of obituaries (facts) for the recently deceased has just the facts.
  • A paperback issue is published every month
  • A list of occupations is found in the front of paper and back of bound issues.
  • A cumulated index covers from 1940 to 1990.
  • Check the most recent bound volume for other cumulative indexes.
