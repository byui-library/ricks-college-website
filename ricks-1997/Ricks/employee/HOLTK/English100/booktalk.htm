BOOKTALK

DESCRIPTION:  Your assignment is to do a BOOKTALK and you will be a BOOKTALKER. A BOOKTALK is given orally and is a brief preview for a book. It is an intriguing glimpse into the book, designed to entice your audience into reading the book for themselves. Good BOOKTALKERS enjoy their books so much they want others to experience them for themselves; a BOOKTALKER advertises and sells the book so others will want to read it.

GUIDELINES: The oral presentation must be 2-4 minutes in length. It must briefly introduce the major characters, mention major plot events, and hint at the theme (but don't give away the climax or resolution of the conflict). Be sure to mention the book's title and author. It must also include at least one quotation from the book. At the time of your presentation, you must turn in a typed, one page copy of your BOOKTALK.

 SUGGESTIONS: Each BOOKTALK is the reader's personal, creative, and original response to his book. You may play background music, wear a costume or involve your audience in an activity (questions, games, roleplaying, extras in a scene). Below are some suggestions you may want to consider as you plan the most exciting way to sell your book.

write a poem   make a collage
act out a scene do a video
interpret a character make a mobile
use background music write a newspaper article
write a song perform a dance
paint a picture become a character and tell part of the story
puppet show be a newsbroadcaster
give an emergency bulletin

LAST ADVICE: Don't just summarize the story; tell about the story. Make your BOOKTALK exciting by using descriptive words. Tease your audience into curiosity--give only an introduction to a plot event and then leave them hanging. Make certain you can accurately pronounce your author's name, character names and locations. Remember to be enthusiastic!
