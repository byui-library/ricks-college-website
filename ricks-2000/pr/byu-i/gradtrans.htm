News interview with President Bednar

The following news article was published in The Rexburg Standard Journal July 21, 2000

Be wary of the rumor mill
BYU-Idaho transition will be gradual

By Kris Comstock
Staff writer

Ricks College President David Bednar wants people of the Upper Snake River Valley to know that the transition from a two-year school to a four-year school is going to be gradual and that we shouldn’t expect to see dramatic changes in a couple of years.

“I can’t emphasize this enough,” he said this week during an interview to clear up any misconceptions people in the community may have. He says, he has heard all kinds of rumors, one putting student enrollment at 28,000 compared with 9,000 now.

That figure is flat out wrong, he says.

Meantime, he and others at the Rexburg college with responsibility for planning and guiding the transition have no more specific answers to question than anyone else.

They are in the process of trying to find those answers themselves. And he wants to emphasize: “As soon as we know we will let the city and community know. Our intent is not to frustrate, but to be prudent, reliable, and accurate in processing our facts and information.”

But for now, this can be said:

Student numbers: The current requirement of admitting no more than 8,650 student will be increased. But how much higher that number will go and just when is impossible to say.

Faculty: There will be an increase in the number of faculty. But again, no timetable is possible yet. There are about 940 faculty and staff members now.

Building: When Gordon B. Hinckley, president of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, made the announcement June 21 that Ricks would become a four-year university, he said there may be a need for one or two new buildings. But there is no massive building program planned at the school. Demolition of the Spori Building will continue as planned and the replacement building may be altered somewhat in light of the new mission of the school. But construction will start next spring.

Name Change: Will probably start going by the name of Brigham Young University-Idaho at the beginning of the 2001 school year.

Sports: Intercollegiate sports will not be a part of the new mission of the school. But the added programs in competitive sports, music, and many other types of activities for students will be aimed at giving every student at the school an opportunity to participate.

Time: It is anticipated that junior level classes could begin in the fall of 2001 or it may be a year later in 2002.

Future Vision: Bednar in his own right is the most enthusiastic supporter of the changes to come. In talking with him, one can’t help but be caught up in his excitement or realize how strongly he believes in this long-term project. This is a historic and an incredible period of time for the church and its members. The transition is probably one of the biggest changes taking place in the church education system in the past 50 years and the biggest change in higher education in Idaho in a long time.

In the interview, Bednar said, “We have to understand that not just one man made the decision to make the change.” There is a purpose to the change and he, like others, thinks the change is of a divine nature.

Bednar is knowledgeable and his excitement and animation when talking about he future of BYU-Idaho is catching. It’s hard not to be enveloped in his enthusiasm.

He hopes that people in the community as well as the students will regard this change as an opportunity to participate in the making of Mormon history.

“The process of transition is multifaceted,” he said. “It’s not what do we do, but what do we want to become. We as a faculty as staff must now decide where we go from here, what do we want to happen in the future, how can we become better.

“Right now we don’t know, and when we do know, we’ll let everyone else know. It’s still new to us, too. We have set up committees and have started researching all possibilities that would make us a better four-year school.”

And before Ricks can make these changes it must go through a phase process which includes accreditation for four-year status. To become accredited, Ricks College must prepare a prospectus (which could take months) and then present it to the Northwest Association of Schools and Colleges in Bellevue, Wash., for review.

This review could take up to 90 days, he said. At that point, if the association approves the transition plan, Ricks would then achieve candidacy status. From candidacy status then, the Ricks College academic council will set guidelines and choose which programs could and would become four year programs. At that time, purpose and programming will be evaluated again.

“We have to have a clear sense and purpose of our mission and we will be evaluated with that in mind,” he said.

Just before the announcement was made, Bednar said that Ricks had complete the accreditation process for two year status. “The day after the announcement was made, I sent out a letter indicating our intent to move to become a four-year school.”

The second phase of the transition would be getting faculty and staff on line. “We have an incredible faculty that is overwhelmingly supportive of the changes about to take place.”

Just because Ricks will get a name change does not mean that the faculty and staff will change characteristics. “A lot of people have been worried about that,” he said, “We are not going to be a mirror image of what BYU-Provo or BYU-Hawaii is. We will probably offer some things each of those schools don’t offer and we will pioneer some things on our own.”

“We have always been student-oriented and our faculty has always been student-accessible. We will remain that way. Our teachers will remain teachers.”

About 30 percent of the faculty at Ricks have Ph.D. degrees but “the status of our teachers will not change. All have master’s degrees, and we will not need the graduated status of assistant professor to professorships, like the university atmosphere of BYU-Provo and BYU-Hawaii,” Bednar said.

Bednar is excited about the future of Ricks College. He said (quoting from President Hinckley when the announcement was made) that the change is “clearly intended to be a new wine in a new bottle” (as quoted from a scripture in the New Testament). He anticipates many wonderful and exciting changes in the composition of Ricks College.

As far as becoming a solely academic-based university without intercollegiate sports, Bednar said there are a lot of schools that do not have an intercollegiate athletic program. “It’s not that the athletic program is being disbanded here. It’s being redefined.

“We have a committee working together to redefine the sports program into becoming an intramural college based program. We feel this program will provide opportunities for all students to participate in a sports program, not just a few.”

“And the intramural program will not be confined to just sports. The program will expand to fine arts as well as academics. We want all students to be able to participate. There are a great number of young people who have not been able to play football or baseball or softball or even compete in music or dance festivals. This way they will have that opportunity. And we feel that based on the intramural activities, we’ll draw a lot more students.”

“It’s premature to predict the number of student that will be coming to campus once these changes take place. And to try and make that prediction is unrealistic,” Bednar said. “People are frustrated because we aren’t giving many details, but right now, we don’t have many. I have to stress that I am willing to visit with anyone about what’s going on, but at this stage of the game we have very few projections.”

Bednar realizes the importance of housing issues and the city involvement with planning and he emphasized that at this point as far as he can tell modifications will be modest and there will be adjustments made, but there are no formal plans for huge building projects. “Once again as soon as we know we will let the city and community know. Our intent is not to frustrate but to be prudent, reliable, and accurate in processing our facts and information.”

All in all the change from Ricks College to BYU-Idaho will be a gradual one. “I was surprised as everyone by the announcement and in the past few weeks I have spent a great deal of time just trying to comprehend the majesty and magnitude of what we are doing and going to be doing here at Ricks, and I am excited to be part of it.”

As far as advice for the community goes, Bednar says, “Please be patient with us while we do our homework. We will be forthright and open when we have projections, estimates, and numbers.”
