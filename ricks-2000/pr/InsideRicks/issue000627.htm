TRANSITION TO BRIGHAM YOUNG UNIVERSITY-IDAHO

"I think it is important to characterize this announcement as initiating a process of transition. This is not announcing a finished product," said President David A. Bednar in response to the announcement that Ricks College will become a four-year college known as Brigham Young University-Idaho. The decision by the Board of Trustees was announced on July 21 by President Gordon B. Hinckley, chairman of the Board.

President Hinckley paid tribute to the college in a press conference when he stated, "May I say that if you have not been to Ricks recently, you have a surprise awaiting you. That campus has been renewed - new buildings, modified buildings . . . It isn't a little run-down country college. It is a great institution with a wonderful campus, great facilities, a very caring faculty, a very strong and innovative president, and everything that we feel is needed now to make a program of this kind succeed."

President Bednar indicated the first step in the transition is to evaluate what is needed academically and otherwise to be an extraordinarily effective teaching-oriented institution.

President's Council recognizes the need for open communication to avoid misinformation that "feeds the rumor mill." The college is developing a web site to address the issues and help keep the public informed of the progress. When completed, it will link directly from the front page at www.byui.edu.

ATHLETIC DEPARTMENT LOOKS AT FUTURE

"I have faith that things will work out. . . Maybe we can bless more lives," reflected Lori Woodland, head women's basketball coach, after hearing that the emphasis in Ricks College athletics will shift from intercollegiate competition to an expanded activity program.

The concern of many of the coaches turned to their student athletes who have already been recruited for the upcoming seasons. In a meeting with President Bednar, they were reassured that prior commitments to the students and to the league will be kept. "From that point on we need to assess our programs. We do not have all of the definite answers yet, but we will counsel with our coaches, players, and the leagues as we make our recommendations," said Garth Hall, athletic director.

CELEBRATE WITH "DANCE AMERICAN STYLE"

Just in time to help commemorate Independence Day, the American Folk Dancers will be performing to the guitar strummin' and banjo pickin' of the Bluegrass Band. Their program of "Dance American Style" will be presented on Monday, July 3, at a 2 p.m. matinee in the Kirkham Auditorium. The ticket price of $6 helps to defray part of the travel expense as they embark on a month-long performance tour in Spain and Portugal. For more information or to order tickets, call 356-2230 or go online at www.byui.edu/tickets. The pre-tour concert traditionally has been a popular concert as performances take a historic journey back to the graceful period of colonial United States, through the romantic simplicity of traveling pioneers, and on to the energetic pace clogging to a more modern beat. "We try to represent all the eras of American folk dancing," said the director Charles West.

NO-TILL FIELD DAY

Ricks College will host its 24th Annual No-Till Field Day Thursday, July 13, from 9 a.m. to 12:30 p.m. The day will begin at the Ag Engineering Building on the south end of the Ricks campus for area farmers and other interested parties. The tour will feature college demonstration plots as well as cover other topics including global positioning systems, niche crops, Monsanto's Maverick herbicide, wild oat and broadleaf weed control, Tilt and Stratego wheat fungicides, spring what varieties, and hard red spring wheat fertility plots. Hosting the field day will be John D. Walker and his fellow associates, Greg Blaser and Blake Willis. Walker says, "We have developed this program to offer something for everyone. We will cater information for both dry land as well as irrigation farms in the region."
