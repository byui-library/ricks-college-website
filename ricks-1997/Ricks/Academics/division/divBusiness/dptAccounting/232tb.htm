Course Description and Objectives: This course covers simple interest, compound interest, present and future values, annuities, and financial applications of the above. The course is designed to enable students to understand and solve problems involving interest computations. The course will utilize a financial calculator and some computerized spreadsheet applications in the learning process. The students will learn how to interpret and solve common financial problems involving the time value of money.

Mid-term Exam: There will be 1 mid-term exam given during the course of the semester. It will be administered in the Testing Center. Please take note of the dates for this test in the tentative course outline. There are no grade improvement projects (extra credit) and no retakes of the test. If you have a scheduling conflict, please arrange with the instructor to take the test before it is scheduled to be given. It is the student's responsibility to be aware of the Testing Center hours and to make sure that adequate time is allowed for completing the exam. Although no time limit penalties will be assessed, students should be aware that speed is important too, not just accuracy (the CPA Exam is a timed exam). The mid-term is worth 100 points.

Final Exam: The final exam will be held in class according to the schedule in the back of the Ricks College class schedule book. Please plan on taking the final when you are scheduled to take it. The final will cover the last half of the assigned material and, thus, will not be comprehensive. The final is worth 100 points.

Calculators are required for taking the exams. However, programmable calculators should not be programmed with notes and other aids for taking the test. Only the calculator functions should be used. The exams consist of multiple choice questions. They test student memory, understanding, and ability to analyze and apply concepts covered in the course.

Homework: The course revolves around the preparation, completion, and explanation of homework. Homework problems are assigned to help students learn and experience the principles covered in the course. Although some memorization is essential, accounting usually has to be experienced before a thorough understanding can be achieved. Be prepared to spend many hours in solving homework problems. Time and effort are just as important as the correct solution.

The homework assignments will be given to you in class. Homework assignments are due at the beginning of the following class period. There are 12 homework assignments and 5 points possible for each assignment, regardless of the number of problems assigned. The homework will be corrected by the students in class. The homework assignments are to be done in pencil and should be done very neatly (both form and content are important). Any corrections made to homework should be done in pen in order to give the instructor a good impression of where mistakes are being made. The homework will not be graded based on accuracy. It will be graded on being complete and timely. Any sloppy, negligent, or incomplete work will receive at least a 1 point deduction.

Late Assignments: Any homework submitted after the assigned class period will receive a 1 point per-school-day deduction. No homework will be accepted after the test for that material has been administered. Tests may be taken late only under extreme extenuating circumstances and with prior permission from the instructor and will receive a 5 point per-school-day deduction.
