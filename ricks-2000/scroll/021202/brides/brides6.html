LDS emphasize eternal marriage bonds

by Sarah Lunt
LUN99013@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Marriage is a significant commitment inside and outside of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.

“The Family: A Proclamation to the World” is an important document of the Church that sets the foundation for LDS beliefs on marriage and the family.

“We, the First Presidency and the Council of the Twelve Apostles of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, solemnly proclaim that marriage between a man and woman is ordained of God ... ,” according to the proclamation.

“The divine plan of happiness enables family relationships to be perpetuated beyond the grave. Sacred ordinances and covenants available in holy temples make it possible for individuals to return to the presence of God and for families to be united eternally,” according to the proclamation.

The Church believes in special ordinances and covenants made in temples that make it possible for a man and a woman to be married throughout all eternity, not just “until death do you part.”

“...Celestial marriage, ordained by God and performed by his authority in his holy temples, is eternal, and couples so united are sealed for time and all eternity, and their children are born into the covenant of the everlasting gospel,” former first counselor in the First Presidency of the Church, President N. Eldon Tanner, said. “They will be an eternal family according to their faithfulness.”

Th temple marriage commitment is so binding that Elder Hugh B. Brown described the marriage covenant as “...a pledge, or solemn covenant, a spiritual sign or bond between the contracting parties themselves and between them and God.”

This idea of the serious commitment in marriage is often not mirrored in the rest of the world. Divorce rates in the United States top 50 percent. Many couples cohabitate before marriage and many decide never to marry at all.

Church leaders strongly discourage the view that marriage is not necessary or desirable in today’s society.

President David O. McKay discouraged the view that marriage is not serious or permanent.

“...To look upon marriage as a mere contract that may be entered into at pleasure in response to a romantic whim or for selfish purposes and severed at the first difficulty or misunderstanding that may arise ... [is not an appropriate approach to marriage],” he said.

“When two Latter-day Saints are united together in marriage [inside the covenant], promises are made to them concerning their offspring that reach from eternity to eternity,” President Lorenzo Snow, fifth president of the Church, said.
