SCATTERING AND GATHERING IN ISRAEL


Chapter Nineteen:

Those of Israel who escaped the scattering which took place earlier in history, even Judah, will be scattered for their rejection of the Holy One of Israel. Then Nephi gives us a key as to why he tells us of these events. It is in verse 18 where he states: "And, I Nephi, have written these things unto my people, that perhaps I might persuade them that they would remember the Lord their Redeemer. (19) Wherefore I speak unto all the house of Israel, if it so be that they should obtain these things." Then in verse 23, latter portion, we read that Nephi read the words of Isaiah and other prophets to his "brethren" for a purpose. That purpose is given again: "...that I might more fully persuade them to believe in the Lord their Redeemer...for I did liken all scriptures unto us, that it might be for our profit and learning." Finally in vers 24 we read a statement that indicates we ought to listen carefully to the words that follow as well: "Hear ye the words of the prophet, ye who are a remnant of the house of Israel, a branch who have been broken off; hear ye the words of the prophet, which were written unto all the house of Israel, and liken (use) them unto yourselves, that ye may have hope as well as your brethren from whom ye have been broken off..."

To whom are these things written, then? Certainly Nephi's people. Then, of course, to the people of Isaiahs time, but more importantly, anyone who is of the House of Israel or any of her remnants in any dispensation. The fact that Nephi used it tells us he intended it for Book of Mormon People, but the fact that Mormon was inspired to put it in the book tells us that GOD intended it for our time. Therefore we must look at this record in that context.

Please understand that we are looking at these scriptures from my perspective. I hope it gives you food for thought, but it is not intended to be the final word, and certainly not authoritative.

CHAPTER TWENTY

Verses 1-6--Isaiah knew of the weaknesses of Israel and he speaks openly of them. In the very first verse quoted by Nephi Isaiah clearly states that he is speaking to those who are of the House of Jacob, called by the name of Israel, who are baptized into the covenant, or the Church of God, but do it in hypocrisy. They are those of Israel who "swear not in truth, nor in righteousness." Those who call themselves of the "holy city" of Zion, but do not "stay themselves upon the God of Israel." In other words, they do not lean on or trust God, but are members by baptism only--"obstinate", their necks as "sinew", and their "brow brass." In other words, they will not bow before the Lord, do his will, but insist on their own. A people who follow idols more readily than they follow God. In his day, of course, he is referring to Laman and Lemuel, but Mormon is inspired to place this information in the Book of Mormon for us, and these words are meant for members today who have the same tendencys.
 

Verses 9-11--But God defers his anger, for his sake (and thus for the sake of all his children), he will not cut Israel off. Why?

First--though referring to the entire house as rebellious, many noble and great ones will come through that house, chosen in the furnace of affliction (internal challenges) to do God's work to bring to pass the immortality and eternal life of man. It is to these, particularly the tribe of Ephraim, that the prophet Isaiah refers in chapter 21.

Second--the promise and commitment to Israel is foreordained and he will not "give my glory (name) unto another." (Verse 11). He will stick with Israel because it has been set in heaven, and through Israel, even though many in Israel have been rebellious, he will do his work, even a great work.
Elder Orson F. Whitney has said:
"they were chosen in the heavens, before they came in the flesh, and were sent forth from the presence of God with a mission. We who call ourselves Latter-day Saints are a branch of the house of Israel, gathered out from among the Gentiles; we are a portion of that martyred nation, chosen of God and sent upon earth to suffer and endure for His sake and for the sake of all mankind; to bear the oracles of God, and be His representatives in the midst of the human race."(Orson F. Whitney, Conference Report, October, 1905, p. 91).
Elder Benson has said:
"For nearly six thousand years, God has held you in reserve to make your appearance in the final days before the Second Coning of the Lord...  While our generation will be comparable in wickedness to the days of Noah, when the Lord cleansed the earth by flood, there is a major difference this time. It is that God has saved for the final inning some of his strongest children, who will help bear off the kingdom triumphantly. And that is where you come in, for you are the generation that must be prepared to meet your God. (Ezra Taft Benson "In His Steps," in Speeches of the Year, 1979, Provo: BYU Press, 1980, pg. 59).

Note the use of the term "our generation." He is not referring just to the youth of today, but to a generation of saints. We belong to that generation.

As President Benson also said:
"All through the ages the prophets have looked down through the corridors of time to our day. Billions of the deceased and those yet to be born have their eyes on us. Make no mistake about it- -we are a marked generation. There has never been more expected of the faithful in such a short period of time as there is of us. Never before on the face of this earth have the forces of evil and the forces of good been as well organized. Now is the great day of the devils power, with the greatest mass murderers of all time living among us. But now is also the great day of the Lord's power, with the greatest number ever of priesthood holders on the earth. And the showdown is fast approaching." (Ibid).

Verses 12-22--The challenge to Jacob/Israel is given. All of Israel are to:
a. Listen and heed "His" challenge (vs 12-13). We are to assemble, no matter what the dispensation may be, and hear. This is a call to stand up for righteousness, to follow the lead of the prophets, and to reject the lead of the world. It is not a call to walk away from the world, but to be a part of it without partaking of it's idols and its wickedness.

b. Listen to the prophets. (Vs 14-15). Those who hath declared are to be followed. If we do, we will prosper. Vs 16 is a declaration by the prophets themselves. Vs. 17-19 are the Lords declaration that had Israel followed this rule her "seed also had been as the sand." Instead their name was cut off from the Lord.

c. Leave Babylon. Quite bringing wickedness into Israel, keep it out of the Church. (Vs 20-21). Such is the only way Israel can fulfill her mission and become his.

CHAPTER TWENTY ONE

This chapter seems to be more pointed--directly speaking to those who are "broken off," or the remnants of the house whom the Lord has scattered. Note in verse one that they are broken off because of the wickedness of their "pastors," or leaders.

21:1-7
Who is the "me" of verse one where it states, "the Lord hath called me from the womb..." Verse three tells us it is Israel but note that it is "broken off" Israel. Therefore, this chapter is to a specific group within Israel who have a foreordained calling to accomplish. It is to Ephraim.

In verse two we have some interesting comments about this portion of Israel having a "mouth like a sharp sword," and being hidden in the "shadow of His (the Lord's) hand. It also states this group to be a polished shaft, and hid in "his" (the Lord's) quiver. All this indicates traits and elements of the calling and election of Ephraim. The words "mouth like a sharp sword" refers to the power of the message the leader of the house of Israel will carry to all mankind. Being hid in the "shadow of his hand" or in "his quiver," indicate the protection of God for this portion of Israel as she is driven among the nations because of the wickedness of her leaders. That, even though there is wickedness in the house from time to time, the Lord continues to keep an important portion of Israel as his weapon for future use in bringing about his purposes. Not only will he keep her safe to use later but he will make her as a "polished shaft" a more effective weapon, through her sufferings. He has set aside Ephraim for the last days when she will become the administrative branch of Israel and carry the gospel to her brethren and sisters and to all nations.
 

Let me take a moment hear to share a few quotes with you about the calling of Ephraim:
Erastus Snow stated:

For he has had his eyes upon the chosen spirits that have come upon the earth in the various ages from the beginning of the world up to this time; and as he said to Abraham, speaking of the multitudes of spirits that were shown unto him in heavenly vision, you see that some are more noble than others? Yes. Then you may know there were some others even more noble than they; and he speaks in the same manner of the multitude of the heavenly bodies... The Lord has sent those noble spirits into the world to perform a special work, and appointed their times; and they have always fulfilled the mission given them, and their future glory and exaltation is secured unto them; and that is what I understand by the doctrine of election spoken of by the Apostle Paul and other sacred writers... Such were called and chosen and elected of God to perform a certain work at a certain time of the world's history and in due time he fitted them for that work...And so he elected the seed of Ephraim to be that peculiar people I have referred to, that holy nation, a kingdom of Priests, a people to receive the covenants and oracles, and to be witnesses to certain nations of the God of Israel. (Erastus Snow, Journal of Discourses, Vol. 23, p. 184-185, May 6 1892).

Melvin J. Ballard shared this interesting experience:
"I was thrilled not long ago when I read an article appearing in one of our national magazines. The author was discussing the question: "Are the Jews the chosen people of God?" He denied them the right to claim for themselves and their posterity all the blessings pronounced upon all the sons of Jacob, but limited their blessings to their own particular line--the tribe of Judah. After tracing the history of the Jews and the several tribes of Israel the author concludes: "If we could find in the earth somewhere today the descendants of Joseph, we would find the chosen people of God, because the blessings of Joseph were so much greater than the blessings of his brethren that you will recall they despised him and were jealous of his blessings, and sold him into Egypt; but the Lord was with him and raised him up and made him mighty to become the deliverer of his fathers household."

I say, when I read that statement my soul thrilled with a new sensation of gratitude to God that I live to see the day when Joseph's children are being gathered again, and realize that the Latter-day Saints are of Joseph, children of his favored son, Ephraim, gathered "one of city and two of family," a few from the midst of the nations of the earth, whither they have been scattered, and brought to the land of Zion. The Lord has selected and picked them. They were chosen spirits before they were born. He knew them in the Spirit world. He held them in reserve to come forth at the right time. (Melvin J. Ballard, Conference Report, Oct. 1924, p. 28).
 

In another place Elder Erastus Snow said:
And it is the foremost of those spirits whom the Lord has prepared to receive the Gospel when it was presented to them, and who did not wait for the Elders to him them from the hills and corners of the earth, but they were hunting for the Elders, impelled by the spirit which they could not understand; and for this reason were they among the first Elders of the Church; they and the fathers having been watched over from the days that God promised those blessings upon Isaac and Jacob and Joseph and Ephraim. And these are they that will be found in the front ranks of all that is noble and good in their day and time, and who will be found among those whose efforts are directed in establishing upon the earth those heaven-born principles which tend directly to blessing and salvation, to ameliorating the condition of their fellow-men, and elevating them in the scale of their being; and among those also who receive the fullness of the Everlasting Gospel, and the keys of Priesthood in the last days, through whom God determined to gather up again unto himself a peculiar people, a holy nation, a pure seed that shall stand upon Mount Zion as saviors, not only to the house of Israel but also to the house of Esau (the Gentiles). (Erastus Snow, Journal of Discourses, Vol. 23, p. 185-186, May 6, 1882).

21:3
Note in verse three that He (the Lord Jehovah) will be glorified through Israel. That is his purpose for protecting her. To be glorified is to be perfected, exalted. The Lord Jehovah will use Ephraim to achieve his ultimate glory--to become like the Father and receive all that the Father has, then he will give that same glory to those of Israel who did not fail him, even those Ephraim, called and elected to this high and holy calling. Therefore, for Ephraim to do her duty is for Ephraim to reach her own glorification through the Son.

21:4
Who is the I in this verse? Of course, it continues to be Ephraim who gets discouraged at times as they see their work is getting few converts. But, Ephraim is willing to leave the judgement of that work with the Lord (Jehovah), and with God (probably the Father).

21:5
Gives us several truths. 1. It reminds us again that Ephraim was foreordained to gather Israel (Jacob). 2. That even though some of Israel will refuse to be gathered the Lord (Jehovah) will still glorify those of Ephraim who make the attempt. 3. At the time of judgement of Ephraim and their work God will be her "strength." In other words he will support Ephraim and call them his because they were diligent in their attempt.
 

21:6
In this verse the Lord reminds Ephraim that gathering Israel will be a "light" thing compared to helping all others. I think this is seen in how quickly those of Israel hear the truth and cleave to it and how hard it is to get a Gentile to accept it. As Elder Robert L Simpson stated about the many wonderful points of the gospel and its life changing message:
"Now, any one of the foregoing facts should rate banner headlines in every newspaper in the world, but about the only response we get from more than three billion people who are supposedly thirsting for truth is, in effect: "Don't rock the boat," or "Who do you think you are to make such fantastic claims as visits from on high, the only true priesthood, additional scriptures, and a living prophet?" (Robert L. Simpson, in Conference Report, Oct. 1971, 113; or Ensign, Dec. 1971, 102)

As it says in the end of the verse, Ephraim is the "salvation to the world." The trouble is, those of the world do not always wish to be saved.

21:7
However, as verse seven indicates, even kings and princes will accept the truth." Because the "Lord is faithful," he won't give up. And though many will despise Ephraim, some will accept her message.

21:8
Jehovah will give Ephraim to all nations as a covenant to them and to give them a chance at the truth and an inheritance. The desolate heritages is probably in reference to blessings within Israel left open (made desolate) by apostasy within Israel.

21:9
Ephraim is to call the prisoners of the nations (all men held captive by the world) forth. She is to go to those who "sit in darkness" and feed them and lead them to pastures in high places (probably the covenants found in the temples--the high places of the Lord).

21:10-11
Those that do come shall no longer hunger or thirst but shall be led to springs of water (an allusion to the atonement--the living waters) by Ephraim until the "mountains" (temples" become their way and the Lord's "highways" (probably doctrines and ordinances which lead to God) shall be exalted in their lives.
 

21:12-13
And some of the Gentiles will come from among many nations and they shall have their "feet" established; and shall break forth into singing. In my opinion, this is reference to their becoming part of Israel. The term "people of the East" was often used in reference to the Asian part of the Persian Empire, including Syria, Arabia, and Mesopotamia. As one non-LDS source states: "They and the Egyptians constituted what was understood as, more or less, the world." (The Anchor Bible Dictionary, Vol. 2, p. 249). Thus, this verse refers to the Gentiles, or those of the world, who have their "feet established" and are "smitten no more" with ignorance and lack of the saving ordinances Ephraim can provide. The Lord comforts them as "his people" and will have mercy upon them because of their affliction (life without meaning). They have come to the truth and have become Israel.
 

As Joseph Smith taught:
"It (the Holy Ghost) is more powerful in expanding the mind, enlightening the understanding...of a man who is the literal seed of Abraham, than one that is a Gentile, though it may not have half as much visible effect upon the body; for as the Holy Ghost falls upon on of the literal seed of Abraham, it is calm and serene; and his whole soul and body are only exercised by the pure spirit of intelligence; while the effect of the Holy Ghost upon a Gentile, is to purge out the old blood, and make him actually of the seed of Abraham. That man that has none of the blood of Abraham (naturally) must have a new creation by the Holy Ghost. In such a case, there may be more of a powerful effect upon the body, and visible to the eye, than upon an Israelite, while the Israelite at first might be far before the Gentile in pure intelligence. (Teachings of the Prophet Joseph Smith, The Deseret News Press, 1938 Edition, Pgs. 149-150).

They, along with those of Israel already gathered, shall rejoice. Administrative Israel has been gathered, and as he attempts to gather the rest of the house, he also gathers Gentiles.

But what of the Jews? That portion of Israel that Isaiah lived and taught among? Where are they? What of them in this time of gathering? Isaiah approaches this subject next.

21:14-15
The Zion referred to here is Jerusalem, thus Judah. During this period in which Ephraim gathers scattered Israel and works among the Gentiles Judah laments that she has been forsaken. God will show her that he has not, for even though a woman may forget her child, the fruit of her womb--a most difficult thing to understand except in our day--God will not forget Judah anymore than he will forget any of blood Israel, those born into the house he has chosen.

21:16
Explains why he cannot forget them. He died for them, atoned for their sins, and their "walls" (barriers) are ever before him. He knows of their suffering, their challenges, the things that keep them separate from him.

21:17
The descendants of Judah (her children) shall finally begin to throw off the bands of her persecutors and begin to make progress against them, causing them to go "forth of thee." To leave them be. We see this in the events of history, particularly since 1948.

21:18
They are told in this verse to lift up there eyes and note that many others gather themselves together, and shall come to them. This is in reference to Ephraim, the rest of the house and those Gentiles who have gathered into the Gospel and the fact that they will also come to Judah. Not also, that Judah will surely be clothed the same as all the others, and she will have the chance to become the Lords bride.

21:19
Continues this message of hope. Judah's waste places and the land of her destruction (the places where she resides in a wicked world (Germany and her holocaust--Russia and her pogroms) shall "even now be to narrow by reason of the inhabitants." In other words, so many will live in these places, that there will not be room enough for them. It is to constricting, confining, both physically and spiritually and they cry to be free. This is in reference to the time just prior to Judah's return. Persecution and violence constrict and lay waste to her--she is tired and wishes to be free of it. I believe the last sentence gives us a point of time. Those that have and are swallowing up Judah shall be "far away." Gone from this world. This is God's promise that those who persecuted her shall be destroyed by their own wickedness and will not be able to stop what God will do for Judah as he begins his gathering of them.

21:20-21
The children of the lost and apostate Judah will cry for a land. This is a direct reference to the Zionist movement begun in the late 1800's and culminating in the partitioning of Palestine in 1948-49. And Judah will be amazed at the new generation and their greatness in return. Old Judah will be in awe of the new Judah.

21:22
The promises to Judah continue. Their God boldly states to them that he will answer the cry of Judah's children and will lift up his hand among the "gentile nations" and set up his standard to the people. These two statements have regard to two different groups--governments and the kingdom of God--but both shall bring the sons of Judah back to that which they have lost. The kings and queens (leaders) of governments, will return Judah to the land; the kings and queens of Gods kingdom, shall return them to the covenant.

21:23
Both groups will "bow down to thee..." and "lick up the dust of thy feet," making it possible for Judah to know that Jehovah is Lord. Literally, kings and queens of governments will bow down to Judah and honor her place in the world of governments. As well, the kings and queens of the covenant (those who have been to the temple and are covenant Israel) will honor Judah as they return to the house. We will bow down to her, not as an indication of her predominance, but as a sign of our willingness to have Judah join us. "Dust" is symbol for the earth out of which comes life (as in the case of Adam being created from the dust of the earth). As Judah returns to the God who gave her life, governments will recognize the life that is in her and want to partake thereof, to be a part of her great return. As well, those of Israel already joined to God through the covenant will be humbled by Judah's return to the covenant (this spiritual transformation comes later than the physical one) and will honor Judah for her decision. This honor given to Judah will be in the form of partaking of the new life Judah will bring into Israel. What a glorious moment it will be when Judah returns to the fold, both for her and for us! And what an honor it will be to welcome them as they enter the kingdom of God, never to leave it again.

Also note in verse 23 that this honor is given to Judah so that SHE will know that Jehovah (Jesus Christ) is the Lord. Our honor of Judah is a sign to them that we are his kingdom.

The last sentence "For they shall not be ashamed that wait for thee" has reference to both Judah and the "kings and queens" of Israel. Our faith in his plan of gathering will be vindicated in this event. It will truly be an occasion of joy and rejoicing in the blessings of the Lord upon his people who have waited patiently for this great day in which he will bring us all together.

21:24-26
Prey in this verse refers to Judah. It is a rhetorical question of confirmation. Yes, the prey (Judah) will be freed from the mighty, and those "lawful captives" will be delivered. In what way has Judah been lawfully captive? She broke Gods law and received the just reward, captivity among the nations in which Judah would become a hiss and byword, driven and persecuted.

The Lord emphasizes in 25 that the captive is delivered by Him for he contends with Judah's enemies. In 26 he tells them that if nations fight him by trying to stop his deliverance, both by governments and by the kings and queens of Israel, they will be destroyed by Him. In this thing, if we are not with Jehovah and his planned return of Judah, we are against him. Our dissent will be rewarded with a harsh punishment.

It is important to note here that the gathering of Judah most often seen in the scriptures, is a spiritual gathering, not a political one. However, In this and several other instances the Lord makes it clear that a political or physical gathering of sorts will take place prior to the spiritual gathering. Israel will not be returned to Judah by politics and governments. What we see happening in Israel today is only a small portion of what the scriptures talk about. Judah, or what will be left of her after future wars and hard persecutions, will return to Jehovah spiritually, then He will give Judah the long sought for inheritance she desires in the land of Israel. Only then will complete peace come to that place.

In addition, political peace may come to Judah and the present day land of Israel in bits and pieces prior to Judah turning to Ephraim and God for her more important spiritual blessings, but true peace--the peace only the Gospel and its knowledge can bring, will not come to that land through political means. Political nursing kings and queens will play a role in the deliverance of Judah, but it will be a preparatory and minor role, far less significant than that played by the nursing fathers and mothers of the gospel kingdom.
