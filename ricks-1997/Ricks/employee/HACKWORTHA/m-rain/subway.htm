Subway
audio
for D. A. Hanks
 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

 
Below Paris, deep within the ground,
Heavy trains roll and pound
Through machine-chewed worm holes.
 
Rocking, braking, rushing over iron rails,
Well-lit snakes carry
From station to station
The robust and frail.
 
Beautiful, alert passengers,
Crowding, early and late, take a break
To furtively glance at one another.
But that's where it ends.
Handsome men in dark suits read papers.
Then, after reaching their stop,
These know-where-I'm-going riders hop
Through automatic doors
And scurry out of sight.
 
Sitting with dignity and poise,
Braless women cross their bare, tan legs
And seem unaware of their magic.
 
Hauling backpacks, tourists galore,
Study maps and make sure
They are not as lost as before.
Such was the day our roundabout way
Led to Hugo's and Chopin's ancient crypt.
On the subway, drama filled our car
With marvelous yet regrettable isolation.
 
If, in the stony silence,
A few voices were heard,
Meanings soon blurred
With the clack of the wheels.
 
I yearned to know some of these people,
To hear a few stories only,
But a wall, as ancient as Hadrian's,
Kept me separated and lonely.
But then the unexpected thrilled me.
Pulling down a seat,
I plopped beside a Black woman,
And she treated me with her smile.
 
More oval than round,
The lady had a pretty face
And a place in the night for kindness.
 
On her lap wiggled a two-year-old darling.
This tot, not knowing about fear,
Reached her soft hands toward me.  
 
Her little fingers, with the power of a saint,
Melted effortlessly through that quaint,
Granite impediment
That makes earth's travelers strangers.
This innocent, chocolate cherub,
With dark eyes and a perfect complexion,
Wanted to sit on my lap.
 
So the child I touched and lifted lightly
As the mother happily handed me her gift.
 
Instantly loving the baby,
I wanted to kiss her soft cheek.  
But sensing the meek infant's tenderness,
And knowing only a fragile bond,
I fondly held her only to my lap.
 
Not knowing French,
I could not compliment the mother,
But I felt the vivid joy
Which comes when we uncover
The separation which afflicts us all.
