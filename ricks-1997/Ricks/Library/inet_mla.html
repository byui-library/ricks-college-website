Sample Internet Citations - MLA Style

 

Citing Articles Taken from ProQuest Direct

The article citations below reflect the suggested formats for citing articles taken from ProQuest Direct. They are based on guidelines in Section 4.9.2, page 161, of the MLA Handbook for Writers of Research Papers, 4th edition, 1995. To see the original source for this information, go to ProQuest Direct, click on "Help Guide", then "How do I...?", then "Citing articles taken from ProQuest Direct.

Generic Bibliographic Citation:
Author's Last Name, First Name. "Article Title." Title of Source Publication. Date of Source Publication, Edition: Volume. Issue. UMI-ProQuest Direct. Online. Date of Access. 
Specific Bibliographic Citation:
Dinan, Stephen. "Meteors to Provide Celestial Light Show." Washington Times. 11 Aug. 1997, 2nd ed.: C6. UMI-ProQuest Direct. Online. Sept. 1997. 
Generic Footnote Citation:
Author's First-Name Last-Name, "Article Title," Title of Source Publication, Date of Source Publication, Edition: Volume, Issue, UMI-ProQuest Direct, Online, Date of Access. 
Specific Footnote Citation:
Stephen Dinan, "Meteors to Provide Celestial Light Show," Washington Times, 11 Aug. 1997, 2nd ed.: C6, UMI-ProQuest Direct, Online, Sept. 1997.

Citing Information From Other Electronic Sources

World Wide Web Site

Basic Format
     Author.  "Title."  Internet address (Access date).
Example
     Godiva Chocolatier Inc. "Age Old Obsession:  A Brief History of
          Chocolate."  http://www.godiva.com/resources/history.html.
          (30 July 1996).

E-Mail Site

Basic Format
     Author or sender.  "Subject."  Recipient's e-mail address (Date
          of message.)
Example
     Day, Martha.  "Review of Film-Bad Lieutenant."  XLI@MOOSE.uvm.
          edu.    (30 July 1995.)

Listserv Site

Basic Format
      Author.  "Title or subject."  Listname@e-mail address.  
          (Access date).
Example
      Smith, Joseph.  "Daily Schedules."  LIBREF-L@Kent.edu  
          (10 May 1995).

Online Journal Site

Basic Format
     Author.  "Title of article."  Journal Title.  Volume issue 
          (year): paging or indicator of length.  Internet address  
          (Access date).
     (Note: underline Journal Title)
Example
     Inada, Kenneth.  "A Buddhist Response to the Nature of Human
          Rights."  Journal of Buddhist Ethics 2 (1995): 9 para.
          http://ww.cac.psu.edu/jbel/twocont.html   (21 June 1995).
     (Note: underline Journal of Buddhist Ethics)
Based on Walker, Janice R., MLA Style Citations of Electronic Sources, January, 1995 (Rev. 4/95)
