Why go to college?

Sooner or later college students should ask themselves why they are in school. Sooner is better than later. This question should be raised as often as the student feels the need, for the process of answering it again and again over time will reveal personal development and may lead to self knowledge. Some of you have already asked this question. For those who havent and for those who need a re-evaluation of their purpose in being schooled, we raise it again here.

The quote from Idries Shah, a 20th century Muslim thinker, reveals quite a bit about the process of being educated. The disciple in this quote has developed sufficient acumen to discern between knowledge and opinion. You will be exposed to vast amounts of opinion in college. You will receive it from every one of your teachers, no matter what academic discipline they advocate. This is proper. Those who have knowledge should encourage others to seek it by sharing their opinions. However, they cannot share their knowledge itself, for it is derived from their personal experience. One can relate ones experience, but cannot truly share it. For this reason, we are bound, as teachers, to share opinion and testimony. Testimony is the declaration of deep experience and conviction, which great teachers possess. Teachers hope that in the sharing of opinion and testimony they can motivate and inform the student to the point where the student moves beyond opinion, to experience, and then to knowledge. It is a process of transformation. It is the difference between an educated person and an opinionated one.

We now come to the second half of the quote from Idries Shah. If students knew what they needed to know and how to learn it there would be no need for a master, a guide, a teacher. As mentioned before, some can accomplish great personal development without teachers and formal education, the Joseph Smiths, Einsteins, DaVincis, Nephis, and the Buddhas of this world. They did not, however, become educated without paying a price: their discipline was the path to their knowledge. In order to facilitate this discipline, your teacher will often teach you what you do not want, in a way you do not want. In other words, teachers will often push you outside your comfort zone and then require you to gain new discipline in the hope that you will learn to seek knowledge.

The process of gaining knowledge is not osmotic. Because gaining knowledge involves gaining experience, the teacher attempts to give the student educational experiences. If mastery of facts alone were sufficient to gain knowledge, then it could easily be acquired in a library. What your college experience should give you should first solidify your grasp of factual information, then move into the domain of exploration and self-improvement. Some can gain this without a college experience, if they are determined and insightful, if they are honest self-critics. Many, however need a schoolmaster.

When we first approached the development of this course, we knew that we wanted to provide a healthy environment in which we could urge you out of your comfort zone and into a new discipline. This guide represents our effort to give a structured discipline to your study. This structure is hopefully solid yet dynamic. In anticipation of differing student interests and needs, we have created three different individual learning tracks: creative, critical and exploratory. Our goal is that, in some measure you become educated through this process, that is that you learn to seek knowledge even though this may not be what you want to learn or the way in which you want to learn it. We are confident, however, that when the Holy Spirit is invited into the classroom and into your personal study, whatever weaknesses remain in us and in this class structure will be mitigated and hopefully replaced with a more sure knowledge.

 

Why study at Ricks College?

Because Ricks is a two-year college, special opportunities are available to freshmen and sophomores that are not commonly available at four-year institutions. These opportunities include service in campus church callings commonly given upperclassmen, service in the student body association, and even being the concertmaster of the Symphony Orchestra. Dont forget that teachers at Ricks are oriented toward providing the best possible classroom experience. Only rarely can an underclassman command the attention of a PhD at four year schools and universities. But Ricks is committed to your needs in these and other areas. The Ricks College Mission Statement sets forth the institutional commitment to you. Ricks College is committed to:

  • Build testimonies of the restored gospel of Jesus Christ and encourage live its principles.
  • Provide a quality education for students of diverse interests and abilities.
  • Prepare students for further education and employment, and for their roles as citizens and parents.
  • Maintain a wholesome academic, cultural, social and spiritual environment.

Are you committed to taking full advantage of these opportunities? No one else can do it for you. You are in charge of your own education. We are here to help. Brigham Young reminds us that we are in charge of our destiny and responsible for our own education:

The catalogue of [a] mans discipline he must compile for himself: he [is] under the necessity of tracing it himself through every avenue of his life. He is obliged to catechize and train himself. Journal of Discourses 6:315

While at Ricks make a personal covenant to take charge of your education, to become a learner, a seeker of knowledge. We are here to help you, if you are committed to helping yourself.

Why study History?

God ... created all things ... both things to act and things to be acted upon.

-- II Nephi 2:14

... Truth is the knowledge of things as they are, as they were, and as they are to come.

-- D&C 93:24

Stand fast, therefore, in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free, and be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage.

-- Galatians 5:1

You will no doubt hear me say that history gives context to our lives. What does that mean? How can you come to understand the importance and significance of historical context?

An understanding of the Plan of Salvation is an indispensable key to understanding the need for context. Modern revelation informs us that we came from a pre-existential environment (a context for that level of existence) with experiences that have become an integral part of who and what we are. Through birth we entered into a new level of existence, and armed with agency we will gain new and additional experiences, which will modify who and what we are. We will again enter into other levels of existence, and will take with us our previous experiences.

Our agency gives us power to act. In order to exercise this power we must understand the choices before us and appreciate the consequences of one path versus another. Lehi informs us that if we do not understand these two aspects of exercising agency, we will be acted upon, rather than acting upon the choices available to us. Through the effort of our own intellect, coupled with a humble search for spiritual guidance, we can come to understand the significance and consequences of our choices. The Spirit and our intellect will guide us to knowledge of the truth. Acquiring knowledge of the truth involves acquiring knowledge of the past. As stated above, truth is, in part, knowledge of things as they were, that is, history. When knowledge of things past, is coupled with the scriptural knowledge of things past and future, we can understand the significance of the present -- the very moment in time when we make choices and exercise agency. Armed with knowledge of the truth, insofar as we are able to understand it at a given moment, we are liberated from the bondage of spiritual and intellectual ignorance. Thus, the liberty provided through Christ can make us free and responsible agents.

World Civilization provides several desirable opportunities to learn of the past. It begins in the dimmest past, inquiring about our origins. It attempts to discuss the major events and peoples of the entire globe, moving from prehistory to the recent past. It provides temporal depth and the broadest scope of subject matter. This view of history can become the core for your personal investigation of the past.

We have identified three phases of individual development as students of history: exploration or historical topics and methods, personal engagement in historical issues, and individual transformation as we grow in understanding. This study guide is designed to assist you in assessing that phase you may be in, and how to progress from one to another. Our hope is that you will experience what we have experienced through spiritual inquiry coupled with the study of history -- the liberty provided through Christ, and freedom from the bondage of ignorance. The quest for liberty is a lifelong one. More experiences await us all. View this course as another step along the path to liberty and the lifelong search for free agency.

 

A word about General Education

Joseph Smith taught us the need to broaden our horizons. in an age of increasingly narrow focus and specialization we may lose sight of the eternal benefit of becoming generally educated in a variety of areas. The Prophet Joseph said:

Thy mind, O man! If thou would lead a soul unto salvation, must stretch high as the utmost heavens, and search into and contemplate the darkest abyss, and the broad expanse of eternity.
Teachings of the Prophet Joseph Smith, 137.

That covers a lot of "territory." Academically speaking, the prophet seems to encourage us to study nearly every subject. Indeed, the soul you lead unto salvation through study and investigation may be your own.

Students have a treasure that many others covet. You have the luxury of time: time set aside to improve yourself through the study of a broad range of subjects. Many professors willingly sacrifice the potential for higher pay in order to gain time for self improvement. They understand the value of time for study and improvement.

Occasionally, I get the impression that students regard professors of general education courses as members of the "Borg Collective" who announce to helpless students: "resistance is futile; you will be assimilated." this is, of course, not the case. Your teachers want you to learn and grow. Refer again to the quote by Idries Shah. General education courses may teach you what you believe you do not want to learn, in a way you do not want. But they are designed to teach you how to seek knowledge for yourself, to take responsibility for, and to take charge of, your education. Thus, you are not "assimilated" by the "Borg," but are empowered to live a full life, enjoying the roles you may adopt in the future.

Have you considered your potential future roles: parent, citizen, church member, etc.? General education courses can help you prepare to serve in these capacities, to the best of your ability. For example, your children may one day ask for help doing homework in math, science, history, art, music, etc. Will you be able to help them? During an important election the most significant difference between two candidates may involve economic policy. Will you be able to cast an intelligent vote? You may be asked to teach a Sunday School course on Church History. Do you understand enough about historical method to feel truly comfortable fulfilling this assignment? General education courses can strengthen your ability to perform well in these situations. Only when you are strengthened can you give strength to others. Commit now to take advantage of the time given to you as a student to improve yourself, and to become "educated." Again, the soul you save may be your own.
