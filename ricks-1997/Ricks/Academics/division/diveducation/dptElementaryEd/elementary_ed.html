Department Mission Statement

We believe that students are best served by teachers that:

1. Possess and demonstrate comprehension, communication, and creativity.

2. Value and implement an integrated liberal arts curriculum.

3. Conceive and structure experiences that allow students to become self-directed learners.

4. Use and adapt technology as a tool to meet student needs and further self-directed learning.

5. Develop and maintain balance through self-renewal.

Matriculating within the Elementary Education Dept.

Currently Ricks College serves approximately 8,600 full time students. Over 900 of those students are interested in graduating from the Elementary Education Department. Because of limited resources it is necessary to govern entry into the department.

Initial Major

When incoming Freshmen apply to Ricks College they are asked to declare a major. Students interested in the Elementary Education Department must first enter as General Studies advised in Education. The major code to be entered on the application is 300.

Incoming freshmen may not enter as any other major within the Elementary Education program.

Matriculating

Freshmen must complete at least one semester at Ricks before being admitted to the Elementary Education Department. To matriculate, the student must:

  • have finished one semester of college course work,
  • have completed ElEd 150 (Introduction to Education) with a "B" or better,
  • have earned a cumulative 2.75 or higher grade point average for all college work, and
  • complete a matriculation form, obtain her or his advisor's signature, and submit the matriculation form, along with an unofficial transcript, to the Elementary Education Department secretary.

The matriculation form may be picked up in Smith 294c, the Elementary Education Department office, or viewed and printed via PDF here. The unofficial transcript may be obtained from the Registrars' office in the Administration building; there is no cost for an unofficial transcript.

Notification

Students who are accepted into the Elementary Education Department will be notified by phone or by mail. Students may also be notified by e-mail.

Following Through

Once a student has been admitted to a major within the Elementary Education Department, the student should make an appointment with her or his new advisor immediately. With the help of the advisor, the student should plan out the course work and the time frame in which the course work will be taken in order to graduate successfully.

It is the student's responsibility to complete all course work, and to track progress. It is the advisor's responsibility to assist the student by answering questions concerning which course work is most appropriate to facilitate a smooth transfer after Ricks to the university or college of the student's choice.

Ricks College Courses

ElEd 150 - Introduction to Education and Computer Technology

An exploration of the roles and challenges of the teaching profession. Activities include school visitations, professional meetings, current readings in education, and self evaluation. Basic skills invovling word processing, spreadsheet, database, and graphic applications as applied to teaching and classroom use are covered.

ElEd 202R - Professional Seminar

Discussion of current educational topics and trasfer questions to help keep education majors current in their field.

ElEd 205 - Education of Exceptional Children

See EdPsy 400 for course description; must be taken concurrently with CSE 400.

ElEd 240 - Children's Literature

See ElEd 340 for course description, must be taken concurrently with ElEd 340.

ElEd 290 - Special Topics

Independent projects as suggested or required by the Department and/or the instructor.

Music 270 - Music for the Elementary Classroom Teacher

See description under music 378. Concurrent enrollment with Music 378 required.

Related Courses

Ed 107 - Tutoring - Writing

Practical experience in tutoring. May be repeated for credit.

Ed 108 - Tutoring - General

Practical experience in tutoring. May be repeated for credit.

LS 111 - Use of Books and Libraries

Efficient use of library materials.

Brigham Young University Courses offered at Ricks

DA 326 - Rhythm and Dance

Methodology course for elementary education majors; creative dance and structured rhythmic dance forms.

ElEd 310R - Introduction to Learning and Teaching

Course consists of two major components: (1) teaching for learning, and (2) cognitive understanding of effective teaching and learning. Demonstrated competence in both components is required for admittance to the professional program.

ElEd 340 - Children's Literature

Trends and titles in children's and adolescent literature. Reading and critiquing as well as practical uses of children's literature in both school and home. Must be taken concurrently with ElEd 240.

ElEd 351 - Multicultural Education

Cultural issues related to public education that promote constructive interaction among people of differing economic, social, racial, ethnic, and religious backgrounds.

ElEd 493R - Independent Projects

Independent readings and / or projects within education.

EdLF 452 - Professional Teacher

The professional, historical, philosophical, sociological and financial aspects of education in the United States.

EdPsy 400 - Exceptional Child

Basic principles and legislative issues in effectively communicating and collaborating with professionals, parents, and other service providers in meeting the needs of individuals with disabilities in inclusive, educational environments. Must be taken concurrently with ElEd 205.

Eng 313 - Advanced Writing for Elementary Teachers

Improving writing proficiency by focusing on the writing process, collaborative writing, and techniques for teaching writing. Library research paper. Carries GE advanced writing credit.

FamSci315 - Language Development

Philosophical, social, intellectual, and emotional contexts of language acquisition and usage.

HLTH 361 - Health for Elementary Teachers

This course emphasizes the teacher's role as health educator; appraisal of typical elementary student health problems.

IPT 286 - Instructional Technology in Teaching

Using instructional design, visual design, and differentiated staffing principles along with multimedia authoring systems, telecommunication and other computer-based tools in the development of education applications.

IPT 301 - Learning

Basic principles of Educational Psychology applied to teaching including learning theory, instructional strategies, assessment of learning outcomes, and classroom inquiry.

Music 378

Establishing a classroom environment for musical development, emphasis on appropriate songs, activities, resources, and teaching strategies. Concurrent enrollment with Music 270 required.

PE 169 - Skill Acquisition and Analysis

Introduction and drill in basic skills.

ThF 372 - Drama in the Elementary Classroom

Creative dramatics/improvisation, puppetry, storytelling, reader's theater/choral speaking, theater production with children.

VAEdu 326 - Art for Elementary Teachers

Children's conceptual and expressive development in art. A balanced program emphasizing content from the art disciplines: (1) studio production, (2) art criticism, (3) art history, and (4) aesthetics.

Graduating with an A.A. degree in Elementary Education


All pertinent information regarding graduation requirements may be found in the Ricks College catalog. The information provided below is general in nature and may not pertain to every student in every circumstance. Refer to an advisor and the catalog for answers to questions that may affect you.

Students in Elementary Education must realize that not only do they have to meet Ricks' graduation criteria, but they must also be concerned with transfer institution and state teacher certification requirements. Fulfilling all of these programs can be a challenge, but it can be done successfully with careful planning, thorough reading of catalogs, and frequent meetings with an advisor. Occasionally, a phone call or e-mail contact to an advisor at the transfer institution may also be required. Don't be afraid to ask questions!

Finally, because transfer institutions differ in their requirements it is critical that you decide early-on as to your transfer school of choice. Then, develop a study plan to meet the transfer school's requirements while at Ricks College. Problems most frequently arise when you don't know where you want to go.
The requirements below represent the recommended program, which in some cases exceed the minimum program stated in the Ricks Catalog.

Credits needed: 64

GPA: 2.75

6 credits (One basic and one advanced composition class)

3 credits (Math 110 or higher, except Math 205 and 206)

6 credits (Two disciplines must be represented)

Arts and Letters

6 credits (1 class in each area and each student must complete FA 100. Students transferring to Idaho schools should take classes which are prefixed Art or Music, not Humanities)

Faculty and Staff of the Elementary Education Department

The Elementary Education Dept. at Ricks College was established in 1989. At that time only three faculty members constituted the department. Since 1989 the department has expanded to seven faculty members, a full-time secretary, and over 900 students. The current faculty and staff members include:

Joseph Alldredge, Ed.D.

Joseph Alldredge has taught at Ricks College for the past 19 years. His areas of expertise are Children's Literature and reading. Joe began working with youth as an executive with the Boy Scouts of America. His public school experience includes teaching third and fifth grades and serving as an elementary principal. Dr. Alldredge has degrees from BYU, Southwest Missouri State, and an Ed.D. From BYU. Joe and his wife have eight children.

Sharon Hansen, Ed.D.

Sharon Hansen taught for 13 years in the public schools, kindergarten through eighth grade. She has degrees from BYU, Utah State and the Univ.of Idaho. Currently she is involved with undergraduate students in early field experiences and is also working with accomplished teachers who are involved in the Natrional Board for Professional Teaching Standards (NBPTS) certification. Her hobbies include reading and sewing. Sharon married Steve Hansen in 1993; combined they have eight children .

Lynda Hawkes, M.Ed.

The 1998-99 school year is Lynda Hawkes' first full-time year after three years of part-time teaching at Ricks. Prior to that she worked for the Madison School District in Rexburg for seven years in grades K-5. Sister Hawkes and her husband, Jeff, have four children ranging in age from 13 to 22. As a family, they share many of the same interests including outdoor activities, canoeing, skiing and family! Lynda received her education at Ricks College, BYU and an M.Ed. from Idaho State University. She is a Cooperative Learning trainer and conducts training at the college level and in school districts. Her favorite joke to tell in her college classes is, "What is the difference between a fifth grader and a college kid?" When you tell a joke to a fifth grader, they laugh. When you tell the same joke to a college kid they say,"should I write that down, will that be on the next test?" She loves teaching at Ricks College. She loves the students and thinks that there is no better place in the world that she would rather work and serve.
