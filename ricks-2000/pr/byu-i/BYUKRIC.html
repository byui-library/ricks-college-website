Ricks Issues - BYU-Idaho Transition

KRIC Radio Report, August 2000

The following was prepared by Mark Bailey, KRIC Radio news director. It includes sound bites that you can click on and hear audio recordings:

On June 21 of this year an announcement made by Mormon Church President Gordon B. Hinckley changed the future of Ricks College. He said the Board of Trustees had decided to turn the nation’s largest privately owned junior college into a four-year institution and with it, a name change to BYU-Idaho. Since then, many people have been asking when will the transition begin.

Ricks College President David Bednar says a very rapid process must take place in making the transition, but at the same time an extensive one of “give and take and push and pull” must also be part of the change. That’s why nothing has been decided yet on academic programs that will be carried, deleted, or expanded. He says there are discussions taking place regarding which proposals might work and which ones might not . . .

“Out of that will come a series of guidelines for what we’re going to be and how we’re going to do it; that will assist us in process of deciding which programs ought to be. We haven’t finalized those guidelines. Therefore, there are no decisions about what specific programs when.”
[Click here to listen]

President Bednar says the college won’t be stampeded into arbitrary deadlines because the administration wants to do this transition right, and it will take time to do. The deans of the colleges on campus, as well as the academic council, have been meeting . . . laying out proposals, but nothing has been approved.

Academic Vice President Don Bird says discussion is still going on as to which programs will go four year. But once that’s done it will have to be decided when to start which programs. He says there will be a staggered phase in with certain academic programs beginning at different semesters or even years . . .

 “We’re basically taking another two-year college and setting it on top of an existing college. With minimum facilities and so on there is going to have to be some adjustment there. You just don’t plan a building overnight and get it built overnight. Those take two to three years when you’re lucky. Some of these programs can’t start until we have facilities and offices and those types of things. All of that timetable has to be worked out with what we have presently as resources, offices and those types of things, and what we have for needs in the future. All of that stuff, once we decide, then we have to put the thing into action. And it’s still going to take quite a bit of time to make that all happen.” [Click here to listen]

Ricks College is working to become accredited as a university by preparing a strategic plan on how the college will make the transition. This takes time, including visits from the accreditation team. Right now, Ricks has applied for candidacy status as a university. Formal accreditation will come down the road. President Bednar says it’s hard to say exactly when everything will fall into place . . .

“Until we’ve gone through this process of give and take and push and pull, and assess the resources and look at the space – I’m not willing to say this is exactly when it really starts. It is conceivable that some programs could be ready for the fall of 2001 or in January of 2002. And at this point to say “Yeah that’s when it’s going to happen,” that’s the part I’m backing off of. I just am not willing to say when it will start. But as we go through the process this fall those timetables will be a part of what we’re nailing down.” [Click here to listen]

President Bednar says the school’s Board of Trustees and the Northwest Accreditation Committee have not set any timetables as to when the transition to BYU-Idaho will begin. The Executive Committee will meet with college officials in September, but final decisions won’t even be made then. Bednar says some proposals might come out of that meeting that will help resolve some unanswered questions at this time.
