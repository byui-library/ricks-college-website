A Wild House Cat
by Adam Hess
 
When I was 14, I went trapping with a friend,  Matt Bailey. We built several traps to capture a raccoon in the river bottoms near the mouth of Sardine Canyon near Brigham City, Utah.  In the traps we used canned fish for bait.

The next day, when we checked the traps, one had been set off, but nothing was in it. Upon inspection, we found a raccoon had clawed under the door, lifted it, and escaped. The second trap had also been set off, but there was something in it. We approached slowly, and to our surprise, when the animal in the trap saw us it lept to the side of the cage at us. To our surprise, it was a large, wild, house cat. We backed up and watched it for several minutes.

It was obvious this cat wanted nothing to do with humans- except maybe to eat them. We marveled at how wild and aggressive the little animal was. Then another thought came to our minds: what were we to do with it?

We were young and consequently carried no guns.  Then again, it would have been a shame to kill such a courageous animal. We wanted to let it go, but neither of us volunteered for the job. We decide that despite its small size, it would be dangerous to open the door of the cage from arms  length.

We finally fastened a piece of rope to the door, the cat hissing and swatting all the   time. Then we looped the rope over an overhanging branch and backed up to the end of the rope. As we pulled the door open the cat ran off with lightning speed.

Later, as I learned about mountain lions, that cat came to my mind time after time. I am grateful that it wasn’t any bigger than it was. I can only imagine the strength and instincts that that run through the veins of its larger cousin, the cougar.
