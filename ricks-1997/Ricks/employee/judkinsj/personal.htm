How I came to Ricks

Jacob Spori Building

Although this story may not seem extraordinary to anyone but myself, it is miraculous to me to be back at Ricks College.  I vividly remember the day when I, as a freshman student, sat in my dorm, carefully and prayerfully reading course requirements for each major from the Ricks College Catalogue.  I remember thinking that I was interested in too many different things.  When  I read the requirements for the Communicative Disorders major, a calm excitement came over me.  The required courses seemed to pull together the wide range of my academic interests.  The spirit confirmed that pursuing a major in Communicative Disorders would be a good direction for me. 

I met with Brother David Hillier, the advisor to Communicative Disorders majors,  to find out more about the program.  He gently challenged my decision, but then provided the support and vision I needed.  A year later he hired me as a student aid to help him administer hearing screenings and teach a phonetics lab.  Reflecting on that time, I realize I was not a polished assistant or a perfect student, but Brother Hillier always gently instructed and encouraged me.  He had a wonderful way about him.  The more I watched him as he worked, and interacted with people, the more I came to admire him.  He loved what he was doing.  I knew it must be an incredible blessing to work at Ricks College.  One day, near the end of my sophomore year, Brother Hillier asked the students in one of his classes where they wanted to be in 10 years.  I knew exactly what to say when it was my turn.  "I want your job," I said.  And I did, more than anything, want to take Brother Hillier's place.  But everyone in the class just laughed, and so did I.

Besides Brother Hillier, there were many other teachers and leaders at Ricks who had a significant impact on me.  I watched and admired the way they lived and taught with the spirit.  They were powerful, spiritual giants, heroes, whose words and deeds went straight into my heart and strengthened me.

After graduation, I transferred to Idaho State University, where I married an audiology student and earned my degree in speech-language pathology.  I then worked for three years at Magic Valley Speech & Language Services, a private practice in Twin Falls, Idaho.   Thoughts of working at Ricks College had nearly disappeared, until one day in January of 1996 my wife called me at work to tell me that Brother Hillier had called.  He had told her he was retiring, and wanted to find out if I still wanted his job.  A longing but peaceful excitement came over me.  My wife, who once told me she never wanted to live in Rexburg, felt it too.  I did want his job.  He told me that it wasn't up to him to decide who was hired, but that he wanted me to know the job would be available if I wanted to apply.   Despite my excitement, however, I felt that with no teaching experience and only three years in the field, I didn't have a chance, not to mention that I could never fill Brother Hillier's shoes.  But I simply couldn't pass up the opportunity, and knew that even if I wasn't selected, I had to apply.

Throughout the application and interview process, I had some wonderful experiences that were both humbling and gratifying.  I have been a Communication Department faculty since the Fall of 1996, and I thoroughly enjoy it.  I relish the opportunity to touch people's lives and, in a small way, contribute to building the kingdom of God.  I am honored and grateful  to be a part of this institution, and know I have a lot of work to do to be worthy of this blessing.
