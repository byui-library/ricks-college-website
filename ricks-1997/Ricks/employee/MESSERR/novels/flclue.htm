Clues

1. When the Princess and Zinaida visited Pyotr Vassilitch' home the first time "She followed him with her eyes."


2. When they visited the second time, the father "Now and then glanced at her, and from time to time she looked at him -- but so strangely, almost with hostility."

 

3. "After breakfast, my father put his arm through mine and, taking me into the garden, made me give him a full account of all I had seen at the Zasyekins."

 

4. The father "disappeared behind the gate. I saw his hat moving along the hedge: he went into the Zasyekins' house." Hew did not stay there more than an hour. Then he went straight off to the town and stayed away till evening." ... "The door into the next room opened slightly. Through the gap Zinaida's face appeared -- pale, pensive, her hair carelessly thrown back. She looked at me (Woldemar) with large, cold eyes and softly closed the door."

 

5. "What induces you to receive Monsieur Malevsky?' I once asked her.

"Ah, but he has such beautiful little moustaches," she replied. "And anyway that is not your province."

"Perhaps you think that I love him?" she said to me on another occasion. "No! I cannot love people whom I find that I look down on. I need someone who would himself master me, but then, goodness, me, I shall never come across anyone like that. I will never fall into anybody's clutches, never, never."

 

6. "In our house, she became transformed into a young lady, a princess, and this made me shy of her. I was frightened of giving myself away to my mother. She did not think at all well of Zinaida, and watched us with disapproval. I was not so nervous of my father. He behaved as if he did not notice me, and did not say much to her. But when he did say seemed somehow specially wise and significant."

 

7. "One day (Zinaida said to Woldemar) you will discover...only don't be angry with me" ... I was jealous of her before, but only at that instant did the though that she was in love flash through my mind."... A change had come over her, that was evident. She began to go for long, solitary walks. Sometimes she refused to see her visitors. For hours she sat alone in her room."

 

8. She asks Maidenov to describe a very romantic scene (page 62) "She is in love," my lips whispered involuntarily, "but with whom."

 

9. The continued fights between Vladimir's father and mother.

 

10. She asks Vladimir to jump from a fence 14ft high. (Pg 67) And she also pulls out a swatch of his hair, though each time she is sorry and declares her "love" for the boy. It is actually the father she is trying to rule, but cannot.

 

11. First Big clue: "Ah, that's my own affair, Sir Beast. In that case I will ask Pyotr Vassilievich..." (My father's name was Pyotr Vassilievich. I was astonished by her light, easy way of using his name -- as if she were very certain of his readiness to do her a service. "I see," retorted Byelovzorov, "it's him you mean to go riding with?"

 

12. Second Big Clue: I saw my father and Zinaida. They were riding side by side....The noble animal tossed its head, pranced, snorted, while the rider at the same time held it back and spurred it on. I moved to once side, out of their way. My father gathered up the reins, and leant back away from Zinaida; she slowly lifted her eyes towards him, and they galloped off.

 

13. Third Big Clue: "Let us be friends -- that's what." Zinaida gave me the rose to smell. "Listen, I am, after all, much older than you, I really might be your aunt -- oh, well, perhaps not aunt, but elder sister. And you..."

 

14. Fourth Big Clue: "But there, by the fountain, by the plashing water, he whose slave I am awaits me. He wears neither gorgeous raiment nor precious stones, no one knows him, but he awaits me, sure that I shall come -- and I shall come -- and there is no power in the world that can stop me when I want to go to him, to be with him, to lose myself with him there in the darkness of the garden, with the rustling of the trees and the murmur of the fountain..." Zinaida was silent.

"And is this -- fiction?" Malevsky asked craftily.

Zinaida did not even look at him.

 

15. The boy's first suspicion (page 88)

I started...I though I heard the creak of a door opening, then the faint sound of a snapping twig... In two leaps I got down from the ruin...I stood frozen to the spot. There was a sound -- quite distinct -- of footsteps, rapid, light, but cautious, in the garden...They were coming towards me..."here he is...her he is at last," raced through my heart. Convulsively I whipped the knife out of my pocket and frantically I forced it open. Queer red spots danced before my yes, and my hair stood on end in an agony of fury and terror -- the footsteps were coming straight towards me. I stooped and crouched forward to meet them--a man appeared--...it was my father!"

...Jealous Othello, ready for murder, was suddenly transformed into a schoolboy...

All at once I was quit sober again; on my way back to our house I did, however, go up to my seat under the elder tree and glance up at the window of Zinaida's bedroom. ...Beyond...I saw...a whitish blind pulled down cautiously and gently to the window sill, and it stayed down, like that, quite still....

"What's all this?" I said aloud...I did not dare let myself dwell on them....

 

16. After the boy finds out:

"I did not wonder how it was that I had not guessed it earlier--guessed it long ago. I did not even harbor bitter thoughts about my father...what I had learned was too much for me to manage. The sudden revelation crushed me;; all was ended. "
