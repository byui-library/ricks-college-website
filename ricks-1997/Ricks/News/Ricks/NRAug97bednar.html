Ricks College News Release, August 6, 1997

New president likes what he sees,
wants to preserve `spirit of Ricks'

REXBURG, Idaho--While David Bednar had never stepped foot on campus until May 27 when his appointment as president was announced, the new college president says he quickly felt the "spirit of Ricks."

He now says one of his main goals will be to preserve and enhance the "spirit of Ricks" as the college experiences growth in its enrollment. While the physical facilities won't be expanding, enrollment that has hovered between 7,500 and 8,000 students in recent years is expected to climb to about 8,250 this year.
Bednar says part of the uniqueness of the Ricks spirit has been the result of its size, the small classes and the closeness that students feel with faculty members, fellow students and their religious faith.

Having never been to Ricks, he was particularly impressed by the physical facilities when he arrived for the announcement of his appointment.
"It's even better than I expected," he says. "I frankly was surprised to see how impressive the facilities are. The size and quality of the facilities are extraordinary, particularly for a junior college."

While he was initially impressed with the physical beauty of the campus, he's even more impressed by the faculty and students. "The dedication and loyalty of the faculty are extraordinary the people are even more impressive than the place."

Bednar says Ricks is unique among junior colleges, many of which are much more vocationally oriented. "This really isn't a junior college in the traditional sense," he says. "For many of our students, this is the first two years of a four-year college experience."

At age 45, Bednar says he never aspired to become a college president because there's "too much politics" at most secular institutions. "I loved teaching," says the former professor of business management at the University of Arkansas.

"The best job in the world is to be a full professor," he says. "But I left the best job in the world for an even better job."

He didn't come up through the usual ranks as most college presidents do and the appointment was unexpected. It was his work in the LDS Church that brought him to the attention of church officials who made the appointment. The college professor had served twice as a stake president, then as a regional representative and most recently as an Area Authority Seventy in the North America Southwest Area that spans from Arkansas to southeastern California.

"It's an incredible opportunity," says Bednar, who will serve as both president of Ricks and an Area Authority Seventy in the Fifth Quorum of the Seventy.
Bednar says, "We have four vice presidents who are extraordinarily competent and capable." While the president often gets a lot of credit, he says there are lots of people who do the work. His main goal is to help set policy and direction.

"One thing I will be careful not to do is to impose David Bednar's footprint at Ricks College," he says. "The mission of this college goes way beyond one individual."

Bednar became interested in business management and organizational behavior following his church mission in Germany during the early 1970s where he experienced leadership and organizational challenges. Besides teaching, in recent years he also ran a private consulting business where he worked with such corporations as Wal-Mart, which is headquartered in Arkansas.

He believes his mission is clear. "What we do at Ricks we do very well. My responsibility is to help us do even better that which we now do very well. I certainly will not try to change the college in a radical way."

Given the nature of the sponsoring institution, the main goal will be spiritual in nature. "We have the responsibility to be builders of the kingdom in the entire world," he says. "We can have an impact in various states and countries it can be a huge influence in the rolling forth of the kingdom of God."

In recent weeks he's spent some time reading about the college's 110-year history. "It's a remarkable history. There were times when it didn't seem there was much of a chance to survive," he says. "It's grown out of the blood, sweat and tears of some terrific people."

He was particularly interested in the troubled years when it looked like the college would be moved to Idaho Falls. His wife's mother, a Clement from Lewisville, is a niece of the late Delbert Taylor, who was serving as the president of the Rexburg Stake during this controversial period. "It hit home because my wife's family was very intimately involved in stating that Ricks College belonged in Rexburg," he says.

He's also been meeting people on campus and in the community. He's visiting work areas and holding early morning meetings with small groups of students, staff and faculty. He's even sat in on a few classes.

He says his family was greeted with open arms when t hey moved into the president's home in mid July. The Bednars have three sons. Jeffrey will be a sophomore at Madison High School; Eric, who recently returned from the Finland Helsinki Mission, will be a sophomore at Brigham Young University in the fall; and Michael is in the LDS Church's Missionary Training Center and headed for a two-year mission in Bolivia.

Bednar was raised in San Leandro, Calif., near San Francisco. He met his wife, Susan, in a student ward at BYU following his two-year church mission.

Although thinking about becoming a lawyer, he discovered a love for teaching when in the mission field and afterward when asked to assist a professor in teaching a communications class. "I was absolutely smitten," he says. "I loved it . . . my love of teaching was cultivated in the mission field and then when I had the opportunity to teach in a college setting and experience that lifestyle, I said, `This was the way to go.' "

He earned a master's degree in organizational communication at BYU in 1977 and completed his doctorate at Purdue in 1980. Since 1980, he was on the business school faculty at the University of Arkansas except for 1984-86 when he taught at Texas Tech University. He recently has been teaching courses in organizational behavior, team management and managerial communication.
