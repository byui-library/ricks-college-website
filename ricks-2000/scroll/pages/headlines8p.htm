IF STUDENTS FALTER, ALL IS NOT LOST

by Timothy Robison
scroll staff

During a quest for perfection, if students falter all is not lost, LaMar Barrus, a retired Ricks College faculty member said during devotional Tuesday.

Barrus related striving for perfection to Sir Galahad, of King Arthur's court. Sir Galahad was commanded to devote his life to searching for the Holy Grail. During his quest he was required to be pure in all aspects of his life.

"Sir Galahad kept his armor polished to reflect the true light of Christ," said Barrus.

Barrus related how during his childhood, he reasoned that all he had to do to obtain eternal salvation was to live a perfect life.

"I remember the horror of committing my first sin knowingly," Barrus said. "I saw my grail fall to the ground in pieces."

To obtain an eternal goal students must be diligent their entire life.

"The quest for our personal holy grail is a life long struggle," he said.

He reminded students that without Christ's atonement, they would be unable to achieve eternal salvation.

"It is only through Jesus Christ and his atoning sacrifice that we can obtain our everlasting goal," he said.

Barrus quoted from Leonard Bernstein's theater piece "Mass." Bernstein's work tells the story of a man and his struggles between choosing between right and wrong, and the opposition he faces. Barrus related the character's experiences to the opposition students face and how easily they might find themselves in sin.

"How easily things get broken," he quoted from Bernstein's work.

Barrus instructed students to strive towards repentance, no matter how daunting the task may seem.

"We should piece together the broken pieces of our grail," he said.

A young woman came to Barrus during his service as a bishop in a Ricks College Ward seeking to lift the burdens of sin she had been carrying. She wanted to be relieved of the guilt she felt, but did not immediately have the willingness to do any more than confess her sins.

"She did not have an understanding of the atonement," he said.

It was not until she had an understanding that she felt decided to change her life.

"She restored her Holy Grail of purity," Barrus said.

Sharon Tuckett, Ricks College Director of Housing, will speak at the next devotional July 31 in the Taylor Chapel.
