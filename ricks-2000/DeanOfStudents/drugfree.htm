GENERAL STATEMENT

Ricks College is affiliated with The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints and is deeply committed to high standards of moral conduct and personal living as reflected by its Mission Statement. This personal commitment is rooted in Board of Trustees policy which requires that every student, faculty, staff, and administrator sign a commitment to the Honor Code prior to admission or employment and to update the commitment on an annual basis in order to maintain their eligibility to continue at the institution in their employment or student status. Each member of the campus community is expected to abide by their commitment at all times and in all places and to work together in an effort to lift one another and encourage obedience and compliance with the provisions of their commitment.

RICKS COLLEGE DRUG POLICY

The campus community embraces the scriptural injunction which says that "the worth of souls is great in the sight of God." The Honor Code commitment specifies that every employee or student will "abstain from [the use of] alcoholic beverages, coffee, tea, tobacco, and substance abuse." This principle has root in the Word of Wisdom which is God's law of health provided to man in the latter days to promote a healthy life style by shunning those things which are addictive, habit forming, mind altering or harmful to the body. Each year a few members of the campus community are terminated from employment or dismissed from continued enrollment as a student because of inappropriate use of alcohol or other substance abuse. These terminations or dismissals are unfortunate for both the individual involved, their family, and the campus community as a whole as this signifies a failure in the teaching or obedience processes to the underlying principles which the institution espouses as being highly significant. Because of the serious nature and consequence of inappropriate substance involvement, it is important that all members of the college community understand the drug and alcohol policy.

It is the policy of the Board of Trustees of Ricks College that as a condition of employment or enrollment all employees and students, regardless of the length of a student's program of study, completely abstain, whether on or off campus, from the possession, use, or distribution of any illegal drug, or alcohol and to also abstain from the use of any controlled legal substance, without specific medical authorization, which may be harmful to the body. The college interprets the Board of Trustees' directive as including (1) the use of illegal substances such as marijuana, methamphetamine, cocaine, etc., (2) the intentional misuse of prescription medications, (3) the possession of illegal drugs or drug paraphernalia for use or distribution, and (4) the involvement or association with other persons in substance or alcohol use without taking deterrent action.

The college's Board of Trustee's has also directed that involvement with illegal drugs will result in immediate termination of employment or dismissal from the college on the first offense except in those circumstances where the only contact with the drug was limited and where the employee or student has repented before disciplinary action has begun. When it is determined that an individuals contact with the abuse of a substance was brief or casual and where the exposure to the behavior has been decisively abandoned prior to disciplinary action being instituted, then the individual may be allowed to remain employed or enrolled at the institution on a strict probation. The repentance provision is interpreted as requiring confession to an appropriate ecclesiastical authority and/or requesting assistance from the Ricks College Drug Abuse Prevention Office prior to public discovery, knowledge, arrest, or initial action being taken by the college.

The administration recognizes there are differences between the intentional misuse of illegal and prescription drugs and the unintentional misuse of prescription drugs prescribed for medical reasons. Those individuals involved in the unintentional misuse of prescription drugs will not normally be subject to disciplinary sanctions, but rather are encouraged to seek assistance from the Drug Abuse Prevention Office, the Counseling Center, private counseling or therapy, or rehabilitation through a licensed approved program.

There are substantial legal sanctions pursuant to local, state or federal law which may be levied against an individual for the unlawful use, possession, distribution or manufacturing of an illegal drug or controlled substance. Likewise criminal law prescribes penalties for a minor who possesses or uses alcohol and defines substantial penalties for adults who dispose or provide alcohol for use by minors. The law often treats drug offenses as a criminal matter punishable by substantial fines, imprisonment, or other severe sanctions. The Dean of Students Office or the Personnel Office may make their own respective investigation of any student or employee who is reported or found to be in violation of this policy and take appropriate disciplinary action regardless of any criminal court action. A description of the applicable legal penalties under local, state or federal law for the unlawful possession or distribution of illegal drugs and alcohol is provided annually to each student and employee through distribution via the campus e-mail system. In addition, paper copies are available for those who do not have e-mail accounts or computer access or for any individual desiring or requesting a copy of the information.

The health risks associated with the inappropriate use of drugs include, but are not limited to, physical and psychological addiction, physical, psychological and spiritual deterioration, disease and death. A description of the applicable health risks associated with the use of illegal drugs and the abuse of alcohol is provided annually to each student and employee in the same manner as described above.

The college supports participation in programs for the prevention of the inappropriate use of drugs, controlled substances and tobacco or alcohol abuse. The Ricks College Drug Abuse Prevention Office can provide confidential assistance with drug and alcohol abuse problems. Assistance is available in the form of educational training programs, health information, and preliminary evaluation and counseling for possible referral for outside medical assistance. Individuals may get more information about, or enter the college program through the Drug Abuse Prevention Office in Kimball 230 or by calling 356-2296.

By providing this explanation of board policy, the college hopes to encourage elimination of all forms of substance abuse, to help those who currently may be involved to stop such use and get appropriate help, and to assist in stopping the spread of this behavior at our institution and in society.

EVALUATION OF CURRENT PROGRAMS

All college employees and specifically student personnel professionals provide input in campus programs directed at alcohol and other substance abuse. A wide range of expertise can be found on the campus in health care professionals, health care educators, licensed counselors, police personnel, students, student life professional personnel, and ecclesiastical leaders. The college has chosen to make use of many of these professionals and volunteers in planning, implementing, evaluating, and revising its Drug-Free School Policy. Student input is sought and through the principle of shadow leadership students plan and implement programs directed at encouraging the campus community as a whole in abstinence from the use of alcohol or drugs and advocating commitment and adherence to a higher standard of personal conduct through a strong personal commitment to and compliance with the Board of Trustees Drug Policy.

Student involvement in these programs reaches into the local community in the form of alcohol and other drug prevention programs in conjunction with elementary and secondary school programs and other local programs. This interaction strengthens the position of the institutional policy with the local community and prepares potential students by helping them to understand institutional expectations prior to enrollment.

The data and charts in the accompanying material show a summary of drug and alcohol contacts reported to or by college administrators in each area specified during the review period. Where possible, comparative figures are provided in the Comparative Assessment section in an effort to assist student personnel administrators in evaluating successes or weaknesses in programs. After consultation with the college attorney, it was determined to change the period of the Biennial Report of Institutional Compliance from October of even number years to the end of the calendar year of even numbered years. This coincides with accepted statistical reporting periods for law enforcement agencies and institutional units working with students. This report would covers the period of October 1998 through December 2000.

Athletics

The Ricks College Athletic Director has direct responsibility to oversee all coaches, staff, and trainers involved in intercollegiate athletics. Student athletes involved in the use of drugs will not be permitted to participate in sports programs and will be asked to leave the institution as would any other student. Coaches, staff, and trainers as a part of their team rules and guidelines discuss the use of illicit drugs which are prohibited under NJCAA rules and regulations and advise athletes about the potential losses resulting from their involvement in drugs or alcohol. Discussions included both college policy, team rules, and NJCAA rules. Athletes are counseled about the life long affects of drug use on their bodies and emotional well being. Coaches work with the dean of students office or the drug prevention office in efforts to assist student athletes seeking help with drug or alcohol problems.

Campus Police

The Campus Division of the Rexburg City Police Department are paid college employees who are sworn City Police officers and report to the Rexburg City Chief of Police. As sworn city police officers, they are responsible to enforce city, county, and state laws on the campus. While functionally responsible to the Rexburg City Chief of Police, the Director of the Campus Division reports to the Student Life vice president and is also a member of the Student Life Council. Their police authority permits them to work within the City of Rexburg and allows for coordination of police efforts for the benefit of both the city and campus divisions.

During the course of a year, arrests for alcohol and/or other drug use or possession violations may be made by officers of the campus division of individuals who are not affiliated or associated with the campus or who are not participating in any campus function. These persons generally are drivers or pedestrians passing directly through the campus or driving on the periphery of campus and found to be in violation of an alcohol or other drug use/possession law. For comparative purposes the statistics shown reflect all arrests made on the campus for drug or alcohol use or misuse as well as the total number of arrests within the City of Rexburg for drug or alcohol use or misuse. Individuals arrested on campus who are not affiliated with the campus are not included in the statistics but are included, as are all campus arrests, in the total arrests made within the city.

Counseling Center

One of the goals of the Ricks College Counseling Center is to help students maintain balance in their lives by identifying and resolving emotional problems which interfere with effective spiritual, intellectual, social, or physical well-being. The majority of students who request substance abuse counseling are not currently using but may have used alcohol or other drugs in the past.

The Counseling Center is well staffed and professionally qualified in dealing with the treatment of alcohol and other drug problems or addictions. Students generally seek counseling on their own, or are referred to the counseling center by a member of the campus community, or peers may seek assistance for a roommate or friend. Student personnel professionals such as housing administrators, members of the Dean of Students staff, health care professionals, health care educators and other college staff, faculty, and administrative employees refer students to the counseling center for assistance with these types of problems.

The unique affiliation of Ricks College provides each student with an ecclesiastical leader who oversees approximately 170-200 students within a ward (church unit) established for students. These 57 bishoprics (three men) are members of the local communities and donate their time to assist and work with these students. These individuals are very helpful in referring students who need assistance in resolving alcohol or other drug related problems.

Dean of Students

The Dean of Students Office provides a number of services to students in addition to being responsible for disciplinary sanctions for violation of a students personal commitment to the Honor Code. Prior to enrolling at Ricks, each prospective student is interviewed and endorsed by his/her ecclesiastical leader before being admitted to Ricks College and at that time signs a personal commitment to abstain from alcohol or other drug use during the period or terms of his/her enrollment. This commitment includes obedience to the Honor Code adopted by the Board of Trustees. To bolster their commitment, students are provided a copy of the "R-Book" which contains the Honor Code and all the rules and regulations promulgated by the college. The "R-Book" also contains a listing of possible disciplinary sanctions which may be implemented for violation of their commitment to the expected standard including a violation of the alcohol and drug policy.

In addition, each new incoming or former student is mailed informational material outlining the position of the college in regard to alcohol and other drug use while the student is enrolled at Ricks. Just prior to arriving on campus, pre-orientation materials are provided which outline the basic rules and regulations expected of students enrolled at Ricks or living in college approved housing. Shortly after the materials are mailed to the student, a letter is sent to the student's parents or guardian asking them to sit with their son/daughter and review the information so that both the student and the parent will have an understanding of the rules and expectations as well as possible disciplinary action that can be taken in the event the rules are violated. On arrival at Ricks, a new student orientation is held prior to the start of every term or semester and students receive direction in regard to institutional behavioral expectations, possible disciplinary action, and helps available.

The dean of students office investigates all reports of substance use or abuse and interviews those found to be involved to determine the extent of involvement. At times students enter into a "secret combination" in an effort to thwart the investigation as a drug case is reported and investigated. A collective denial by students offering "similar stories" makes efforts to take action regarding alcohol or other drug use difficult. Peers may be intimidated by offending students to the point they either recant their original statement or refuse to make any open statement in a disciplinary hearing of what they saw or heard. In any given academic year, there are abundant rumors about "high profile students" using alcohol or illegal substances without any action being taken by the Dean of Students Office. In depth reviews or investigations are made with reported users usually making adamant denials of involvement. In some cases peers speak out against the denials by those involved or sometimes participants may, after leaving Ricks, admit to the violation thus substantiating the original allegations. In cases of this nature, either the CES institution where the student transferred to is notified or the responsible ecclesiastical leader is contacted and provided the information permitting them to follow up on the matter in an effort to help the student. Statistics are not gathered in these cases as there is generally no follow through to find the action taken by ecclesiastical leaders or other institutions.

Towards the end of the Winter 2000 semester a random survey of 400 currently registered students was made in regards to alcohol and drugs and student awareness of the campus rules, civil laws, and penalties as well as student use or awareness of substance abuse problems. (See attached copy of the survey and the response summary.) Of the 400 students surveyed, 190 responded to the survey. The responses generally indicate that about 82% of respondents were aware of the College rules and regulations regarding alcohol and drugs with 96% being aware of the health risks involved in their use. The survey reflected that 43% of the students were not aware that conviction for substance abuse could affect their federal financial aid while only 30% were aware of counseling, treatment, or rehabilitation programs available to students through campus departments. Use of drugs by those surveyed showed that approximately 98% of respondents were not involved in drug use during the last year while 5% indicated that they had witnessed marijuana being used while at Ricks College. Overall the survey provided positive statistical support to the institutional philosophy and attitude in regards to the drug policy.

After appropriate investigation and review of each report, action was taken commensurate with the violation and the facts surrounding the incident. Drug Prevention Office

The Drug Prevention Office is a resource center for employees and students to seek help without fear of disciplinary action by the college. The office serves as a facilitation center for individuals to freely discuss personal concerns about their alcohol or drug problem and to seek help and assistance in overcoming those problems. Since the beginning of the Drug-Free Schools and Campuses Act, Ricks College has determined to include the "Drug-Free School Policy" statement with the "Ricks College Campus Security Report." Since that time the policy is included as a part of that report and is distributed to each student at the start of a new semester by e-mailing the information to the students campus e-mail address. The report is also sent by e-mail to all employees with a notation indicating that a paper copy may be obtained by contacting the dean of students office. Employees who do not have an e-mail address are provided a paper copy of the report which is distributed through the campus mail system directly to each employee. The overall budgeting, preparation, printing, and distribution of the report is coordinated by the dean of students office. Copies are available to the public on request or to potential new students or employees and is also posted on the college web site under the dean of students home page. The web site address is www.byui.edu. The user would then access Student Life and then the Dean of Students.

A phone line is maintained on campus for the sole purpose and use of any student, staff, faculty, or administrator who desires to seek help with personal problems relating to the possession, use or distribution of alcohol or other drugs. This line is advertised in the phone directory, "R-Book," and other printed materials provided to students and is answered in the Dean of Students Office and directed immediately to the drug prevention officer.

Ricks Against Alcohol and Drugs (RAAD)

RAAD is a student advisory organization, which provides alcohol and other drug use prevention education services on campus. This group coordinates its efforts with local community prevention programs. Membership in RAAD consists of Ricks College students who volunteer their time and services to assist in promoting prevention activities on campus and in the community.

Over the last year and a half (1999-2000), the RAAD Club has continued the fight against and prevention of drugs and alcohol usage at Ricks using several methods. RAAD used Red Ribbon week as an opportunity to inform the student body about alcohol and drug problems and also to introduce the HOPE Program as a resource for students. To Educate RAAD members, a demonstration was done by local police officers to explain drug-dog techniques and facts about the enforcement of substance abuse laws. The club's main strength has been the utilization of an anti-drug/alcohol campaign throughout many school activities, including the Homecoming Parade, the Christmas Tree Lane contest, and the Halloween Carnival using striking posters and demonstrations. (Monica Sheffield, RAAD President)

During the period 1998 through 2000, RAAD has been involved in the following activities:

Red Ribbon Week: Red Ribbon Week is held during the month of October and is nationally recognized as Drug Prevention Week. RAAD members pinned ribbons on nearly 3,000 students, faculty and administrators. They also conducted a survey regarding the number of times a student has consumed alcohol or other drugs and how many times they had seen another student consume alcohol or other drugs during the school year.

High School Red Ribbon Week: RAAD members went into the local high school and displayed numerous posters to educate the high school students and pinned 500 red ribbons on students, faculty and administrators. The high school students were very receptive and RAAD has been asked to return.

"Dare" Graduation: RAAD members volunteered their services to assist the Dare officer with graduation and teaching elementary students the importance of staying drug free.

Hope Program: RAAD utilized "Red Ribbon Week", articles in the campus newspaper and posters around campus to inform students of the HOPE program. The HOPE program is a resource for students with substance abuse problems.

Drug K-9's: Two K-9 dogs from area law enforcement agencies demonstrated their role in the fight against drugs. A demonstration was conducted on the skills of these dogs and how they are used to find drugs.

HOPE Program

The HOPE program is a course of group or individual education sessions directed at assisting students with an alcohol or substance abuse history or problem. Participants enter the program by referral from the Dean of students Office, Counseling Center, RAAD, campus ecclesiastical leaders, or self-referral. Participants complete an informal assessment to define the extent of their substance abuse problem. This assessment is then used to recommend an appropriate course of action for the student or employee to follow.

A confidential telephone help line is provided for individuals seeking assistance. Self-referred students utilizing HOPE through the Drug Prevention Office are treated without threat of disciplinary action and all interventions are kept confidential.

Since the inception of the HOPE program in 1992, a total of 101 students have participated in the program. Of that number 18 were self-referrals, 3 were referred by campus ecclesiastical leaders and 79 were referred by the Dean of Students Office as a condition of their disciplinary probation.

Radar Network

In 1995 Ricks College became a member of the Idaho Regional Alcohol and Drug Awareness Resource (RADAR) Network Center. The RADAR Networks are sponsored by the federal government's Center for Substance Abuse Prevention. This affiliation makes Ricks a local site for disseminating alcohol and drug prevention-related information to Ricks College students, faculty, staff, administrators, and the local community as a whole. The RADAR network operates as a two-way communication system between the national level and the grass roots level and offers the campus community and the members of the local community priority access to information, publications, and audiovisual materials developed by federal agencies, other network members, or national organizations.

Health Center

The Ricks College Health Center provides full services designed to assist students with day-to-day health care needs. The Health Center is staffed by three full-time physicians, a family practitioner, registered nurses, registered lab and X-ray technicians, and a full-time registered pharmacist. These health care professionals are cognizant of the potential for substance abuse among the student population. The Health Center staff monitors very closely those with whom they work and the prescriptions they are issued. In addition, care is taken to ensure that prescription pads are inventoried and kept on the doctor's person to prevent them from being stolen and forged. The pharmacy is secured in an enclosed area with an alarm system and walls and the ceiling are designed and reinforced to prevent intrusion. All three physicians work in other health care facilities in the community and in Idaho Falls and are in a position to observe any students who may be seen at these facilities who may be abusing drugs and in need of assistance.

The Health Center provides medical services for student athletes and provides counseling and drug use prevention information during physical examinations, treatment appointments, and on request from students, coaches, or other interested parties. This information includes steroid use, prescription drugs, and substance abuse. The Health Center lab has the capability to draw blood and urine for drug screens and drug levels. The results of these tests are legally binding.

Housing Office

The Housing Office acts as a resource to provide listings of housing options for Ricks College students; provides mediation services to assist in student/landlord conflict resolution; and provides training to owners, managers, and resident assistants in matters of discipline and other aspects of residential living. Ricks College single students are required to live in an "approved" housing complex. This means that specific guidelines must be met by the housing unit in the interest of safety and comfort for the student. On-site management is also required at each housing complex to assist students as the need arises.

Students are required to adhere to the Honor Code while living in approved housing complexes. An environment which strengthens students spiritual, physical, social, and intellectual development is encouraged and enhanced by close manager-student relationships and roommates working together to make their apartments a home-away-from-home. Managers and roommates are encouraged to assist all students to live by the Honor Code and in instances where students do not respond to that encouragement, a referral to the dean of students office is made.

Training seminars for on- and off-campus owners, managers, and resident assistants are conducted each semester by the housing office. A resident assistant Leadership class (GS 290C) is available to managers and resident assistants in which weekly training is provided in all aspects of residential living with an emphasis on developing strong leaders who can teach residents and thereby, strengthen individual development. The "drug and alcohol" sessions of the class are taught by trained police officers and are open to all owners, managers, and resident assistants. Drug basics are taught as well as information is given to those in attendance about college programs which are available as resources of help for students in need.

Housing personnel in individual units often refer students directly to the dean of students office for violations involving drugs or alcohol. College policy provides that when it is suspected that drugs are being used in a housing unit that the matter should be referred to the appropriate police agency having jurisdiction for their disposition. Incidents involving alcohol use should be referred to the police if any participants are under age. All other cases are referred directly to the dean of students.

Personnel Office

The personnel office serves as a resource to campus employees in helping employees to maintain their commitment to the Honor Code. The Board of Trustees has established an ecclesiastical endorsement program requiring that each employee be endorsed annually by his ecclesiastical leader to ensure that employee conduct is in accordance with the values and standards espoused by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. This endorsement includes a personal commitment to refrain from alcohol or other drug use at all times.

Ricks College implemented a Commercial Vehicle Drivers Drug and Alcohol Testing program in November 1997 in compliance with the regulations implementing the Omnibus Transportation Employee Testing Act of 1991. Ricks College has a contract with Minert & Associates Inc. from Meridian, Idaho, an independent company who coordinates the alcohol and drug testing according to the federal regulations. As of this report, all tests have come back negative.

The personnel office provides Internet access to the "Ricks College Campus Security Report, Drug Policy, and Graduation Rate" through its web site for employees to access at any time. A statement on how to access this information is printed on employment applications for perspective employees. All newly hired employees receive orientation materials which refer to this policy and tell them how to access the information. Annually, in conjunction with the dean of students office the personnel office provides to each employee, both full and part time, either by e-mail or regular mail, or campus mail the college annual report entitled "Ricks College Campus Security Report, Drug Policy, and Graduation Rate." The pamphlet reiterates the Church, Board of Trustees, and college policy regarding alcohol and other drug use and the resources available for help.

During the reporting period, the personnel office was not aware of and did not receive any reports of alcohol or drug problems among full- or part-time employees.

Where applicable, comments are made by way of possible explanation in an effort to gain understanding in differences.

Campus Police

At Ricks, students arrested for driving under the influence of alcohol are generally underage individuals. Students arrested for minor in possession or minor consuming alcohol and those arrested for DUI are often found to be first-time or occasional users of alcohol. Those arrested or reported by the police are subject to disciplinary sanction by the college and, as a result, either quit drinking and renew their personal commitment to the Honor Code or are extremely careful in their use of alcohol so as to not get caught. Many times these individuals will drink in areas removed from the campus where it is less likely that they will be caught or reported. However, some students are arrested in neighboring states and communities and the information filters back to the campus through a variety of means. If the college becomes aware of this the dean of students office will investigate the report and take appropriate action. No statistics are available in this regard even though some students may end up being arrested or cited for alcohol violations as they are included in statistics kept by the dean of students but not categorized by location of behavior.

Other drug use arrests are usually the result of reports to the police of "someone smoking marijuana" in one of the on-campus dormitories or other students reporting use of drugs by roommates or peers. An immediate follow-up investigation usually results in the arrest of the offender(s), criminal charges are filed, and disciplinary sanctions are imposed by the dean of students office. Disciplinary action may precede criminal charges and criminal charges are not necessary in order for disciplinary action to be pursued. A cooperative effort between the police, prosecutor, and the dean of students office results in a higher degree of student accountability for their behavior.

Counseling Center

Referrals to the Counseling Center for alcohol or substance abuse usually are initiated by an ecclesiastical leader, the student seeking help, a roommate or friend, or a parent seeking help for their son or daughter who is considering enrollment or is currently enrolled. Some students choose to attend AA meetings in one of the local chapters (seeking help away from the college), not wishing to divulge to the institution their problem. Some of the students involved in these programs come to Ricks having worked with similar programs at home prior to enrollment at Ricks. Some have been ordered by a criminal court to obtain counseling or rehabilitative help in lieu of further criminal penalties.

The members of the Counseling Center staff are keenly aware of resources available in the local communities in alcohol and substance abuse treatment but prefer to work with the students in the campus environment rather than place the student in jeopardy by associating with others who may have a more serious problem. The Ricks College community is a very supportive environment with very caring peers, roommates, faculty, staff, administrators, and ecclesiastical leaders. This allows a student to maintain regular close contact with individuals who are deeply committed to abstinence from alcohol or substance abuse, continue with their educational pursuits and receive professional help in solving their problem.

The number of students seeking help for alcohol or illegal substance use problems through the Counseling Center is relatively low and may be attributed to (1) the low number of students "believing" they have a problem, and (2) the large number of ecclesiastical leaders who are available to provide help to students and (3) a fear that if they discuss their problem with any representative of the institution it will be disclosed and they will be asked to leave. This may result in lower use of the counseling center as a resource when the strength of the help the student really needs can be found in the multiplicity of natural support groups available. In addition, given the overall expectation of the Board of Trustees, the College, and their affiliation with The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, some students may assume that no assistance is offered because of the strong prohibition on alcohol and substance abuse or because of the fear of recrimination choose to deal with their burden in their own way.

The reality of the matter is that Ricks College espouses a doctrine of repentance and forgiveness for transgressions of this nature and the institution and administration respects the confidential relationships between the student and the counselor or the student and the ecclesiastical leader. Action is only instituted by the college if awareness of the matter comes before the dean of students office through sources which are not protected. Students who may have difficulties are encouraged to seek appropriate help in resolving these issues.

Dean of Students

Referrals to the dean of students office for violations of the Honor Code come from a variety of sources including housing, peers, roommates, local community businesses, or local citizens. The disparity between individuals referred from year to year is very evident in reviewing statistical information. Students who drink alcohol or become involved in substance abuse at Ricks College place their continuing student status in jeopardy. Their commitment to abide by the standards at Ricks includes their signature to keep that commitment and they know that "a single violation may be grounds for dismissal from the college." This strict code of conduct may be root cause for a student's attitude of "hiding" alcohol and substance abuse behavior because of the potential consequences.

With the current annual enrollment averaging around 9,000 students only 18 students, or two tenths of one percent of the student body were disciplined for alcohol use and 11 students, or one tenth of one percent of the student body were disciplined for substance abuse during the reporting period of October 1998 through December 2000. This does not reflect an accurate picture of the actual numbers of students involved as they only represent those individuals who are reported or caught and admit to or confess the allegation. We suspect that illicit substance use is viewed by the students as a more serious contravention of their commitment to the Honor Code and hence is more widely reported while alcohol use is perceived as less serious and therefore is less likely to be reported or even tolerated by peers as an experimental phase for this age group. While this may be acceptable at other institutions of higher education throughout the world, we would view any use as being unacceptable. It is most likely that alcohol use is much more prevalent than illicit substance use. It should be noted that the overall rate of reporting of use varies significantly from year to year indicating either a tolerant approach to those using, sophistication in the ability to cover use up, or a lack of enforcement of the standard. For whatever reason, we suspect that the culture at Ricks College is contributing to less reporting of incidences of use. While on the contrary, it is clear that the campus community as a whole is strongly opposed to alcohol and other drug use.

Drug referrals come through arrests by police, those reported by housing personnel or roommates, and those who admit to their use when confronted as a result of an investigation by a member of the dean of students staff. Some cases are the culmination of many different reports to the dean of students office resulting in a review by the deans and linking together of the various reports received to determine the connection between users. Continued investigation usually results in some students admitting use while others deny involvement. During some semesters, unusually large groups are connected together in a loose confederation with individuals being involved in several different groups over the same time period. This phenomenon tends to cause episodic and significant changes in the statistical report of use and probably does not accurately reflect true use trends.

GENERAL ASSESSMENT

In a general sense, the alcohol and illicit substance prevention programs at Ricks College are very actively pursued but receive very little attention. This does not lessen their effectiveness but enhances the overall mission of the institution. There is no known alcohol or illicit substance use by employees. Other drug use and abuse among students is quite uncommon. On the other hand, alcohol use among students, although not as rare as other drug use, is quite limited and is generally restricted to occasional or experimental use by a small portion (less than 1 percent) of the student body. This relatively low incidence of alcohol and other drug use is accomplished through a program of strict adherence to the doctrines of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints which requires abstinence from alcohol use and illicit substance use/abuse as a condition of employment or enrollment at Ricks College. The direction provided by the Ricks College Board of Trustees regarding illicit substance use is very strict and states: "There is no second chance. The use or possession of illicit substances will cause for immediate dismissal from school. The same policy will apply to any person having any part in supplying illicit substances or assisting others to procure or use illicit substances." The only exception to the policy is ". . . except when the only contact with an illicit substance was limited (brief and casual contact) and the student has repented before disciplinary action is begun." This has been interpreted as the use was a first-time experiment and was the only contact with the drug, and the individual sought immediate help and direction from an ecclesiastical leader.

Those involved with the use of alcohol and who are reported to the dean of students office will be individually assessed as to the extent of the alcohol use. These students generally are invited to participate in the HOPE program which may include extensive counseling through the counseling center prior to and after completion of the HOPE program. Students who request help through the Drug Prevention Office are not subject to the disciplinary sanctions of the college if they seek the help of the college in resolving a problem. In the case where there is continued use by the student and this is reported to the dean of students office, the student may be then subject to discipline after careful review and staffing by the deans.
