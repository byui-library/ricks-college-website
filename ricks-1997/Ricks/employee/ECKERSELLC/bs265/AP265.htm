    Course Objectives:

      To prepare students in the health and medical fields to efficiently and effectively advance into their fields with a good background in anatomy and physiology of the human body.  Also to prepare those students who just like to know more about themselves sufficiently  well to appreciate and thus respect their bodies

    Text:  Seeley, Stephens and Tate Anatomy and Physiology fifth edition

    Course Requirements:

    1.     Honor Code: Each student agrees to adhere to the Honor Code when he or she applies to BYU-Idaho.  Please read and become familiar with the dress and grooming standards. It is expected that your dress, grooming, and conduct will show integrity in honoring that agreement.
    1.     Attendance: Roll will not usually be taken in class.  The pace of the class is very fast and therefore it is imperative that each student comes to class unless circumstances such as illnesses, death, birth, weddings and school-approved activities prevent attendance. Lecture exams are constructed from material presented in lectures therefore it is to your advantage to attend every lecture. Quizzes are given in lab each week and lab exams are based upon information presented exclusively in lab.  Attendance at both lab and lecture are essential for your success in this course.
    1.     Exams: Five lectures exams will be given during the course.  All lecture exams except for Exam 5 will be given in the Testing Center; each exam will be open for at least two days.  The fifth exam will be in class during the week of finals at the scheduled time.   The exams will consist of multiple choice, matching, fill in short answer and essay type questions.  Exams will test a student’s knowledge of information covered in the lectures, labs or in the textbook.  Exams may consist of an objective section and a written section. The objective section will consist of multiple choice and matching questions and will be graded at the time that the student hands in his or her exam at the Testing Center.  The written section will be graded by the instructor and will be handed back in class.  The score for the exam will be the sum of the objective and written scores.  Each exam will be worth 100 points.  Two laboratory exams will be given during the semester. Each lab exam is worth 100  points (See Laboratory Schedule). In addition approximately eight quizzes will be given at  the beginning of each lab and will total 100 points.
