Accounting Services
Each student has an account at BYU-Idaho. Different categories in each account allows students to identify the different charges (tuition, housing, bookstore, etc.) and payments made on those charges. Deposits available for pick up at the Cashiers Office are also identified on the account (personal, payroll, and financial aid).

Student account clerks in the Accounting Office, located in Kimball 130, are available from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. each weekday to assist and answer questions. Financial information is also available on the web at www.byui.edu/accountingoffice.

Statements are mailed monthly showing the charges, payments, deposits, and balances. Payment for all unpaid charges shown on the statement is due by the end of the month following the statement date. Questions should be referred to the Accounting Office. Students may also access their account information online.

1. Registration Deposit

Students are required to pay a registration deposit before they will be allowed to register. Registration deposits will be kept on students’ accounts to allow registration for subsequent semesters or terms. When students leave the university or do not continue, this deposit will be applied to any outstanding debts and the balance will be returned to the students.

2. Tuition Payment Deadlines

Tuition payment deadlines are published in the school catalog and class schedules. There is no official billing mailed to students for tuition and fees. However, students may obtain information as well as the total amount owed by accessing the Accounting Office web site. Students may also obtain a Fee and Schedule Form over the web or by calling the Phone Registration system ext. 1006, or by calling the Accounting Office at ext.1050. FAILURE TO BE AWARE OF THE AMOUNT OWED DOES NOT EXCUSE THE STUDENT FROM MEETING THE PAYMENT DEADLINE. There will be a 5% late charge on the unpaid balance.

3. Books and Supplies

Books and Supplies purchased at the BYU-Idaho Bookstore may be charged to students’ personal accounts at the university. These charges become due the following month; however, financial aid may be used immediately to pay for these charges. Students may also make these purchases with cash, checks, or back cards.

See Bookstore

4. Financial Aid

BYU-Idaho scholarships and grants are automatically credited to students’ accounts on the first day of classes and used to pay for tuition, fees, books, etc. Off-campus scholarships and grants should be mailed or brought to the Scholarship Office in Kimball 120 for processing. Pell Grants and Stafford Loans will be applied first to tuition and fees and then to campus housing and meals. Then with students’ permission, they will be applied to books and other debts owed by the student. However, before Federal Aid can be processed, students must complete and submit to the Accounting Office a Title IV Authorization form stating whether or not this permission is given. This form can also be completed over the web. The remaining balance of financial aid, after debts have been paid, can be picked up at the Cashiers Office on or after the first day of classes.

See Cashiers Office

5. Student Insurance

Students are automatically charged for health and accident insurance when they register, unless they have submitted a waiver form showing they are covered by private insurance. The deadlines for waiving student insurance are listed in the school catalog and class schedules.

See Insurance

6. Campus Payroll

Paydays for campus student employees are the 8th and 23rd of each month for pay periods which end the 15th and the last day of each month, respectively. If the payday falls on a weekend, students may pick up their pay the previous Friday. The payroll is deposited into student accounts and can be picked up at the Cashiers Office either on or after each payday. Notifications of payroll information for each payday are available on the accounting office web site. Students are encouraged to have their pay deposited directly into their bank account by bringing a blank voided check or a savings account deposit slip to the Accounting Office and asking for the Payroll Clerk.

7. Personal Deposits

For their convenience, students may deposit money in their personal accounts at BYU-Idaho. These funds may then be used to pay for debts incurred at the university or may be withdrawn for personal use at students’ discretion. Up to $400 in cash or any amount in check can be withdrawn at the Cashiers Office between 8:15 a.m. and 4:30 p.m. Monday through Friday.

Students may also open a checking account at one of the local banks or at the Beehive Credit Union in Rexburg. These financial institutions are pleased to have student customers and are within walking distance of the campus. Students can have 24 hour access to their money in these institutions by using the Automated Teller Machines (ATM) located on campus and in Rexburg. There is no charge for use of ATMs on campus or at the Beehive Credit Union.

8. Finance Charges on Past Due Accounts

All debts owed to the university must be received or paid at the Cashiers Office by the due dates. They may also be paid by a debit or credit card over the phone or on the web. In addition to specific late charges, a monthly finance charge in the amount of 1% per month will be made against all past debts.

9. Dishonored Checks

Appropriate late charges will be assessed when a dishonored check has been used to pay tuition and other charges. A return check charge of $5.00 will also be charged for each dishonored check. Check cashing privileges may also be terminated.

10. Refunds

Refer to information listed in the catalog and class schedules.

Attendance at Class
Students are responsible for their attendance in class. No absences, whether approved by the university for participation in university-sponsored activities or due to illness or other personal emergencies, are "excusable" in the sense of relieving students of the responsibility for work assigned or participated in by the class during their absence. In most cases, repeated absences may cause students to receive a lower grade or even fail a class.

Bicycles
All bicycles ridden on campus must be registered with the Campus Police. Registration helps prevent theft and facilitates the recovery of stolen bicycles. There is no cost for this service. Bicycles can be registered at the Campus Police Office, located in Kimball 150. Bicycle riders must yield to pedestrians on sidewalks and ride at walking speeds or dismount, if necessary, so as not to constitute a hazard to any pedestrian on campus. Non-compliance will be enforced by Campus Police under Rexburg City Ordinance #759. Racks are provided near the entrances to most buildings and near the on-campus residence halls. To prevent theft, bicycles should be locked to racks. They should not be locked to trees, shrubbery, buildings, or building apparatus. Improperly parked and/or unregistered bicycles are subject to impoundment and a fine.

Bookstore
The BYU-Idaho Bookstore offers a wide selection of merchandise to meet students academic and personal needs. The Bookstore is located on the first floor of the Manwaring Center. See www.byui.edu/bookstore.

1. Textbook Buyback

Book buyback is held at the end of each semester. Quantities purchased are determined by instructor requests. A textbook wholesaler will purchase used books that are not being bought by the bookstore. Wholesale prices offered are based on national demand for the books. It is recommended that students hold their books until the buyback for best pricing.

2. Textbook Returns

Textbooks purchased from the bookstore at the beginning of a semester may be returned for full refund until the last day of the no penalty add/drop period. Returned books must be in resalable condition and a receipt must be provided as proof of purchase. Books purchased after the add/drop period may also be returned within two business days. There are NO refunds on textbooks purchased the last week of any semester or term or during final exams unless they are purchased for class use during the next semester or term.

3. Textbook Reservations

Students who reserve textbooks online will have their books pulled, boxed and waiting for them when they arrive at school. Reservations can be placed by clicking on the Textbook Reservation icon from the Registration link on the home page.

Computer Crime is a Criminal Offense. Students who knowingly and without permission access or attempt to access any campus computer, computer system or computer program without prior authorization is committing Computer Fraud. Individuals may be charged with a misdemeanor or felony and/or be suspended from BYU-Idaho should they become involved in any form of computer crime.

Computer Pornography
University computers and computer networks are intended to be used for uplifting and righteous activities only. All university owned computers or computers which hook up to any university network may be monitored for appropriate use. Individuals who use any university computer equipment for any purpose that is not in keeping with the Honor Code will be subject to disciplinary action by the Dean of Students Office. This includes but is not limited to internet or e-mail services and software or equipment misuse or abuse.

Students who use a university computer for the purpose of accessing pornographic materials may lose their computer or network access privilege or be suspended from school.

Likewise, the accessing of pornographic sites from non university owned property or equipment may result in similar disciplinary sanctions. Individuals who purchase, receive, subscribe to, or possess or provides pornographic materials for others may be suspended from school.

Copyright
The copyright law of the United States governs the making of photocopies or other reproduction of copyrighted material. Photocopies or other reproductions can be furnished only under certain conditions, if they will be used solely for private study, scholarship, or research. Use of the reproduction for other purposes may make the user liable for copyright infringement. BYU-Idaho reserves the right to refuse to accept a copying order if, in its judgment, fulfillment of the order would involve violation of copyright laws.

Counseling Center
Students frequently experience stress and other emotional discomforts. These may range from challenges adjusting to college life to severe feelings of depression or anxiety; from concerns about careers to feelings of low self-esteem. Help in dealing with these problems is available at the Counseling Center located in Kimball 270. Students will receive reliable, professional assistance in an atmosphere of warmth and understanding. Complete confidentiality is maintained by the counselors.

Credit by Exam
Several courses at BYU-Idaho may be challenged through special examination by permission of the appropriate Department Chair. Credit by examination must be done by application. These forms are available at the Registrar's Office and must be signed and approved by the appropriate Department Chair. Prior to taking the exam, a fee for the purchase of the credit must be paid at the Cashiers Office. The fee is $15 for 0.5 to 3.0 credits and $5 per additional credit. After the exam has been taken, the instructor records the grade on the application which is forwarded to the Registrar's Office where it is recorded on your transcript.

Crime Reporting
BYU-Idaho encourages the prompt and accurate reporting of crimes to the Campus Police. Students who wish to report crimes occurring off-campus are to contact the Rexburg City Police. All crimes reported will be investigated and disposed of in a professional and appropriate manner. Any suspicious or emergency situation should be quickly reported by dialing 911 from any phone or by activating any of the emergency telephones located on campus. Non-emergencies can be reported to the Campus Police, located in Kimball 150, or by calling ext. 2448. See www.byui.edu/police/default.html.

Curfew
Hours are the same for off-campus and on-campus housing units and apply equally to single male and female students. All students must be in their apartment or housing unit by 11 p.m. on nights preceding a school day, and by 1 a.m. on nights not preceding a school day. Arrangements may be made for a late or overnight pass. Curfew hours are in effect during finals or test week even though some individuals may have completed their exams.
