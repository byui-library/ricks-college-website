TRANSITION TO BYU-IDAHO BEGINS TO TAKE SHAPE

A first glimpse of the future of Ricks College shows it will undertake an innovative direction as it becomes a baccalaureate-granting university. Poised to be renamed Brigham Young University-Idaho sometime next year, President David A. Bednar announced it will begin some upper division classes in the fall of 2001.

Bednar said the baccalaureate degrees will be phased in over an unspecified period of time. Space limitations will require some majors to be delayed for a few years until additional classrooms and faculty office space become available. While he did not announce any specific new building plans, he said officials are working on plans for a new ecclesiastical building that would be used for both religious and academic activities.

The president explained the basic framework for the transition to employees and students following his meeting with the Board of Trustees. He explained BYU-Idaho will be a two-tiered institution that will offer specialized associate degrees and bachelor's degrees. It will use 120 credit hours as its standard for bachelor's degrees. Bednar said the university will feature integrated baccalaureate degrees that require 45 hours or less in a major. It will also offer some specialized baccalaureate degrees requiring more than 45 hours. He explained that integrated degrees will require students to study in multiple disciplines that will provide broad-based preparation for employment and life. By linking creative curriculum, complementary disciplines, and internships, these degrees will provide unique educational opportunities.

Bednar said BYU-Idaho may see "a modest increase" in the student body size during the next few years. It will maintain its present level of approximately 8,600 freshman and sophomore students and its student-faculty ratio of 25 to 1. To accommodate the greatest number of students, the university will utilize its facilities year round. Students will be admitted to one of four enrollment tracks: fall-winter, winter-summer, summer-fall, or the year round Fast Track program.

Watch the Internet at www.byui.edu for additional details and updates of time lines and initial majors.


REAL DAIRY BOWL ANNOUNCES TEAMS FOR 2000 GAMES

The Ricks College football team will make its 14th appearance at the annual Real Dairy Bowl Friday, Dec. 1, in Holt Arena on the campus of Idaho State University in Pocatello, facing Snow College. The annual junior college gridiron classic is sponsored by the United Dairymen of Idaho. "We are pleased to offer fans in southeastern Idaho a chance to witness a rematch of a hard-fought Snow-Ricks game," said John Novosel, chairman of the bowl committee. "When these two teams meet, the outcome is always doubtful."

Tickets are on sale at Holt Arena and the Ricks College Ticket Office (208) 356-2230. A family pass is $20 for an immediate family. Single admission is $8 with a $2 discount with a Real Dairy seal. Children under 12 not attending with their family will be admitted for $4 each.

THREE NAMED AS EXEMPLARY EMPLOYEES

 

Cornell Davidson, Susan Thurman and George Cromwell have been named exemplary employees for rendering outstanding service to Ricks College. Each semester the college selects one administrator and two staff members who make exemplary contributions said Carla Ricks, director of Personnel Services.

For the last 10 years of his 20-year career at Ricks, Davidson has been an electronic, pneumatic controls technician in the Heating Ventilation and Air Conditioning shop. For the first 10 years, he worked with sheet metal, gas appliances, steam traps, air filtration systems and the swimming pool. Davidson graduated from Ricks in 1971 in industrial technology and went on to earn a bachelor's degree in the same field from BYU in 1977. He was commissioned as a second lieutenant in the Army Air Defense Artillery. He is currently a lieutenant colonel in the Army Reserve and a part-time ROTC instructor at Ricks. In the winter semester teaches in the construction management department. He and his wife, Venna, have six children.

Thurman grew up in Rexburg and graduated from Ricks in 1967 in secretarial business. She and her husband, Reed, met while they were attending Ricks. After marriage, she worked as a secretary at Utah State University while husband finished school. She returned to Rexburg while her husband was in the Army. They settled in Rexburg and raised six children. Thurman has worked at the college for 15 years as the secretary for the home economics department. "If you like where you're at, I couldn't see switching," she says of her job. "Ricks has been good to our family."

Cromwell, a native of Ririe, graduated from Ricks in the consumer electronics servicing program in 1979. He was then self-employed until he started to work at Ricks in 1985 in electronics servicing. He fixed audiovisual equipment for about four years before moving to computer maintenance. About five years ago he became a software analyst for Information Technology. He is heavily involved in the implementation of Windows 2000 and the transition of Ricks to BYU-Idaho from a network perspective. Cromwell and his wife, Cindy, have eight children.
