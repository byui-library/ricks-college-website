Syllabus - Eight Week Summer Semester, 2001

Instructor: Larry L. Thurgood                        Office: Taylor 213                                 Phone: 356-1503

Office Hours: Monday, Wednesday and Friday, 1:30-2:30 p.m.

COURSE DESCRIPTION: The Book of Mormon course provides one of the greatest opportunities to feel and know the power and spirit of the gospel of Jesus Christ. The sacred writings and teachings of some of the greatest of God's prophets were engraved and preserved to come forth in our day. These scriptures contain the words of eternal life and teach us how we can truly "come unto Christ". Through faithful and prayerful study of this magnificent book, we come to a better understanding and a greater testimony of what the Lord would have us do. We will feel the Spirit permeate our lives and we will have a stronger desire to keep the commandments and to serve others. This course covers 1 Nephi through Alma 16. We are truly blessed to study this great book.

CLASS OBJECTIVES:

1. To assist the student to gain a better understanding of and appreciation for the teachings of the Book of Mormon.
2. To assist the student to develop and stronger testimony of the gospel of Jesus Christ.
3. To assist the student to "liken all scriptures unto us" (1 Nephi 19:23) and apply the teachings to their own lives.

REQUIREMENTS:

1. Attendance: This is NOT a home study course. Regular attendance is essential in order to receive credit. 200 points are possible for perfect attendance. 40 points will be deducted for each absence over 2. Only school excused (and approved) absences will not be penalized. More than 8 absences will result in a loss of credit and in failing the class.

2. Scripture study: Each student is to read the assigned text BEFORE class. You will report on the completion of this assignment each day. You cannot report your reading as complete unless you have also written your journal entries. 150 points are possible. 15 points will be deducted for each assignment not completed BEFORE class.

3. Scripture Study Journal:  In connection with your personal study, you are to keep a journal. This journal should contain examples of the knowledge, understanding and feelings about the gospel principles you have discovered, or rediscovered during your personal scripture study. This journal can be handwritten and is personal, but will be evaluated. At least 2 gospel principles should be identified from each reading assignment during the course. At least 1, significant paragraph should be written for each principle selected. These paragraphs should include WHAT the principle or idea is and the scriptural REFERENCE that brought it to your mind; WHY it is important to you, and  HOW the principle can help you in your life. You may also want to set goals and plan for the implementation of these principles. See my web page for additional "Journal Ideas" that will add variety to your journal entries. This journal should be written in a small binder or even a spiral notebook. This project is worth 150 points. It will be evaluated three times during the term. * To help you learn the most, you must have completed your journal writing in order to count the reading assignment as complete.

4. Worksheets:: Two worksheets will be given; a midterm and a final. Each will be worth 100 points.

5. Doctrinal Research Papers: Four, 2 to 3 page typewritten papers are required ON OR BEFORE the due date. A doctrine, principle, phrase, verse or block of verses should be selected from any of the reading assignments given prior to the due date of the paper. This topic should be one that you would like to better understand. This is NOT to be written as a talk. It is a research paper. You should use cross references, footnotes, topical guide/index, bible dictionary, and extracts from General Conferences as primary sources to clarify and deepen your understanding of this topic. Secondary sources such as commentaries, should be limited. You will find that a great amount of understanding can come from the scriptures themselves. These papers should include the following:

A. Student name, course (i.e. Rel 121 Book of Mormon) and time of class (i.e. 11:20)
B. Identification of the topic you selected.
C. Inclusion of at least 7 scriptural clarifications of the topic (from standard works).
D. Inclusion of at least 5 General Conference or other quotes from The Brethren. You must include the NAME of the authority, and the REFERENCE or source of the quote, including PAGE NUMBER. For example:  Bruce R. McConkie, Mormon Doctrine, p. 267 or Boyd K. Packer, Ensign, March 1997, p. 22.
E. A comprehensive summary of what you have learned about the topic. It should be detailed. It is critical that you condense the information you have gathered into your summary. One or two brief paragraphs is NOT sufficient. Papers are to be written in the format described above and as shown in class. Papers are worth 75 points each. Late assignments are penalized by 50%

6. Textbooks and materials: You are to have the standard works with you at each class. The Book of Mormon Student Manual is highly recommended, but not require.
