Course Description:   A foundation course in finance that will teach principles, concepts, and skills used in the financial arena.  Topics include financial analysis, capital budgeting, time value of money, capital markets, investment banking, stocks, bonds, personal finance, and current events.  Basic mathematics of finance will be taught and spreadsheet analysis will be used in this course.

 

Objectives:                 Students that complete this course will develop problem-solving and quantitative reasoning skills that will enhance their understanding of finance.  Particular emphasis will be made in the following areas:

                                   

1.      Discussions about real-world issues that face finance professionals on a regular basis.

                                    2.   Emphasis on the application of financial concepts.

                                    3.   Development of spreadsheet models that reinforce principles and concepts discussed in class.

                                    4.   Focus on current market events with emphasis on their relationship to class discussions.

                                    5.   Understanding of finance-related vocabulary.

Homework Assignments

Ten homework assignments will be collected and graded for credit.  Homework assignments will generally be given the prior class period before the assignment is due.  If you are unable to attend class, you will be responsible for making sure that your homework has been completed and turned in to the instructor before class or brought to class by another student.  Late assignments will be accepted, but will be marked down 50%.  The only exception for not marking down a late assignment would be a medical or family emergency (trips home are not an excuse for late work).

 

If you missed the prior class and did not receive the homework assignment, the assignment will be posted on the instructor’s web page shortly after the assignment is given and will be displayed until the assignment is due.

 

Research Paper

One research paper covering a discussion or topic discussed in class will be due no later than November 29.  The paper must be typed and be no longer than three pages.
