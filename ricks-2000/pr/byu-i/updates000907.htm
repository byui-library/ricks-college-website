The Transition: Updates September 7, 2000

A general question-and-answer session for students was conducted by President David A. Bednar with members of President's Council on September 7.  Several of the questions focused on the transition of becoming BYU-Idaho.  Below are responses which give new or updated observations:

Will previous graduates with an associate degree be able to automatically come back or have to reapply?

Brent Kinghorn, Community Services Vice President: There are three categories of students: new, continuing, and returning. "New" students are incoming freshmen. "Continuing" are those who are here and without interruption continue through (not including those who traditionally go fall and winter and take off the summer months). "Returning" are those who lay out a semester or more. We have in place a policy requiring "returning" students to reapply, and there is an abbreviated application form for those who are out only one semester. Returning missionaries use a different form which also is abbreviated. The policy of reapplying if they have been out more than one semester will apply to previous graduates.

Can we plan to stay for four years?

President Bednar: We presently are in the process of evaluating our academic programs, but no final decisions have been made yet. We anticipate that many students will come for two years and continue to transfer. At this point a student should plan two courses of action - one to transfer and one to stay if the major you need becomes available in a timely way. Do not put all your eggs in one basket.

 

How difficult will it be for someone with a degree from BYU-Idaho to transfer to a prestigious graduate program in the East?

Don Bird: That depends on you and if you have filled the prerequisites to get into your program. We have an excellent reputation at the two-year level. We have to establish a reputation as a four-year school. Our standards for quality in programs and faculty remain. We have all the confidence in the world that when you get a degree from BYU-Idaho it will be a good, solid degree.


The meeting is appreciated. At what point can we as students become actively involved in helping this transition?

President Bednar: At Ricks College, everyone is a teacher. We have nearly 9,000 student teachers. (1) Become a teacher. Do not feed rumor mills. Help people know when they are disseminating inaccurate information. (2) Help other people become equipped to be teachers. We will be open and provide information when we have it. Bring a friend with you to the next session.

Where can we find updates on the transition?

President Bednar: The college has a transition page on the Internet at www.ricks.edu. Each month a summary of the question-and-answer sessions is broadcast on KRIC-FM 100.5 You can also listen to KRIC sound bites from the web page.
