Ricks College News Release
August 18, 2000

Ricks has been busy place for construction this summer

REXBURG, Idaho - "This has been the most intensive summer construction schedule I've ever seen," says Chuck Frost, director of Physical Plant at Ricks College.

Having worked at Ricks since 1971, Frost says there's been construction everywhere he's looked this summer. "Several million dollars have been spent on renovation and remodeling projects," he says. "At one point we had almost one-fourth of the campus under renovation at one time."

Some of the work has been in preparation for closure of the Jacob Spori Building. All the classes and offices in the building have had to be moved elsewhere either on or off campus.

Faculty offices have been relocated to other buildings, the student newspaper (The Scroll) was moved to the former alumni office in the Manwaring Center, a broadcasting studio and classroom has been moved to the Kirkham Building and much of the art department has been moved downtown.

Because there was no room on campus, the art department will be based at 16 E. Main St. in a building most recently occupied by Sandra and Co. Design classes that require computers will be taught in a remodeled area of the game room in the Manwaring Center.

Last to move out of the Spori Building will be KRIC and KWBH radio stations and Graphic Services. They are waiting for the completion of a new building expected to be finished in mid-September. The 4,225 square foot building is located between the Kimball Student and Administrative Services Building and the tennis courts along East Viking Drive. The one-story, wood-frame building is being constructed by Vern Clark and Sons Construction of Idaho Falls.

Ricks officials have selected the architectural firm of FFKR of Salt Lake City to design the new building that will replace the Spori. The design process has slowed down a bit while Ricks officials determine if there are additional needs that may be required when the college starts offering upper division classes. It is hoped that design work will be completed in early 2001 with construction of a new building starting in the spring. It is estimated that the new building will take from 20 to 24 months to complete.

The Spori Building is expected to stand until after homecoming Oct. 13 and 14. Several homecoming activities will commemorate the historic building and its namesake, Jacob Spori.

David O. McKay Library

Work is about four months ahead of schedule on a major addition and renovation to the David O. McKay Library.

Frost says the former administration building that is being transformed into the east wing of the library will likely be open to use by mid-October. The east wing project involved nearly gutting the building, constructing new rooms and installing a new heating, ventilation and air conditioning system, new electrical system, new lighting and new carpeting.

The third floor of the existing library also has been extensively remodeled this summer and will be ready about the same time.

Frost says the contractor, Ormond Builders of Idaho Falls, plans to be completely finished this winter and the entire library will be opened to use by late winter or early spring.

The library expansion was made possible by the freeing up of administrative space following completion of the new Spencer W. Kimball Student and Administrative Services Building last year. Building Chairman Max Checketts says the library expansion will boost the amount of seating for students from about 700 to 1,100 to 1,200 in the library when it's completed. The library will also feature increased computer access. The number of group study rooms will also increase from about four at present time to about 20. There also is a major expansion of the Learning Assistance Lab that includes the writing center, reading lab and math lab.

Smith Building

The Joseph Fielding Smith Building, a large classroom and faculty office building, has gotten a major overhaul this summer. It was closed April 29 immediately following graduation and was reopened to faculty members Aug. 21 just in time for fall classes.

"The contractor (R.E. Beck of Idaho Falls) has worked extremely hard and under very tight deadlines," Frost says. "They literally performed construction miracles to get the building ready to go in less than 90 days."

While much of the work is now covered by walls and ceilings, its most obvious change is the replacement of old windows with new tinted, energy-efficient windows.

Frost says the building was constructed during the 1960s during the age of typewriters. Inadequate ventilation has become a significant problem with between 400 and 500 computers in use throughout the classroom building.

"There have been revolutionary changes in the way they are teaching business and English," Frost says. "With so many PCs, it gets hot and stuffy in the computer labs."

Work during the summer included asbestos abatement in sections of the first floor and mechanical chaseways. The asbestos was found in sprayed-on, fire-proofing insulation. The chiller and ductwork were replaced on the first floor and electrical service to the building also was replaced. An economics computer lab on the north end of the first floor was doubled in size. And the two stairwells on the east side of the building were enclosed to comply with current fire codes.

While work is finished for this year, Frost says additional work will be required during the next two summers on the three top floors.

Other miscellaneous projects

The Manwaring Center was kept open this summer even with an extensive renovation to the buildings's ventilation system. New ductwork, ceilings and lights were added to about one-half the building, and more work is planned for next summer.

Fifteen computer labs were either rewired or renovated this summer.

Work is being completed on an overhaul of the planetarium in the Romney Building and should be finished by the end of September.

Major work has been done on the walls of the swimming pool in the Hart Building. Unexpected work on the roof delayed the project and the pool likely will not be finished before classes begin.

A new fire alarm system was installed in the Hart Building to increase public safety.

Portions of roofs have been replaced on the Austin, Kirkham and Hart buildings, and at the stadium.

Several underground ductbanks for utilities were reconstructed during the summer.

A farmhouse at the Livestock Center west of Rexburg was renovated into a classroom.

A new picnic kiosk is being built in the floral demonstration gardens and restrooms will be finished at the college arboretum.

A new trolley system was installed in the Kirkham Arena Theater to allow students to safety work with lighting above the stage.

Still to come this fall

A 3,000-foot-long asphalt trail will be built this fall from 7th South to the college arboretum. This walking trail will tie the floral demonstration gardens to the arboretum.

A corroding steam line north of the library that runs east and west across campus must be replaced this fall. This will cause pedestrian flow problems and require the construction of two foot bridges.

East Viking Drive near South 2nd East will be temporarily closed and repaved this fall.
