Digital Cameras

The Technology


Digital Cameras, unlike conventional cameras, use CCD (Charged Coupling Device) technology rather than film to capture images. CCD's are photoelectric sensors that convert light to electricity. Each sensor creates a voltage that varies with the amount of light received. Individual CCD elements are arranged in an array, and each element creates a pixel in the captured image.
There are two types of array's, linear and area. The cameras used within the TEd Dept., use area arrays. The area array captures the entire image in one exposure without having to move the array. Is this technical? You bet! For practical purposes, the area array allows for strobe use (flash), but also returns less resolution. The greatest area array resolution is 2.048 x 2.048 pixels, whereas the greatest linear array can produce resolutions of 7,500 x 6,000 pixels. The cannon Xapshot and Apple Quicktake can both produce images of 320x240 pixels, but the Apple Quicktake can also produce images of 640 x 480. Of course the higher the number the greater the resolution.

Linear Array Operation


Linear arrays capture an image by scanning it one column of pixels at a time, making them impractical for capturing moving images. They produce the highest-resolution images.*


Area Array Operation


Area arrays capture an entire image at once. The larger number of CCD elements being used to capture the picture all at once make the camera useful for movement shots, but they also produce lower-resolution images.*
