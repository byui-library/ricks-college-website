Ricks College News Release
March 8, 2001

Devotional: Elder Alexander B. Morrison

REXBURG, Idaho — People need to see the whole picture of a changing world, said Elder Alexander B. Morrison, an emeritus member of the Quorum of the Seventy, during the March 6 weekly devotional at Ricks College.

As The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints changes, people need to keep up with those changes and see a full perspective.

"Understand that the world is a web and that adjustments here will have an effect over there. Everything is interconnected," Morrison said. "We need those who can take a broader view of the world around us in an attempt to make sense of its immense interrelated complexities."

With this expanding world, Morrison said the Church has three challenges that it faces while counteracting these changes in the world.

First, the revolution of communication that technology has given the world will directly affect the Church.

"Sin conducted in private with the aid of the Internet for example is not only real, but made much easier both to commit and conceal by the means of its commission. As never before people of all ages have to refrain from sin and must do so from internal convictions and not external constraints," Morrison said.

Secondly, bringing the Church out of obscurity will be ever present as the Church continues to grow at such a rapid rate. A related issue is managing the growth worldwide.

"One of the most effective ways to bring the Church up out of obscurity is to spread the restoration message through missionary efforts around the world . . . that obligation is placed upon us by Christ ourselves," Morrison said. "Over half of the world’s current population has not as of yet had the opportunity at all to hear the gospel message. One of our major challenges over the next 100 years is to learn how to use technology fully and effectively."

Lastly, Morrison said that intellectual apostasy must be avoided.

"Our need to maintain purity of the doctrine and of the practices will engage much of the attention of the senior church leadership into the 21st century. Sadly we live in a post-Christian world, where increasingly the only sin is that of so-called intolerance. Increasingly in all western society, including the United States, people are at best nominally Christian. The disparity of what many Christians believe and the official teachings of their churches is even larger and ever larger than before. The levels of Christian practices are shrinking on all sides," Morrison said.

In closing, he reminded students that those who are doing the things the Lord asks now should continue on the path in order to keep up with the changes.

"The 21st century will be replete with opportunities and challenges for the Church. Though the number of Church members will continue to grow, they will be few in comparison to the world population as Nephi of Old pointed out. There will be more stakes and more missions until all nations will hear the gospel as a witness before the Savior will come. Temples will dot the land. The power of God will be sustained," Morrison said.

Next Tuesday’s speaker will be Elder Dieter F. Uchtdorf, a member of the Church’s First Quorum of the Seventy. He will speak at 2 p.m. in the Hart Auditorium. Devotionals are broadcast live and repeated at 9 p.m. on KRIC-FM.
