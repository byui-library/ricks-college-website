Ricks College News Release
April 10, 2001 
Devotional: Elaine L. Jack

REXBURG, Idaho — "Fly on and fly as one" was the topic of Elaine Jack, former president of the General Relief Society of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, at Tuesday’s Ricks College devotional.

Jack, who grew up in Cardston, Alberta, related the habits of Canada geese to everyday life.

"Geese do not fly alone," she said. "As each goose flaps its wings, it creates an uplift for the birds following. By flying in a V formation, the whole flock adds as much as 70 percent more flying range than if each bird flew alone. Those who share a common direction and means of achieving community can get where they are going quicker and easier because they are traveling on the thrust of each other."

Second, geese are resilient, hearty, determined and tireless. "They instinctively seek to develop the character traits that contribute to the experience of each participant," she said. "Bring out the best in each other and accept everyone no matter their circumstances."

Third, geese attach to each other. "Young adult geese select mates and stay with that partner for life. They are loyal and committed," she said. "Know the worth of building relationships."

Fourth, geese have an uncanny sense of direction. "They know where they are and where they are going at any moment in their flight," Jack said. "They have a shared purpose and a common goal."

Fifth, geese trust each other. "Geese take turns. Their leadership and feeling of belonging is not a matter of where they serve, but how."

She offered suggestions on how these lessons she learned from the geese can apply to life. To be able to fly as one, the lesson of serving with one heart and one mind is necessary.

"Everyone of you is important, every effort is valuable . . . and all the others contributing to a dramatic geometric pattern in the sky," she said.

Recognizing the responsibilities for others is learning to fly as one. "When one gets sick, wounded or shot down, two drop out of formation and follow their fellow member down to the ground to help and provide protection. They stay with this member of the flock until he or she either is able to fly again or dies," Jack said. "Compassion, generosity, patience, kindness – these are the selfless qualities that make a difference. But they are not the qualities that make the headlines or the 10 o’clock news."

She said to expect the flight to be bumpy. "Young geese have to learn that they can only land against the wind; if they land with the wind they may buckle and tumble to the ground, and so it is with us," she said. "It is when our faces are to the wind that we make clear statements about what really matters. People matter."

She encouraged students to be strong, especially in less desirable situations. "Accept disappointment. Forgive others and forgive yourself. Don’t take offense, don’t be sharp or callous," Jack said. "This character trait is the single most mature and endearing quality you can develop."

She added, "Shine the spotlight on others and be excited when they do well; rescue them when they flounder. Learn names, call people by their names. The Lord know not only our names, he knows our thoughts."

The next suggestion was to trust those you lead and those you follow. "Geese in formation honk from behind to encourage those in the front to keep up their speed," she said. "When we speak of being one, we are suggesting that all of us reach beyond the borders of our own lives. We must fly in formation with them."

Finally, she said to know where you are going. "It is a magnificent experience to see geese arrive in the sky, begin to circle and then land. These geese have a flight plan. It includes their route, their stops and their landing field," she said. "We, too, filed such a plan when we joined with Jesus Christ in the preexistence. We chose our direction and with it came definite and glorious responsibility. We recognize then the power of the Lord’s plan to get us all the way home."

In conclusion, she said, I pray that you will understand the significance of that power and with faith, humility and hope, draw upon it as we fly on, with unity of purpose."

Next Tuesday’s speaker will be James D. Gordon III, a professor at the J. Reuben Clark Law School at Brigham Young University. He will speak at 2 p.m. in the Hart Auditorium. Devotionals are broadcast live and repeated at 9 p.m. on KRIC-FM.
