Using Quotations in the Research Paper

Handle quotations effectively by using them in the following ways:

  • as a focal point for the essay. State a quotation you will respond to in the body of the paper.

  • as a representative statement of an opinion or idea.

  • as a voice that adds authority to an assertion you have made.

  • to show diversity of opinion.

  • to clarify a point.

  • to demonstrate the complexity of a point

Helpful Hints

  1. Never use a quotation as a way to avoid writing.

  2. Introduce or frame all direct quotations in some way.

  3. Tailor quotations to fit the focus of your paper or argument. (However, be careful.  Do not twist quotation out of context.)

  4. Use ellipses (three spaced periods .  .  . ) to indicate where you have cut material from direct quotations.

  5. Use square brackets [   ] to add necessary information to a quotation.

  6. Use [sic] to indicate an obvious error copied faithfully from a quotation.

  7. Tailor your language so that direct quotations fit into the grammar of your sentences.

  8. Place quotations shorter than four typed lines between quotation marks.

  9. Indent ten spaces any prose quotations longer than four typed lines.   (Double space; keep flush with right side.)

  10. Indent more than three lines of poetry.

  11. Refer to events in works of fiction, poems, plays, movies, and television shows in the present tense.
