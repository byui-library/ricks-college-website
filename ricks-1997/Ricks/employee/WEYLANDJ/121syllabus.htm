Course Description:

Somewhere on a dark and stormy night, a driver slams on his brakes to avoid a fallen tree. The road is slippery and the car goes into a skid. Luckily, the driver escapes serious injury but will never forget the experience.

We can look at the experience in two different ways:

      • How it felt to the driver.
      • In terms of the basic laws of physics.

Physics 121 is not just a course about equations. It is about training your mind to combine intuition and past experience with mathematics to understand the fundamental laws of nature. The goal is to combine what the body understands about force, acceleration, etc with higher levels of thinking involving equations based on the laws of physics.

Once you obtain a bridge between what you know by experience and what graphs and equations say, then you understand physics. If you neglect any of these realities, then what you learn will not produce fundamental and lasting changes in your thought processes..

When you have completed this course, you should be able to do the following:

    • Understand fundamental principles of physics such as Newton's Laws of Motion, conservation of mechanical energy, conservation of momentum, and conservation of angular momentum.
    • Be able to apply these principles mathematically (using calculus) to solve problems.

Course Requirements

There will be four "hour" exams. Your lowest hour exam will be dropped. "Hour" exams typically take 1-3 hours.

Homework and quizzes count one sixth of your grade. Because homework will be collected and graded, and because each class period will have a quiz or other material handed in for grading, at the end of the semester, the quiz points, etc, may add up to several hundred points. These will then be normalized to 100 points. No matter how well you do on exams, it will not be possible to get an A on the course unless you earn at least 70% on homework and quizzes.

Homework will not be accepted late. It will not be possible to make up missed quizzes. If extenuating circumstances, such as sickness, family emergencies, etc, occur, phone Brother Weyland as soon as you can for a possible exception to this policy.

Course Policies

Two of the five class periods will be spent with you working on problems in small groups. Some of the quizzes given in class will be group quizzes. Your group should get together often to work on problems and discuss the concepts you are studying.

Plan to spend two hours between each class period studying the textbook, discussing the concepts, and working problems. Learn to work together. Those who understand should explain it to those who don't. In that way everyone's understanding will increase.

Work hard, but work smart. Don't spend hours on problems without a clue. Get help.

In turning in homework, the following format should be followed:

  • Use engineer's computation paper.
  • Write on only one side of the paper. Problems should be in numerical order, that is, problem 1, and then problem 2, and then problem 3.
  • In the upper right hand side of the first page, write:

Your Name

Physics 121

The date you turn in the assignment

In your homework, show clearly how you obtain your answer. Correct answers without supporting work will not receive full credit.
