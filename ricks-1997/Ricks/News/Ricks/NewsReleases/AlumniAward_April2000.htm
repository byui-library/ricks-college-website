Ricks College News Release
April 3, 2000

Former Ricks students honored
by alumni group

REXBURG, Idaho — The Ricks College Alumni Association has honored five former students for outstanding service in their various fields of endeavor. They are William R. "Bill" Walker of Sandy, Utah; Dr. Rex Head, formerly of Rexburg; Michael W. "Mike" Mosman of West Linn, Ore.; Mary Beth Cooper Cheney of Livermore, Calif.; and Patricia Boyle Christensen of Ucon.

Walker, who is managing director and global sales director for Barclays International Funds of London was given the Distinguished Alumni Award for Professional Accomplishments. He attended Ricks in 1967 and has spent his professional life as a stockbroker, securities wholesaler and in real estate sales/marketing.

Head, who attended Ricks in 1980-81, left Rexburg last month for Ecuador to direct Semester of International Service. As founder of the non-profit organization, he was given the Distinguished Alumni Award for Humanitarian Service. He was Ricks Student Health Service physician since 1991. He was accompanied to Ecuador by his wife and five children.

Mosman is an assistant U.S. attorney in Portland, Ore., and the recipient of the Outstanding Young Alumni Award. He attended Ricks in 1975-76 and again in 1978-79 prior to graduating from Brigham Young University J. Reuben Clark Law School in 1984. As a federal prosecutor he has handled felony crimes, drug smuggling, and organized crime cases.

Cheney was given the Eliza R. Snow Award for the Performing and Fine Arts. She heads Valley Voices, a private children’s chorus, and is one of three founders of the Blankenship Memorial Project that provides piano lessons and singing experiences to children of low income families in the Bay Area of California. She attended Ricks in 1969-71.

Christensen was given the Alumni Outstanding Teacher Award. She has been an educator for 25 years. She taught English in the Bonneville School District for about 20 years. She retired last year and will leave for Mexico City this summer with her husband, Mark, to volunteer-teach English in at a LDS Church-owned school. She attended Ricks in 1957-59.

In a departure from previous years, the award recipients were invited on campus at varying times to instruct and visit with students. A luncheon was held in their honor and they were introduced to the studentbody at a devotional assembly.
