Religion Courses

Doctrine & Covenants 88:118

And as all have not faith, seek ye diligently and teach one another words of wisdom; yea, seek ye out of the best books words of wisdom; seek learning even by study and by faith.

The pioneers who founded BYU-Idaho were inspired with the understanding that no educational pursuit should take precedence over gaining a testimony of the Gospel of Jesus Christ.  The study of religion is the core of academics at BYU-Idaho and provides the basis for the university's existence.  

The following quotes discuss the nature of church education by current and past presidents of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.

 

 

   

Brigham Young counseled Brigham Young Academy President Karl G. Maeser that "you ought not to teach even the alphabet or the multiplication tables without the Spirit of God."

 

 

 

David O. McKay said, "True education does not consist merely in the acquiring of a few facts of science, history, literature, or art, but in the development of character."

 

 

 
Spencer W. Kimball  said the [Church's schools of higher education] "have no justification for its existence unless it builds character, creates and develops faith, and makes men and women of strength and courage, fortitude and service - men and women who will become stalwarts in the kingdom and bear witness of the divinity of the gospel of Jesus Christ."   It is justified only "if the morality of the graduates of  [these institutions] provide the music of hope for the inhabitants of this planet."

 

Gordon B. Hinckley said "What a tremendous thing it is, when all is said and done, to educate young people, to plant into their hearts and minds a desire for learning, to qualify themselves to take places of significance in this society of which they will become a part" 

"We are very proud of (BYU-Idaho).  It is a great school.  It is a great institution, and it has accomplished vast good."
