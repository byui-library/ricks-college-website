READING CENTER & E-L-D  (McKay Library 290)  356-1164 Center director - Karen Holt

mailto:holtk@ricks.edu

The Reading Center helps students with basic reading skills, as well as with skills to study college textbooks. Each student receives individual diagnosis, advising, and specific instruction. Most instruction is one-on-one with a tutor, supplemented by small group meetings and regular class work. Students may choose to register for credit or to work without credit.

Topics for literal comprehension include phonics, vocabulary, main idea, spelling, grammar, and rapid reading. Also taught are interpretive comprehension skills taught of inference, organizing ideas, purpose, and argument. Instruction in learning strategies skills include marking, flash cards, Cornell notes, mapping, note cards,
summary writing, and critical thinking. Reading classes are listed under English in the catalog.

English Language Development [E-L-D] (McKay Library 290) 356-1164

Center director - Greg Hazard    mailto:hazardg@ricks.edu

Students who are non-native speakers of English can receive extra help in the Reading Center. The English Language Development (ELD) program, located in the Reading Center, is designed to help students improve their listening, speaking, reading and writing skills. Students can get individual help with such things as pronunciation, grammar, vocabulary building, and listening comprehension. The one-on-one instruction can help students understand and adjust more quickly to the new culture they have entered. The Reading Center is a place where students can practice, get help, and improve their English under non-threatening and authentic situations. English 108 and 109 are courses especially designed to help non-native speakers of English improve oral and written language. These classes are listed under English in the catalog.

WRITING CENTER (McKay Library 288) 356-1189

(Click here for Writing Center home page)

Writing Center director - Sharon Papworth  mailto:papworths@ricks.edu

The Writing Center provides a relaxed environment for students who want to improve their writing skills. Qualified student assistants work individually with writers in free, confidential, twenty-minute sessions. Writing Assistants help students analyze drafts and learn writing strategies to help them create, revise, and edit college level papers. Because the Center teaches writing as "re- writing," a student may expect help revising a paper from rough draft form through to the final stages of editing. Writing Assistants and students work together to understand the following principles:


How to--

Discover and generate intriguing ideas

Write strong introductions and conclusions

Develop focused ideas through detailed, concrete discussions

Construct connecting sentences and transitions


Grammatical and mechanical errors are dealt with while students work with Assistants to shape ideas. Writing Assistants also aid students in organizing and focusing research papers, incorporating paraphrases and quotations, and documenting texts, using both the MLA and APA formats. The Writing Assistants involve students directly in the critical thinking/writing process. The Writing Center is a great resource for students especially when they come for assistance early in the drafting process.

356-1162 

mailto:croasmunt@ricks.edu

director - Tom Croasmun

 

The purpose of the Study Skills program is to provide assistance to students in developing, or improving, more effective study techniques which will lead to a successful academic career.

The program offers three options:

  1. Class (GenS 102) 2 hrs per/wk (block)

  2. Center study for credit

  3. Center study without credit

 

The Class:

GenS 102 is offered each block and meets two hours per week for 8 weeks. You will find it listed as General Studies 102 in the college catalog and the class schedule.

The Center:

Students may enroll in the study skills modules listed below by filling out an add/drop card in the Study Skills Center. Students are limited to studying one module at a time and two per block. Enrollment for credit must begin before the 12th week of the semester.

SERVICES FOR STUDENTS WITH A DISABILITY (SSD) (McKay Library 127) 356-1159 mailto:taylorr@ricks.edu

Counseling, advocacy, and coordination of services for students with a disability are available through the Office for Students with a Disability (SSD), located in the Learning Assistance office on the 1st floor of the McKay Library in room 127.  The purpose of the office is to help students overcome physical, educational, or attitudinal barriers which may prevent them from reaching their full educational potential. Staff members coordinate college support services thus aiding students with a disability to become integrated into the campus.
