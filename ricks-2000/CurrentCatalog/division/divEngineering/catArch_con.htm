Architecture and Construction industries encompass an incredibly wide range of careers. Educational programs are now offered to prepare students for transfer to other schools as well as job entry into challenging areas of employment.

 

The Department of Architecture and Construction offers five degree programs. Prior to enrollment in any of these majors, students should have developed good math, science, art, and manual skills. However, the required subject areas vary with major selected. Any questions regarding these courses or courses taken prior to Ricks should be directed to the major advisor.

 

The major in Architectural Drafting Technology (047), is a two-year specialized program for those individuals who desire to enter the architectural drafting job market at the completion of the program. This program can also be tailored for transfer to universities or colleges in the area of Construction Management. (Consult your advisor for transfer schools.)

 

The major in Carpentry and Building Construction (219), is a two-year specialized program for those individuals who desire to enter the construction industry upon graduation. Graduates with this degree and field experience can expect to fill such construction positions as carpenters, construction superintendents, general contractors and other positions in the construction industry.

 

The major in Technology Education (276), is a program that prepares industrial/technology/vocational teachers for junior high/middle schools, high schools, and post-high-school programs. Additional education is required to prepare for teacher education in college or university. This is a transfer program designed to complete all general education requirements for transfer to a four-year university to complete a Bachelor of Science degree in Technology Education.

 

The major in Pre-Architecture (293), is a rigorous, college level, transfer oriented program. This transfer program is designed to meet many of the first two-year core requirements for an architectural curriculum. To accomplish this objective the student must consult his advisor to tailor the transfer curriculum to meet the core requirements of the students selected college of architecture.

 

The major in Construction Management (303), is a transfer program that provides basic construction classes and allows students to complete all of the General Education requirements for transfer to a four-year university to complete a Bachelor of Science degree in Construction Management.

The Department also offers woodworking and construction classes of general interest open to the general student body and community.

COURSE DESCRIPTIONS

 

ARCHITECTURE

 

ARCH 134 DataCAD (2)

Two 2-hour classes per week

 

Introduction to computer-aided drafting using DataCAD software. Microcomputer software experiences dealing with architectural drawing creation (floor plans and details), dimensioning and text, storage, retrieval, plotting, and entity modification associated with drawing changes and revisions.

 

ARCH 155 Residential Drafting and Design (3)

Two 2-hour classes per week, includes studio lab, two 1-hour periods per week

 

A beginning class covering fundamental principles of efficient house planning with the correlation of drafting techniques. Emphasis includes building materials and practices, codes and working drawings. Working drawings include plot plan, footing and foundations, floor plans, elevations, sections/details, and electrical plans.

 

ARCH 156 Commercial Structural Drawing (3)

Two 2-hour classes per week

Studio lab two 1-hour periods per week

Prerequisite: ARCH 155 Residential Drafting and Design

 

Design and delineation of commercial buildings and details. Design processes, space standards and relationships. Static analysis of structural members. Delineation technique and presentation of working drawings for commercial construction. (Fall)

 

ARCH 240 Architectural Detailing (3)

Prerequisite: ARCH 155 or consent of instructor

Two 2-hour classes per week includes studio lab, Two 1-hour periods per week

The course is designed to provide the student with drafting training and experience in detailing of construction problems dealing with wood, steel, concrete, and masonry. Plot plan, subdivision planning, cabinet elevation and detailing, construction terminology, drafting technique, and current detailing technique practices will be emphasized. (Winter and Summer)

 

ARCH 256 Rendering & Perspective (3)

Prerequisite: ARCH 155 Residential Drafting and Planning or consent of instructor

Two 2-hour classes per week

Methods and procedures involved in rendering and drawing one, two, and three point perspectives. (Winter)

 

ARCH 290 Special Problems (1-4)

Selected problem solving in a variety of design and drafting areas. Computer graphics, design, architecture, etc.

 

CONSTRUCTION

 

CONST 100 Introduction to Architecture and Construction (2)

Two class hours per week

 

A survey course designed to acquaint the student with the professional aspect of the architecture and construction industry. A special focus on employability and personal skills needed for the professions. For majors and non-majors. (Fall)

CONST 101 Woodwork Fundamentals (3)

Three 2-hour classes per week

 

A study of basic woodworking skills for both the novice and more advanced woodworker. Project planning and design, wood and wood products, hand tool , power tool, and joinery techniques will be emphasized.

 

CONST 200 Advanced Woodworking (3)

Prerequisite: CONST 101 or equivalent; check with your advisor

Three 2-hour classes per week

 

Advanced study and practices of woodworking techniques used in the design and construction of furniture. Emphasis will be placed on the use of materials and fine joinery techniques. Open for general projects.

 

CONST 201 Cabinet Construction (3)

Prerequisite: CONST 101 or equivalent;

check with your advisor

Two 3-hour classes per week

 

A study of construction materials and methods used in professional cabinetmaking. Classroom, lab experiences give students the understanding of designing, planning, estimating, materials and methods used in making kitchen and other built-in type of cabinets. Both traditional and modern cabinetmaking methods will be emphasized.

 

CONST 208 Heating, Ventilating, and Air Conditioning Systems (2)

 

Prerequisite: CONST 210

Three 1-hour classes per week

 

Thermal and psychometrics environment in buildings. Heating, ventilation, and air conditioning; heating loads, air distribution, equipment selection; energy efficient and solar construction. (Winter)

 

CONST 210 Framing Systems (3)

Three 2-hour classes per week

 

A study of construction materials and methods of construction framing. Classroom, lab experiences, and construction site tours give students an understanding of procedure, framing parts, and applications of materials. Construction safety, codes, blueprint reading, new framing technology, and both residential and commercial construction will be emphasized.

 

CONST 211 Finishing Systems in Construction (2)

Two 2-hour classes per week

 

Managing carpentry finish work; application of interior and exterior trim. Recognizing, specifying, and requiring quality workmanship. (Winter)

 

CONST 215 Construction Estimating (3)

 

Prerequisite: CONST 210, ARCH 155 or CIS 140, CONST 217

Two 2-hour classes per week

 

Interpreting plans and specifications; estimating material quantities, labor, cost, overhead, and profit on residential and commercial structures; bidding procedures, competitive bidding. (Winter)

 

CONST 217 Concrete and Masonry Technology (3)

Three 2-hour classes per week

 

A study of the versatility, durability, and mix design of quality concrete. Also included is the study of the use of masonry in modern construction.

 

CONST 218 Plumbing Systems (2)

Two 2-hour classes per week

 

Basic plumbing design, installation and applications; understanding the plumbing trade to effectively manage construction projects. (Fall)

 

CONST 241 Electrical Systems in Construction (2)

Two 2-hour classes per week

 

Study of materials, design of circuits, and inspection for electrical heat, light and power installation in homes and small buildings. The class covers the National Electrical Code and local codes in Idaho communities. (Winter)

 

CONST 290 Special Problems (1-4)

 

Selected problem solving in a variety of construction areas. May involve special assignment, laboratory, and on the job experience. Approved after consultation with instructor in charge.
