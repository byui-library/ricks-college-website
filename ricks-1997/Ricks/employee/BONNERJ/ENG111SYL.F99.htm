WELCOME to English 111! Our basic objective is to write (and/or revise) 5,000 words, as clearly and accurately as possible. We will approach this goal with the following writing assignments:



1. Several titled, attractively formatted, in-class papers of 500-700 words each. The first of these carries 50 possible points; the rest are 10 possible (equivalent to weekly quiz). First is DUE on Friday, September 3!



2. A documented (research) paper on a subject of your choice, 1,000 to 1,500 words (2,000 word maximum). Use MLA or APA documentation. May be done in partnership. (50 points possible.) DUE on Friday, September 24!



3. A careful revision of one of the fifty-points-possible papers listed in items 1.-2. above. This revision will constitute the final exam. The revision (final exam) grade is based on the grade of the original paper and the corrections or improvements made. (There are no automatic grades; 25 points possible.)



  • Individual / Group Portfolio. Individually, or as a small group of two to four students, please submit a collection (portfolio) of four essays, 500-700 words each. You may reuse one or more of your in-class writings for this project. If you do use a formerly submitted paper, please re-submit a fresh, unmarked, ungraded and revised copy. You may also use a newly-composed, previously ungraded essay. Possible subjects for newly-created papers are found under "Writing Topics" in this syllabus. Other possibilities can be found in our text. Please use a title page and/or a table of contents for the portfolio. Each essay should also be individually titled at the top of its first page. As usual, please double space and use only one side of the paper.


5. For those who are interested it is possible to receive an extra writing grade by either of the following methods:



1--Keep a daily journal (at least one page per day) for each day of the semester. (This is figured on a seven day per week basis although it is possible to write several pages one day and then skip a day or two of writing.)



2--Share a revised version of one of your previously graded major (50 points possible) papers with the class by reading the paper to us. (This revised/shared paper will also count as your final exam, making it worth an additional 25 possible points.)



If either of the above options is taken then the lowest writing score will be dropped. (Choose only one option; there is no automatic grade given on the items above!)

WRITING TOPICS:



1. A documented (research) paper--open choice of subjects. (May be done in partnership.)

2. A personal history (autobiography)--normally done in class



3. A short biography (a paper about someone you know personally or about a contemporary or historical figure, secular or religious)



4. A description: fruit, a mechanical device, a room, person, etc.



5. A cause and effect paper: how one thing causes another



6. A process analysis: how something works; how to make or do something



7. A comparison/contrast: show the similarities and/or differences between two things



8. A scene: a photograph, painting, landscape or city scene and your feelings about it



9. A book, movie, talk, CD or performance review



10. Classification: place like things together



11. Definition: sometimes an explanation of a word can be expanded into an essay by using details, examples, and so forth



12. A case study (original): the problem, possible solutions, the probable outcome(s)



13. An evaluation of a piece of equipment, machine, etc.



14. A dialogue or conversation



15. A free writing (stream-of-consciousness)



16. "Things have a way of turning out so badly" T. Williams (Or, so well).



17. A letter.



18. My favorite place to be alone with my thoughts.



19. The people who have influenced me most and why.



  • Argumentation (establishing truth), Persuasion (convincing the reader), Exposition (explaining, giving reasons, describing a process-how to make or do something).




FURTHER INFORMATION ON WRITING (AND READING) ASSIGNMENTS:



1. Writing projects suggested in the Riverside Guide may be acceptable for filling some of our writing assignments.



2. Please re-submit one of your major (50 points possible), previously graded papers for the final exam. Turn in the original paper (including the grade) and the revised version of the paper. (Put the revised paper on top and label it "Revision" [in addition to the regular title]--25 points possible.) The revised grade is based on the original grade and is usually somewhat higher if all of the marked errors are corrected and a response is made to suggestions for improvement.



3. In writing your paper, pay particular attention to an effective beginning and ending, and the transitions between paragraphs.



4. All papers are to be typed as follows:



a. Double space



b. Leave a one inch margin all around except at the top of the first page where a two inch margin is required. This two inch margin allows room to type your title (centered), and/or heading. If desired you may use a title page or type the identifying information (except for the title, of course) at the end of the paper.



c. Please number your pages (if you have more than one).



d. Please do not use plastic or cardboard covers or blank pages. If you use a printer, and have more than one page, please separate and staple the pages.



5. Writings are graded approximately half (50%) on content (what you say and how you say it) and half (the other 50%) on mechanics (spelling, punctuation, usage, neatness, format, etc.). If you do not understand a proofreader or correction symbol or comment, please ask about it. You are welcome to discuss a paper with me before or after it is graded. See me before or after class or during office hour. (Please note: It is not appropriate to ask an instructor to proofread your paper. I t is appropriate, however, to ask for his overall impression of it and to ask specific questions about usage (grammar), word choice, etc. This is usually done by discussing the paper together.)





6. A twenty-five point system is used in grading writing assignments. Equivalent letter grades are as follows: 23=A, 22.5=A-, 22=B+, 21=B, 20=B-, 19.5=C+, 19=C, 17.5=C-, 17=D+, 16=D, 15=D-, below 15=F. (Fifty-points-possible papers are graded with this same system but the points are doubled [or added together in the case of split grades].) The grading is holistic, based on a blended evaluation of content and writing mechanics.



7. Our audience is the class, the family, and others we know well. The instructor is the grader but probably should not be considered the main audience in most cases. Voice is how we sound on paper. Our sense of audience affects our voice, our writing personality.



8. Again, our main goals are to write accurately and clearly.



9. In addition to the typed writing assignments there will be preparation quizzes based on readings, class discussions, etc. No make-up work will be given for missed quizzes but two low scores will be dropped. (It may be possible to take a quiz in advance in the case of a pre-arranged, excused absence.) Some quizzes are on the syllabus; others are not. Generally each quiz is worth ten possible points.



10. Final grades are calculated by dividing the points you have earned by the total points possible. This percentage is then converted to a letter grade. (For example, 70%-72%=C-, 73%-76%=C, 77%-79%=C+, etc.)



11. Attendance and participation may be factors in grading. (For example, three or more unexcused absences lower your final grade.) Please be on time for class.



12. Please read the assigned selections before coming to class, and before the listed due date. In class, be prepared to read aloud, to comment on or explain what you have read, to teach what you have learned to someone else, and otherwise to make timely and appropriate contributions to our learning experience (including by careful listening). Occasionally we will read in class--either silently as individuals, or aloud in small groups or as a full class.



13. Please keep all graded papers and quizzes until after the final grade is received.
