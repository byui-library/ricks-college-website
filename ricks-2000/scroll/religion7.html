Missionaries find success serving in Rexburg despite LDS majority

by Katie McMurray
mcm00003@byui.edu
Scroll Staff

Everyday they check the mail. When it arrives, all family members gather around, waiting impatiently for the envelope to be torn.

The anxiety turns inside each stomach. The letter is finally out of the envelope:

“You have been called to serve as a missionary for The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints in the Idaho Pocatello Mission.”

This is the mission that missionaries serve in the Rexburg area and at BYU-Idaho.

“I was never disappointed. I was hoping to go foreign but when I got called state-side it didn’t really matter. I was excited to come,” Elder Jared Martin, from Glen Carvin, Ill., currently serving in Rexburg, said. “Pocatello had never crossed my mind. It was cool to be called to the states and somewhere I’d been.”

“I was sort of ambivalent, but [I thought] that’s where I’m supposed to go, so that’s where I’ll go,” Elder William Woolston from Baltimore, Md., said.

“After I recovered from flying in the puddle jumper [of a plane], I was ready to meet my mission president,” Woolston said.

“[After landing in Idaho,] it reminded me of home with the small-town atmosphere. I’d been here before so it wasn’t a big shock,” Martin said.

“There’s a lot of members, but there’s a lot of people who are not of our faith who need the gospel in their life. There’s a lot of work to be done,” Martin said. “During this past week, we’ve had an explosion of referrals. On average we probably have two discussions a week, but it will probably go up.”

“There is a high percentage of LDS people and it can be tough, but anywhere will have it’s pros and cons,” Woolston said. “Members are nice and helpful, but its difficult to find people to teach. While tracting, one out of 15 houses might be a nonmember.”

“We always work through members. We have to, or we would never have anyone to teach,” Woolston said.

“The only frustration comes from the members who act like this is Zion, that everyone is a member, when really it’s not. We try to get them missionary minded,” Martin said.

In a place like Rexburg, missionary work can become frustrating. In order to overcome frustration you have to keep a positive attitude, Woolston said. It all comes from attitude.

Elder Martin’s favorite thing about being a missionary is “to bring the gospel to those who don’t have it and to see how their lives will change.”

“[I like] seeing people change. it’s great to see people decide to make that change in their life,” Woolston said.
