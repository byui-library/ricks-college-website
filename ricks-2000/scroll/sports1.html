Volleyball goes 2-1 on conference road trip

Nathan Hathaway
nat97013@byui.edu
scroll staff

They got the two wins, but it took a little more work than expected.

The Vikings expected to walk into Rangely, Colo., cruise to an easy win over Colorado Northwestern Community College and breeze out of town. After all, CNCC had not won a league game all season.
The Ricks College Vikings came out a little slowly and could not get much momentum in game one, but still managed to win 30-17.

Ricks got progressively better as the match went on. Coach JoAnn Reeve did some substituting in game two, putting Lauralee Budd in for team powerhouse Laura Nielsen and subbing Amanda Sutton in for team captain Malia Koelliker. The moves proved wise as the subs helped Ricks win the game 30-12.

The third game seemed to pose no challenge for the Vikings as Ricks blew the Spartans out of the water 30-9.

The Viking’s next match was with the NJCAA's No. 4 team, Utah Valley State College.

Ricks could not get past UVSC, losing in three games. The Lady Vikings did not seem to show up for the first two games, losing by 15 and 17 points, respectively. The opening moments seemed to set the tempo as Ricks botched two early serves. Setter Ashley Fairbanks said the missed serves may have been the turning point for the match.

“It got us started out on the wrong foot,” Fairbanks said. “If we would have had that [first serve], things may have turned out a little bit differently.”

Those first two losses lit a fire under the team. Game three was markedly different. Ricks came out strong and was ahead before falling 31-29 to the Wolverines.
“Our team has never really played well against the great teams," Reeve said. "If you're going to go after a school like that, you've at least got to serve.”

The College of Eastern Utah looked to be another walk in, win and breeze out kind of match. But that can be tough to do when a person shoots himself in the foot. Ricks could not totally shut out CEU in the first game, though the Vikings still managed to skate by with a 30-27 win.

Game two proved less lucky for the Vikings as they lost 30-26, due in large part to eight missed serves.

“There's no excuse for that,” Reeve said.

After two games, a few of the Vikings' key players were looking at negative hitting percentages. In the second game, Ricks killed only seven percent of its hits.

Reeve tried to calm her team down during the break between games two and three, but the third game wasn't much better as Ricks narrowly won 30 28.

“The hitters were doing what they could with the sets," Fairbanks said. "It was still a rough game, but we just kept pushing one point at a time and came out on top.”

After struggling through two wins and a loss in the match, the Vikings killed 60 percent of their hits in the fourth game to put it away 30-14.

“All of a sudden we came out, and we blew them out of the water," Fairbanks said. "I think we realized that we could beat them, and we wanted to go home, so we beat them really fast.”

“I was happy to come out with the wins we did get,” Reeve said. “They all played hard, so I'm happy with that.”
