November 11, 2001

BYU-Idaho teacher

spends year in China


REXBURG, Idaho –Returning home from long trips is always a relief. Returning home from a year of teaching in a foreign country is probably another feeling entirely. Just ask Jim Greene and his family of Rexburg.

The Greenes returned to the United States this summer after spending almost a full year in China teaching English as part of a program sponsored by Brigham Young University in Provo, Utah.

"We had mixed feelings," said Jim. "It was hard to leave our friends. But it was a good feeling to come back. We had two grandchildren born while we were gone."

Jim, who served an LDS mission in Hong Kong in the early 1960, says he always wanted to go back, but was tied up with work and family responsibilities.

With the approval of his youngest daughter, Jessica, who was 15 at the time and playing volleyball, Jim and his wife JoAnn decided it was time to take advantage of his sabbatical time from Ricks College where he teaches in the Family Science Department.

Through the BYU Kennedy Center for International Studies at BYU, they were assigned to Shandong Teachers University in Jinan, a city of 5 million people located about 200 miles south of Beijing.

The original plan was for Jim and JoAnn to teach while Jessica took high school courses over the Internet and through home study. Jim was an instructor in junior level English and taught about six classes of students ages 21 to 23 years. JoAnn taught two freshman classes, one sophomore class and one post-graduate course, all in reading, writing and oral English.

"At first I was terrified of the post-graduate class – many of them were university professors. These students had eight years of schooling in English. But I loved every minute of it," said JoAnn.

An unexpected event occurred when the university offered a teaching position to Jessica. "They offered me 12 classes of 74 students each," said Jessica, who turned 16 while she was in China. "But that was too many. So I taught six classes of 74 to 80 students, children from 11 to 13-years old. I taught oral English, mostly through music."

Jessica had some significant experiences while teaching in China. She provided the voice for a recording of an English translation of Chinese elementary books, a recording that is now used by around 10,000 students in the Shandong province to practice English.

"We also got to do some television commercials," Jessica said. "I had to taste test soy milk on TV, and the first time it wasn’t good enough, so we had to do it again. I still don’t like soy milk very much."

The Greenes were in China when a Chinese jet and an American military plane collided, causing tensions between the two nations. "We thought that everyone would be angry at us and at America," Jim said. "But many of the students and faculty members came up to us and expressed their regrets over the accident."

Other experiences the Greenes had include a close friendship with Howard Adams, who was captured during the Korean War and whose release was never pursued. Angered by the way he felt the American government treated him, he decided to stay in Asia and has lived there ever since.

Jessica particularly enjoyed being able to go dancing and shopping on the weekends. "I learned very fast that many American young people don’t know how to dance," she said. "And shopping in the street shops was a blast because everything was so cheap."

JoAnn’s favorite experience came during Christmas when the university allows an annual Christmas program, and JoAnn threw herself whole-heartedly into the event. "It was a great time to explain a little more about Christ and his life," said JoAnn. "We had Christmas stories, and Christmas songs, and Santa Claus even showed up. But the best part was being able to talk about Christ. The theme of the party was Christmas is Love. I know we planted a lot of seeds."

The Greenes witnessed many signs of spiritual hunger; of students wanting to believe in something higher than themselves. "It reminded me of the scripture that speaks of those who are would accept that truth, but they know not where to find it," Jim said.
