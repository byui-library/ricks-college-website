Locals choose to live in apartments

by Elizabeth Hatchett
HAT01004@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

The majority of students at BYU-Idaho have traveled some distance to come here.

Then there are the local students who live at home while attending college because it is cheaper or they feel more comfortable in the home environment.

Some of the locals choose to live away from home.

Kacy Neibaur, a sophomore, has lived in Rexburg the majority of her life.

“I choose to live away from home because I wasn’t involved in the ward [on campus]. I wasn’t getting to know other students,” Neibaur said.

Living away from home, students have the opportunity to meet new people; they may also have more opportunities to participate in activities. When friends get a group together they are more inclined to stop by your apartment and invite you to come along.

“When you live at home people don’t just stop by, and often you don’t know what is going on,” Ashley Mackay, a sophomore from Rexburg, said.

Living away from campus, Neibaur always had to worry about having a car or battling for parking spaces in the mornings when she came for classes. When she moved into student-approved housing, it became easier, she said.

“It is more convenient to live near the town and campus,” Neibaur said. “You can walk everywhere and you don’t have to worry about parking spaces.”

Many students choose to live away from home because they want to be more independent. They want to learn how to be responsible for their own finances. Others want to learn how to interact with people besides their family.

"It is fun to have roommates and to learn how to deal with other people. I am also learning how to manage my time and money,” Neibaur said.

The major disadvantage for living away from home is the price. Housing bills, food, laundry and even phone bills must be paid.

“It costs so much more to live away from home,” Barbara Scoresby, a sophomore from Rexburg, said.

For some, an advantage of living away from home is making small decisions, like what food to purchase.

Because Neibaur is so close to home, she returns home about once a week to visit her family.

“The advantages of living away from home are that you are more social and involved. It is more convenient and you learn how to deal with other people,” Neibaur said.

“You get the best of both worlds when you live away but close to home. Home is close by, but you get the college experience,” Candace Fitzgerald, a junior from Rexburg, said.
