News release and statistics - Issued day of announcement

CANDIDACY STATUS ANNOUNCEMENT
March 22, 2001
President David A. Bednar

Brothers and sisters, good afternoon. Welcome. We are delighted you would come and spend a few minutes with us.

It was almost exactly nine months ago that most or all of us were gathered together in the Hart Auditorium to hear the announcement by President Gordon B. Hinckley that Ricks College would become a baccalaureate institution named BYU-Idaho. I commented then that President Hinckley had announced the beginning of a process of transition, not a finished product. I would emphasize again today that is an important perspective for all of us to keep in mind during this ongoing transition.

Today I am pleased to announce that we have arrived at an important way station on the journey to becoming BYU-Idaho. Let me emphasize we have not arrived at our ultimate destination, but we are certainly making rapid progress. Yesterday I received a letter from Sandra E. Elman, the executive director of the Northwest Commission on Colleges and Universities. Before I share with you the text of the letter, I would like you to note that it is one page long. Those of you who deal with accreditation issues know brevity is a very good sign. So just as a missionary will feel the mission call envelope for thickness to try to determine if it is foreign or domestic, yesterday as I was walking into my office I was squeezing the envelope. Is this thick or is this skinny? And I was very pleased it was skinny. So let me share with you just a few items from this brief letter:

Dear President Bednar:

This is to report the action of the Commission on Colleges and Universities regarding the major substantive change proposal submitted by Ricks College in February of 2001. The proposal requested approval to introduce 46 degree programs at the baccalaureate level, a

level higher than that presently approved for the institution by the Commission. In addition, the proposal included a change in the mission and goals of the institution as well as the change in name from Ricks College to Brigham Young University-Idaho (BYU-Idaho).

After careful and thoughtful review of the proposal and related supplemental documentation, the Commission approved all of the elements of this major substantive change . . . .

That is good news! Northwest was careful to emphasize a particular standard which focuses on the employment of professionally qualified faculty with primary commitment to the institution and representative of each field or program in which it offers major work. The Commission wants to be sure we are using a process that will bring to this campus the professionally qualified faculty we will need in our baccalaureate programs.

That is it; that is what they said. Dr. Elman concluded with this paragraph:

. . . the Commission requests that: (1) the progress report scheduled for fall 2002 which is to address all five general recommendations of the fall 1999 Evaluation Committee Report be expanded to include a section which informs the Commission of the institution’s progress in selection and employment of appropriately qualified faculty; . . .

Let me remind you there were five recommendations from our accreditation review in 1999. A report is due on our progress to meet those recommendations in 2002. That is what is being referred to here.

The second key point from the Commission is that "the full-scale reevaluation of the college [for accreditation] be scheduled for spring 2004."

So candidacy status has been achieved. We are scheduled for a full-scale accreditation review in 2004, and there will be an interim report in 2002—now expanded to describe the process we are using to obtain appropriately qualified faculty. That is the news. I think you might want to clap.

It seems to me this is a most appropriate opportunity to express thanks and appreciation. Now as I do so, would you please recognize that this is a way station along the road, not the final destination. But in these few moments let us catch our breath and acknowledge things are headed in the right direction and look good. There are a number of folks I want to acknowledge.

First and foremost, I express appreciation to all of you. This process and what we are doing, in my personal judgment, is nothing short of miraculous. There was not a planning process that preceded the announcement. There was an announcement and then the beginning of a planning process. To be where we were on June 21, 2000, and to be where we are today on March 22 is, frankly, miraculous. There has been a great deal of work and sweat equity and some tears and probably some blood from a number of people; and that is not over and will continue during this transition. But to all of you here and across the entire campus and to the people who have had any role in making this process work, thank you very much on behalf of the institution. And I convey that appreciation on behalf of the Brethren as well.

Secondly, I want to express appreciation to the faculty. This transition, in particular, touches all of the faculty members on the campus; and I express appreciation to them. This is a monumental undertaking to move from associate degrees to baccalaureate degrees with some of the parameters within which we are working. And so, to all of the faculty, thank you for the work you have done and for the work you will continue to do.

To the department heads who have marshaled and directed the efforts within each of the departments, thank you. And I express appreciation for your good work.

I would like to ask all the members of the Academic Council who are in attendance today to please stand. The Academic Council provides overall direction to our academic programs at Ricks College. There is no single body on this campus that has worked more tirelessly and more effectively than the Academic Council during this process. These folks deserve a round of applause.

Will Scott Bergstrom, Jim Gee, and Max Checketts please stand? I would likewise like to compliment these three men. Taking all of the information generated from the faculty, the departments, and the Academic Council and fashioning it into a prospectus in the time period in which it has been done is no small chore. It is Herculean in nature. And as we visit with the folks from Northwest, they are universally positive in their praise for the quality of the materials they have received. The prospectus is a function of many, many people. But a few individuals have shaped and fashioned it in a particular way, and these are the three men who have made that happen. They deserve a real round of applause.

And the last person I would like to highlight is Don Bird. Don, please stand up. I do not know how to say this because words cannot convey what I have observed and experienced with Don Bird. I have watched him be as nervous as a cat on a hot tin roof, I have seen him on mornings when he has obviously not slept very much, and I have seen him receive inspiration and direction from heaven. He does not like this kind of attention, but he does deserve a round of applause as the academic vice president for the leadership he has provided in this effort. Don, I think we ought to take a minute and hear anything you would like to say to these good folks.

Don Bird

We are pleased today to see us at this point. Basically, we have drawn the blueprints and are still tweaking them. We have footings and foundations and framing and a lot to go. We are confident we will get there. We are pleased with what we are seeing in terms of the blueprints.

Let me just comment on one aspect of us getting to this point. I really appreciate the faculty department chairs and the deans for the great work they have done in getting people to apply for faculty positions. We have 38 positions—25 new positions and 13 positions to replace people who are retiring. So we have advertised for about 38 positions. We have had over 650 applicants. What is much more exciting than that is the quality of the applicants. You cannot believe the quality of these people! They come from all walks of life in terms of business, industry, colleges, and universities. They are very well qualified, and we are thrilled. They are wonderful people. And we are excited about where we are going in the future.

Again, thanks so much for so many of you who have been players in this. And it is just getting started. We have got so much further to go, but it is exciting. A group of us met this past hour. You push and tug and pull and so on, but fabulous ideas came out of different groups and different divisions and different departments. We came together, did some great talking, and came up with some wonderful ideas. And we have seen that so many times, and we expect that type of cooperative effort will continue. We are trying to look down the road ten years from now and are trying to benefit the students. They are the ones who will be blessed by this planning and some of the things we are putting together, and that is our goal—to make sure that students are benefited by their great experience here at Ricks College. Thank you.

President Bednar

Let me take just a moment and review some issues relative to time lines. We have indicated all along that when candidacy status is achieved, at that point we can officially become BYU-Idaho. Please listen carefully. We will not do so immediately. As of today, we have achieved candidacy status but we are still Ricks College. Commencement in April will be as Ricks College. Commencement in June will be as Ricks College. During the summer there will be a number of transition issues through which we will have to work. If you will think in terms of a threshold, then we cross over the threshold this fall and officially become Brigham Young University-Idaho. We will be referred to as such when we commence the fall semester and begin offering our first junior-level classes. As we begin in the fall, we will, in fact, start answering the phones Brigham Young University-Idaho rather than Ricks College.

I would conclude by emphasizing two things. First, the work is just beginning. Just as last June was the announcement of a process of transition, what we announced today is that the work is just beginning. Much has been done, but there is much yet to do. And I would say to those areas other than academics, I think the academic areas to this point have felt the major weight of the responsibility for the transition in the planning, the pushing, the pulling, and so forth. But in many of the support areas, you can really feel the weight now resting upon you. And that will continue. There are ripple effects and consequences growing out of our plans and decisions that will impact every area of the campus.

We appreciate you for taking the time to come to this meeting. I thought it would be appropriate for us to gather together, especially on a day when we have a significant event like this, and conclude with a benediction of gratitude. I have invited Brother Brent Kinghorn to offer that benediction.
