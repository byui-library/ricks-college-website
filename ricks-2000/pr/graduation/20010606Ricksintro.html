RICKS COLLEGE COMMENCEMENT

Introduction and background of

Thomas E. Ricks

presented by
President David A. Bednar
June 6, 2001

It is now my privilege to introduce our commencement speaker. No one in this audience has ever seen him on the Ricks College campus. But, undoubtedly, you have felt his spirit and influence. He was born in Kentucky on July 21, 1828. He joined the Church in 1845, approximately four years after his parents were baptized in Southern Illinois after meeting the Prophet Joseph Smith.

His family moved to Nauvoo and helped construct the temple there, until leaving for Council Bluffs in February of 1846.

Upon arriving in the Salt Lake Valley, he was asked to head back east and assist a company of emigrants. In November of 1849, he joined a group led by Parley P. Pratt to explore the land as far south of Salt Lake City as present-day St. George. In 1856 he was called by President Brigham Young to help settle Las Vegas. In late 1856, he and a number of others were called to aid the Martin Handcart Company. In 1859, he settled in Cache Valley where he served for three years as sheriff.

In December 1882, he was called to serve as the bishop of the Bannock Ward of Cache Valley Stake, which included present-day Southeastern Idaho. And in January of 1883 he arrived at the present site of Rexburg to establish a colony. Shortly thereafter, the town was named in his honor, using the German version of Ricks, which is Rex.

He was named as the president of the Bannock Stake Board of Education in 1888 and formally dedicated the Bannock Stake Academy on November 12, 1888. Although he had little formal education, he understood its value. And, in honor of him, the name of the Bannock Stake Academy was changed in 1902 to Ricks Academy.

It is indeed an honor for me to introduce Thomas E. Ricks as our commencement speaker.

Commencement Address by Thomas E. Ricks
