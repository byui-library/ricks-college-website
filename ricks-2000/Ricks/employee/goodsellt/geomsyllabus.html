Text: Roads to Geometry 2nd Edition. E. Wallace and S. West.

 

Prerequisites: Math 301 or Instructors permission.

 

Course Objective:  The two main objectives of this course are to provide a solid background in the subject of Geometry and to prepare students to teach this material themselves in the future. To fulfill these objectives we will study the role of axiomatics systems and there use in proving theorems in Euclidean and other geometries.  This will entail learning problem solving skills and strategies.  These skills will also be useful in other branches of mathematics.  We will also learn how to investigate geometry, ask questions and make conjectures and we will emphasize how to accurately and concisely communicate our knowledge.  The history of Euclidean geometry and its central role in western thought will be discussed as well as current trends in geometric research.  As we cover these objectives we will also stress how to teach the material, including using technology and boardwork and how to avoid common misconceptions in geometry.

 

Homework:  The homework assignments are meant to give the students an opportunity to develop their skills in geometry and to assess that the course objectives are indeed being met.  Since there are several objectives for the course there will be a variety of types of assignments given in this course.  They will include the following:       

            Textbook exercises:  These will cover the basic material in geometry and help develop the problem solving and investigative skills needed in geometry and other subjects.

            Papers:  Topics may include historical aspects of geometry and how it relates to other fields and the world around us.

            Board presentations:  These will give the students an opportunity to practice there communication and teaching skills as well as to demonstrate their mastery of the subject.

            Computer projects:  These will give the student experience in using a major geometry software package to further investigate and learn geometry themselves and to train them in its use as future teachers.

 

Classroom interaction and discussion will also be a major component of both learning and assessment.  Thus regular and punctual attendance and participation is required for this course.

 

Portfolio:  All assignments and materials from this course should be compiled in a portfolio.  This will be checked periodically throughout the semester and graded at the end of the semester.
