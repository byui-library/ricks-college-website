Ricks College News Release
August 17, 2000

President urges Ricks employees to start looking for "new cheese"

REXBURG, Idaho - A No. 1 national bestseller on dealing with change has become the most popular book in Rexburg these days.

Ricks College President David A. Bednar hopes the book "Who Moved My Cheese?" by Spencer Johnson will have a significant impact on the nearly 1,000 employees at the LDS Church-owned college that is on the verge of becoming a four-year university.

The book of fewer than 100 pages was given to Ricks employees late last week in preparation for the president's traditional pre-school address Wednesday morning.

Written by the author of "The One Minute Manager," the story is a parable about two mice named Sniff and Scurry and two "little people" named Hem and Haw who are forced to deal with change when their life-sustaining cheese runs out.

Sniff and Scurry immediately head out in search of new cheese, while Hem and Haw, who have been given more complex human brains, are reluctant to adjust to their altered environment. Hem stubbornly refuses to even move, while Haw takes his time but finally ventures out on a successful search through the maze in which they live.

Bednar referred to both the book and to dramatic changes in the growing LDS Church to make several points during Wednesday's talk.

"Ricks College is a place where an unrepentant 'Hem' will be miserable," he said, referring to the little person who refused to adapt to change. He said Hem was ruled primarily by fear rather by faith.

Hem's friend, Haw, took a leap of faith and began searching for cheese. Quoting the book, Bednar read: "He realized his fears were making things worse. So he did what he would do if he wasn't afraid. He moved in a new direction. As he started running down the dark corridor he began to smile. Haw didn't realize it yet, but he was discovering what nourished his soul. He was letting go and trusting what lay ahead for him, even though he did not know exactly what it was."

The president said, "If you or I are looking for a very static and very predicable work environment, Ricks College is not the place to be. . . . Things will not stay the same."

He said it is now time to get over the shock of the announcement and "to apply our minds and our hearts to understanding and inquiring and receiving inspiration . . . so this change can move forward. . . My prayer is we will have eyes to see and ears to hear."

Bednar, who in the past has told employees that they were building "a ship of curious workmanship" (a reference to a story in the Book of Mormon), said, "The ship of curious workmanship has been constructed, it has set sail, it is out of the protective harbor and headed out to sea." LDS Church President Gordon B. Hinckley and the Board of Trustees has changed the ship's course, he said, in reference to the June 21 announcement that the nation's largest private junior college would soon become a four-year school named Brigham Young University - Idaho.

"We are going someplace we have never gone before," he said.

Bednar described the decision as "visionary, forward looking and extremely bold."

However, he said Hinckley and the Board of Trustees gave few details on how the changes are to be achieved. They provided the vision and left the details to the college to work out. "What we develop will touch students on this campus and also reach throughout the world," he said.

Bednar said the decision has four main implications.

First, this is a significant spiritual event and process. "This is not really about two-year versus four-year status. . . . The decision deals mainly with faith." He added, "The first and foremost requirement is each individual understand that this is a process of faith."

Second, he said revelation will be given "to all of us in our respective responsibilities" in making decisions to implement the changes.

Third, "The Lord and the Board have great trust and confidence in us as an institution."

Fourth, "The Lord and the Board expect that Ricks College will be a place of inspired innovation. . . . We have been charged to create new wine and put it in a new bottle."

Bednar said the name change from Ricks to BYU-Idaho is a bold move, but one that will have great influence in the world. "Do you think that President Hinckley and the Board do not know what they are doing by naming this institution BYU-Idaho? . . . The Brethren today see an influence in the world of graduates from BYU whether they come from Idaho, Hawaii or BYU in Provo," he said.

Later in the day during a faculty meeting challenged the faculty to be innovative in their ideas when presenting proposals for four-year baccalaureate programs. While it is necessary to see what others have done, he said, "We've got to look for new and better approaches to education.

"If you do not get excited about this, if your spirit does not stir, I do not know what to say," he told the faculty. "This is a remarkable opportunity and challenge to all of us."
