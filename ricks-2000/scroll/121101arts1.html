Rock concert becomes new hit on campus

by Ashley Andrus
Arts Staff

Lead singer of the Hobgobblins performs “Back Home” at LBC Top 10.
The Live Band Club's rock concert was held on campus, with nine different rock bands playing in a top 10 countdown in the Kirkham Auditorium Saturday night.

As the audience crowded towards the front to dance at the foot of the stage, BYU-Idaho student musicians pounded away on their instruments under flashing lights.

It was a fairly equal mix of original and cover songs. A few opening numbers were done by the band Collective Blue, which who also performed in this semester's Guitars Unplugged concert, and singer Naira Galoustain, a sophomore from Bat-Yam, Israel.

Following the opening songs was a concert introduction in which students were encouraged by Johnny Fricke and Johnny Hanna to "get up, dance and have fun!"

Bodyguards complete with rolled up shirt sleeves and sunglasses lined the stage, ready to deal with any unruly student brave enough to jump on stage.

The three-person band Misdirection kicked off the countdown with their original song "Remmus," and although halfway through the song they realized that someone's guitar was not hooked up right, they managed to get the crowd cheering.

In between set-ups, Fricke and Hanna filled the time sharing pick-up lines with the audience and voiced their annoyance about public displays of affection around campus. A few dating games were interspersed during the concert as well, with students trying to out-do each other in attempt to win free dinners and entertainment.

Before the concert, bands were required to audition for a spot on the top ten line-up. J.J. Weidner, a freshman from Columbus, Ohio, and guitarist and back-up vocalist of First Floor Flood, was one of the finalists for the concert.

"We're just really tight together so I was confident that we would make it, but knowing that we actually made it was exciting," Weidner said. "We were excited that we would be able to have so much fun with it."

Audtion requirements were things such as crowd-pleasing material, good musical ability, comfortable stage prescence and appropriate music, according to the Live Band Club adviser Jack Harrell.

The bands who tried out, which was about 17 total, were judged by Harrell and four other students. The rock concert has been one of many music-related events that have been put on this semester, such as Guitars Unplugged, Acoustics Night and others.

"We would like to make this an annual event," Harrell said. "Things like this are growing in popularity on our campus."

The goal behind the concert was "to show that we can put have this contemporary, popular music and activities, but still make it appropriate," Harrell said. "It is always a challenge, but we want to show that this is good, clean fun. It is a complicated event, but it was successful."
