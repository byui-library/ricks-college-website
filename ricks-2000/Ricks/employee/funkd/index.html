"Let us always be open to acknowledge, respect, and learn from great leaders in any field or discipline. Let us always e able to critique the work of any leader to move forward ideas and substantive knowledge for the betterment of humanity. For, indeed, great progress is largely contingent upon thoughtful reflections, critiques, and the creative use of worthwhile ideas." Florence Nightingale by Madeleine Leininger, Notes on Nursing, pg. 28.

"If a patient has to see, not only to his own but also to his or her nurse's punctuality, or perseverance, or readiness, or calmness, to any or all of these things, he is far better without that nurse than with her or him- however, valuable and handy her services may otherwise be to him, and however incapable he may be of rendering them to himself." Florence Nightingale, Notes on Nursing, pg. 31.(italics added)

"And remember every nurse should be one who is to be depended, upon, in other words, capable to being a "confidential" nurse. She or he does not know how soon she may find herself placed in a such a situation; she or he must be no gossip, no vain talker; she or he should never answer questions about her sick except to those who have a right to ask them; ...she or he because God's precious gift of life is often literally placed in her or his hands; she or he must be a sound, and close, and quick observer; and she must be a woman or man of delicate and decent feeling." Florence Nightingale, Notes on Nursing, pgs. 70, 71.       (italics added)
