Session F1

General Counsel: Friend or Foe

Facilitator:
P. Suzette Askew, University of Nevada Reno

Recorder Name:
Patricia Ring, Seattle University

Presenter:
Mary Dugan, University and Community College System of Nevada

General Counsel is on our team; get to know your general counsel.  Their goal is to help us do the right thing.  They give advice but don't make decisions - the president and administrators make decisions.

Requests for records cannot be accepted over the phone.  All requests must contain identifying information and student signature.

For subpoenas the method of delivery is important.  Subpoenas are not valid if they come over the fax.  It's not always necessary to involve the University Attorney but it may be helpful to.  If you have questions you should contact your attorney.

Always tell your attorney the whole story - don't leave anything out.  It's better that they don't find things out later on.

The best advice Mary could give is to get to know your general counsel and don't be afraid to contact them with questions.

Session F3

Retention Strategies for Student Athlete

Facilitator:
Roberta Lindahl, University of Portland

Recorder Name:
Brenda Simmons, Santa Monica College

Presenter:

Pam Bradetich, Washington State University

Ms. Bradetich's presented a very informative overview of retention strategies for students.  Her discussion focused on the benefit and impact of retaining student athletes in colleges and universities.  She emphasized the importance of assisting these students to develop life skills necessary for survival in a college environment.  She pointed out the pitfalls, myths, and concerns campuses have regarding working with athletes.

She asserted that retention is everyone's responsibility campus wide support is essential in assisting students to gain the knowledge and skills they need to be successful.  Washington State has a five- year plan for retaining athletes.  The institution sets high expectations for their athletes and have set up programs such as the WSU Athletics Life skills Program.  The staff in this program takes a "total person" approach from accessing a student's academic record, being aware of their personal development to providing career/transition services.  Commitment and dedication to the welfare and future success of the student is a key element to the success of the program and the students.

The athletes at WSU are required to enroll in fall orientation credit classes.  The orientation covers topics on campus survival techniques, campus logistics, time and money management, academic advising and tutorial services.

WSU is committed to the successful matriculation of their athletes and ensures that they are enrolled in academic classes and that they maintain the standard if not higher grade point average necessary to remain eligible for graduation.

Session F4

Involving Institutional Research in Recruitment and Student Relations

Facilitator:
Bobbe Miller-Murray, University of Washington Tacoma

Recorder Name:
Kate Johnson, Student Systems and Computer Technology Corporation - San Diego California

Presenter:
Ron Urban, Whitman College

This session described how the office of institutional research at many campuses provides important assistance at each step of the familiar "Admission Funnel."  Techniques that received attention included: using "cluster codes" and other strategies to differentiate and identify student market segments more effectively, administering surveys of admitted students to target admission communications accurately, and computing an "enrolling student prediction model" to permit more efficient use of admission conversion resources in order to increase yield.

Additionally, the ability of IR to make use of both quantitative and qualitative methods to identify categories of students that are most likely to drop out of college was discussed, along with several potential approaches to increase student retention.  Finally, participants were encouraged to develop "partnerships" with their IR offices in order to ensure successful enrollment management efforts.

Session F5

Enrollment Management in the New Millennium

Presenter:
Arlie Stops, Community College of Southern Nevada

The presenter gave information on how an institution can combine enrollment management responsibilities such as admissions, registrar's, records, financial aid, recruitment, and athletics, into a group that generates enrollment and successful students.  He spoke about stepping out on the limb as administrators to get results.  He discussed working with academic affairs, marketing and staff to achieve results.  They were able to accomplish the following:

  • Computer center, now the largest in Nevada

  • Building with contemporary design as well as the landscape

  • Partnership with high schools developed.  Shared computer facilities, classrooms, and teaching.

  • Design schedule presentations to attract attention, increase visibility, reflect possibilities, be user friendly, print and distribute to every home by delivery service.

CCSN has grown from 16,000 to 35,000 students in five years.

Session F7

Advice to New Registrars

Facilitator:  
Robert Nitsos, Loyola Marymount University

Recorder Name:  
Julia Reisinger, Oregon College of Art and Craft

Presenters:  
Carol Jones, California State University, Fullerton  
Rosenia St. Onge, Loyola Marymount University  
Norman Rempel, Fresno Pacify Univeristy

Carol Jones first advised the new registrars to remember the three P's : Process, Priorities, and Politics, which are all interrelated, and cannot be separated. All registrars have processes required as part of their position.  How they complete those processes is determined by the needs of their institution.  Priorities help to determine which processes to invest more of their time and money.  Politics come into play because the Registrar's Office is probably one of the only offices that works with almost all other offices and constituents on campus.  Politics occur when try to communicate and accommodate all of these constituents.

Norman Rempel offered the following suggestions: value and guard personal and professional integrity; be known as the person who is better at meeting challenges than merely spotting problems; remember that you are the role model and "mood manager" for the office, so try to stay positive; don't despise committee work because it helps you have a role in creating policy, and maintain contact with the campus community; figure out who makes the decisions; don't burn bridges; keep things in perspective by asking "will it matter that much in five years?"; remember that things change; learn how to gracefully deal with crisis of the moment; and balance "short-term pain, long-term gain" with "just do it now!"

Rosenia St. Onge finished the session by advising the new registrars to keep in touch with both their staff and their upper level administration so they know where everyone is at.  She also said that "never take anything personally...since policies are the schools and not yours."  Sometimes you have to be the bad guy simply because it is your job to uphold those policies.

All three also emphasized the need to network with other registrars, and this is possible through attending conferences, calling peers, finding an mentor, and joining e-mail list servers such as regist-1 and pacrao-1.
