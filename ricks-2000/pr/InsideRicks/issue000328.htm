PRESIDENT HINCKLEY PRAISES RICKS AT PRESIDENT'S CLUB BANQUET

"We are very proud of Ricks. It is a great school. It is a great institution and it has accomplished vast good," President Gordon B. Hinckley told members of the Ricks College President's Club at the group's annual banquet March 24 on the 26th floor of the Church Office Building in Salt Lake City. "I extend the gratitude of the entire Church for what you have done," President Hinckley told the assembled donors.

President Hinckley and his wife, Marjorie, headed a contingent of Church General Authorities who attended the yearly function representing the Board of Trustees of Ricks College. Other members of the Board at the banquet were President James E. Faust of the First Presidency and his wife, Ruth; three members of the Quorum of the Twelve: Elder David B. Haight and his wife, Ruby; Elder M. Russell Ballard; and Elder Richard G. Scott; and Sister Margaret D. Nadauld, Young Women General President, and her husband, Stephen. Also present was Stanley A. Peterson of the Church Educational System.

"What a tremendous thing it is, when all is said and done, to educate young people," President Hinckley said, "to plant into their hearts and minds a desire for learning, to qualify themselves to take places of significance in this society of which they will become a part."

The president of Ricks College, David A. Bednar, reported that $19.3 million was raised for the fund drive which ended last year, exceeding the original goal by $1.9 million. He briefly outlined the college's philanthropic priority goals for the years 2000 to 2005. Working through the LDS Foundation, the college has set a goal of raising $27.5 million. These funds will be used to expand the doors of educational opportunity for deserving and needy students, enhance the learning environment, support selected building and beautification projects, and extend the influence of Ricks College and prepare for future opportunities.

BUSINESS LEADER OF THE YEAR

William P. Benac, president and CEO of United CreditServe Inc., was honored as Business Leader of the Year during Ricks College's annual Business Summit Thursday, March 23. Benac, who is also chairman of the board of United Credit National Bank, holds a law degree from Pace University School of Law and a bachelor's degree and MBA from Brigham Young University. He sits on the executive committee of the National Advisory Council of the Marriott School of Management at BYU, is currently the president of the Texas International Theatrical Arts Society and on the board of directors of the Dallas Arboretum and Botanical Society. He and his wife, Barbara, have nine children and reside in the Dallas area. He is also bishop of an LDS ward in Dallas.

ALUMNI AWARDS FOR PROFESSIONAL ACCOMPLISHMENTS AND OUTSTANDING TEACHER

"We must educate our children as there are important positions awaiting them," said Thomas E. Ricks, one of the founding fathers for the school which now bears his name. The passing of time has added life to this truth. The Alumni recently acknowleged two outstanding individuals for their accomplishments.

The Distinguished Alumni Award for Professional Accomplishments was given to William R. "Bill" Walker. After moving from stockbroker to securities wholesaler to head of sales and marketing for the largest real estate syndicator in the United States, Walker seemed to be at the peak of his profession in the late 1980s. His subsequent retirement, however, was short-lived when the call came in 1990 to preside over the Japan Tokyo South Mission. Three years later, Walker decided to return to the financial world and was named vice president for Citibank with responsibility for sales and marketing promotion and investment and sales training. As of September 1999, he is the managing director and global sales director for Barclays International Funds of London, England. A native of Raymond, Alberta, Canada, Walker attended Ricks in the late 1960s. He and his wife Vicki reside in Sandy, Utah. They are the parents of five children and have seven grandchildren. He has twice served as a bishop and is currently president of the Sandy Utah Cottonwood Creek Stake.

Patricia Boyle Christensen was given the Alumni Outstanding Teacher Award. Over twenty years of teaching eighth-grade English has not diminished Patricia Boyle Christensen's love of trying to shape the minds of young people. Now retired, she will head for Mexico City for two years this August with her husband Mark to voluntarily teach English at an LDS Church-owned high school called Benemerito. A native of Ririe, Idaho, Patricia graduated from Ricks College in 1959 and from BYU in 1961 with a degree in business education. Her teaching career of about 25 years has been interspersed with time off while raising her family and furthering her own education to certify as an English teacher. Residents of Ucon, Idaho, she and her husband are the parents of five children and have 14 grandchildren. She has served as a ward Relief Society president, a counselor in three Relief Society presidencies, and as the ward organist "for too many years to count," she says.
