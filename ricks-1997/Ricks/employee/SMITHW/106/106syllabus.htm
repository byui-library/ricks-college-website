Course Goals:

    Chemistry 106 is the second semester of a two-semester course in college chemistry.  The year's course is designed to supply non-majors with the chemical tools needed in their chosen field of study.  Accordingly, on completion of the course:
  • you should be able to apply enough concepts, theories, and models to predict qualitatively the properties and reactions of at least three-fourths of the chemical substances you encounter; and
  • you should be skilled in or know of sufficient physical and analytical techniques to determine these properties and reactions quantitatively.
Specific objectives will be handed out with each unit's assignment sheet.

 Lectures

    Brother Smith will present new material three times a week.  He will discuss principles, demonstrate phenomena, and present illustrations.  He will not read the textbook to you; instead, he will emphasize the important parts of the reading.

 Quizzes

    Once a week (usually Thursdays), the class period will be used as a help session.  For the first 30 minutes, any questions you have will be answered.  Then for the next 20 minutes or so, you will take and in-class quiz.  The questions will be straightforward and easy if you have kept current in you studies.  Not counting a lot toward your final grade, they are good indicators of your progress without being too costly.

 Homework

    Problem assignments are made with each unit (chapter in text).  The problems are chosen as typical examples of those you ar expected to master and work with ease.  Most solutions are in the book.  You may choose for yourself how much of each assignment you need to complete, since there are usually too many problems rather than too few.  When you have gotten sufficient value from the assignment (by your standards), you can claim credit.  Note, however, that homework is not extra credit; it is worth 5 % of your grade.

 Exams

    Four open-book, take-home exams will be given during the semester on or around the following dates:
  •  Exam 1: January 21st
  •  Exam 2: February 11th
  •  Exam 3: March 10th
  •  Exam 4: April 7th
The exams will contain mostly problems and some multiple choice.  They will be closely keyed to the published objectives.

 Final Exam

    The open-book final exam will be analogous to the regular exams except that it will cover the entire course and will be slightly longer.  Arrange your weddings, missions, and rides so that you can turn in the final at or before the appointed time:

 Laboratories

    You must have passed the laboratory safety exam from Chemistry 105 (given for the next several weeks in the Testing Center) with a score of 95% or better to enter the lab. After each lab, you will have until the beginning of the next lab lecture to hand in your report.  Late reports will be docked 5 points a day.  In no case can a passing grade be earned in the course without credit for at least 7 of the 10 lab reports.
