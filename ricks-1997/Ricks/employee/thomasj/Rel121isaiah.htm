Learning from Isaiah in the Book of Mormon

 

An Apostle’s Ten Keys to Understanding Isaiah

  1. Gain an overall knowledge of the plan of salvation and of God’s dealings with his earthly children.
  2. Learn the position and destiny of the house of Israel in the Lord’s eternal scheme of things.
  3. Know the chief doctrines about which Isaiah chose to write: (a) latter-day restoration; (b) latter-day gathering of Israel; (c) coming forth of the Book of Mormon; (d) apostate conditions of the nations of the world in latter days; (e) messianic prophecies relative to the Lord’s first coming; (f) second coming and millennial reign of Christ; (g) historical utterances relative to his own day.  Do not “ think of the restoration of the gospel as a past event and the gathering of Israel as…in large measure accomplished. . . . M uch of what Isaiah . . . has to say is yet to be fulfilled”(80).
  4. Use the Book of Mormon. [One third of the book of Isaiah (433verses) is quoted there (see footnote to 2 Ne 12:2); of those verses, 391 refer to “the attributes or mission of Jesus Christ” (Nyman 1980, 7). Prophetic commentary from Nephi, Jacob, Abinadi and Christ himself can be found in many chapters, including 1 Ne. 19, 22; 2 Ne. 6, 9-11, 25-30; Mosiah 12, 15-16; 3 Ne15-16, 20-21.]
  5. Use latter-day revelation.  [At least 66 passages from 31 different Isaiah chapters appear in the Doctrine and Covenants; inspired interpretations of Isaiah appear in sections 101,113, and 133.  Joseph Smith commented on at least 35 Isaiah passages from 21 different chapters in his sermons.]
  6. Learn how the New Testament interprets Isaiah passages from 25 different chapters are cited in that book.]
  7. Study Isaiah in its Old Testament context.  [The footnotes, maps, and dictionaryin the 1979 LDS edition are helpful.]
  8. Learn the manner of prophesying used among the Jews in Isaiah’s day. See 2 Ne 25:1-8.
  9. Have the spirit of prophecy.  “It takes a prophet to understand a prophet , and every faithful member of the Church should have ‘the testimony of Jesus’ which  ‘is the spirit of prophecy’(Rev 19:10)” (83).  See also 2 Ne 25:4; 2 pet 1:20-21.
  10. Devote yourself to hard, conscientious study.  Remember, “Scriptural understanding and great insight . . . are valuable only insofar as they change and perfect the lives of men. . . . What Isaiah wrote is true . . . the glories and wonders he promised for our day will surely come to pass; and if we are true and faithful we will participate in them, whether I life or in death”(83).

(see Bruce R. McConkie, “Ten Keys to understanding Isaiah,” Ensign, Oct. 1973, 78-83)

 

 

Isaiah and the Nephite Prophetic View of History

Nephites looked toward four stages of God’s dealings with Israel and quoted Isaiah to illustrate the events of each phase.

 

Stage1: “The coming of Jesus Christ among the Jews and his rejection because of priestcrafts.”

                Isaiah7:10-24; 8:1-18; 9: 1-17; 48: 12-22; 53: 1-12.

 

Stage 2: “Jews will be scattered and smitten, will suffer in contention and darkness, but the Lord will not forget them.”

                Isaiah 3:1-41; 5:1-25; 6:1-12; 8: 18-21: 48:1-11; 50:1-11.

 

Stage 3: “In the days of the Gentiles, a remnant will be summoned and gathered again.”

                Isaiah 5:26-30; 6:13; 10:20-23: 11:10-16: 49:1-26; 51:1-23; 54:1-17.

 

Stage 4: “In the day of God’s judgment, the wicked will be destroyed and God’s righteousness will be victorious.”

                Isaiah 2:6-9, 11-22; 10:1-19, 24-34; 13:1-22; 14:9-28 (destruction).

                Isaiah 2:1-5, 10-11; 4:2-6; 11:1-9; 12:1-6; 14:1-8,29-32; 52:1-15 (triumph of righteousness).

                (Based on John W. Welch, “Getting Through Isaiah with the Help of the Nephite Prophetic View,” in Parry and Welch, eds., Isaiah in the Book of Mormon [Provo: FARMS, 1998], 35-37).
