Future Plans for the CS Major
CS 171, Programming II
The last time CS 171 will be offered is the Fall 1999 semester. It will be replaced by two classes, CS 173 and CS 246.

CS 173, Web Programming, is a two credit class and covers Web site design, HTML forms, JavaScript, and CGI programming with Perl. CS 144, Introduction to Programming, is the prerequisite for CS 173. CS 173 is not restricted to CS majors.

CS 246, Advanced Object Oriented Programming, is a three credit class and is a continuation in study of object oriented programming. Students learn Java and design and code larger programs, including stand-alone applications and applets. CS 235, Foundations of Computer Science I, is the prerequisite for CS 246. CS 246 will transfer to BYU as CS 240.

CS 233, Introduction to Computer Architecture
A digital logic component will be added to CS 233. However, starting the Winter 2000 semester, CS 233 will be available to both computer science (CS) and electronics engineering technology (EET) majors. EET majors already take a class covering digital logic. Consequently, another class covering digital logic will be required of CS majors but not of EET majors. CS 233L, Digital Logic, is a one credit class and will be first offered the Winter 2000 semester. Adding a digital logic component to CS 233 ensures that CS 233 will transfer to BYU as CS 143.

Math and Science Classes
The university you will transfer to determines the math and science classes you should take, as illustrated in the following table:



University Math Physical Science Biological Science
BYU Math 209 Math 243 Chem 105 Ph 121 BS 200
Utah State Math 162 Math 263 Chem 105 and Chem 106 or Ph 121 and Ph 122 any class satisfying general education requirements
other universities Math 162 Math 263 Ph 121 Ph 122 any class satisfying general education requirements
Students who take math and science classes other than those specified in the catalog are required to submit a "Request for Modification of Major Requirements for Graduation" to document the change.
