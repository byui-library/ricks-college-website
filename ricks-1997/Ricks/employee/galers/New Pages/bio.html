Scott W. Galer was born in Lincoln Park, Michigan (a suburb of Detroit) in 1967. At the age of six, he and his family moved to Colorado, where they eventually ended up in the small town of Paonia on the Western Slope. Scott lived in Paonia until graduating from high school, when he began his undergraduate studies at Brigham Young University. After completing his freshman year, which included two Chinese courses, Scott was called to serve a mission for the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints in Taichung, Taiwan.

Upon completing his mission, Scott returned to BYU for one year before taking another hiatus from formal education for a nine month stint (1989–90) in Wuhan, People’s Republic of China, where he worked as a tutor and interpreter, while studying modern Chinese literature at Huazhong University. Scott returned to BYU and completed his Bachelor’s degree in Chinese, with a minor in business management in 1992. In 1995, he completed a Master’s degree in Chinese literature at the University of Colorado at Boulder. Scott is currently a Ph.D. candidate at the University of Wisconsin, Madison. When not teaching Chinese at Ricks, he is likely working on his dissertation, in which he is reconstructing and analyzing a fourth century commentary on the Shiji (The Grand Scribe’s Records—the first comprehensive history of China, written in the first century BC). He is also a member of a team of translators led by Professor William H. Nienhauser, Jr. of the University of Wisconsin who are in the process of producing a ten-volume, annotated translation of the Shiji into English. Volumes one and seven have already been published, with volume two forthcoming in 1999 (Indiana University Press). Scott was also the Assistant Editor of the second volume of the Indiana Companion to Traditional Chinese Literature, edited by Nienhauser.

When not engaged in academic pursuits, Scott enjoys spending time with his family and working around his one-acre country homestead.
