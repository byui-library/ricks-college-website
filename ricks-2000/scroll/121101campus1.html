BYU-Idaho’s first edition of Summit magazine released

by Lindsey Christensen
chr01014@byui.edu
Scroll Staff

BYU-Idaho’s first edition of Summit magazine was recently released. The Ricks College/ BYU-I alumni publication is distributed bi-annually to 94,000 graduates, friends and donors of the university.
As students near graduation, many prepare to leave BYU-Idaho forever. However, these students can remain in contact with the university and its current events through Summit magazine, a BYU-Idaho/Ricks College alumni publication.

The Fall 2001 edition was the first edition published with the BYU-Idaho name.

“[It] was a special edition; it was a much larger publication that focused on change and how those changes are affecting students and the campus,” Merv Brown, Summit editor said.

This edition included President David A. Bednar’s talk “Line Upon Line, Precept Upon Precept.” It also contained a feature on Homecoming 2001 and an article on the school’s foundation, founders and future.

Articles are chosen to reflect items of interest for the alumni, Steve Davis, director of alumni relations, said.

Summit includes talks, events, the continuing education theme, athletics and alumni news, Kristy Williams, president of the Student Alumni Association, said.

Everyone who attends this school has a wonderful experience — they leave with a love for the school and the spirit of Ricks,Williams said. Summit is a way to keep alumni informed and perpetuate the love and spirit of BYU-I.

“The magazine allows alumni and others to know about fellow alumni successes,” Brown said.“Many of our alumni have been very successful.”

Summit includes events on campus and the direction of the university, he said. Summit is published and distributed biannually.

The main goal of Summit is to keep alumni in touch and still connected to the campus, Davis said.

The school has approximately 15,000 alumni and distributes 94,000 copies of Summit, Davis said. Friends and donors also receive the magazine.

Summit was founded and created by Stephen Moser who recently retired. He was the first editor.

As the editor, Moser wrote most the articles. The magazine has changed to have many contributors rather than the editor of the magazine as the main author. This adds diversity, different points of view and more interesting reading, Brown said.

In 1949, the Ricks College Alumni Newsletter was born and became the forerunner to Summit magazine. Its first makeover came in 1971 when the newsletter became Ricks Tradition in a newspaper format.

The school’s centennial year was 1988. The theme was “A Century of Commitment, Summits Yet to Climb.” The school symbol was the oak tree before 1988, when Ricks adopted the mountains as its symbol. It was that same year that its staff gave not only a face-lift to Ricks tradition but a full-body surgery and the addition of its current name.

The magazine’s name is derived from the school’s newer symbol of the summit, to perpetuate the theme that there are mountains yet to climb, Davis said.

Graduating students can leave their new addresses with the alumni office to receive Summit magazine in order to stay updated with the school’s events, Williams said.

This publication is beneficial to alumni because it is “the most effective way to communication with alumni,” Davis said.
