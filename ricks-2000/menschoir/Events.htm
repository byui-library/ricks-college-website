Highlights from the 2000-2001 year:

 

October 2000: First concert of the season in the Barrus Concert Hall.  Brought down the house with songs like “Hope of Israel” and Paul Rardin’s “Hol’ You’ Han”.  (Click here to listen)

 

November 2000: Homecoming Spectacular performance with the Sound Alliance jazz band. 

 

December 2000: Men’s Choir joined other choirs and instrumental ensembles on campus for Ricks College's annual production of Handel's Messiah—a mammoth production which involved 500 singers and 150 instrumentalists!

 

December 2000: Ricks Musical Christmas, combined with the Chamber Orchestra, members of Sound Alliance, Women’s Choir, and Collegiate Singers.
Listen: Carol of the Drum
Listen: Go Tell It on the Mountain

 

 

 

January 2001: Went to Teton Lodge for a retreat, and then sang in the annual Barbershop festival in the Barrus Concert Hall.  Click here for another excerpt!

 

March 2001: Winter Choirfest–including Franz Biebl’s “Ave Maria” and the first performances of two new hymn arrangements by BYU-Idaho professors Eda Ashby and Randall Kempton.

 

March 2001: Sang at the Saturday afternoon session of General Conference—one of the highlights of the year, and maybe of our lives. 

“What a joyful and hallowed opportunity and experience.  I have never ever had such a blessing.”—David Mayne

“Singing at conference will be an experience I will never forget.”—Jayenti Collins

 

April 2001: Combined with Women’s Choir, Collegiate Singers, professional soloists, and the Symphony Orchestra for the biennial Sacred Music Project–this year composed by well-known choral composer Daniel Gawthrop.  The Passion and the Promise of Our Lord Jesus Christ was almost two hours long and, along with General Conference, one of the absolute highlights of the year.  The Passion and the Promise will be available for purchase on CD and video this fall, through the BYU-Idaho Bookstore or the Music Department.  This oratorio was performed ten times, including in Washington, D.C., the Salt Lake Tabernacle, and our own Barrus Concert Hall.  Exhausting, but well worth the effort!
