SPORI SUMMIT REVIVES TEACHING TRADITIONS

On February 1-3, twenty-one Ricks College faculty and staff members participated in the Jacob Spori Great Teachers Summit at the Quick Water Ranch just east of Victor in the Teton Valley. The Summit is held twice a year (Winter and Fall Semesters) and is patterned after the Great Teachers Summit first conducted by David Gottshall in 1970. The Great Teachers Summit has become a common practice in many colleges and universities in over 18 states and internationally.

The purposes of the Summit are (1) recognition of good teaching; (2) search for instructional ideas not bound by disciplines; (3) the process of instructional problem-solving; and (4) the promotion of faculty sharing and collaboration.
The recent Spori Summit provided a great opportunity to share teaching successes, present teaching challenges and work together in offering solutions to those challenges. Participants were divided into small groups to share and discuss ideas. General sessions encouraged more participation and sharing of ideas from the small groups. Object lessons and demonstrations presented by each individual highlighted the Summit.
Participants find the experience rewarding. One observed, "I loved the exchange of pertinent teaching information, and the acknowledgment of similar problems to those I face in other faculty. I now have a whole host of readily-applicable teaching hints and techniques to inspire my teaching and my students."
Assistant academic vice president Max Checketts said, "The Spori Summit is one of the best things that we have done for our faculty. We will continue to support it because of its great, positive influence and impact it has on our faculty."

WRESTLING TEAM QUALIFY FOR NATIONALS

Ricks College has qualified ten wrestlers, one for each of the weight classes, for the NJCAA National Wrestling Tournament in Rochester, Minn., Feb. 16-17. Wrestlers had to place first, second or third at the regional finals in Yakima, Wash., last Saturday to qualify for the finals.
As a team Ricks placed second with 112 points behind regional champion North Idaho with 149 points. Travis Marx at 149 pounds and Ashton Buswell at 157 pounds were crowned regional champions in their weight classes. Ty Rollins at 125 pounds and heavyweight Ryan Hansen placed second regionally. The other six Ricks wrestlers placed third.
Last year Ricks also took its entire team and placed third nationally. This year Ricks has been ranked second nationally behind North Idaho for most of the season.

RESEARCHERS GIVEN PRESENTATION ON MOLECULAR GENEALOGY

A presentation into the emerging field of molecular genealogy will be held Friday, Feb. 9, in the Manwaring Center rooms 318 and 326. The presentation of work being done by researchers at BYU will begin at 7 p.m. followed by a collection of pedigree charts and blood drawing from 8-10 p.m. DNA testing will be part of the presentation. Those wanting to participate are required to bring a four-generation pedigree chart with names, dates and birth places.

 

GLOSSARY ADDED TO THE INTERNET SITE ON THE TRANSITION

In the process of defining the future for BYU-Idaho, the Comprehensive Advancement Planning Council chaired by Brent Kinghorn, community service vice president, has developed a glossary of terminology as it relates to the transition. The glossary is posted on Internet along with other information on the transition. (See www.byui.edu/pr/byu-i/glossary.htm.)

The glossary was developed to give a reference point of information and to give identity to the process of the transition. The list includes nearly 50 terms such as integrated associate degrees, specialized bachelor's degrees, Track System, Award of Excellence, and candidacy. It also explains the college's relationship to the Church Educational System, Board of Trustees, Executive Committee, and the Northwest Accreditation Committee.
