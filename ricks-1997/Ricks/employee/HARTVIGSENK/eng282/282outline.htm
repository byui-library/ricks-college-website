Five goals as we study together:

1.  An appreciation of literature and the world of ideas open to the person who reads

2.  An understanding of the power of language to communicate our experiences and thoughts

3.  A confidence that allows us to arrive at and defend valid interpretations of what we read

4.  An understanding of the basic conventions of creative literature

5.  And, of course, a greater appreciation for Shakespeare, his skill as a dramatist, poet, and observer of humanity

Supplement A:

Reader=s Theater Presentation

Think of your presentation as a Aperformed paper.@  Following these steps will help your group prepare:

1.  As a group, pose a significant question about the play you are presenting.  (See ADiscussion Questions@ on each play=s study guide for examples.)

2.  Take your favorite question into the assigned play and explore possible answers the text supports.

3.  Answer your question in one sentence.  Let this idea serve as the focus of your presentationByour theme or main point.

4.  Select passages from the playBilluminating scenes, dialog and soliloquiesB that best support the theme or idea you want your audience to see.

5.  Prepare an outline/script that organizes your presentation including necessary narration (also scripted) that connects the passages your group will present and perform.  (Please share a copy of your script with me as you begin your presentation.)

6.  Assign roles to group members.  Also assign a narrator to frame your presentation and provide necessary exposition and transition.

7.  Rehearse!  Around a table, discuss the lines you will present, making certain all readers understand what they are saying and why their characters are the way they are.  Consider possible sub-text interpretations.  Explore words, imagery, voice inflections.  Practice reading with expression and a clear sense of meaning.

8.  If you=d like, during your performance portray your character with a simple costumeBhat, cloak, dress, etc.  Use props and background music if they help convey your idea.

9.  Perform your presentation as a traditional reader=s theater, grouped but stationary, or block action and move about.

10.  Time your presentation to run 15-20 minutes.  Have fun!

Criteria for Grading (50 points)

1.  Clear focus of presentation  (10 points)

2.  Well selected scenes/lines to develop your focus  (10 points)

3.  Flow of presentation and effective narration  (5 points)

4.  Effective delivery of lines  (20 points)

5.  Finesse!  (5 points)

Supplement B:

Analysis Essay

Think of your essay as defending a view you have about any play we read.  Your essay may be submitted at any point you choose during the semester prior to the last day of class.  It should reflect original thinking rather than rehashes of class discussions.  Outside research is not necessary.  I would much rather have you explore an issue interesting to you and come to your own conclusion based on the evidence within the play you have chosen to discuss.

These steps will help you prepare your essay:

1.  Pose a significant question about the play your are analyzing.  Your question may relate to any element of drama you find interestingBtheme, character, setting, irony, language, imagery, cultural contexts.  (You may get ideas about these contexts in our background reading.)

2.  Keeping your question in mind, review the play you=ve chosen to analyze.  Note passages that address your question.

3.  With what you have discovered during your study, write a one-sentence answer to your question.  This sentence will serve as the thesis of your paper.

4.  Organize the evidence you have found to answer your question.  Develop a sketch or outline that enables you to present your evidence coherently and persuasively.

5.  Using your outline/sketch, compose your essay.  As you write, assume your reader knows the plot.  Rather than retelling the play=s storyline, use your essay to defend your thesis.  Show your reader why that thesis is true.  Cite textual support (direct quotation or paraphrase) in parenthesis by act, scene, and line: (4.2.78-95).

6.  Use your intro to set up your thesis and engage your reader=s attention.  Explain why the question you are answering in your paper is interesting to you or relevant to a reader=s understanding of the play.  Use your concluding paragraph to climax your paper; a mere re-statement of your thesis may be anti-climactic.

Criteria for Grading (100 points)

1.  FocusBclear and significant  (10 points)

2.  DevelopmentBample and persuasive  (35 points)

3.  CoherenceBlogical and clear  (25 points)

4.  StyleBreadable and precise  (15 points)

5.  PresentationBedited and effective  (15 points)
