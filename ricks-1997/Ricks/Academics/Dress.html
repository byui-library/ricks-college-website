Dress and Grooming Standards
The dress and grooming of both men and women should always be modest , neat and clean, and consistent with the dignity of representing Ricks College and The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.

Modesty and cleanliness are important values that reflect personal dignity and integrity, through which members of the Ricks College community represent the principles and standards of the Church. The members of that community commit themselves to observe the following standards which reflect the direction of the Ricks College Board of Trustees and the Church publication "For the Strength of Youth".

Clothing is to be modest in fabric, fit and style, and appropriate for the occasion. Skirts are to be knee-length or longer. Clothing that is sleeveless, strapless, or revealing is not acceptable. Modest shorts are appropriate at designated outdoor sporting/recreational events and in living areas but are not acceptable campus attire. (See the dress and grooming supplement for more detail.) Shoes are to be worn in public campus areas. Men's and women's dress should be reflective of their gender.

A clean and well-cared-for appearance is to be maintained. Hairstyles are to be clean and neat, avoiding extreme styles. Men's hair is to be trimmed above the collar, leaving the ears uncovered. If worn, mustaches are to be neatly trimmed and may not extend beyond or below the corner of the mouth; beards are not acceptable. Male students may need to shave on a daily basis in order to avoid the appearance of a "5 o'clock shadow." Earrings for men are unacceptable at any time on or off campus.

Failure to comply with the Code of Honor or dress and grooming standards could result in dismissal from Ricks College.

Dress and Grooming Supplement
The following supplemental information is provided to give students, staff, faculty, and administrators general guidelines in determining appropriate from inappropriate attire and grooming. Common sense, good taste, discretion, consideration, and high moral standards are the watchwords for dress and grooming. Flagrant behavior or extreme dress and grooming at any time or in any place may result in disciplinary action being taken against the student.

Because of the constant change in dress and grooming styles, some trends, fads, or fashions common in the world may be determined inappropriate. The dean of students, after consulting with his staff and/or the executive staff, may determine that a specific style or fad is inappropriate and thereby is unacceptable at Ricks.

Dress Standards for Men and Women
Athletic and Outdoor Events attire:
Dress standards for each event will be specified prior to the event. If no standard is specified, the minimum standard will be classroom attire. Modest length shorts may be worn at specified outdoor athletic and recreational events but are otherwise not acceptable campus attire. Modest casual attire may be worn at specified indoor athletic or recreational events.

Concert and Drama Attire:
The minimum standard will be classroom attire. Care should be given to ensure that your dress and appearance are compatible with the event you are attending. Casual dress or clothing will not be permitted.

Campus and Dance Attire:
Classroom attire is appropriate for regular weekly dances held on campus. Formal and semiformal attire for men includes dress suits
(or dress slacks and sport coats) with ties and dress shoes. For women, Sunday dresses or modest formals that cover the shoulders are acceptable. NO spaghetti straps, low-cut or immodest necklines, open backs, or sheer or unlined transparent material will be permitted. Special theme dances will specify appropriate dress. Modest dress will be emphasized at all dances held.

Procedures and processes for enforcement and appeals are outlined in the R Book.
