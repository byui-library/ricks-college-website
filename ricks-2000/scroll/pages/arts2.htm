WORLD-RENOWNED STRING QUARTET TO PERFORM.

by HILARY INGOLDSBY
scroll staff

World-renowned American String Quartet will perform Jan. 17 and 18 at 7:30 p.m. in the Barrus Concert Hall.

The New York City based quartet's performances will include pieces by Franz Haydn, Bela Bartok and Ludwig Van Beethoven.

In addition to the concerts, the quartet will also coach members of the Ricks Chamber Orchestra for three days, according to director Ted Ashton. A question and answer session will be held today for wind/brass chamber members on musicianship, music careers and further schooling. A master class for all string players will be held with the quartet Wednesday at 4 p.m.

"This is our third time, and for most of our students, it's their highlight of their year in terms of their involvement in an ensemble," Ashton said.

Following the workshops, the orchestra will hold a performance to showcase its work on Jan. 25 at 7:30 p.m. in the Snow Recital Hall.

Not only does the quartet provide concerts, educational programs, seminars, broadcast performances and publish articles, but it also records. Most recently the quartet released a boxed set of the complete Mozart quartets.

The quartet has ongoing series at the Aspen Music Festival, Princeton University, the Orange County Performing Arts Center in California and the Manhattan School of Music in New York. The quartet was also awarded one of the first National Art Endowment grants for its work on college campuses and commitment to contemporary music.

The musicians formed this quartet in 1974 while they were studying at Julliard. That same year, the quartet won the Coleman Competition and Naumburg Award. The group celebrated its 25th anniversary in 1998-1999 with a tour to all 50 states and Europe.

The Los Angeles Times described the quartet as "an impeccable ensemble. They play as one mind, one heart, with faultless polish, with awesome control.." Their performance was described as "a comprehensive display of ensemble mastery, of passion, precision and interpretive smarts in near-perfect synchrony."
