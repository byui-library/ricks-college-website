Religion 343 Study Guide 1 (Thomas)

 

Chapter 36–The Church in the Early Twentieth Century

1. What was the personality and background of Joseph F. Smith, sixth president of the Church?

2. Why was the “Defender of the Faith” excluded from the House of Representatives?

3. Why did some people protest Reed Smoot’s right to serve in the Senate?  How was the protest resolved? How did the “Smoot Case” affect the Church’s reputation?

4. What was the content and significance the “Second Manifesto”?

5. What were the main sources of anti-Mormon press coverage in the United States? What did President Smith counsel Church members to do about their charges?

6. What were the main issues raised by anti-Mormon activists in Britain? What was the effect on missionary work in that country?

7. What were the earliest efforts to host visitors to Temple Square? How did the “bureau of information” work?

8. Which Church historical sites were purchased during President Smith’s tenure? Why were they purchased?

9. What is the story of the production of the History of the Church and A Comprehensive History of the Church? How are the two different?

10. What made Emmeline B. Wells distinctive as a general Relief Society president?

Other notable people: B.H. Roberts, John W. Taylor, Matthias F. Cowley, Hugh B. Brown, Thomas Kearns, Winifred Graham, Willard and Rebecca Bean

*Supplementary reading: In its 1907 Address to the World, how did the First Presidency respond to the following charges: (1) the Church is not Christian; (2) its Priesthood leaders mislead its members; (3) it uses deceit in missionary work; (4) it opposes education; (5) it is destructive of marriage and family; (6) it is commercial rather than religious in its goals; (7) its leaders exercise arbitrary power; (8) it is disloyal to the American government and the constitutional separation of church and state?

 

Chapter 37–Moving Forward into the New Century

1. How did the emphasis of President Lorenzo Snow impact the Church’s financial status under President Smith?

2. What were the Church’s major building projects in Utah in these years? What was the purpose of these buildings?

3. How did the auxiliaries change? How were the changes related to social trends?

4. What changes were made in the organization of the Priesthood? What motivated the adjustments?

5. What were the main LDS periodicals of the era? Who sponsored each periodical?

6. What  long-lasting initiative in gospel teaching did the First Presidency propose in 1915?

7. What were the main doctrinal contributions of “The Father and the Son,” “The Origin of Man”? How were these documents prepared?

8. What were the circumstances in which James E. Talmage’s three well-known books written?

9. What prophecy did Joseph F. Smith make to European Saints in 1906? How did this statement and two new temples relate to the Church’s policy on gathering?

10. How did the Mexican revolution impact Latter-day Saint proselyting and colonization in Mexico? What do you learn from the cases of Rafel Monroy and Anson Call?

11. How did World War I impact Latter-day Saints abroad? How did American Latter-day Saints respond to America’s entry into the war?  How did the Mormon image change in the US during World War I?

12. Under what circumstances was the vision of the redemption of the dead received? What were its major doctrinal contributions? When was it canonized?

Other notable people: David O. McKay, Louie B. Felt, Susa Young Gates, Rey L. Pratt, Rafael Monroy

*Supplementary reading: In his 1917 address, what did President Smith say about the following matters: (1) the causes of World War I; (2) God’s role in and view of the war; (3) what God’s hand is striving to accomplish on earth; (4) the spirit in which LDS soldiers should conduct themselves in war; (5) the keys to individual peace for soldiers and civilians in perilous times; (6) the source of technological innovations; (7) the responsibilities and opportunities for teaching the gospel?

Chapter 38–Change and Consistency

1. What was the personality and background of Heber J. Grant, seventh president of the Church?

2. What lasting administrative changes were made at the beginning of President Grant’s tenure?

3. What disagreements existed among general authorities over the League of Nations? How was the issue resolved?

4. What role did the state of Utah play in the demise of  Prohibition? What did President Grant think of it?

5.  What was the purpose of David O. McKay’s world tour in 1920-1921?  What happened at (a) Peking (b) Maui (c) Samoa (d) Haifa?

6. What initiatives did Church leaders take to renew missionary work after World War I in Europe and South America?

7. Where was the Mission Home and how did it operate? What other efforts were made to enhance the teaching effectiveness of missionaries?

8. How did the Church’s educational programs change in this period? What concerns motivated the changes?

9. What stakes and temples signaled the Church’s secure establishment outside of Utah?

10. What were the main Church uses of radio?

11. What did Church leaders and members do to celebrate the Church’s centennial?

Other notable people: Reed Smoot, Melvin J. Ballard, Hugh J. Cannon, J. Wilford Booth, Percy McCarthur, Gustive O. Larson, Wyley Sessions

 

Chapter 39–The Church during the Great Depression

1. How severely were Utahns affected by the great depression? How did this impact Church programs?

2. What principles did the First Presidency establish to guide Church welfare efforts?

3. Which stake led the way in devising ways to meet the welfare needs of its members? What did it do?  What other Church welfare programs began during the great depression?

4. What initiatives in sharing the gospel were taken in this period? What adjustments were made to maintain missionary work in the face of financial pressures?

5. What two truths did President Clark feel ought to be taught throughout the Church educational system? What concerns prompted him to call for this focus?

6. How did Church governance of its educational institutions change in 1938?

7. What was the purpose of the Committee of Correlation and Coordination?

8. What did Church leaders do to reach out to Latter-day Saints abroad at this time?

9. What was the role of Assistants to the Twelve?

Other notable people: Harold B. Lee, LeGrand Richards, Gordon B. Hinckley, Harold Hansen

 

Chapter 40–The Saints during World War II

1. When they cared, what did the National Socialists like and dislike about Mormonism? How did this movement affect the Church outside America?

2. How did Church leaders coordinate the evacuation of missionaries from Europe? What experiences convinced missionaries that they received divine help in their evacuation?

3. How did the world war affect Church members in the Pacific, South America, and South Africa?

4.  How did the war affect the Church in Europe? What problems and opportunities did it pose for European Saints on both sides of the conflict?

5. What did the First Presidency say about military service and war in 1941 and 1942?

6.  What did Church leaders do to help LDS soldiers live worthy lives under harsh conditions?  What were the fruits of these efforts?

7. Approximately what percentage of Latter-day Saints were directly involved in military service? What did Harold B. Lee say about the significance of war casualties?

8. How did the war affect Church members and programs in the US? How did civilian Mormons participate in the war effort?

Other notable people: J. Reuben Clark, Norman G. Seibold, Wallace and Martha Toronto, Helmuth Hubener, Hermann Moessner, Hugh B. Brown

*Supplementary reading: In its October 1942 address to all Church members, what did the First Presidency say about the following matters: (1) alcohol and the word of wisdom; (2) chastity; (3) parental responsibilities; (4) united loyalty to gospel principles and the Church; (5) standards for those in military service and ways to uphold them; (6) war; (7) Fascism and Communism? What are the main messages of their prayer?

 

Chapter 41–Postwar Recovery

1. What was the personality and background of George Albert Smith, eighth president of the Church? How was he especially well prepared to deal with post-war reorganization of Church activities?

2. What different methods were used initially to get Church humanitarian aid to Europe? What measures were taken to accelerate the flow of relief supplies? How much was sent?

3. Who was sent to renew the work in Europe after WWII?  What were his main challenges and successes?  What promise did he receive from the First Presidency and what events illustrate the fulfillment of the promise?

4. What extraordinary gesture of support was made by Dutch Saints for Germans?

5. Who served a similar mission in the Pacific? What were his main challenges and successes?

6. What initiatives were taken to accelerate the work of sharing the gospel after the war?

7. What were the most important developments in temple and family history work in 1945?

8. What was the typical Sunday schedule of meetings for Latter-day Saints? What other programs during the week were used in an effort to strengthen youth activity?

9. Where, and under what circumstances, was the temple endowment first presented in a language other than English? How did this relate to other initiatives in regards to children of Lehi?

10.  How was the pioneer centennial celebrated in 1947? What milestone in total Church membership occurred that year?

Other notable people: Frederick Babbel, Boyd K. Packer, Richard L. Anderson, Archibald F. Bennett, Spencer W. Kimball
