Answers to
Frequently Asked Questions

The following are answers to some of the most frequently asked student questions.  We hope these responses will be informative, and perhaps even persuasive, in answering some concerns about Campus Education Week.

Why is Campus Education Week held at this time of year?
We realize that the dates the program is held may present significant scheduling conflicts (i.e., vacation, children still in school, graduations, child care, etc.)  for those who would like to attend.  However, we want to provide the finest education experience possible for the maximum number of people.  To do so we need the entire Ricks College campus.  Our campus holds weekday and evening classes, Saturday conferences, and Sunday Church services.  The only week available in the summer is between commencement and the first day of the summer semester.

How is housing assigned?
Housing is given on a first-come, first-served basis.  As registrations are received we begin to make assignments on the information and roommate requests given.  Most of our complexes house six people per apartment.

Why can't we bring babies and children under the age of 14?
We realize, as you do, that many people schedule their entire year's vacation around Campus Education Week.  They spend their savings and often travel hundreds of miles to attend.  Because of such sacrifices, we feel a responsibility to provide the atmosphere that makes this learning experience memorable and motivating.  We enforce the policy of no children in the classrooms because children under 14 may easily become disinterested and disruptive.  Also, babies have their own needs which they often make known in fairly noisy ways, disrupting other students.  For these reasons, we instruct the room hosts/hostesses not to admit children under 14 or individuals with babies to the classrooms.  Ricks College's Division of Continuing Education offers programs at other times of the year for children under the age of 14.  We hope that some of the needs of this age group can be met through these programs.

Why not provide child-care service?
Ricks College is just that--a college.  It really isn't set up for child care.  Also, there are liability issues involved which would make it difficult to operate a child-care service.  Many have suggested getting local residents to tend children for out-of-town guests, but we want area residents to be able to attend and benefit from Campus Education Week as well.

Why are Campus Education Week tickets nontransferable?
Football games, drama productions, movies--a ticket to these events provides the right to a seat.  However, Campus Education Week is an educational program, and each student must be registered in order to attend.  In addition, the handling of mailings, housing assignments, refunds, specific questions, and emergency situations (that may arise involving Campus Education Week students) is enhanced by individual registration.  Therefore, we need to know who really is here attending classes.

Why isn't my favorite teacher listed?
Campus Education Week faculty members are often involved in consulting businesses, government agencies, or other educational institutions.  They may be teaching abroad, doing research, or writing a book.  Sometimes they just can't work Campus Education Week into their schedules.  And with the many fine teachers available, we just are not able to include every teacher or all subject matters.

Why can't I record the lectures?
In planning Campus Education Week, we try to get the finest teachers and, as much as possible, give you direct contact with them.  In recording, we run the risk of copyright violations on printed or recorded material.  Copyrights, as you may know, can cover not just printed works but also ideas, arrangements, and other creative works.  It is easy to bend and break copyright laws, even unintentionally.  Also, there have been occurrences where a teacher's recorded comments were later taken out of context, creating a meaning the teacher never intended.  To avoid complications in either of these areas, we have made a decision not to allow the audio or video recording of presentations.  This is an administrative decision and is not at the discretion of the teacher, so please don't impose on teachers by asking them to break this policy.

This may be a disappointment to some of us who would like to use cassette tapes to record some of the presentations, but it is the only way we can offer certain copyrighted material at Campus Education Week.  The only other option would be to eliminate some information from the presentations, and this would defeat our goal of providing you with as many educational options as we can.

Why can't I save seats?
We spend a lot of time considering options that allow for a fair distributions seating.  First-come, first-served is not a perfect answer, but it is the best one we have been able to come up with.  So if one of your fellow students has planned and organized so that he/she can be in the seat next to you, don't deny him/her the privilege.  Instead of saving the seat next to you for a friend, you  could save your friendship for whomever is in the seat!  This new acquaintance may be a highlight of your Campus Education Week experience.

Why can't I eat or drink in class?
It is distracting to experience the rustle of sandwich wrappers, the crunch of an apple, or the whiff of pastrami during a lecture.  The majority of your fellow students have planned their mealtimes accordingly so that they can enjoy the lecture with no distractions.  The custodial crews also appreciate not having to deal with spilled drinks and other "munchie" refuse.

Why don't you have lunch and dinner breaks?
In fact, we did try that in one of our similar programs.  But everyone went to dinner at the same time, and the lines and confusion were tremendous.  Many students complained that they came to the program to learn, not to eat, and they were upset because we did not offer classes in order to provide meal breaks, we might cut your favorite ones!  This way, the choice is all yours.

What if the class I've chosen is full?
Many people have had the experience described by this student:"When I couldn't get in the class I wanted, I went into any class close by that had room.  I found it to be just as exciting and informative as the first-choice class.  Every class was good."  You may discover whole new areas of interest--and that is part of what Campus Education Week is all about.

Why can't I bring an extra chair into the classroom if all the seats are taken?
Many of us would be willing to stand in the back of the room for the entire lecture or sit on the cold, hard floor for the opportunity to hear our favorite teachers.  However, there are building, safety, and fire codes that will not allow us to pack a room beyond its legal seating capacity.  So if the class you want is full, do as the student above did--take it as a challenge to find an equally stimulating class where there will be a seat welcoming you.
