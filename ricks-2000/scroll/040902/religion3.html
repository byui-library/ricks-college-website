Elder Neal A. Maxwell: Submission to God achieved through law of consecration

by Tammy Walquist
WAL01018@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

ELDER MAXWELL
Elder Neal A. Maxwell spoke on consecrating oneself wholly to God and learning to submit to Him.

“We tend to think of consecration only as yielding up, when divinely directed, our material possessions,” Elder Maxwell said. “But ultimate consecration is the yielding of oneself to God.”

Many may ignore consecration because it seems too hard, but it comes in steps, he said.

“Spiritual submissiveness is not accomplished in an instant, but by incremental improvements and by using successive stepping stones,” Elder Maxwell said.

When people cling to skills more than God, they flinch in the face of the first commandment. More difficulties come from facing God on the surface while still withholding portions of inner selves, signifying that we are not totally His.

“The best way to valiantly testify of Christ is to become steadily more like Him. It is consecration that carves out the emulative character,” Elder Maxwell said.

Even too much attention to good things can diminish devotion, Elder Maxwell said.

“As we develop additional love, patience and meekness, the more we have to give God and humanity,” he said.

Jesus Christ is the ultimate example of submission, he said.

“[The] Breathtaking submission was achieved by the Savior as He faced the anguish and agonies of the Atonement, and ‘would that [He] might not drink the bitter cup and shrink,” Elder Maxwell said.

“The greatest happiness in God’s generous plan is finally reserved for those who are willing to stretch and to pay the costs of journeying to His regal realm,” Elder Maxwell said.
