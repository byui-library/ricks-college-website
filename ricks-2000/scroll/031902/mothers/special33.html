Moen sees world, shares her love

by Laura Bohon
BOH00002@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

One out of every four full-time faculty members at BYU-Idaho is female. This statistic may come as a shock to the student body, since female students outnumber the males by over 1,000 students.

However daunting such statistics may be, many female members of the faculty have used their unique situation and their diverse backgrounds to expand their horizons. One of these women is Joelle Moen, a professor in the English Department.

Raised in the small community of Adna, Wash., Moen was a member of one of the largest classes her high school had ever seen — 32 students. She had to transfer to another high school for her senior year because her previous school had run out of classes for her to take.

After high school, Moen went to BYU where she received a bachelor of arts degree in English and graduated as valedictorian (GPA 3.94). During her stay at BYU, Moen was chosen for the university’s Study Abroad program and spent a semester in London.

“[The Study Abroad program] changed who I was as a person; it changed my life,” she said. “I had been raised in such a small community, I had never seen homeless people before and London was suffering from a huge unemployment rate then. It was humbling to see how much I didn’t know.

“I also saw how even though they have different cultures, people are all alike. They all have families they love, they all tell jokes to each other. You learn so much so fast.”

After she received her degree at BYU, she attended Washington State University where she received a master of arts degree in English. She also served a mission for the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints in the Spain, Bilbao mission.

“You see Americans from different perspectives,” Moen said of her time in Spain. “We [as Americans] think we’re the best nation in the world, but try telling a Spaniard that.”

After her mission and education were completed, Moen taught English in Mexico at the Colegio America in San Marcos. She also taught at Centralia College and Washington State University before she was hired to teach at Ricks College in 1995.

Moen has had to adapt to the uniqueness of her position as a female professor at BYU-I.

“My department is wonderful,” Moen said. “I love the people that I work with ... I don’t think I’ve ever been kept away from an opportunity.”

There have been, however, small instances which have reminded her just what a rarity she is on campus. Once, when she went to the Cashier’s Office to deal with a transaction for the MesoAmerica tour (which she heads), Moen was told she needed a professor’s signature on a check.

“They said, ‘Oh, he [your professor] needs to sign that,’ and I didn’t quite know how to respond,” Moen said. “What was I supposed to say? ‘I’m he?’”

In addition, Moen said many visitors to the English Department assume she is a secretary.

Also, Moen voiced a concern for the ethnic diversity of the faculty.

“We need more minority teachers,” Moen said, “because when you have teachers with diverse backgrounds, you get different perspectives.”

Since she has been teaching at BYU-I, Moen has seen a great increase in the number of women teachers chosen to attend Study Abroad programs.

“Almost 70 percent of the Study Abroad students are women, and so they’ve been getting women teachers to come along since they’ve realized how much we can add to the program,” Moen said.

She has served as assistant director for the European Humanities Tour and is the director for next year’s MesoAmerica Tour. Moen also writes a tour book for the MesoAmerica Tour.

When she’s not teaching, Moen has developed hobbies conducive to Rexburg’s frigid weather.

“I go cross-country skiing a lot (I have my own cross-country skis now), and last year I learned how to snowboard,” Moen said. “In the summer I go play.”

During the summer, Moen and her mother often attend the Cedar City Shakespeare Festival and then go hiking.

“I love to go on vacation with Joelle. She can make Shakespeare fun,” Jarlee McCormick, Moen’s mother, said.
