Equal Opportunity Statement (2-1)

I. POLICY

BYU-Idaho is an educational institution which is wholly owned and controlled by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. As such, it strives to hire and employ only qualified and worthy members in good standing with the Church. (See 41 C.F.R. Section 60-1.5 (a)(5) which permits religious preference in employment.) Within the context of its religious preference, BYU-Idaho considers equal employment opportunity and nondiscrimination to be fundamental to its mission, goals, and objectives. Additionally, BYU-Idaho recognizes that its future success is, in part, dependent upon its ability to successfully accommodate the increasingly diverse nature of its student body and employees, which diversity is promoted by the worldwide growth of its sponsoring church.

This policy recognizes both a moral and a legal obligation to strive toward effective utilization of qualified persons based on job-related qualifications, including church affiliation, and not on unrelated physical or ethnic characteristics. BYU-Idaho, therefore, promotes equal opportunity for qualified applicants and employees. In this regard, BYU-Idaho will not tolerate nor permit known and unlawful discrimination based upon race, color, sex, age, national origin, religion, or disability. All aspects of the employer-employee relationship and all levels and segments of the University are governed by this policy.

This policy also recognizes the institutional objective to accommodate and promote the diverse nature of the University=s employees and students. To this end, BYU-Idaho

1. strives to create an environment wherein all employees and students can work and learn unencumbered by unfair and illegal discrimination,

2. strives to understand and effectively use the diverse background of all employees and students for the betterment of the institution,

3. actively strives to overcome cultural bias through the teaching and practicing of universal Christian ideals applicable to all peoples and cultures.

In addition to promoting equal opportunity and diversity within the institution, BYU-Idaho seeks, consistent with its educational mission, Church affiliation and business judgement to enhance the level of racial, ethnic, and gender balance within the institution. These concerns might be addressed through hiring qualified minority employees in part as role models for students or in recruiting qualified women employees for certain occupational categories. Progress in achieving greater balance and other similar concerns can also be accomplished by

1. encouraging a wider, more diversified group of applicants for employment and enrollments;

2. implementing other non-limiting, nondiscriminatory actions such as direct and/or geographically oriented advertising;

3. encouraging women employees to participate in existing career development programs;

4. evaluating and eliminating inappropriate cultural and physical barriers which discourage minority interest in attendance and/or employment at BYU-Idaho; etc.

To assist in accomplishing this policy, BYU-Idaho shall

1. not allow consideration of illegal and irrelevant, non-merit factors in any personnel decision;

2. promote greater balance in employment by attempting through advertisement and recruiting to create a more diverse group of applicants among worthy and qualified members of the Church in good standing;

3. address issues involving discrimination and diversity in both employment and academics including the elimination of cultural barriers, promoting full compliance with applicable civil rights legislation, disability law, and other concerns relating to civil rights and related legislation through the Personnel Advisory Council.

Employment Guidelines (2-2)

The following policies and procedures are set up to govern employment at BYU-Idaho. However, BYU-Idaho reserves the absolute right to deviate from this policy when circumstances warrant.

A. DEFINITION OF PERSONNEL CLASSIFICATIONS

1. Faculty Personnel

a. Faculty positions are those which are involved directly and primarily with classroom teaching or which require working individually in such an important relationship with the mind or intellect, as in the professional counseling relationship, that the ideas, philosophies, and subjects conveyed would have academic or intellectual significance as important as that of the classroom teacher.

b. Persons whose primary assignments are in administration or staff work, but who teach one or more classes incidentally, would not be classified in this group.

c. Examples of faculty personnel in addition to full-time teaching personnel are: academic division deans, counselors, and persons in professional positions in the library.

2. Exempt Personnel

a. Persons in this classification are administrative officers or professional personnel whose positions are characterized by one or more of the following responsibilities: executive, supervisory (major), managerial, professional, policy recommending independence of action, important contact with the public.

b. Persons in this classification make decisions requiring use of discretion and judgment which affect in a major way the business of the unit, the services it offers, the safety or comfort of the employed personnel, students, or visitors, the expenditure or saving of significant sums of money, relationship of the unit with its public, important records, building, and equipment.

c. Positions assigned to this classification which are primarily professional rather than managerial involve the use of professional skill of high degree which has required special education, such as medicine, auditing, etc., or the performance or original and creative work in a recognized artistic field.

d. Supervision of a type which is primarily passing out work assignments to subordinate employees engaged in relatively simple, repetitive work such as clerical, custodial, grounds, food, etc., and which is confined to responsibility for work methods, production, and results is not a level of supervision which would qualify a position for classification as exempt, unless in addition, other kinds of responsibility as indicated for the exempt classifications are present.

e. Performance of the executive, administrative, or professional duties or work closely and directly related to such duties involves at least 80% of the employee's working time.

3. Non-exempt Personnel

Non-exempt positions are those which directly perform the non-instructional work of the University that is conceived, planned, arranged, or supervised by others. Staff personnel are those employees classified as nonexempt to provisions of the Fair Labor Standards Act, which makes them subject to the overtime pay provisions of the Act.

4. Full-time Personnel

a. May be faculty, administrative, or staff.
b. Hired in a position designated as full-time by the Board of Trustees.
c. Employed to work three-fourths of the academic year or a minimum of 1,560 hours.
d. Paid on a salaried, hourly, or contract basis.

5. Part-time Personnel

a. May be faculty, administrative, or staff.
b. Hired in a position designated as part-time by the Board of Trustees.
c. Employed to work less than three-quarter time during the budget year or less than 1,560 hours.
d. Paid on a salaried, hourly, or contract basis.

6. Temporary Personnel

a. May be faculty, administrative, or staff.
b. Hired to work full time (generally not to exceed nine months), part time, or on call.
c. Paid on a salaried, hourly, or contract basis.
d. May be hired either in an approved position as designated by the Board of Trustees or in a position designated as temporary by the President's Staff--depending on the needs of the University.

B. EMPLOYMENT STANDARD

Consistent with it's Mission, BYU-Idaho is dedicated to providing a wholesome academic, cultural, social and spiritual environment. To this end, prospective employees sign agreements acknowledging compliance with specific items listed in this policy. Observance of the BYU-Idaho's Code of Honor, Dress and Grooming standards, policies and procedures, as well as temple worthiness are specific conditions of employment and continued employment.

C. EMPLOYMENT AT WILL

At BYU-Idaho, the employment relationship of administrative and staff employees exists at the will of either party and may be terminated at any time and for any cause whatsoever or for no cause other than for reasons prohibited by law. Inasmuch as employment is at will, no employee or officer of BYU-Idaho, other than the appropriate vice president, can make a commitment to a person for administrative or staff employment for a specified period of time. Any such commitment must be made in writing, dated and signed by the employee and the appropriate Vice President. The University policies and procedures do not constitute a contract nor an implied contract and may be changed or withdrawn according to need and discretion of the University administration. (See policy 5-6 for faculty.)

CHURCH EDUCATIONAL SYSTEM HONOR CODE (6 November 2000)

Brigham Young University, Brigham Young University-Hawaii, Brigham Young University-Idaho, and LDS Business College exist to provide an education in an atmosphere consistent with the ideals and principles of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. That atmosphere is created and preserved through commitment to conduct that reflects those ideals and principles. Members of the faculty, administration, staff, and student body at BYU, BYU-H, BYU-I, and LDSBC are selected and retained from among individuals who voluntarily live the principles of the gospel of Jesus Christ. Observance of such is a specific condition of employment and admission. Those individuals who are not members of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints are also expected to maintain the same standards of conduct, except church attendance. All who represent BYU, BYU-H, BYU-I, and LDSBC are to maintain the highest standards of honor, integrity, morality, and consideration of others in personal behavior. By accepting employment, continuing in employment, or continuing class enrollment, individuals evidence their commitment to observe the Honor Code standards approved by the Board of Trustees “at all times and... in all places” (Mosiah 18:9).


HONOR CODE STATEMENT

We believe in being honest, true, chaste, benevolent, virtuous, and in doing good to all men.... If there is anything virtuous, lovely, or of good report or praiseworthy, we seek after these things. (Thirteenth Article of Faith).

As a matter of personal commitment, the faculty, administration, staff, and students of Brigham Young University, Brigham Young University-Hawaii, Brigham Young University-Idaho, and LDS Business College seek to demonstrate in daily living on and off-campus those moral virtues encompassed in the gospel of Jesus Christ and will:

1. Abide by the standards of Christian living taught by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. This includes graciousness and consideration for others and the observance of high principles of honor, integrity, and morality.

2. Be honest in all behavior. This means to refrain from cheating, plagiarizing, or knowingly giving false information.

3. Live a chaste and virtuous life. This includes abstinence from all sexual relations outside the bonds of marriage.

4. Obey, honor, and sustain the law.

5. Comply with all of the employing unit’s regulations. This includes compliance with rules relating to campus organizations and to the use of the employing unit or off-campus housing or other facilities.

6. Use clean language.

7. Respect others. This includes:

a. Not physically or verbally abusing any person and not engaging in conduct which threatens or endangers the health or safety of others; and

b. Not obstructing or disrupting the study of others, the performance of official duties by officers or employees, the teaching, research, disciplinary, administrative or other functions of the university or other authorized activities on the premises of the university.

8. Respect property rights. This includes refraining from theft, concealment, damage, or misuse of the property of others.

9. Abstain from alcoholic beverages, tobacco, tea, coffee, and substance abuse. This includes refraining from the possession, use, or distribution of any narcotic or dangerous drug (as defined by applicable law), except as prescribed by a licensed medical practitioner.

10. Participate regularly in church services.

11. Observe BYU-Idaho standards of dress and grooming. See dress and grooming standards below.

12. Observe high standards of taste and decency. This includes refraining from disorderly, lewd, indecent, or obscene conduct or expression.

13. Help others to fulfill their responsibilities under this Code.

Specific policies embodied in the Honor Code include the continuing employee and student ecclesiastical endorsement requirements (refer to these policies for more detailed information).


DRESS AND GROOMING STANDARDS

Employees of BYU-Idaho are expected, in matters of dress and grooming, to observe high standards of modesty, taste, and propriety. Observance of these standards is a specific condition of employment. These standards apply to all employees while they are on the job. Except when circumstances make it impractical to do so, student employees should dress and groom themselves according to these same standards.

Inasmuch as employees occupy a position of role model for the students, a more formal appearance is expected in certain areas of dress and grooming than is expected of students. The attire and grooming of both men and women should always be modest, neat, clean, and consistent with the dignity of a representative of BYU-Idaho and The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. Clothing must be modest in fabric, fit, and style.

1. Male Employees. Male employees are expected to wear a shirt and tie with dress slacks. In areas where shirts, ties, and dress slacks are not appropriate for the work environment, the division dean or director reporting directly to a vice president, and in consultation with that vice president, will determine satisfactory attire, including the wearing of uniforms where appropriate. Extreme or immodest clothing styles are unacceptable. These include such styles as tight-fitting trousers and shirts unbuttoned below the collar button. Jeans are not appropriate professional attire.

A clean and well-cared-for appearance should be maintained.  Hairstyles should be clean and neat, avoiding extreme styles or colors, and trimmed above the collar leaving the ear uncovered.  Sideburns should not extend below the earlobe or onto the cheek.  If worn, mustaches should be neatly trimmed and may not extend beyond or below the corners of the mouth.  Men are expected to be clean shaven; beards are not acceptable.  Earrings and other body piercing are unacceptable.  Shoes should be worn in all public campus areas.

2. Female Employees. Women should wear dresses or blouses and skirts with hemlines below the knee. This is the expected dress standard in the majority of circumstances. Dresses or skirts above the knee or those with slits above the knee are considered immodest and inappropriate. Women should wear appropriate hosiery. Extreme or immodest clothing styles are unacceptable. Excessive ear piercing (more than one per ear) and other body piercing are not acceptable. Shoes should be worn in all public campus areas.

Dress slacks and pant suits may be worn occasionally. Dress slacks or pantsuits must meet the following definition: “A coordinated pantsuit or dress slacks and top that complement each other with similar dressy-style fabric, design, and appropriate pattern. This could be a two- or three-piece outfit of a dressy nature that is appropriate for meeting the public.” This definition does not include casual type slacks with casual sweaters or blouses.

The wearing of dress slacks was approved strictly to relieve possible economic burdens and for extreme weather conditions. Jeans of any color or fabric are not appropriate professional attire. In areas where dresses or skirts are not appropriate for the work environment, the division chair or director who reports directly to a vice president, and in consultation with that vice president, will determine satisfactory attire, including the wearing of uniforms where appropriate.

3. Safety Clothing. In those situations where dress standards may require modification for safety reasons, e.g. working with machinery or chemicals, the department should follow OSHA’s approved safety clothing guidelines. For those situations requiring safety clothing, approval should be obtained from the department head and the appropriate vice president. Where specific safety clothing or uniforms are required by management, it will be provided by the department in question.

4. Dress Standards Enforcement. Supervisors have the responsibility of maintaining dress and grooming standards in their departments. They are also responsible to interpret, as appropriate for the circumstances in which their employees work, the meaning and terms of the dress and grooming standards.

E.  CES ANNUAL ECCLESIASTICAL CLEARANCE FOR EMPLOYEES
     (Approved by the Board of Trustees, January 1996)

I. Policy

The Church Educational System (Brigham Young University, Brigham Young University-Hawaii, BYU-Idaho, LDS Business College, Seminary and Institutes, Elementary and Secondary Schools) will hire and retain as employees only those individuals who are eligible to receive a temple recommend, whether or not they currently have or need one. Comparable standards are required for those Church Educational System employees who are not members of the Church. This includes living a 24-hour standard of the Word of Wisdom and honor code. This policy applies to all employees (including faculty), exempt, nonexempt, full-time, part-time, and temporary. However, non-LDS personnel who are employed for a period of time less than one calendar year are not required to live the Word of Wisdom when they are in strictly private settings. Non-LDS employees hired before February 8, 1996 will continue to be governed by the understanding of their compliance to the Word of Wisdom given to them by their employer when they were hired. No exceptions will be granted for full-time non-LDS personnel hired in the future. Student employees are automatically covered through their Honor Code commitment.

II. Procedures

1. All employees are aware of and, as a condition of employment, participate in the annual clearance by ecclesiastical leaders or interviews by designated administrators.

2. Annually, beginning January 1, each employer will prepare for the Commissioner's signature, letters to the bishop of each employee asking whether or not the employee is eligible for a temple recommend. The employer will provide a return envelope marked "confidential" that is addressed to a designated administrator in human resource services of the employing institution. As these lists are sent to this human resource administrator he/she will ensure that all lists are returned and processed on a confidential basis by the appropriate individuals. Personnel services will maintain the lists in a confidential file separate from the employee's official personnel file.

Employees who are not members of the Church will be interviewed by a person designated by the President/Administrator to ensure that they are living in accordance with this policy. An annual letter of recommendation from the interviewer will be maintained in a confidential file separate from the employee's official personnel file.

3. Procedures for employees who do not receive an ecclesiastical clearance.

a. The employee and a designated administrator will meet to ascertain the employee's intentions.

b. Employees who express a desire to change to become eligible for clearance may, at the administrator's discretion, be placed on probation for a maximum of six months rather than be terminated.

c. At the end of six months probation, the employee's bishop (designated administrator for non-members) will be contacted again. Employees who are unable to receive clearance at the end of probation, will be terminated unless they make an appeal in writing to the applicable administrator. In the written appeal, the employee may give his/her bishop the right to discuss the reason(s) for an LDS employee's ineligibility with the administrator. The administrator will make the final decision as to whether employment will continue.

d. Employees who are not placed on probation will be terminated two weeks following the joint interview outlined in "a," in accordance with the institution's policies on grounds and procedures for termination.

e. An employee who has a history of more than one clearance probation will be subject to termination. The administrator will make the final decision as to whether employment will continue.

f. The initial list from a bishop/designated administrator indicating inability to clear an employee will be retained for five years.

4. As an acceptable alternative for clearance of non-contractual employees located outside the United States, employees may show current temple recommends or provide a letter of clearance from their bishop.

In these cases employers will maintain annual lists of clearances made in this manner. All other policies and procedures remain unmodified for this group of employees.

F. CONFLICT OF INTEREST

Each employee is expected to understand the terms of his or her employment at BYU-Idaho and to give their full measure of time, talent, and loyalty to the University. Any activity or work pattern that adversely affects or influences University employees or employment, in terms of productivity, time worked, or improper influence is in conflict with his or her basic obligation.

Employees are responsible for assuring that they do not engage in activities that could be considered a conflict of interest, and should report any possible conflict of interest to their supervisor.

Additional information specifically related to faculty can be found in Policy 5-7.

1. Personal Conduct. Employees must carefully consider all circumstances and possible consequences of their business and personal dealings that could be a conflict of interest with University employment. Employees are expected to avoid any situation where they use their University positions to influence business transactions to their personal benefit. They must not become involved in any activity that could compromise, or appear to compromise, their ability to perform their duties or to make decisions in their work assignments that are not in the best interest of the University.

2. Conflict Situations. It is not possible in a policy statement to define all of the various circumstances that would constitute a conflict of interest, but the following are examples of situations where a conflict of interest could exist and should therefore be immediately disclosed by employees to their supervisor:

a. Engaging, during an employee's working hours, in business activities that are not a part of the employee's assigned duties.

b. Engaging in a private business that provides goods or services to the University.

c. Providing for compensation, consulting services, research, or instruction to another academic institution.

d. Maintaining a business or professional telephone listing or off-campus office.

e. Using University personnel, equipment, supplies, or space for church, civic, business, or other activities without reimbursing the University.

f. Accepting valuable gifts or favors from businesses which provide goods or services to BYU-Idaho. This also includes traveling or being housed at a vendor's expense.

g. Displaying merchandise, soliciting co-workers, distributing literature, or participating in fund raising for personal interests on campus.

h. Exerting influence on, or being a part of, any business transaction involving the University from which the employee or a relative could receive benefit or gain of any kind.

3. Church Duties and BYU-Idaho Obligations. Employees holding Church positions should not take time from their work for the performance of routine church duties. An employee's office or place of business should normally not be used for church business during working hours. Church duties should be performed in addition to regular working hours as is the case for other members of the church who are employed in business, government or other institutions not sponsored by the church.

It is recognized that emergency or critical matters can arise involving church duties which may have to be handled on an immediate basis during working hours. An example would be the conducting of a funeral by a bishop or a student coming in spontaneously with a personal problem.

Employees should not schedule regular church business meetings or routine consultations or interviews during their working hours, except where assignments to General Boards, priesthood committees, etc., make avoiding such impossible.

Certain assignments, particularly assignments to attend conference or to participate in priesthood committees of the church may require time which is normally part of the workday. Where this occurs, the employee is expected to use vacation for the time taken to fulfill these assignments.

4. Surplus Property. Neither an employee nor persons or organizations through whom the employee may benefit personally should purchase surplus property from the University if such employee participated in a material way in the University's decision to sell such property.

5. Other University Employment. No employee should be paid twice for performing additional work during the same hours as his or her regular work schedule. Therefore, additional employment by an organizational unit of the University, other than the unit in which a person is regularly employed, is normally not allowed. Participation in continuing education programs will not be regarded as a conflict of interest, assuming compliance with existing policies governing participation by University employees in such programs.

Procedure

1. At the time of employment, all administrative and staff employees will sign a Conflict of Interest Disclosure Statement. Situations that might be considered a conflict of interest should be disclosed in writing to the immediate supervisor and reviewed through the management channels with the appropriate vice president.

2. The supervisor will indicate in writing what action has been taken with regard to the conflict of interest disclosed by the employee.

3. If a possible conflict of interest arises during the course of employment, the employee must immediately inform the supervisor in writing for review as explained above.

4. Periodically, all employees will be asked to sign an updated conflict of interest disclosure statement.

They may not be employed where their positions would cause management concerns about preferential treatment of a relative in such things as hiring, promoting, allocating salary, assigning duties, arranging schedules, allocating equipment or facilities, or handling discipline or termination. This policy applies to full-time, part-time, student, and temporary personnel.

H. RECRUITMENT OF ADMINISTRATIVE AND STAFF PERSONNEL
   (see Policy 5-4 for faculty recruitment)

BYU-Idaho seeks to hire the most suitably qualified persons for open positions without regard to race, sex, color, national origin, age, or, if otherwise qualified, disability. BYU-Idaho reserves the right to prefer members in good standing of its sponsor, The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, and to otherwise make employment decisions to further the standards, values, and objectives of the Church.

BYU-Idaho encourages recruitment from within the university community. The area of consideration for each vacancy should be defined as narrowly as possible while still being able to obtain a sufficient number of quality applicants. Examples of common areas of consideration include: all qualified internal and external candidates, all university employees, department or division employees, etc.

When promoting from within the department or making transfers within the University, formal posting of the vacancy is not required. However, management personnel are encouraged to post positions before making a final decision on new hires. Posting positions provides current employees new job opportunities and gives the hiring department a broader range of candidates to select from.

Procedure

1. Personnel Requisition Form. A Personnel Requisition Form must be completed and sent through appropriate approval channels to Personnel Services for replacement of all administrative and staff positions. New positions must be requested during the annual budget process.

2. Internal Transfers. Transfers of employees currently working for the university may be requested following approval of the Personnel Requisition. An employee should have been in their current position for one year prior to requesting a transfer.

3. Advertising. Personnel Services will coordinate the advertising of open positions. Positions are open for the accepting of applications for a minimum of five consecutive days. Each position (except in the case of internal transfers) will be advertised on the Internet, Employment Job Line: 356-1181 and posted at several locations on campus. Requests can be made to Personnel Services for advertisement in local and national media.

4. Interviews. Personnel Services will conduct preliminary screening interviews for open positions according to the qualifications identified in the approved job description. Only those candidates having demonstrated possession of the minimum qualifications during the initial screening will be referred to the hiring unit.

Once the vacancy has been closed, Personnel Services will review applications with the hiring unit and assist them in selecting candidates for further consideration and/or interviews. Personnel Services will provide information about hiring procedures, interview questions, legal issues, selection techniques, etc.

The hiring unit will schedule interviews with the top candidates who meet the qualifications for the position and select the individual they would like to hire.

5. Ecclesiastical Clearance. Personnel Services will obtain the appropriate consent and check with the prospective employee's ecclesiastical leader to determine temple worthiness.

6. Employment Checks. Personnel Services and the hiring department will jointly determine how employment reference checks will be made.

7. Extending Job Offers. When necessary clearances are made for the prospective employee, Personnel Services will work with the hiring unit to determine salary. No employment offers can be made without clearance from Personnel Services.

Either the hiring unit or Personnel Services may make the job offer to the prospective employee. A letter verifying the employment offer should be sent to the prospective employee. Personnel Services will provide a sample letter of information that should be included in the offer.

8. Individuals Not Hired. When the hiring decision has been made, the hiring unit should forward employment reference check information and a list of individuals who were interviewed, but not hired, along with the reasons for not hiring to Personnel Services.

9. Physical Examination. Personnel Services will make an appointment for a post-offer physical examination when required (most blue collar positions). Job offers are dependent upon successfully passing of the physical examination. Employees who require a physical examination may not begin work prior to completion of the examination and clearance from the Heath Center.

10. Contact of Job Candidates Not Selected. The hiring unit is responsible to contact all individuals they interviewed for the position. Personnel Services will provide feedback to candidates who were not selected for interviews by the hiring unit.

11. Employment Eligibility Verification. The new employee must report to Personnel Services on or before the first day of work to complete required employment forms. University policy stipulates that Form I-9 must be completed for the new employee on or before the first day of work.

12. New-Employee Orientation. After the hire is approved, employees will be invited to attend a New Employee Orientation where the University benefits will be explained.

 I. HOURS OF WORK AND WORKWEEK

1. Workweek

The standard workweek begins at 12:01 a.m. Saturday and ends at midnight the following Friday.

2. Working Hours

a. Standard working hours for all areas on campus are 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. with an hour off for lunch from 12:00 p.m. to 1:00 p.m.

b. Departments may allow individuals to stagger their lunch hours in order to maintain office coverage during the standard lunch hour. However, an individual may not take a lunch break such that it shortens the work day at either end.

c. Areas which have only one receptionist will maintain the standard working hours.

d. Departments which, as a whole, desire working hours other than the standard working hours specified above, must submit a request in writing for an exception to this policy to the Director, Personnel Services.

3. Rest Periods

a. A rest period normally will be scheduled by the supervisor for 10 minutes in each consecutive four-hour work period.

b. The rest period may not be used to shorten the work day or extend the lunch period. Rest periods may not be accumulated.

J. OUTSIDE EMPLOYMENT

1. Outside Employment. An employee may obtain outside employment if:

a. Performance in the current job is not adversely affected by the outside employment (see G. Conflict of Interest)

b. The employee does not engage in any phase of outside employment during working hours at BYU-Idaho.

c. The normal working schedule is not adjusted to allow time for the outside employment.

2. Second BYU-Idaho Job. BYU-Idaho personnel may only be employed in one full-time or part-time position at a time.

K. TEMPORARY EMPLOYMENT

BYU-Idaho has two classifications of temporary employees:

1. On-call temporaries approved through Personnel Services for work during peak times, seasonal rush work, or to fill in for employees on vacation or intermittent sick leave.

2. Full-time temporaries, approved through President's Council, to fill in for someone on an extended leave or to complete a special assignment.  These temporaries are generally hired for less than 6 months but should not exceed 9 months.

The following guidelines apply to on-call temporary employment.

1. Annual Update.  Each year, hiring units develop and submit a list of persons the unit wishes to use during the upcoming school year.  The list should include each person's ward to facilitate clearance and approval for hiring.

2. Pay Rates.  After receiving clearance from Personnel Services, the hiring unit, using approved temporary pay guidelines, submits the rate of pay for each individual to Personnel Services for approval.

3. Notification. The hiring unit invites the individuals to come to work outlining to them their expected length of service, salary, working hours, etc.

4. Update of Payroll Paperwork. New temporary employees added to the list are required to come to Personnel Services on or before their first day of work to fill out the necessary payroll forms.

5. Approvals. Only individuals approved for hire by Personnel Services may be used. As needed, Personnel Services will work with the employing unit to find additional temporary personnel.

6. Non-Exempt Employees Working on a Temporary Basis. Occasionally, non-exempt employees are asked to work on a temporary basis. These assignments should not interfere with the employee's primary job function. When the temporary work causes a non-exempt employee to work over 40 hours during the workweek, the employee will be paid overtime (time and one-half) at the rate of pay established for the temporary work.

The following guidelines apply to full-time temporaries:

1. Approval Process.  The hiring unit should complete a Personnel Requisition Form and submit it through Personnel Services for President's Council approval.  The request should include a written justification of the need, the job duties, and length of time needed.

2. Recruitment Process.  The approved requisition form is returned to Personnel Services by the vice president.  A formal posting and interviewing process is not required if a person can be identified to fill the position.  However, the hiring unit will need to provide the person's ward information to Personnel Services for clearance purposes prior to starting work.  Otherwise, Personnel Services will assist the department in finding a suitable candidate to fill the temporary position.  Personnel Services will obtain ecclesiastical clearances and work with the hiring unit to determine an appropriate salary.

3. Notification. The hiring unit invites the individual to come to work and gives the individual hired a letter on or before the first day of work outlining the expected length of service, job expectations, salary, working hours, etc.  A copy of the letter should be sent to Personnel Services.

4. Payroll Paperwork.  The employee is required to come to Personnel Services on or before his/her first day of work to fill out the necessary payroll forms.

Changes to Temporary Work Assignments.  Any changes to the temporary assignment must be approved by President's Council.

L. TRANSFER, PROMOTION AND DEMOTION OF EMPLOYEES

Insofar as highly qualified applicants are available, BYU-Idaho will strive to promote from within to fill a vacant position and attempt to accommodate employee's needs for different positions by allowing employees to transfer. Promotions, transfers and demotions need to be closely coordinated with Personnel Services.

1. Transfers

a. BYU-Idaho will give preference to current employees desiring to transfer as long as they are suitably qualified for the position.

b. Employees who desire to transfer must notify their current supervisors of their desire to transfer before they may be considered for a transfer. After notifying their supervisors, employees notify Personnel Services of their desire.

c. The effective date of a transfer should be mutually agreed upon between the losing unit and the gaining unit as courtesy dictates and should normally be at the beginning of a new pay period.

d. An employee should have been employed in his or her current position for one year before requesting a transfer. Exceptions must be approved by Personnel Services.

2. Promotions

a. BYU-Idaho will promote the most suitably qualified employee regardless of race, sex, color, national origin, age, or disability.

b. An employee may be promoted if there is a more responsible position available, and the employee meets the qualifications for the job.

3. Demotions

a. Employees who move or are moved to lesser positions will generally not have their salaries reduced unless required by circumstances.

b. The immediate supervisor or Personnel Services will notify the employee in writing of the impact of the move on the employee's salary (including future raises), months of service in a year, and the effect of the move on other benefits (including sick leave, vacation, and retirement). A copy of the written notification will be placed in the employee's official personnel file.

Employee Compensation (2-4)

A. JOB DESCRIPTIONS AND JOB AUDITS

1. Descriptions on File. Personnel Services maintains a file of approved job descriptions.

2. Responsibility to Prepare and Update Descriptions. Division and department heads have the responsibility to see that job descriptions are written for newly created positions. When job duties change significantly, the job description should be changed as soon as possible, but not later than six months after the change.

3. Use of Job Descriptions. Job descriptions are used to determine the value of each job in order to assign a specific salary and to explain job expectations to the employee. It is essential, therefore, that each description accurately describe the essential functions and duties of the job.

4. Required before Hiring. New personnel can be recruited and hired only if current job descriptions are on file in Personnel Services.

5. Job Audits. Division and department heads should periodically review the job descriptions for the positions reporting to them to see that they accurately describe the duties being performed. Updates on job descriptions should be sent to Personnel Services. An audit of each position against its job description will be conducted under the direction of Personnel Services as needed.

B. JOB TITLES

The Job Evaluation Committee is responsible for approving employee job titles, including any special descriptive titles, to see that titles are used consistently throughout the organization.

C. JOB EVALUATION

Job evaluation is the process used to determine the relative value of a specific job as compared with other jobs on campus. The results of the evaluations are used in conjunction with salary surveys to establish the salary range for each position. Salary ranges relate to the responsibilities, duties, and skills required to successfully perform the job and not to the qualifications of the person currently occupying the position.

Any change in major duties assigned to a position should be coordinated with Personnel Services to assess any possible impact on the salary range.

D. EXEMPT AND NON-EXEMPT

The determination to classify a position as exempt or non-exempt is governed by legal requirements set forth in the Fair Labor Standards Act. Personnel Services will make these decisions by comparing the job with the tests outlined in the Fair Labor Standards Act.

 E. UPGRADING OF EXISTING ADMINISTRATIVE & STAFF POSITIONS

1. Filling a Vacated Position. An administrative or staff position that has been approved and evaluated shall, when vacated, remain under the same job assignment, at the same evaluation level, and essentially the same definition or responsibilities as originally approved. When a vacancy is to be filled, it is with the understanding that the person selected will be chosen to fulfill essentially the same responsibilities as originally approved and evaluated.

2. Changing Responsibilities of a Position. Duties of a position may be changed with approval of the Director, Personnel Services if it remains at essentially the same level with no expectation of a change in job evaluation points.

3. Procedure for Requesting Upgrading of Existing Positions.

a. A department seeking approval to upgrade an existing position should send a written request, signed by the division dean or administrative head concerned, to the Director, Personnel Services. The request should include a statement of new and additional duties to be added to the present position, duties to be deleted, reasons requiring the need for such changes and an indication of the source of funding to cover an increase in salary.

b. A review will be conducted by Personnel Services and a recommendation will be submitted, together with the department's request to the Job Evaluation Committee. Requests to change a position from staff to administrative or vice versa must first be approved by President's Council and meet legal requirements set forth in the Fair Labor Standards Act.

c. The Job Evaluation Committee will make the decision as to whether or not the requested change is approved and the department will be notified of that decision through the Personnel Services office.

F. REQUESTING NEW POSITIONS

New positions should be requested during the annual budget process. If a new position is needed outside the regular budget process, the following procedure applies:

1. The requesting unit will submit a written request for the new position with any supporting documents to the appropriate vice president. Non-budgeted units should show, as part of their request, that an additional position will not adversely affect their profitability.

2. If the responsible vice president approves of the request, he/she will present the request in President's Council.

3. The responsible vice president will inform Personnel Services in writing of any decision to create a new position.

G. SALARY COMMITMENTS

No salary commitments may be made to new or current employees without clearance through Personnel Services.

H. MINIMUM WAGE

The University will pay at least the current federal minimum wage.

I. EQUAL PAY

No department will discriminate between employees on the basis of sex by paying wages at a rate less than the rate paid to individuals of the opposite sex for work which requires equal skill, effort, and responsibility, and which is performed under similar working conditions.

J. REPORTING HOURS WORKED

1. Non-exempt employees are required to report all time worked and all time taken off.

2. Non-exempt employees are to be paid for hours actually worked. They are not to be allowed time off in one week which is to be made up in another week. This time taken off must be reported as vacation or leave without pay.

K. OVERTIME

1. Overtime Payments. Generally, overtime is not permitted at the University. However, if overtime is warranted, it must be approved before the fact by the appropriate supervisor or director.

2. Exempt Employees. Exempt personnel are expected to work the time necessary to accomplish their assigned responsibilities. Exempt employees are not paid overtime. Compensatory time off may be used to compensate exempt persons when they are required to work excessive hours of overtime over long periods of time.

3. Non-exempt Employees. Non-exempt persons must be paid overtime at time and one-half rates for any time worked in excess of the standard 40-hour week. When a nonexempt employee works for a second department, that is, a department that is not the employee's main place of employment, the second department must pay time and a half for any overtime the non-exempt employee works at the rate of pay established for the second job. Compensatory time is not allowed when working for a second department.

4. Workweek. A workweek is usually defined as beginning at 12:01 a.m. on Saturday and ending at 12:00 midnight the following Friday.

a. Workweek Adjustments. If it becomes necessary to require a non-exempt person to work more than the standard eight-hour day, it is desirable, when possible, to adjust the workweek rather than pay time and one-half. For example, if a person works 12 hours Monday and 12 hours Tuesday, he may be given Wednesday off so that with the regular eight-hour day on Thursday and Friday the total time worked would not be in excess of 40 hours. It is possible to adjust a workweek in this manner only during the same workweek in which the overtime is worked.

b. Compensatory Time Off. Non-exempt personnel may be given hour-for-hour compensatory time off in lieu of cash overtime payments if the time off is given in the same week that the overtime is worked. Each workweek must stand alone.

Compensatory time off may also be given in another week so long as the time off is (1) given within the same pay period that the overtime is worked, and (2) given at the rate of one and one-half hours for each hour of overtime worked.

5. Paid Holidays and Other Leaves. Days absent from the job for vacation, sick leave, or holidays do not count as time worked during the week for the purpose of computing overtime pay.

6. Working on Holidays. An employee who is required to work on a holiday which occurs during the normal workweek (Monday through Friday) will be given time off on another working day in the same workweek. If an employee, required to work on a holiday, cannot be given time off in that workweek, the employee will be paid for the time worked on the holiday plus an additional eight hours. See exception for Labor Day weekend under item 7.

7. Labor Day Weekend Exception. When full-time, part-time, or temporary employees are required to work Saturday or Monday on the Labor Day weekend for school start-up, the hours worked on those days will be paid at time and one-half (full-time employees would also receive their regular holiday pay). Full-time employees may be given the option of taking another day off during that week for the holiday rather than receive time and one-half pay. This exception does not apply to employees whose job duties already require weekend and holiday shifts.

8. On Call. In order to be considered working overtime an employee must be at his or her workstation or the place he or she is required by a supervisor to work. Being on call does not constitute an overtime situation unless the employee has restrictions placed on them such as distance from work, response time to calls, etc.

L. HOLIDAYS WHILE ON LEAVE WITHOUT PAY

Employees on leave without pay will not be paid for a holiday which occurs during their leave without pay. Employees whose leave without pay begins on the working day following the holiday will be paid for the holiday. Employees who return from leave without pay on the working day following a holiday will not be paid for the holiday.

M. PREPARATION OF PAYCHECKS

To provide timely preparation of paychecks, time and attendance reports must be received in the Payroll Office by noon of the workday following the last workday of each pay period. Pay periods end on the 15th and on the last day of the month.

N. RECORD KEEPING REQUIREMENTS

The Fair Labor Standards Act requires extensive payroll records be maintained by the employer. Payroll and time and attendance forms must be retained for three years by the Payroll Office.

O. TRAVEL TIME

BYU-Idaho will follow the guidelines defined in the Fair Labor Standards Act as explained below:

1. Home to Work Travel. Ordinary home-to-work and work-to-home travel is not working time.

2. Home to Work on Special One-Day Assignment in Another City. For one day assignments in another city, most of the hours spent traveling and working are considered work time. Normal meal periods may be deducted, as well as the time usually spent getting from home to the normal work location and back.

3. Travel That Is All In The Day's Work. Time spent by an employee in travel as part of his principal activity, such as travel from job site to job site during the workday, must be counted as time worked.

4. Travel Away from Home. Travel away from home is considered worktime when it cuts across the employee's workday (i.e. travel during the normal working hours counts as worktime; travel outside the regular work hours does not count as worktime).

Travel away from home outside of regular workdays (such as Saturday or Sunday) is considered worktime when the travel occurs during the employee's regularly scheduled work hours.  Time spent in travel as a passenger on an airplane, train, boat, bus, or in an automobile does not count as worktime when it occurs outside the regularly scheduled work hours.

5. Work Performed While Traveling. Any work which an employee is required to perform while traveling must be counted as hours worked. Any employee who drives a truck, bus, automobile, boat or airplane, or an employee who is required to ride therein as an assistant or helper, is working while riding, except during bona fide meal periods or when permitted to sleep in adequate facilities furnished by the employer.

P. WORKER'S COMPENSATION

1. Description. BYU-Idaho provides a program covering standard medical and hospital services, medicines, and appliances necessary for treatment of on-the-job injuries and illnesses. This coverage is provided at no cost to the employee and compensates full-time, part-time, and temporary employees as provided by state law. It also covers student club advisors during activities associated with the clubs.

2. Legal Aspect. All claims are governed by the laws of the State of Idaho. Copies of all claims are kept on file in the personnel office, the Industrial Commission Office in Boise, and the Church Worker's Compensation Office in Salt Lake City.

3. Payment of Claims. Compensation is paid to the disabled employee or the beneficiary of a fatally-injured employee at the rate prescribed by state law.

4. Sick Leave Use. In the event of lost time, accrued sick leave may be used by the employee up to a maximum of five (5) calendar days in order to keep the employee at his or her full rate of pay until worker's compensation is granted beginning the sixth calendar day. After worker's compensation is granted, employees may use available sick leave to supplement their compensation so as to receive a full pay check.

Time off beyond five calendar days must be counted as leave without pay with a notation the employee is on worker's compensation. Employees whose leave extends beyond 14 calendar days will receive worker's compensation for the first five days off work. This pay is granted regardless of other pay the employee received by using sick leave.

5. Vacation and Sick Leave Accumulation. Employees will accumulate vacation and sick leave while on leave without pay for work injuries. If the time off is more than 45 calendar days, the employee should apply for long-term disability. The employee will not continue to receive vacation and sick leave accumulation while on disability for a work-related injuries.

6. Employees Paid on Contract. Employees such as faculty members and others hired on a contract basis who continue to receive their contracted pay even though they are off for on-the-job injuries are not eligible to receive lost time wages under worker's compensation.

7. Absence from Work. Absence from work, due to work-connected injury or illness, is compensable only when supported by a doctor's report submitted in writing to Personnel Services.

8. Change of Doctors. Change of doctors is permitted only by referral from the treating physician.

9. Filing Claims. Supervisors are required to instruct all employees that in the event of injury they must advise the attending physician and/or medical facility that the injury is work related so that proper billing is made against the worker's compensation fund and not against the employee or the University medical insurance program. Supervisors are also asked to remind those who require medical assistance that they should first report to the BYU-Idaho Health Center before going to the family physician.

10. Relationship to Family Medical Leave. Employees on worker's compensation may also have the time off counted as family medical leave, depending on the duration of the time off.

11. Relationship to Long-Term Disability. Employees who must be off work longer than 45 calendar days due to a work injury, should also submit paperwork to DMBA for long-term disability.

12. Denial of Claims. Claims not reported to the employer within 60 days of the work injury will be denied.

Reporting Procedures

1. All work related accidents, injuries, and illnesses are to be reported to the supervisor immediately (always within 24 hours of the injury).

2. A "Notice of Injury and Claim for Benefits" form must be completed by the employee and signed by the supervisor.

3. Claim forms must be submitted to the institutional safety administrator at the BYU-Idaho Safety Office.

4. Any bills in connection with the accident or injury are to be submitted to the Personnel Services Department, which are then sent to the General Adjustment Bureau (GAB) in Boise for payment.

5. Payment of medical bills and worker's compensation is dependent on timely reporting of on-the-job injuries. Work injuries not reported to the employer within 60 days, will be denied for payment by GAB.

Q. PAYMENTS TO SURVIVORS FOLLOWING THE DEATH OF FULL-TIME EMPLOYEES

BYU-Idaho desires to make prompt payment of any earned salary to the spouse or surviving children or estate of a full-time employee who dies while employed at BYU-Idaho. This is in addition to a voluntary gift from the University when appropriate.

When a full-time employee dies while employed at the University, the spouse or surviving children or the administrator of the estate (as appropriate in the circumstances) will receive all the salary earned to the end of the month in which the employee dies. The Payroll Office will deduct appropriate federal, state, and university deductions.

1. Faculty Members on Contract. The percentage of the contract completed through the month in which the employee died will be applied to the total contract to determine the amount due. 

2. Staff and Administrative Personnel. In addition to the salary for the month in which the employee dies, current earned vacation pay for unused vacation time will be added to the check.

3. Voluntary Gift. BYU-Idaho may provide an additional voluntary gift as an expression of sympathy to the surviving spouse or dependent children as appropriate. This payment will not be made if there is no surviving spouse or dependent children. Normally, this amount may be up to two months salary. The amount is to be rounded to the nearest $100.

BYU-Idaho is required to report this gift to the Internal Revenue Service on Form 1099. It is recommended that recipients review the tax ramification of the gift with their tax adviser.

4. Responsibility for Administering Payments. Payments to survivors is administered by the President and Vice Presidents. Decisions on administration and/or exceptions to this policy will be made by them.

C. DISCIPLINE AND DISCHARGE OF ADMINISTRATIVE AND STAFF EMPLOYEES
     (see Policy 5-6 for faculty)

BYU-Idaho values each individual employee and desires to assist each employee to succeed in their respective responsibilities. Therefore, when discipline is required, the action to be taken will depend on the nature of the offense and the employee's previous record. Actions that may be taken range from informal counseling to immediate dismissal.

1. The employment relationship between BYU-Idaho and an employee exists at the will of either party and may be terminated at any time with appropriate notice for any cause whatsoever or no cause, other than for reasons prohibited by law.

2. Some of the reasons which may lead to termination or discipline include, but are not limited to, the following:

a. Misconduct - violation of BYU-Idaho standards as outlined in the Code of Honor.

b. Insubordination - refusal or failure to follow a reasonable directive of a superior.

c. Unsatisfactory performance - unacceptable work, poor attitude, incompetence, or carelessness.

d. Unacceptable behavior - unexcused absences, excessive tardiness, sleeping or loafing on the job, leaving without permission, or excessive breaks.

3. The decision and responsibility to discipline or terminate an employee rests with line management. However, a contact should be made with the director of Personnel Services before proceeding with discipline or termination procedures.

4. Employees who feel that they have been unfairly dealt with may file a grievance through appropriate management channels. See "Work-Related Concerns" Section J.

Procedure

1. For issues which are not serious enough to merit immediate discharge, the following guidelines will generally be adhered to:

a. Verbal Counseling. Unless a situation requires more serious and immediate action, a supervisor may respond to unacceptable performance or behavior with verbal counseling.

b. First Warning (written). In the event of subsequent offenses or continued unacceptable performance, a written warning should be issued. It is permissible to initiate disciplinary action with a written warning if the issue involved requires more serious and immediate action than verbal counseling would accomplish or if the verbal counseling has not remedied the situation.

The written warning should be clearly labeled so that the nature of the document is fully understood. The contents of the letter should accomplish the following:

1) Describe specific deficiencies, problems or offenses (what is the problem behavior).

2) Describe what specific changes need to be accomplished (what is appropriate behavior).

3) Describe future possible consequences if changes are not enacted.

4) Indicate an evaluation date that allows the employee a reasonable amount of time to make the necessary changes.

The employee should sign and date the document signifying acknowledgment and receipt of the warning. A copy should be provided to the employee and a signed copy should be sent to the director of Personnel Services.

c. Second Warning (written). In the event of continued offenses or poor performance following a written warning, several actions may occur. In situations deemed serious or severe, termination may be recommended. In situations that appear to have substantial potential for a remedy, a second written warning may be given. This warning should contain all of the elements of the first written warning. It should be clearly labeled as a written warning and should be signed by the employee acknowledging receipt of the warning. A signed copy should be sent to the director of Personnel Services. In addition, the possibility of termination should be clearly and specifically defined so that the employee does not misunderstand the consequences of failing to comply with the contents of the warning.

d. Suspension. At times, under certain circumstances, suspending an employee may be an appropriate disciplinary action. If a suspension is warranted, this will normally occur after the employee has received two written warnings. However, a suspension is not required before terminating an employee and may be appropriately used as a disciplinary measure at times other than after two written warnings. The length of the suspension should be commensurate with the nature of the problem and the employee's past record. As with other forms of discipline, the director of Personnel Services should be consulted with before issuing a suspension.

e. Recently Hired Employees. Individuals who have been recently employed and are clearly unsuited for the position they occupy may be terminated following verbal counseling and a reasonable opportunity to make necessary changes.

2. Violation of BYU-Idaho standards (Policy 2-2 B-G), occurring on or off the job, will generally result in immediate termination. This is also true of any actions that constitute a threat to the well-being of students or employees. An exception to this may occur when the university administration determines that circumstances surrounding the incident warrant a period of attempted correction.

H. PUBLIC SERVICE

1. Eligibility. An employee may run for public office if campaigning does not interfere with their employment duties, and if prior to making commitment to run, they receive appropriate approval that the campaigning will not interfere with their employment duties.

2. Approval for Running for Office. Before an employee agrees to become a candidate for public office, whether local, state, or national, he must first obtain permission in writing from:

a. If faculty, from the President of the University and the Academic Vice President.

b. If staff or administrative personnel, from the immediate supervisor, through each successively higher administrator to the President.

In all cases, candidates for public office must clear with the Office of the President prior to making any announcement of their intent to run for office.

If authorization is granted to run for public office, it should be understood that the campaigning shall not interfere with the employee's duties at his place of employment. If it does, the approval may be revoked.

3. Campaigning for Office. Candidates for office are not to use the facilities of the employing unit to obtain lists of names, or any information (except for library research), to use in a political campaign. Also, the mail system of the employing unit is not to be used to distribute campaign literature or to promote votes. Also, supplies, equipment, and personnel are not to be used in connection with the political activities of the candidate.

4. Campaign Contributions. As an institution, BYU-Idaho does not make contributions toward political campaigns.

5. Compensation and Appointment or Election to Office. If a person is elected to a public office, then the following will apply:

a. If the office requires the full time of the individual, then he or she should resign before taking office.

b. If the office requires only part time, such as the State Legislature, the handling of compensation will be as follows:

1) Faculty. If the elected person is a member of the faculty and on a regular contractual basis, the employee may continue to receive full compensation for the public office provided arrangements are made for all classes or other duties to be completely and adequately covered by substitutes approved by the chairman during that time it is necessary for the employee to be away from the campus or school. To the extent it may become necessary to hire competent and additional help to take care of the responsibilities, or to reduce work load, the cost of this will be reimbursed by the employee up to the amount of compensation received for service in the public office.

2) Administrative and Staff Employees. If the elected person is an employee classified under either the administrative or staff categories and the employment is on a twelve-month, year-round basis, the employee will continue to receive regular compensation; but that amount paid as compensation for the public office shall either be returned to the employer, or, if chosen, the salary will be reduced by the amount of compensation received for service in the public office.

The term "compensation" as used above is not intended to include any payments for items generally classified as "expense."

6. Continuing Activities on Committees. When the elected person is involved in continuing activity on committees or other assignments beyond those which are regularly assumed, such as a legislative session in the case of legislators, it is expected that these other activities will be so arranged that the employee's regular working schedule will not be interrupted. If this is not possible, additional arrangements must be made for participation in these activities through the same procedure required for obtaining permission to run for office.

I. SEXUAL HARASSMENT

BYU-Idaho is committed to assuring an environment for all employees and students where the dignity of each individual is recognized and respected. To this end, this policy has been developed to provide guidelines for avoiding and/or dealing with sexual harassment.

1. Philosophy. Sexual harassment is contrary to the standards of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, the BYU-Idaho Code of Honor, and existing federal and state Civil Rights laws and regulations. The BYU-Idaho Code of Honor which defines behavioral standards of conduct, states in part that students and employees will abide by the standards of Christian living taught by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. This includes graciousness and consideration for others and observance of high principles of honor, integrity, and morality.

2. Harassment Definition. Title VII of the Civil rights Act of 1964 prohibits harassment on the basis of sex. It states: "Unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favors and other verbal or physical conduct of a sexual nature constitute sexual harassment when, submission to such conduct is made either explicitly or implicitly a term or condition of an individual's employment, submission to or rejection of such conduct by an individual is used as a basis for employment decisions affecting such individuals, or such conduct has the purpose or effect of substantially interfering with the individual's work performance or creating an intimidating, hostile, or offensive work environment."

3. Students and Sexual Harassment. BYU-Idaho applies the same protection to students in the academic learning environment.

4. Harassment Offenses. A wide variety of offenses can constitute sexual harassment. Such harassment can range from inappropriate put-downs or unwelcome sexual flirtations to serious physical abuses. Sometimes the offenses are overt, such as demands for sexual favors in return for academic advancement. Sometimes the offenses are less direct, such as persistent, unwanted attempts to change a professional relationship to a personal one. Sexual harassment has occurred any time an employee, in an attempt to obtain sexual favor, exercises or threatens to exercise authority or power to affect the job or academic standing of another person.

Sexual harassment can be verbal, visual or physical. It includes, but is not limited to, any unwelcome or offensive sexual advances; requests for sexual favors; other verbal or physical conduct of a sexual nature, such as uninvited touching or sexually related comments; or visually suggestive or offensive materials.

Sexual harassment also exists when the conduct in question would tend to create an intimidating, coercive, or threatening work or learning environment, or to interfere with an employee's work or a student's study. Since BYU-Idaho deplores such conduct as an abuse of authority and a breach of professional ethics, sexual harassment of either employees or students will not be tolerated.

5. Sexual harassment is regarded as a form of employee misconduct.

Sexual Harassment Investigation Procedure

1. All complaints of sexual harassment must be promptly referred to the Personnel Services office for investigation and coordination. Due to the sensitive nature of alleged charges of sexual harassment and the potential for irreparable damage to a person's reputation, all charges will be investigated confidentially. The investigation will be coordinated by the Personnel Services Office. When an investigation determines that an allegation is valid, necessary corrective or disciplinary remedies will be taken as may be determined to be sufficient to correct the situation.

2. Complaining parties shall be required to swear or affirm that the facts stated in the complaint are true to the best of the person's beliefs, knowledge and information. The alleged perpetrator will be provided a notice of the charge.

Sanctions

Sexual Harassment can result in the discipline and dismissal of involved employees or students, and such penalties, sanctions and impositions against other individuals or parties as may be available to the university given the nature of the contractual or business relationship that may be established.

Reporting Sexual Harassment

1. Managers, supervisors, division deans and department chairs are responsible for the implementation of all aspects of the University's sexual harassment policy.

2. Any employee or student who believes he or she has been subjected to sexual harassment should report the alleged act immediately to the employee's immediate supervisor (if the immediate supervisor is involved in the complaint, the incident should be reported to that person's immediate supervisor) or to the Director, Personnel Services. Such reports will be held in strict confidence until a course of action is determined and then only persons who will be involved in the resolution will be informed.

3. Students alleging sexual harassment on the part of a faculty member should report the alleged act to the appropriate department dean or division chair or Dean of Student's Office.

Employee Rights

1. An employee may pursue a complaint of sexual harassment through the BYU-Idaho work-related concerns procedure, explained in section A of this policy.

2. External remedies are also available to an employee suffering from sexual harassment. Complaints may be pursued through the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission.

 J. WORK-RELATED CONCERNS
    (see Policy 5-2 for faculty)

All employees deserve an opportunity to present employment concerns to management and to have those concerns addressed in a fair and objective manner. Any, and all, complaints should be received and resolved at the lowest administrative level possible and as quickly as is reasonable.

Procedure

1. The approved procedure to discuss work-related concerns through proper management channels is:

Step 1. Employees should try to resolve work-related concerns with their immediate supervisor. Though employees and their supervisors are expected to follow their designated line of supervision to process a complaint, they may, when necessary, meet with the Director, Personnel Services to discuss their concerns and to receive guidance in the proper procedures to resolve work-related concerns.

Step 2. If the supervisor cannot resolve the complaint to the employee's satisfaction, within a reasonable period, the employee shall put the grievance in writing and file the written grievance with the next higher administrator in the employee's reporting structure. The administrator shall meet with the employee as soon as is reasonably convenient and shall attempt to resolve the problem.

Step 3. If the employee is not satisfied with the actions of this administrator, he or she shall file the written grievance with the appropriate vice president, who shall meet promptly with the aggrieved employee and attempt to resolve the problem. The Director of Personnel should be involved in an advisory role as requested by the employee, the supervisor, or the vice president.

Step 4. If the vice president is unable to resolve the problem to the employee's satisfaction, the vice president will, on written request, afford the employee a hearing before the Personnel Committee and such other persons as may be appointed by the President. The decision of this panel shall be final.

Note: If the employee's concern involves dissatisfaction with the actions of a person with whom the employee would ordinarily file a complaint under this procedure, the employee may skip that person and take the complaint to the next person in the supervisory line.

2. Filing Work-Related Concerns. Work-related concerns should, where possible, be filed promptly and in any case within two months of occurrence. Supervisors are encouraged to meet with employees to try to resolve their complaints as expeditiously as possible.

3. Non-retaliation. At no time should an employee suffer retaliation or harassment for having submitted a work-related concern. Any retaliatory action taken by any employee against another employee as the result of that person's seeking redress under these procedures is prohibited and may be regarded as a separate cause for complaint under these procedures.

4. Documentation. A complete record should be maintained of work-related concerns that cannot be solved with the immediate supervisor and become formalized. Copies of all documentation should be sent to the Director of Personnel Services.

PHILOSOPHY

BYU-Idaho provides leave to eligible employees to help them in various personal circumstances.

A. FAMILY AND MEDICAL LEAVE (FMLA)

1. Definition. A family and/or medical leave of absence is an approved absence available to eligible employees for up to twelve workweeks of unpaid leave in any 12-month period for a serious health condition related to an illness, injury, impairment or physical or mental condition for:

a. Themselves, if the condition makes the employee unable to do his or her job.
b. Birth of a child (right expires one year after birth).
c. Adoption of a child (right expires one year after adoption).
d. Care for a spouse (husband or wife), parent (either biological or one who stood in place as a parent to an employee when the employee was a son or daughter), or child (son or daughter under 18 years old, or if over 18 incapable of self-care because of a mental or physical disability).

The serious health condition must involve:

a. Inpatient care at a hospital, hospice, or medical care facility; or
b. Continuing treatment by a health care provider.

2. Eligibility. Employees must have been employed for at least twelve months total, and must have worked at least 1250 hours during the twelve month period preceding the commencement of the leave request and the effective date of the Family and Medical Leave Act which is August 5, 1993. Eligibility for FMLA leave will be determined with reference to a rolling 12-month period previous from the date the employee uses any FMLA leave.

3. Use of Paid Leave. Employees are to use their accrued vacation and sick leave before taking leave without pay. Once vacation and sick leave are exhausted, the employee will be granted leave without pay for the balance of the approved leave. Any sick leave taken will be counted against or subtracted from the time available under FMLA.

4. Conditions of Leave. Employees must provide medical certification to support a claim for leave for their own serious health condition or to care for a seriously ill child, spouse, or parent which includes:

a. Date the serious health condition started;
b. Probable duration;
c. Medical facts of the health condition;
d. For care of seriously ill family member, statement the employee is needed to care for son, daughter, spouse, or parent and estimate of time needed;
e. For employee, statement the employee is unable to perform the essential functions of the position;
f. For intermittent leave for planned medical treatment, date treatment is to be given and the duration.
g. For intermittent leave for employee care, statement of medical necessity for the intermittent leave and expected duration;
h. For intermittent leave to care for family member, statement that intermittent leave is necessary and expected duration and schedule.

If there is reason to doubt a certification, BYU-Idaho may require a second opinion of a health care provider approved by the university and at the university's expense, as long as the health care provider is not employed on a regular basis by BYU-Idaho. Subsequent recertification may be required by BYU-Idaho on the same terms.

Spouses who are both employed by BYU-Idaho are entitled to a total combined twelve weeks of FMLA leave (rather than twelve weeks each) for the birth or adoption of a child or for care of a family member.

5. Notification and Reporting Requirements. When the need for leave is foreseeable, such as the birth or adoption of a child, or planned medical treatment, the employee must provide reasonable prior notice, and make efforts to schedule leave so as not to disrupt the work. In cases of illness, the employee must report periodically on his or her leave status and intention to return to work.

6. Status of Employee Benefits During Leave of Absence. Employees on approved leave may retain the group insurance coverage by arranging to pay their portion of the premium during the period of unpaid absence.

For employees on an authorized leave without pay, continuous employment status will not be interrupted; however, the time on leave will not count toward certain benefits such as eligibility for tuition, additional vacation, retirement credit, and sick leave.

Absences which are not authorized will be considered as terminations.

7. Holiday Pay While on Family Medical Leave. Employees on a paid family medical leave, using their own accumulated vacation and sick leave, will receive holiday pay if a holiday occurs during the time off. Employees on leave without pay when a holiday occurs, will not be paid for the holiday. See Section C, Holidays.

8. Accumulation of Vacation and Sick Leave While on Family Medical Leave. Employees on paid family medical leave, using their own accumulated vacation and sick leave, will continue to accumulate vacation and sick leave for each full pay period of leave where vacation and/or sick leave is used. Employees on leave without pay for family medical leave will not accumulate vacation or sick leave.

9. Long-Term Disability. Employees whose personal illness requires extended time off work may also be eligible for long-term disability. Employees should refer to their Benefits Handbook on long-term disability for information on this benefit.

10. Worker's Compensation. Leave for employees whose absence is due to a work related accident is covered under the Compensation Policy. Leaves due to a work-related absence is also counted as family medical leave.

11. Return to Work. Employees may be returned to the position he or she held when the leave began, or to an equivalent position, with equivalent employment benefits, pay, and other terms and conditions of employment as outlined in the university policies. Return to work requires certification from the employee's health care provider that the employee is able to resume work.

12. Leave Beyond 12 Weeks. Requests for leaves beyond 12 weeks must be justified by the employee and will be granted at the sole discretion of the university.

Procedure

Supervisors are responsible to inform their employees when a leave request will be counted as Family Medical Leave and to submit that information to Personnel Services for appropriate tracking to meet federal regulations.

A Request for Family and Medical Leave of Absence Form must be completed and signed by the employee, submitted to the immediate supervisor for proper approvals, and forwarded to Personnel Services. If possible, the request should be submitted thirty days in advance of the effective date of the leave.

If the leave involves intermittent or a reduced leave schedule, the employee and the immediate supervisor should agree on the timing of the leave so that it serves the employee's needs while causing minimal disruption to the workplace. Intermittent leave will in no case be available in increments of less than one hour per day. The FMLA is characterized by extensive federal regulations. University personnel with questions about FMLA should call Personnel Services.

parent sister
child grandparent
in-law grandchild

Time taken off to attend such funerals must be counted as annual leave or leave without pay except where the time involved is less than four (4) hours.

b. Reported as vacation time or leave without pay (administrators and staff).

5. Appearing as Plaintiff or Defendant. Absence for the purpose of appearing as plaintiff or defendant in a court action does not qualify for jury duty leave under this policy.

E. LEAVE WITHOUT PAY

Leave without pay is a program of the University to provide valued employees with leave for special needs while retaining their status as employees. Leave without pay is principally for reasons outlined in this policy under:

1. Family and Medical Leave (Item A)
2. Military Leave (Item F)
3. Mission Leave (Item G)
4. Disability (See DMBA Benefits Handbook for Information)

Leave without pay due to insufficient vacation and sick leave is covered under those items in this policy. Leave without pay for reasons other than those outlined in this policy may be granted at the discretion of the University.

Conditions of Leave.

1. Notification. Leave without pay should be approved at least one month before the leave begins by the appropriate department or division head.

2. Duration. Leave without pay for more than a month's duration will generally not be granted to employees who have been employed less than one year. Exceptions to this policy will be reviewed on a case by case basis.

3. Return to Work. Reemployment of an individual returning from a leave without pay of more than one month's duration in his or her former position may not be practicable. Therefore, reemployment will be conditioned on the employer's needs and upon the returning employee's accepting a position that is commensurate with his or her skills and length of service.

4. Benefits While on Leave. For employees on an authorized leave without pay, continuous employment status will not be interrupted. However, employees do not accumulate vacation or sick leave while on leave without pay.

5. Administrative Employees. Except as related to Family Medical Leave, administrative employees may not be granted leave without pay in less than one day increments.

6. Unauthorized Absences. Absences which are not authorized will be considered as terminations.

Procedure

1. An employee who desires leave without pay should submit a written request to his or her supervisor.

2. The supervisor will make a recommendation to the division dean or department director as to whether the request is justified and why.

3. The division dean or department director will approve or disapprove the request and forward the documentation to the Director of Personnel Services.

4. Upon returning from a leave without pay, the employee should notify Personnel Services so arrangements, if practicable, can be made for reemployment.

F. MILITARY LEAVE

1. Active Duty. A full-time employee who is called to active duty to serve with the U.S. armed forces will be eligible for a military leave without pay.

2. Return to Employment upon Completion of Active Duty. Upon reapplying for work, if still qualified to perform the duties of the previous position, the former employee will be reinstated in a position of like seniority, status, and pay if the following conditions are met:

a. The period of service cannot exceed four years of active duty (unless an additional one year is requested by and for the convenience of the federal government in the case of an enlistee).

b. The employee must reapply within ninety (90) days after release from active duty or from hospitalization continuing after discharge.

c. The employee must have obtained a release from service under honorable conditions.

If the former employee is not able to perform the duties of the previous position because of disability sustained during military service, he/she shall be reasonably accommodated through placement to another available position for which he/she is qualified with appropriate seniority, status, and pay.

The university will make reasonable effort under the circumstances to comply with the conditions outlined for return to work unless changing circumstances make an undue hardship on the university to do so.

3. Discharge from Work. To comply with the Veterans Reemployment Act, employees reinstated to employment may not be discharged without cause for one-year after restoration or reemployment.

4. Training - National Guard or Reserves. A full-time employee leaving to complete an initial training period will be granted leave without pay and will be reemployed under the following conditions:

a. Reapplication must be made within thirty-one (31) days after release from duty.

b. The employee must have obtained an honorable release.

c. The university will make efforts under the circumstances to provide for reemployment as explained above unless changing circumstances make it an undue hardship on the university to do so.

After reemployment, the employee may not be discharged without cause for a period of six (6) months.

5. Salary During Active or Inactive Military Training Duty. A full-time employee who has periodic training duty assignments will be placed on leave without pay and may receive differential pay under the following circumstances:

a. The leave will only be provided for ordered or required training tours.

b. Upon release from training duty, the employee must report for work on the next regularly scheduled working day following the last day reasonably needed to return to the place of work.

c. The length of the leave is defined as the length of the required tour of duty, mandatory extensions, and reasonable travel time not to exceed a total of eighteen (18) working days per academic year.

When the leave is approved, the employee will be paid the difference between their regular university salary and the actual salary paid by the military unit--provided the university salary is the greater of the two. The employee should bring the military check verifying the base pay to Personnel Services. Personnel Services will authorize the differential payments.

Differential pay does not apply to initial training.

6. Medical and Dental Insurance. Refer to the DMBA Benefits Handbook.

7. Group Term Life Insurance, Supplemental Group Term Life Insurance, Accidental Death & Dismemberment Insurance, Disability, and 24-Hour AD & D Coverage. Refer to the DMBA Benefits Handbook.

8. Savings Plans. Refer to the DMBA Benefits Handbook.

9. Benefits During Approved Military Leaves. Time on leave will count as time worked towards service required to receive certain benefits such as increased number of days of annual vacation; eligibility for staff, spouse, and children tuition benefits; and retirement credit. However, an employee will not accrue vacation and sick leave while on military leave.

Procedure

Employees desiring to be placed on military leave should send a written request to his/her immediate supervisor.

I. PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT LEAVE - ADMINISTRATIVE AND STAFF EMPLOYEES

1. Purpose. To help employees desiring to further their education and enhance their careers by providing professional development leave.

2. Objective of Professional Development Leave. Professional development leave is a privilege granted for self-improvement which will result in greater benefit to BYU-Idaho and to the employee.

Professional development may be accomplished through many sources. This policy specifically covers educational leaves for course-work and the employee's tuition benefits beyond what is available through BYU-Idaho.

3. Requirements for Professional Development Leave.

a. Full-time employee.

b. Completion of three years (36 months) of continuous service at BYU-Idaho. Subsequent requests for leaves may be granted after six additional years (72 months) of service following the first leave.

c. Commitment to complete at least one year of service with BYU-Idaho following the leave.

d. Request is for advanced education beyond what BYU-Idaho provides.

e. History of valued past performance to the University as determined by employee's management and the Personnel Committee and anticipated benefit to the University following the employee's leave.

f. Commitment to pay back the entire gross salary paid during leave if employee does not return to BYU-Idaho, including the University's matching Social Security tax and group insurance premiums. Repayment will be made by agreement and completed within one year following the leave. Repayment will include a reasonable interest rate.

4. Common Types of Leave

a. One semester at full salary.
b. Two semesters at half salary.
c. Special schooling/training requiring several weeks of study at full salary.
d. Up to one year with no financial assistance or salary.

5. Special Provisions and/or Limitations

a. No break in insurance with employee and employer continuing to pay their shares.
b. Tuition participation, depending on funds available, up to but not exceeding half the total tuition. Tuition participation beyond one year will not be granted. No participation on books.
c. Eligibility to receive salary increases while on leave.
d. No accumulation of sick leave or vacation leave during leave.

6. Approval Process

a. Submission of leave request to Director, Personnel Services at least three (3) months prior to leave (forms obtained from Personnel Office).
b. Recommendation and approval by employee's management.
c. Approval and decision by Personnel Advisory Council.

7. Returning to BYU-Idaho

a. Employees approved for professional development leave are required to return to work at BYU-Idaho for at least one year following the leave.

b. Reasonable efforts will be made to preserve the applicant's job during the professional development leave. Preferably, the employee's work should be covered by other full-time employees, particularly for leaves of one semester or less. For leaves of more than one semester, temporary employees may be hired after approval of the Vice President over the area and providing funds are available to cover the additional help needed. If the job cannot be preserved, the applicant will be placed in another position upon return.

8. Exceptions. Exceptions to this policy will be reviewed and approved by the Personnel Advisory Council.

J. PUBLIC SERVICE (see Employee Relations Policy for information on campaigns)

1. Eligibility. An employee may run for public office if campaigning does not interfere with their employment duties, and if prior to making commitment to run, they receive appropriate approval that the campaigning will not interfere with their employment duties.

2. Compensation and Appointment or Election to Office. If a person is elected to a public office, then the following will apply:

a. If the office requires the full time of the individual, then he or she should resign before taking office.

b. If the office requires only part time, such as the State Legislature, the handling of compensation will be as follows:

If the elected person is a member of the faculty and on a regular contractual basis, the employee may continue to receive full compensation for the public office provided arrangements are made for all classes or other duties to be completely and adequately covered by substitutes approved by the department chair during that time it is necessary for the employee to be away from the campus or school. To the extent it may become necessary to hire competent and additional help to take care of the responsibilities, or to reduce work load, the cost of this will be reimbursed by the employee up to the amount of compensation received for service in the public office.

2) Administrative and Staff Employees. If the elected person is an employee classified under either the administrative or staff categories and the employment is on a twelve-month, year-round basis, the employee will continue to receive regular compensation; but that amount paid as compensation for the public office shall either be returned to the employer, or, if chosen, the salary will be reduced by the amount of compensation received for service in the public office.

The term "compensation" as used above is not intended to include any payments for items generally classified as "expense."

When the elected person is involved in continuing activity on committees or other assignments beyond those which are regularly assumed, such as a legislative session in the case of legislators, it is expected that these other activities will be so arranged that the employee's regular working schedule will not be interrupted. If this is not possible, additional arrangements must be made for participation in these activities through the same procedure required for obtaining permission to run for office.

K. SICK LEAVE - ADMINISTRATIVE AND STAFF EMPLOYEES

1. Definition. Sick leave, unlike vacation, is an employee privilege rather than a right. Sick leave is a type of insurance policy which the University maintains to provide income during periods of short- and long-term illness and recuperation from injury. Sick leave is to be taken only as needed.

2. Amount of Sick Leave Accrued. A full-time employee working forty hours per week accrues four hours of sick leave for each pay period worked up to a maximum of 12 days per year. Full-time employees working less than 40 hours per week accumulate sick leave on a prorated basis according to the number of hours they work.

3. Sick Leave for Family Members Living with Employee. At the discretion of the supervisor, an employee may use sick leave within reasonable limitations to care for immediate family members (spouse, children, and parents) living in the employee's home. Employees may also use sick leave for doctor and dentist visits, including preventative care, for themselves and members of their immediate family. If an employee needs to use sick leave for scheduled preventative care the employee should discuss it with the employee's supervisor and get the supervisor's prior approval.

4. When Sick Leave Becomes Available. Sick leave becomes available for use immediately after the pay period in which it is accumulated.

5. Not Accrued if on Leave Without Pay. An employee on leave without pay does not accrue sick leave while on leave without pay.

6. When Sick Leave May Not Be Used. An employee may not use sick leave for any other reasons than explained by this policy or to receive pay while on leave without pay.

7. Sickness During Vacation. If an employee becomes ill while on vacation, the employee may substitute sick leave for vacation. The supervisor may require a doctor's statement.

8. Substitution or Vacation or Leave Without Pay for Sick Leave. Staff employees who do not have sufficient leave to cover an illness may use vacation. If these together are not sufficient, the employee should apply for leave without pay.

Administrative employees who do not have sufficient vacation to substitute for sick leave may take leave without pay. However, unless the leave is for approved, intermittent Family Medical Leave, the leave must be taken as a full day of leave without pay.

9. Sick Leave and Disability Benefits. If an employee has an extended illness, disability benefits may be available. Employees should refer to their Benefits Handbook for disability benefits. Employees are encouraged to accumulate sick leave credit so they can use it as necessary before they become eligible for disability benefits and/or to supplement disability benefits. Employees do not accumulate sick leave while on disability. When an employee returns to work sick leave accrual will begin if the employee works at least four hours/day; however, sick leave will be prorated according to the number of hours worked for work less than full-time work.

10. Reporting Sick Leave. An employee must report unexpected sickness or injury to the employee's supervisor as early as possible, but no later than four hours after the job's starting time or as soon as reasonably possible.

11. Release to Return to Work. Upon returning to work after hospitalization, an employee must present to his or her supervisor a written statement from the employee's physician releasing the employee to return to work and outlining any restrictions on the employee's ability to work.

12. Doctor's Statement Certifying Reason for Absence. Frequent, unsubstantiated use of sick leave may be grounds for suspension or discharge. BYU-Idaho may require a doctor's statement:

a. when management believes an employee has excessive absences.
b. when the employee has a definite pattern of absenteeism.
c. when management suspects an employee is misusing sick leave.
d. when an employee takes a leave of absence for illness or accident for themselves or family members (see Section A of this policy).
e. other reasons as determined by management.

13. Sick Leave Credit at Termination. No pay will be given to terminating employees for their unused sick leave.

14. Sick Leave Not to be Counted as Hours Worked. Sick leave hours will not be counted as hours worked for computing overtime or for providing compensatory time off.

L. SICK LEAVE ASSISTANCE

1. Definition. The Sick Leave Assistance Program provides a means for staff and administrative personnel to donate vacation days to other employees who have exhausted all accrued vacation and sick leave due to a medical emergency. This program is not intended to underwrite abusive use of sick leave but is meant to provide assistance to those who require extended absence and whose sick leave and vacation accrual have been or will be exhausted.

2. Donating Hours. The sick leave assistance bank will consist of vacation days voluntarily donated by university employees.

a. Employees eligible for vacation benefits may donate time to the Sick Leave Assistance Program.

b. Employees will be limited in the number of days they may donate only to the extent of their accrued but unused vacation days. Vacation days which would normally be lost due to the year-end carryover policy may not be donated.

3. Use of Donated Days. Full-time staff and administrative personnel are eligible to participate in this program. Donated days will be used as follows:

a. Days donated but not used will be returned to the donating individuals.

b. The first day donated by all individuals will be used first; the second day donated will be used next; etc. By using donated days in this way, unused vacation days will be returned to those having donated the highest number of days.

4. Sick Leave and Vacation Accrual During Illness. Sick leave and vacation days will continue to accrue at the normal rate for employees utilizing the Sick Leave Assistance Program.

5. Limitations. Days donated to the Sick Leave Assistance Program will be used after other forms of accrued leave are exhausted and when applicable disability benefits are not yet available or are exhausted. Sick leave assistance days may not be utilized at the same time as Worker's Compensation or disability benefits.

Procedure

1. A full-time administrative or staff employee who has had a serious accident or illness and whose accrued vacation and sick leave benefits have been or will be exhausted may request sick leave assistance. Application should be made in writing to the supervisor prior to the employee going on leave without pay status.

2. The employee's supervisor will make a recommendation to the division chairman or director and appropriate vice president as to justification of the request for sick leave assistance; the supervisor may also request the benefit on behalf of an employee.

3. The written request should include the employee's name; the date when vacation/sick leave accrual will be exhausted; the date the employee would be eligible for long-term disability; and a statement from a physician describing the illness, its severity, the prognosis, and an estimate of how much work will be missed.

4. Upon approval, the director/vice president will then notify the employees in his/her area of the need for donated days, giving the name of the individual and the estimated number of days needed. There will be no solicitation for donated days by the employee needing assistance nor by other employees. Solicitation for donated days shall be limited to the notifications coming from the director/vice president's office.

5. Employees donating time to the Sick Leave Assistance Program will specify the number of days they wish to donate and the name of the intended recipient.

6. The person responsible for reporting/processing vacation and sick leave usage will debit the donor's leave record and notify Personnel Services of the number of days donated. The record of the employee needing assistance will be credited with the appropriate number of days and a copy of the approval will be filed in his/her personnel file.

7. Unused days will be returned to the record of the donor.

8. The Sick Leave Assistance Program will be administered through the office of the director/vice president.

9. It will be appropriate for the employee to know the names of those donating vacation days but not the number of days donated.

M. SICK LEAVE - FACULTY

1. Coverage During Illness. Because of the nature of the responsibilities assigned to faculty, as well as the desirability and necessity for continuity in their work assignments, these employees, in the event of illness should promptly notify their supervisor. Arrangements will be made with other faculty or administrative personnel to meet the assignments of the person who is ill. Unless the illness is very protracted, it is expected that colleagues of the person who is ill will voluntarily assist in meeting his or her assignments.

3. Doctor's Release. Upon returning to work after hospitalization, the employee must present to the Academic Vice President a written statement from his or her physician releasing the employee to return to work and outlining any restrictions on the employee's ability to work.

4. Long-Term Disability. After an absence of forty-five (45) calendar days, the Deseret Mutual Benefit Association insurance program may become operative if the employee is eligible for long-term disability. The employee will remain on long-term disability until released by his or her physician to return to work. If the employee does not have disability coverage, the university will pay the employee's regular salary only through the end of the academic contract year in which the illness or injury occurs.

N. SNOW LEAVE

1. Definition. Snow leave is leave with pay available to staff and administrative employees eligible for benefits who, because of extreme, adverse weather conditions, cannot come to work or must leave work early.

2. When Snow Leave May Be Used. Snow leave may be granted up to four hours per day, not to exceed forty hours in a calendar year.

3. Limitations on Snow Leave Hours. After the four hours per day of snow leave, employees are required to take leave without pay or vacation, at their option, for the time they do not come to work.

Procedure

1. Employees who find that they cannot come to work or must leave work early due to extreme weather conditions are to notify their supervisors as soon as possible.

2. Supervisors are to make the determination whether or not the person actually cannot come to work or must leave work early because of the weather conditions.

3. Employees who, in the opinion of their supervisors, cannot come to work or must leave work early because of adverse weather conditions are granted four hours snow leave per day.

4. Employees who cannot come to work after four hours are to be contacted to determine whether they want the additional part of their shift counted as leave without pay or vacation.

P. VACATION

1. Employees Eligible for Vacation. Full-time administrative and staff employees earn paid vacation. Part-time employees and faculty do not earn vacation.

2. Purpose of Vacation. Vacation is provided so employees can have time off from work with pay for rest and renewal, and be able to spend time with their families, etc. Management encourages employees to use their annual vacation leave each year. However, if employees are not able to use it all they may carry over unused vacation from year to year as explained below.

a. Administrators earn vacation at the rate of 7.34 hours per pay period. This will result in:

1) 22 days (176 hours) per year for 12-month employees
2) 18 days (146 hours) per year for 10-month employees
3) 16 days (132 hours) per year for 9-month employees

Administrators may carry more than 22 days vacation throughout the year; however, on 31 December of each year, employees can only carry-over to the next year a maximum of 22 days vacation.

b. Full-time staff employees accumulate vacation for each full pay period worked as outlined below.

New Employees - accumulates 4 hours per pay period (accumulates 12 days/yr)

5th Anniversary - begin accumulating 5 hours per pay period (accumulates 15 days/yr)

10th Anniversary - begin accumulating 6 hours per pay period (accumulates 18 days/yr)

15th Anniversary - begin accumulating 7.34 hours per pay period (accumulates 22 days/yr)

Full-time staff employees working less than 12 months or 8 hours per day accumulate vacation on a prorated basis according to the number of hours worked.

Staff employees may carry more than 18 or 22 days vacation throughout the year; however, on December 31 of each year, employees with less than 10 years of service may only carry-over 18 days vacation from one year to the next. Beginning on their 10th anniversary, staff employees may carry-over 22 days vacation from one year to the next. Unused vacation days above those allowed will be forfeited.

Employees working less than 12 months may also carry-over 18 or 22 days from one year to the next, depending on their years of service as outlined above.

3. Credit for increased vacation. Nine- and ten-month employees receive credit for each year of service worked as though they had worked a full twelve months.

4. Taking Vacation. Employees may take vacation as soon as it is earned. Vacation is earned at the end of each full pay period worked. Vacation may not be taken until it is earned. Employees working less than 12 months must use vacation during their regular working months.

5. Accumulation During Leave. During periods of approved leaves, whether with or without pay, vacation time will not accrue. When an employee returns from leave of absence, the accumulation will resume; however vacation accumulation may be prorated based on number of hours worked upon return to work.

6. Scheduling Vacation. In scheduling vacation, the employee's preference will be considered and granted whenever convenient; however, vacations must be scheduled at times that will not unduly interfere with the operation of the employing unit and must be approved by the employee's supervisor.

Employees are encouraged to take at least one week of vacation during five (5) consecutive working days. Any balance of vacation leave can be taken as mutually agreed between the employee and supervisor.

7. Paid Holidays During Vacation Leave. When a holiday falls within an employee's vacation period, vacation will not be charged for that day.

8. Sick Leave During Vacation. If an employee becomes ill while on vacation, sick leave may be substituted for vacation only if approved by the supervisor, who may request a doctor's statement.

9. Pay in Lieu of Vacation. Pay in lieu of vacation will not be allowed except in cases of a terminating employee.

10. Unused Vacation at Termination. Upon termination employees will be paid for unused vacation up to a maximum of 22 working days.

11. Vacation Not to be Counted as Hours Worked. Vacation hours taken will not be counted as hours worked for computing overtime or for providing compensatory time off.

12. Substitution of Leave Without Pay in Lieu of Vacation. Employees may not substitute leave without pay in lieu of using vacation. Leave without pay is authorized only for family medical leave, military leave and mission leave. Any exceptions require a justification approved in advance by the supervisor and personnel services.

Tuition Benefits (2-7)

A. PHILOSOPHY  

BYU-Idaho waives regular tuition for its employees and their dependents within the limitations described in this policy. These benefits apply to BYU-Idaho employees and retirees, their spouses and children; unmarried children of General Authorities and mission presidents.

B. ELIGIBILITY

1. Employee Eligibility. Eligibility for employees will begin with the start of the semester or term following the employee's hire date. The program does not provide for a reduced rate for a portion of a semester's work where the hiring occurs during the course of a semester or term.

2. Spouse and Dependent Children Eligibility. For a spouse and/or dependents to be eligible for tuition benefits, an employee must (a) be at least age twenty-seven and have completed at least one year of full-time service or (b) have completed two (2) years of service at BYU-Idaho or (c) have completed one year of service with another Church Education System employer.

Children of full-time BYU-Idaho personnel must have graduated from high school or otherwise received permission to register for BYU-Idaho classes from the Admissions Office to be eligible for any tuition waiver.

3. Tuition Benefit During Approved Leaves. Eligible children may continue to receive benefits under this program when the parents are on official leave from the university and where employment is not terminated.

4. Children Other Than Direct Dependents. Children raised as dependents of an employee who have been supported by such employee over a period of years may be considered eligible depending on the facts of the case. Children who are simply living in, Indian placement children, or welfare foster children who are receiving support for a temporary period of time would generally not be eligible. Each case will be determined on its own merits.

5. Children of Divorced Employees. When university employees are divorced and their children do not reside with them, but are financially dependent upon the university employee for support, the children will be considered eligible for the program.

6. Children of Retired Employees. Children of retired employees will be eligible for this program if they meet the qualifications as to the duration of the benefit (see D).

7. Part-Time Employees (faculty, administrators, staff). Part-time employees are eligible for tuition benefits for themselves only.

8. Spouses and Dependents of Part-Time Employees. Spouses and children of part-time faculty and non-faculty employees are not eligible for a tuition waiver benefit.

9. General Authorities and Mission Presidents. Unmarried children of General Authorities and mission presidents are eligible for tuition benefits. Children of mission presidents must have prior approval from the Missionary Department in Salt Lake City.

10. Other LDS Church employees – Corporation of the President employees, and LDS Foundation employees are not eligible for tuition benefits except for those who were grand-fathered under the old policy.

C. POLICY

1. Full-time employees are allowed to take up to six (6) credit hours per semester. Those wishing to take classes during daytime hours of 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. must have written approval of their supervisor and division chair or director over the area. Employees will be expected to make up the time on a regular basis as approved by the supervisor.

2. Spouses and dependent children of eligible full-time employees may register for courses of study up to 21 credit hours per semester without being required to pay tuition. Over-load hours will be charged at the rate outlined in the catalog. Tuition waiver does not cover overload fees above 21 credit hours per semester. Dependent children may also register for classes during their junior and senior years under the tuition waiver benefit.

3. Part-time faculty may register for up to the number of hours they currently teach, but not to exceed five hours per semester.

4. Part-time staff may register for up to three (3) credit hours per semester. Classes must be taken outside of regular working hours.

D. DURATION OF BENEFITS

1. Tuition benefits are available to active full- and part-time employees and to BYU-Idaho retirees.

2. Dependent children eligible for the tuition waiver may continue to receive benefits without regard to marital status until they reach the equivalent of 76 credit hours of tuition waiver or age 30, whichever comes first.

3. Credits received from high school advanced placement programs, language exam credits, or credits paid for prior to employee's becoming eligible for tuition benefits are not counted as part of the 76 credit hour limit.

 E. APPLICATION OF BENEFITS

1. Benefits will apply in the following:

a. Day school (including summer terms)

b. Evening school

c. Semester abroad (tuition only, no fees or travel costs included)

d. BYU-Idaho Continuing Education courses (including BYU credit courses, except for graduate level courses)

e. Credit classes taken for official audit

f. Outdoor education classes (tuition only, no fees or travel costs included)

g. One-student classes - (BYU-Idaho tuition only)

h. Non-credit classes

i. College Level Examination Program (CLEP)

j. Classes retaken for better grades

k. Classes where students received "W"

2. Benefits under this policy will not include:

a. Graduate courses, including those offered through continuing education

b. Special fees (lab or supply fees, parking fees, music fees, etc.)

c. Payment of tuition costs that exceed the BYU-Idaho tuition rate

d. Course texts

e. Travel study

f. Home study

g. Payment of cash for expenses over and above tuition where scholarships may be involved.

h. Short-term non-credit CES workshops, lectures or programs, such as "Know Your Religion," "Education Week," "Education Day," and other similar programs.

i. Any exception will be considered on an individual case basis and must be authorized by the appropriate vice president.

3. Education benefits beyond those offered at BYU-Idaho under the tuition waiver policy may be available through professional development funds or individual departments. Faculty should contact their department chair, dean or the Asst VP for Instruction about education opportunities; administrators and staff should contact the Personnel Office.

Procedure

1. Verification of Eligibility. Employees, spouses, and other dependents should go to the Personnel Services Office for verification of eligibility prior to their first semester at BYU-Idaho.

2. Tuition Options for Dependent Children. At the beginning of each semester, the parent or dependent child should complete a tuition waiver option form in the Accounting Office choosing either full or half tuition for that semester. Once the option is selected and tuition is due for the semester, the option may not be changed for the semester. If an option is not selected, the tuition will default to full payment.

3. Notification of Changes. In all subsequent semesters, registration can be handled in the normal manner at the Registrars Office. The Personnel Office must be notified of any change in employment or in any other eligibility factor.

4. Classes During Working Hours. Employees desiring to take classes during working hours are required to fill out the form, "Request to Take Classes During Working Hours," and receive signed approval from their supervisor and director or division chairman. This form must be presented to the secretary in Personnel Services before tuition will be waived.

F. INTERNET CLASSES

Employees and their spouses who take BYU-Idaho sponsored classes via the Internet may be reimbursed for those classes upon successful completion of the courses. Dependent children, attending BYU-Idaho as full-time students, may take a combination of Internet and regular classes and be covered under the tuition waiver benefit.

Employees who wish to take Internet courses that are not sponsored by BYU-Idaho may receive up to half-tuition through their department upon successful completion of the courses. Employees must obtain approval through appropriate management channels prior to enrolling in courses to guarantee reimbursement. The immediate supervisor and dean/department will make decisions on reimbursement based on funds available in their budget.

In most circumstances, classes cannot be taken during regular working hours nor should class work be done during regular work hours. If employees use university supplies for classes, the employee must reimburse the university for any paper supplies or materials used in connection with the class.

G. RECIPROCAL AGREEMENT

Dependent children who are eligible for tuition benefits at BYU-Idaho may use all or part of their 76 credit hours at BYU-Provo or LDS Business College. Benefits are not available at BYU-Hawaii. The benefit will be applied as follows:

1. Tuition for BYU-Provo and LDS Business College credit will be reimbursed upon verification of successful completion of courses through the Personnel Office. The parent or student may request reimbursement for all or half of the credits until the equivalent of 76 full credit hours has been met. Reimbursement for BYU-Provo credits will be at the BYU-Idaho tuition rate.

2. The benefit applies only to tuition and not to books or other fees.

3. This reciprocal agreement does not apply to the spouse of dependents, graduate students, or any others excluded from the approved tuition benefits policy.

Early Retirement Program (2-8)

The Board of Trustees approved an early retirement program for BYU-Idaho employees which became effective September 1, 1993.

This retirement program is subject to changes in, and interpretation of, federal and state laws and to changes in the University retirement program. There is no guarantee how long the program as currently outlined will be effective.

Employees must be at least age 62 and have 20 years retirement credit under the DMBA retirement program to be eligible. Participation is strictly voluntary.

The retirement program will provide:

1. The difference in retirement pay due to an age reduction factor in the retirement benefit from DMBA. Employees who choose to retire prior to age 65 and have less than 30 years of service are subject to a three percent per year reduction in their monthly benefit payment because the benefit starts earlier and is paid over a longer period of time. BYU-Idaho will make up that difference in a lump sum payment.

2. A social security bridge payment which is the lesser of either 25% of the annual salary or the amount of the single social security benefit that would be paid at the age an individual becomes eligible for the single full benefit as defined by the Social Security Administration. The annual amount will be divided into 24 equal payments and will continue each pay period through the month the employee is eligible for the single full social security benefit, which may be past age 65 for employees born in 1938 and beyond.

This program is available to employees prior to age 65. Once an employee turns age 65, there is no reduction in the DMBA retirement benefit; however, there may be a social security benefit, depending on the year the employee was born.

Faculty members are encouraged to work through the end of the academic year in which they wish to retire. Administrators and staff may retire at any time following age 62 provided sufficient notice is given to allow a smooth transition for their replacement.

Employees may request retirement calculations through the Personnel Office and should allow a minimum of four weeks to process their request.

Employees taking advantage of the early retirement program must sign an age discrimination claim waiver that will be provided to them from the Personnel Office.

Electronic Communication (2-10)

The intended use of BYU-Idaho’s electronic communication systems, i.e. personal computers, e-mail, voice mail, network, the Intranet and the Internet, etc., is for business-related purposes only. Incidental and occasional use of these systems for personal communications within BYU-Idaho is permitted, but such messages will be treated no differently from other business messages. Personal use should be minimal and should not occur under circumstances that interfere with work responsibilities or that inappropriately consume University resources or materials. BYU-Idaho will monitor employee use of University-owned electronic communications equipment. Examples of inappropriate use include, but are not limited to:

1. Viewing, storing, or transmitting pornographic, erotic, indecent, or other offensive material.

2. Using work time to look up information for personal use or to play games.

3. Using communication systems for personal business purposes or for personal gain.

4. Using communications systems for advertising, selling, or soliciting.

5. Printing information for personal use without reimbursing the University for supplies. Incidental and occasional printing of information such as e-mail from family members is permitted.

6. Engaging in any activity that causes harm to communication systems including creating or propagating viruses, sending chain or junk e-mail, using e-mail to send frivolous messages or games, etc.

7. Communicating with others using a false name or an unauthorized assumed identity.

8. Using profane, lewd, or indecent language in electronic communications.

9. Using e-mail to harass, intimidate, or annoy another person.

10. Allowing family members, friends, etc. access to university equipment for personal use.

Inappropriate use of electronic communication systems shall be subject to disciplinary action as outlined under the University’s discipline and discharge policy. Employees who access inappropriate material via the Internet, using University-owned equipment, are in violation of the Code of Honor as well as the intended use of the Internet, and shall be subject to immediate termination.

Employment of Students and Youth (2-11)

A. PURPOSE

To outline guidelines for employment of students and youth by BYU-Idaho.

B. REFERENCES

1. BYU-Idaho Policy and Procedures Manual
2. BYU-Idaho Catalog: Academic Policy Section
3. Immigration Reform and Control Act of 1986
4. U.S. Department of Justice, Immigration, and Naturalization Service, Regulation 213.2m (13)

 C. DEFINITIONS

1. Full-time Student: Matriculated student correctly registered for 12 or more semester credits, 6 or more block credits, or 4 or more summer term credits.

2. Academic Probation: As defined by the current academic policy of BYU-Idaho, defined in the BYU-Idaho catalog.

3. Disciplinary Probation: As defined by the BYU-Idaho Dean of Students Office.

4. Student Employment: Positions designated as student positions, not to exceed 20 hours per week during the academic year.

5. Academic Year: Beginning fall semester through the end of spring semester.

6. Incoming Student: A new student that is registered for the following term or semester.

7. Continuing Student: Has completed an academic semester and will be continuing into the next academic period.

8. Returning Student: Has previously attended BYU-Idaho and is returning after not attending BYU-Idaho at least one regular semester.

9. "First Come, First Served": Jobs posted through Student Employment Services generally filled on a "First Come, First Served" basis.

D. ELIGIBILITY

An individual is eligible for student employment if he/she:

1. Is a full-time student with exception of full-time summer employment as explained under K.

2. Has not completed more than 80 cumulative BYU-Idaho credits, unless approved by the Student Employment Office.

3. Is not on academic probation.

4. Is meeting disciplinary conditions, as established by the Dean of Students Office.

5. Is not yet eligible for a tuition waiver as a spouse or dependent of a BYU-Idaho employee.

6. Is at least 17 years old. A 16-year-old may work only if the job cannot be filled by someone 17 years or older. Under no circumstances will a person be employed who has not passed their sixteenth birthday.

7. Can provide acceptable documentation proving eligibility to work in the United States and a picture ID (as required by U.S. Immigration and Naturalization Service to complete an I-9 Form) and is displayed on the student master menu. International students should have an original Social Security card BEFORE going to the Student Employment office to obtain an interview for a job.

8. Meets requirements (for international students on F-1 visas) outlined in federal regulations. All international students must be cleared by the International Student Advisor in the Dean of Students Office prior to any offers of employment.

9. Is not employed in an area where a near relative works as defined by BYU-Idaho policy 2-2 Sec. H, Employment of Relatives.

BYU-Idaho will not discriminate on the basis of race, color, sex, national origin, age, or any non-disqualifying physical or mental disability in its student employment.

However, BYU-Idaho reserves the right to prefer members in good standing of its sponsor, The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, in its student employment and to otherwise make student employment decisions to further the standards, values, and objectives of the Church.

E. HIRING PROCEDURES

Campus departments are encouraged to post student jobs through the Student Employment office to allow as many students as possible fair competition for jobs through a "first-come first-served" basis.

However, some areas may need specialized skills that are difficult to find through the regular student employment process. In these cases, which should be limited, a campus department may request a specific student to fill the position. No offers of employment may be made to the student until clearance has been received from the Student Employment Office to ensure the student meets the employment eligibility criteria.

Hiring Procedures for Positions Posted through Student Employment

1. Department completes Request for Student Employee(s) and submits it to Student Employment to post on job board.

2. Student applies for posted positions through Student Employment.

3. Student Employment verifies job skills required by job posting, verifies eligibility requirements, has student read and sign Student Employment Agreement, and refers students to department on "first-come first-served" basis.

4. Department interviews student(s) and makes hiring decision.

5. Department completes and signs bottom portion of Student Employment Agreement for students hired and has student return to Student Employment with Student Employment Agreement to complete appropriate paperwork before beginning work. Federal law requires every employee to complete an I-9 (Immigration and Naturalization form) to prove eligibility to work in the United States before they begin work. The I-9 form requires a picture ID and proof of U.S. citizenship or authorization to work in the United States such as a Social Security Card, Passport, birth certificate, etc. Students must also complete a W-4.

6. Student completes paperwork for Student Employment Office and receives Work Release Authorization.

7. Student gives Work Release Authorization to department. Departments may not allow student to begin work without a Work Release Authorization.

8. Student Employment adds students to payroll after they have issued the Work Release Authorization.

9. Department contacts students not hired so they can apply for other positions.


Hiring Procedures for Students Selected by Campus Departments.

1. Department completes and sends Request for Student Employee(s) to Student Employment to ensure student selected meets eligibility criteria. No offer of employment can be made before clearance through Student Employment.

2. Student Employment contacts department when student has been cleared.

3. Department sends cleared student(s) to Student Employment prior to beginning work to sign a Student Employment Agreement, complete the I-9 and W-4 forms, and receive a Work Release Authorization.

4. Student returns Work Release Authorization to department. Student must not begin work without a Work Release Authorization.

F. EXCEPTIONS

A. Petitions for exception to this policy and procedure may be submitted to the Student Employment Office for consideration.

B. The student will be notified of the decision in a timely manner.

G. PAY RATE

Student employees will be paid according to the wage scaled established at the beginning of each year by Personnel Services.

H. PRIORITY JOB REFERRALS AND ASSISTANTSHIPS

1. Priority job referrals and assistantships may be given in lieu of athletic or admission scholarships. The Directors of Admissions and Athletics will provide the Student Employment Office with a list of names and Social Security numbers of students who qualify for priority/assistantship referrals, along with the hiring department's name, the job title, and awarded number of work hours per week.

2. Students who receive priority or assistantship jobs must complete all documents necessary for employment, including payroll and I-9 forms.

3. Students awarded priority and assistantship job referrals must meet all of the eligibility requirements for student employment.

I. MULTIPLE JOBS

1. As a general rule, students will not be allowed to hold more than one student job at any given time.

2. In the event a student has more than one student job, the student may work no more than 20 hours per week during the academic year.

3. Exceptions will be considered on an individual basis.

J. PRE-HIRING

1. For one month, 15th March to 15th April, qualified students may be pre-hired for employment for the following Fall semester.

2. All paperwork, I-9's, W-4's, and clearances must be completed during pre-hiring period.

3. Pre-hired students must be current full-time students and must meet all employment criteria.

K. SUMMER SCHOOL ELIGIBILITY

Students must be enrolled for a minimum of one credit during each summer term they work, except as noted below.

1. When a department requires a student employee to work full time, current summer term enrollment is optional. However, students must be registered for fall semester to qualify for full time summer work.

2. Requests for 40-hour-work-week jobs should be approved by the appropriate director or division chairman. The majority of summer jobs should be 20 hours and the need for 40 hour jobs kept at a minimum.

3. Preference for summer employment is given to BYU-Idaho employee dependents.

4. Employers for selected jobs for summer may hire any eligible student at any point of time in summer.

L. PROCEDURES FOR TRANSFERRING JOBS

1. Students desiring to change jobs must be interviewed by the Student Employment Office.

B. Employers must agree, in writing, to terminate a student from their present job before another job referral will be issued.

M. EMPLOYMENT AT WILL

Student employees are employees "at will." Student employees are expected to conform to the policies of BYU-Idaho for initial and continued employment. Student employment and compensation may be terminated at any time for any cause whatsoever or for no cause at the option of either BYU-Idaho or of the student.

In addition, no supervisor or representative of BYU-Idaho, other than the President of the University, has any authority to enter into any agreement for employment for any specified period of time, or to make any agreement contrary to this policy.

N. WORK STUDY HIRING PROCEDURES

Phase 1: Securing Work Positions

The Student Employment Director:

1. Contacts employers
2. Executes contract with employers.
3. Provides copy of contract of Payroll Office.
4. Obtains job descriptions and specific qualifications.
5. Notifies department chairman of available position(s).

Phase 2: Student Selection

The Student Employment Director:

1. Notifies department advisors of available position(s).
2. Approves student selection:

a. Determines that student meets definition of "educational need".
b. Signs advisor's letter of recommendation for placement approval.
c. Provides selected student with signed assignment.

Firearms and Weapons Policy (2-12)
Approved: July 20, 2001

BYU-Idaho prohibits the possession or use of firearms and weapons on property owned or controlled by the University, including residence halls (see Idaho Code 18-3302C), and at programs sponsored by BYU-Idaho which occur off campus property (i.e. discovery, travel related to academic studies, student activities, etc.). For the purpose of this policy, firearms or weapons includes any device which can expel a projectile, and/or other dangerous weapons, including knives, explosives, or other items which, in their intended use, are capable of inflicting serious injury. Because these items pose a clear risk to the persons and property on BYU-Idaho campus, violation of this policy will result in appropriate disciplinary sanctions.

No one (personnel, students, or visitors) except law enforcement officers of municipal, county, State of Idaho or Federal Law Enforcement Agency, shall be permitted to possess or carry firearms or other weapons, concealed or not concealed, with or without a concealed weapon permit, while upon the properties owned or controlled by the University without specific written permission from the Director of Campus Police.

Approved housing units: Firearms, knives, ammunition, explosives, or any devices which intended use is capable of inflicting injury or damaging property, including but not limited to martial arts weapons, BB guns, slingshots, pellet guns, paint-ball guns, wrist rockets, swords, hunting knives and bows, are prohibited in on- or off-campus apartments. Facsimiles of firearms or dangerous and destructive weapons are also prohibited. These dangerous devices are discouraged and it is recommended that they be left at home unless arrangements are available for approved storage. On- and off-campus approved housing unit owners and managers may assume the responsibility of storing any weapons, firearms, or ammunition on the premises only if these items are kept under lock and key outside of student apartments. Any questions regarding weapons in housing units may be directed to the housing director.

Members of the campus ROTC units may possess and/or carry firearms or weapons as part of official ROTC functions, when authorized by the ROTC administrator.
