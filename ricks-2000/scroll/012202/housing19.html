Increase your numbers: make apartments inviting for opposite gender

by Sarah Lunt
LUN99013@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

So, last night you and your roommates sat in your living room, watching reruns of ER and wondering why no boys had stopped in — for the 20th night in a row.

You may wonder, “Was it something we did?”

Well, that is possible. Nobody can help you there. But if you and your roommates are not guilty of some major infraction, the problem could lie in your apartment.

Yes, it is true. Certain attributes of an apartment can attract or repel those of the opposite sex.

Both genders find that there are certain qualities in the homes of members of the opposite sex, ones that make them want to sit down and stay a while. Here are a few tips given by BYU-Idaho students:

First and foremost, no matter what complex men or women live in, or their ages, they emphasized the power of a clean living room and kitchen.

“Hello, cleanliness!” Jacob Roberts, a sophomore from Amity, Ore., said.

“[Remind guys that] trash corners are out,” Krista Close, a junior from Sacramento, Calif., said.

This means keeping the trash out of the corner of your apartment that may not make it into the trash can. Another problem could be the corner behind the couch where the empty pop cans and candy bar wrappers mysteriously appear.

Smell also seems to be a big issue. Watch out for onions. A good idea is to keep your door or windows open when cooking. Taking out the trash often and keeping the refrigerator nicely cleaned out also seem to be major contributors to a pleasant aroma.

When you notice a visitor who comes in, takes a quick sniff and leaves, do this: Get down on your hands and knees, put your nose to the ground, and locate that odor. It works.

Another common consensus between the two sexes is that neither appreciates “scandalous” pin-ups, Dan Kinnersley, a freshman from Salt Lake City, said. Examples given from others were Britney Spears and Heath Ledger. Whether the pictured celebrities are scantily clad or not, they make some visitors uncomfortable.

Although it may not seem like a big issue, students may find these posters a turn-off.

There are some things that are turn-ons, however.

Girls, guys actually do like it and notice when your apartment is nicely decorated.

“[It shows] comraderie between roommates” if all have worked together to have fun and tasteful decorations, Roberts said. If the girls seem like they get along well together, he would feel like they would probably get along with him.

Girls, as well as guys, eat it up when there are spiritual reminders on apartment walls.

“I am impressed when I go in and see the prophet on the wall, or Christ or something from their mission,” Liz Allen, a sophomore from Richland, Wash., said.

Many students agree that factors such as cleanliness, a pleasant smell, and tasteful decorations combine to bring a good feeling into an apartment, to make others, and not just of the opposite sex, want to take off their coats and stay a while.

Who knows? When you get rid of that stench or the dirty dishes piled up on your counters, that “special someone” may finally stroll in. This could be your big chance to have him sit down, stay a while and watch ER with you.
