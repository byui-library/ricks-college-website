Introduction

Welcome to German 201! This is an intermediate course designed for students who have completed two semesters of college (or at least three years of high school) German, or who have had an equivalent background. It is assumed that you already have been introduced to all of the basic German grammar concepts and have a general familiarity with an introductory vocabulary of approx. 1200 high-frequency words. In reintroducing grammar, therefore, the pace will be somewhat accelerated.



If you are consistent in your study habits and in the completion of homework assignments, this course can be one of your most rewarding college experiences-one that may open up a variety of future opportunities, such as a mission, a major in German, or multiple professional opportunities in the business world. You are highly encouraged to do your very best and to make the most of this exciting opportunity.



Course Objectives

German 201 is designed not only to expand your overall knowledge of the language, but to deepen your understanding and appreciation of the people and the culture. To realize these aims, you will be required to use all four skills-listening, speaking, reading, writing-as we examine a variety of interesting and significant themes.



By the end of the course you should have: 1) strengthened your knowledge of German grammar and increased your ability to use standard, acceptable language in everyday situations; 2) improved your ability to understand conversational German; 3) developed oral and writing skills necessary to communicate thoughts on topics of general interest; 4) increased both your active and passive vocabularies; 5) become sensitive to some of the issues and problems of contemporary life in German-speaking regions of Europe.

Study/Homework

This study guide contains all Hausaufgaben (Ha)-homework assignments-for each lesson. All written work should be written neatly either in the Übungsbuch or the notebook. Consistent homework is extremely important in learning a foreign language.



You must come to class prepared to participate. To do so will require you to study somewhere between one and two hours prior to each class. If you consistently prepare and actively participate, you will find learning German doable and fun. Lack of preparation will cause you feel lost and frustrated.

Helpful Hints

Repetition and continued review are the keys to learning a language. Learning a language is a process. Don't try to cram before a test. It will not work!

Make every class a learning experience! So don't be afraid to speak up in class or to make mistakes. You learn to communicate by trying, even if you're unsure how.

You must practice! Language is a practical skill, the development of which requires constant reinforcement through practice and repetition-both in class and at home.

Study often out loud. Reading aloud helps to learn new material.
