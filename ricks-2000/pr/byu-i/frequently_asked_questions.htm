The Transition: Frequently asked questions & answers

 

What is the reason for the change from a two-year to a four-year institution?
The change, which was made by the Church Board of Education, will allow more students to receive a four-year education at a Church-sponsored school. LDS Church President Gordon B. Hinckley stated in his announcement: "This change of status is consistent with the ongoing tradition of evaluation and progress that has brought Ricks College from infant beginnings to its present position as the largest privately owned two-year institution of higher education in America."

Will the focus of BYU-Idaho be different than the focus of Ricks College?
BYU-Idaho will continue to be teaching oriented and student focused. The institution will emphasize undergraduate education and will award associate and baccalaureate degrees. Graduate degree programs will not be offered. The transition will be accomplished in such a way as to preserve the school's autonomy and identity. Faculty rank will not be part of the academic structure of the new four-year institution.

When will the first upper division classes be offered?
Changes will be phased in. To give an exact time table would be premature.

What academic programs will be added?
We are just beginning an evaluation process to identify which academic programs we can and should offer. Predictably, the school will need to change and even eliminate some long-standing and beneficial programs as it focuses on key academic disciplines and activities. President David A. Bednar stated, "We will begin by asking the question:  'What is needed academically and otherwise for us to be an extraordinary and effective teaching-oriented institution?' "

Will students still be able to earn associate degrees?
Yes. We will continue to offer all associate degrees during the 2000-2001 school year. When we begin to add upper division courses, some of our associate degrees may change. However, we envision offering associate degrees for many years to come.

Will students still be able to transfer to BYU-Provo?
Yes. During the transition period, transfers to BYU-Provo will continue as in the past. Elder Eyring said, "One of the things that will remain is some of the distinctive features of Ricks College which have included some people coming there for a brief time to transfer to other places. That surely will continue. So there will be . . . just an addition of then the possibility of young people staying for the four years if they choose. But I would assume that much of what has gone on in the past would continue with a nice addition."

Will BYU-Idaho grow in size because of the changes?
Yes, growth is likely but stating a specific number is premature. Because our physical facility will not grow dramatically, we will use new approaches such as better use of our campus during the summer months, technological advances, innovative calendaring, etc. Our enrollment target currently is 8,600 students, and no new number has been announced. President Hinckley stated: "With some additions and modification, the physical facilities now in place in Rexburg are adequate to handle the new program. Undoubtedly, some changes to the campus will be necessary. However, they will be modest in nature and scope."

Will BYU-Idaho be governed by BYU-Provo?
Obviously we will work very closely with BYU-Provo. However, BYU-Idaho will remain autonomous and report directly to the Commissioner of Education just as it has in the past. In making the announcement, President Hinckley stated: "For the immediate future, the president of BYU-Idaho will report directly to the Commissioner of the Church Educational System."

When will the name change take effect?
For the time being, we will continue as Ricks College. We will not begin using the name Brigham Young University-Idaho in the immediate future. A letter has been mailed to the Northwest Accreditation Commission informing it of our intent to change to a four-year institution. We also are required to file a prospectus outlining our strategic plan and programs. After the prospectus is filed, the Northwest Commission will respond to our request and either accept or reject us as a candidate for accreditation at the four-year level. Only at the point of being accepted as a candidate will it be appropriate for us to begin using the name of Brigham Young University-Idaho.

What was the reason for the name change?
In making the announcement, President Gordon B. Hinckley stated: "The new four-year school will be known as Brigham Young University-Idaho, with the name change designed to give the school immediate national and international recognition."

Will Thomas E. Ricks be honored in any way?
President Hinckley stated: "The memory of Thomas E. Ricks will continue to be appropriately honored and perpetuated."

Will we still have the "Spirit of Ricks"?
We will always be committed to preserving and enhancing the "Spirit of Ricks." The "Spirit of Ricks" will continue as long as people are dedicated to preserving the nurturing, caring attitudes that have always been a central part of the Ricks College experience. Elder Eyring referred to this in his response to the press on June 21. He said: "I would think Ricks College, because of its history of having been a two-year school, has always been seen as a place where students could come with a little more expectation of nurturing and personal attention that they might at some other institutions, although it's excellent at all of our campuses. But I would think if there's a slight distinction, it's come out of that two-year history and a little bit as was suggested earlier, a place where people went sometimes and were surprised at how well they did because they didn't see themselves as having quite the potential they found at Ricks College. That spirit, I think, has been there and surely that will continue. The feeling of a student finding themselves and doing even better than they thought they could do and finding a faculty that are eager to help them."

Will tuition increase?
Tuition will remain $1,090 per semester for Church members for the 2000-2001 school year. 

What admissions changes will there be?
Our plan at the present time is to continue the admission policies we have in place.

When will intercollegiate athletics be discontinued?
Intercollegiate athletics will be phased out. Current commitments to players and leagues will be honored. The precise timetable for the phase-out will depend on the individual decisions of coaches, players, and the leagues.

Why is intercollegiate athletics being phased out?
President Hinckley stated, "It takes too much money. It takes a great amount of time and energy. We think that in this instance we would like to change the emphasis. In the first place, Ricks is a long ways from everybody. You have to travel great distances to play. That's a factor in it. It's a very costly program. We would hope that with a intramural program of athletics and sports, that the need could be met and that the great emphasis would be on academics."

In light of these changes, will work on replacing the Spori Building continue?
Yes. We are proceeding with plans to vacate the Spori Building later this summer. Plans to remove the building are also proceeding for the fall. However, because we now have new space requirements, the design of a new building is proceeding more slowly while departments re-evaluate the needs for potentially expanded programs. It is possible the new building will need to be reconfigured to accommodate the new needs.
