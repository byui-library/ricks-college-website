Prepare for winter with proper attire

by Bridget Dalin
dal00001@byui.edu
Scroll Staff

With the fall season in full swing and winter on its way, BYU-Idaho students will soon be changing the way they dress.

Students returning to Rexburg or those who have lived in areas with cold, snowy winters know what has to be done to keep warm when the winter months arrive.

“My fingers are very important to me,” Kristina Farnsworth, a sophomore from Mesa, Ariz., said.

Farnsworth said her gloves are the most important piece of clothing in the winter. She also believes it is very important to keep her neck covered.

Dr. Murray Hamlet, cold weather expert with the Army Research Institute of Environmental Medicine for 30 years, gives tips on how to stay warm in the winter weather.

“The key to cold weather is the rational use of clothing. Once you understand what to do and why you do it, you should never get cold again,” Hamlet said.

The best method to keeping warm is to wear layers. Keeping the skin cool (not cold) with the proper layers is the best method because the body doesn’t perspire. Hamlet insists that cotton is the death fiber. Cotton holds 40 percent of its weight in water and the human body actually loses heat trying to get rid of the water.

Wearing proper layers is essential to understanding how to stay warm, safe and healthy.

The National Ski Patrol has given many ways to layer winter clothes.

The first layer, located right next to the skin, is known as the wicking layer. This layer consists of long underwear.

The second layer is the insulating layer and includes sweaters, sweat pants, spandex, etc.

The third and final layer is the protection layer. This includes coats, snow pants, gloves, hats, etc.

Aside from the clothing necessary for the winter weather, what a person eats during the winter can also determine if they will stay warm during those cold months.

Eating more calories from slow-burning foods in the winter helps keep the body warm. Drinking one to two quarts of water each day is also important. Dehydration is just as severe in the winter as it is in the summer, Hamlet said.

Taking the right steps to dressing properly in the winter is important for students to follow so they can remain healthy and enjoy the winter season.
