SYLLABUS

COURSE OBJECTIVES:
 

 1. To acquaint the student with the profession of social work.

 2. To introduce the social forces and dynamics that created the need for social welfare - history of                    social  welfare.
 3. To examine the current philosophies and problems relating to the design and delivery of social services.
 4. To familiarize students with the basic philosophies, values, and ethics in social work.
 5. To examine the phenomena of social problems and examine the social services created to 'deal' with some of these problems.
 6. To introduce some of the skills and areas of practice important to the social work profession.
 

CLASS OUTLINE:
 

I. SOCIAL WORK PROGRAMS

A. Requirements for the Social Work major/ Social Work programs at C.S.W.E. institutions.
  

II. BACKGROUND SOCIAL WELFARE, SOCIAL WORK, AND SOCIAL SERVICES.

A. An examination of what Social Work is   Social Work as a maturing profession..

B. Problems of Social Functioning.

C. The evolution of social welfare in America. A historical look at the social and economic forces that led to the emergence of social welfare. 

D. Case management

E. Education for Social Work.

III. SOCIAL WORK METHODS:

A. Historical overview of Social Work values and ethical practice.

B. (1) Social Work practice with Individuals

     (2) Social Work practice with groups

     (3) Social Work practice with communities

     (4) Administration and Research
 

IV. SOCIAL WORK SERVICES/ CONSTITUENCIES WHO ARE SERVED BY SOCIAL WORKERS. INCLUDED HERE WILL BE AN EMPHASIS ON MINORITY ISSUES AND SOCIAL POLICY ISSUES AS THEY RELATE TO SOCIAL WORK SERVICES.

A. Minorities and Social Work   B. Public Welfare   C. Rural Social Work

V. A MULTI-MEDIA APPROACH TO TEACHING  IS USED.  STUDENTS ARE EXPECTED TO READ ASSIGNMENTS AND PARTICIPATE IN CLASS DISCUSSIONS. THERE WILL BE GROUP WORK AND/OR LECTURE-DISCUSSION LED BY THE INSTRUCTOR. GUEST SPEAKERS IN SOCIAL WORK PRACTICE WILL DISCUSS AND ANSWER QUESTIONS ABOUT VARIOUS SOCIAL WORK SERVICES WHICH THEY ARE EMPLOYED IN. 

VI. EXAMINATIONS WILL BE ON MATERIAL COVERED IN READINGS AND LECTURE/DISCUSSIONS. THERE ARE FOUR EXAMS INCLUDING THE FINAL. TESTS WILL BE MULTIPLE CHOICE AND/OR SHORT ANSWER ESSAY. THESE EXAMS WILL BE GRADED ON 100% POINT SCALE AND ASSIGNED A LETTER GRADE USING THE BYU-I COLLEGE GRADING SYSTEM. QUIZZES WILL ALSO BE GIVEN PERIODICALLY.
