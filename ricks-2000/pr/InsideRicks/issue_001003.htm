EXECUTIVE COMMITTEE SEES GREAT FUTURE FOR BYU-IDAHO

It was a night to look to the future as supporters of Ricks College listened to church leaders and President David A. Bednar describe the transition of the junior college to Brigham Young University-Idaho.

"The Lord knows the future," Elder Henry B. Eyring said to members of the President's Club Wednesday night. "The Lord has known this opportunity was going to come here." The former Ricks president, who now serves as an Apostle and Commissioner of Education , was in Rexburg for meetings with the Executive Committee of the Ricks College Board of Trustees. During a banquet in the Manwaring Center, he said some people might think that Rexburg is an unlikely place for an institution like BYU-Idaho. But he said BYU-Idaho will have a great influence on the world, and that people already have been led here to help with the forthcoming changes. "The Lord not only leads the Church, but He loves the institutions of the Church Educational System," he said.

Elder Richard G. Scott, also an Apostle and a member of the Executive Committee, said in his comments, "This is clearly going to be a blessing that will resound around the world." Elder Scott added that when President Gordon B. Hinckley told of his vision of converting Ricks to BYU-Idaho there was enthusiastic support among the church leaders. "There was a feeling in the room that we were participating in a very important moment, and that the President was acting under inspiration."

Elder M. Russell Ballard, also an Apostle and chairman of the Executive Committee stated, "What is happening at Ricks is a result of revelation." He said the Lord has handpicked a team of educators that includes having Ricks College President David Bednar and his wife Susan at Ricks at this time, Elder Merrill J. Bateman as president of BYU in Provo, Utah, and Elder Eyring as Commissioner of Church Education. "Whatever occurs in the next few years, I hope all of you will sustain and support it," Elder Ballard said. "It will be a tremendous thing for this university for graduates to go into the world and say we graduated from Brigham Young University-Idaho."

In his comments, President Bednar said, "The challenge is to keep things the same while many things change." He explained, "The spirit of Ricks is not found in a building. It is not found in a place. It is found in the people, the students, the teachers."President Bednar said he is surprised when he sometimes hears a person say the spirit of Ricks was changed on the day of the announcement. "The spirit of Ricks will not be diminished. In fact, if we do this transition properly, it will be greatly enhanced."

President Bednar recalled a conversation with President Hinckley when the Church President said the time was at hand for Ricks College to become a university. "That is all I needed to know. I have no doubts," he said of the prophet's vision. "This is truly a spiritual event." He added, "We truly are going places in higher education where no one has ever gone before."

PROPOSED BUILDING TO RETAIN SPORI NAME

A proposed building that will replace the historic Jacob Spori Building will retain the name of the college's first administrator. Jacob Spori's name and legacy will live on at Ricks College, said President David A. Bednar in making the announcement Wednesday night at the President's Club banquet attended by members of the Executive Committee of the Board of Trustees and donors to the college who belong to the President's Club. Although the design of the new building has not been finalized, Bednar says the new building will reflect the "spirit of Ricks" and be a fitting tribute to the school's first principal.

During Homecoming, Ricks College will display commissioned art work that will help preserve the legacy and memories of the original Spori Building. The pieces include a bas relief bronze, an oil painting, a sepia silver gelatin photo print, a display of digital photographs, a scale model of the Spori Building, and an all digital video of the Spori and its interior.

JOHN D. WALKER RECOGNIZED FOR INNOVATIONS IN NO-TILL FARMING

No-Till Farmer and the National No-Tillage Conference have named John D. Walker of Rexburg as the research division winner for their annual No-Till Innovator Awards program. Walker, who has been with Ricks College since 1977, will be recognized Jan. 10-13 at the ninth annual National No-Tillage Conference in Cincinnati, Ohio. He has been invited to be speak at the event which draws more than 700 of the country's most innovative no-tillers annually. The University of Idaho College of Agriculture also honored Walker with the Distinguished Associate Alumni Award presented Sept. 30.

ADJUNCT TEACHER HONORED BY TYCA

A Ricks College adjunct faculty member was honored by the Two Year English Teachers Association (TYCA) at its annual conference in Tempe, Arizona. On September 29, Susan Grover of the Ricks College English Department received the TYCA-West Part-time Teaching Award. TYCA-West represents schools in Arizona, Nevada, Utah, Wyoming, Idaho and Hawaii. Grover has been adjunct faculty at Ricks College since 1983 teaching predominantly advanced writing and critical reading. She is a leader in local community literacy programs, especially those programs associated with other-learning-ability students. She is the adjunct faculty representative for the Ricks College Faculty Association.
