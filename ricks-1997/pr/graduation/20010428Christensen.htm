RICKS COLLEGE COMMENCEMENT ADDRESS

REMEMBER WHO YOU ARE
Roger Christensen
April 28, 2001

I am honored to be invited to speak briefly at this penultimate Ricks College Commencement. On behalf of the Board of Trustees and Elder Eyring, as the Commissioner of Education, I bring their love and greetings.

President Hinckley has stated, "We stand on the summit of the ages, awed by a great and solemn sense of history." This Commencement is an historical moment. Those here today have been blessed by so much that has gone before and have many opportunities that yet lie ahead. Knowing which way to go from here depends largely on understanding who you are. The Lord sees the eternal value of each of His children and wants all of us to take the path that will lead us back to Him. He said, "Remember the worth of souls is great in the sight of God." (D&C 18:10) I hope that you understand that your soul is of infinite worth.

One powerful scene from the Disney movie, "The Lion King," comes when Simba confronts a crossroad in his life and is struggling with a decision. He sees the image of his father, Mufasa, in the clouds and hears his father's voice reminding him, "remember who you are." I would like to talk about that message this morning.

Throughout life, you will encounter many crossroads which will require important decisions. The decisions you make determine the direction you go. Understanding who you are and relying on the Lord will influence not only the direction, but also your final destination.

At one important crossroad you have encountered so far, you chose to come to Ricks to get an education. You have chosen wisely. Ricks College is a marvelous place! It is a place for learning, a place for growth, a place for discovering your potential, a place for becoming more than perhaps you ever dreamed possible.

Upon graduation, you will come to another of many crossroads in your life. Some of you will be going on missions, some will be getting married, some will be going to work, some will be going to other schools to further your education, and some of you may choose to go to BYU-Idaho. Wherever you go, you will be faced with some interesting challenges. In the world, your abilities will be challenged, your values will be challenged, even your self-worth will be challenged. Satan will try his best to get you to change your perspective of who you really are.

A metaphysical example of this can be seen if you have ever been to a "Fun House." Most have wavy mirrors that distort your image. Seeing ourselves in a warped reflection may appear funny at first, but if we look at that image long enough, we may begin to believe that that is what we really are like. A friend of mine, Elder Ronald J. Hammond, an Area Authority Seventy and recently called mission president, wrote this poem that describes these perceptions.

Mirrors

A wavy mirror gives images unclear,
A rather curious thing to see,
Fat, tall, or thin...puffed out or punched in,
All you never really wanted to be.

You can smile and tease, even laugh with ease,
For you are quite certain inside,
That what you see isn't true, the thing looking back isn't you,
It's the wavy mirror that has lied.

But there is something wrong with gazing too long,
At the mirror's distorted view,
For the mirror deceives till the beholder believes,
That the reflection is actually true.

Now, God is our Father in whose image we are,
If we gaze in His mirror, we will see,
We're of noble birth, we're of infinite worth,
Who we are confirms what we can be!

But Satan holds a mirror that is wavy and unclear,
The father of lies will deceive,
Till the very elect have lost self -respect,
Because his mirror they choose to believe.

He'll say you're no hero, a born loser, a zero,
The real you, he'll not let you see;
From things as they are, the elect wander far,
Believing only what seems to be.

But truth can't conceal and God will reveal,
He will clear the distorted view,
Satan's mirror's disguise will fall from your eyes,
And you'll see clearly the real you.

Yes, God can clear the view, prove Satan's mirror untrue,
He will help you see yourself clearer,
But you'll miss those divine views, if His mirror you refuse,
And choose Satan's distorted mirror.

So rise, don't fall...it's your judgment call,
Which mirror you choose to accept,
Will it be truth or lie, low destiny or high,
A zero...or the VERY ELECT?!

When I graduated from BYU-Provo, Alex Haley was awarded an honorary doctorate degree for his work in researching and writing the book Roots based on his family history. He gave an interesting message about the process he followed in searching his heritage back through the slave ships to a village in western Africa. The General Authority speaker at that graduation was Elder Boyd K. Packer. He graciously acknowledged the work and message given by Mr. Haley, and then taught a profound lesson that has had a lasting impact on me. He noted that physically, we all have many generations of ancestry that have come together to make us who we are; however, spiritually, we are each just one generation away from God as His sons and daughters.

Each of you has goals and a direction for your life; however, He who understands the crossroads will allow you to be challenged and may cause you to make course corrections in order to accomplish His plan for you. Change, if required, is not always comfortable, but if you are firmly anchored in Christ, you will always be on safe ground. President Howard W. Hunter noted, "If our lives and our faith are centered upon Jesus Christ and his restored gospel, nothing can ever go permanently wrong. On the other hand, if our lives are not centered on the Savior and his teaching, no other success can ever be permanently right."

As you face the crossroads of life, today and wherever you encounter them, always look up to your Father. You have received His image in your countenance. Remember who you are and seek to do His will.
