October 16, 2001

Ice skating rink to  

built at BYU-Idaho


REXBURG, Idaho –A large outdoor ice-skating rink will be constructed later this year as part of the new Student Activities Program at Brigham Young University-Idaho.

The rink will be built on an intramural field located south of the Austin Building along South Center Street.

Depending on the weather, the ice rink is tentatively scheduled to be built around Thanksgiving.

"It has to be cold enough to freeze all of the water, says Scott Hurst, director of outdoor activities for the Student Activities Program. "That may be Thanksgiving or it might be Christmas. Naturally, the rink will only be operational as long as the weather permits. Typically, an outdoor ice rink is usable for one to three months. We’ll just have to see."

In order to construct the rink, the university has purchased 2-foot-high sideboards to serve as walls for the skaters. A liner will be laid over the intramural field designed to prevent the water from seeping into the ground as well as to protect the grass. Approximately 47,000 gallons of water will be poured onto the field. This will create an ice rink 100 by 200 feet with 6-12 inches of ice.

In addition to building the ice rink, 300 pairs of figure and hockey skates have been purchased by the school and will be available for rental. Students and area residents who have their own ice skates will be allowed to use the rink for free.

"We’re planning to designate one night a week as family night," said Hurst. "The early evening or late afternoon of that day will be set apart specifically for families." The lights already in place at the intramural fields will provide nighttime illumination.

As director of outdoor activities, Hurst is part of the physical activities area of the new Student Activities Program. The program is still under development and when fully operational the program will have an array of year-round activities in three areas: service and social, visual and performing arts, and physical activities.

According to Hurst, other pilot programs by the Student Activities Program have been very successful. Recently, a large number of students turned out for rock climbing and canoeing activities.
