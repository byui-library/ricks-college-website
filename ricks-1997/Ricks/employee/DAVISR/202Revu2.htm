The Renaissance concluded that the best ruler was a good king (not God), so the next age was called "The Age of Kings," and since Man now believed he could discover and understand all things this new age was also called The Age of Reason (and by 1700 it changed its name to The Enlightenment). All things were to be called into question and studied. But by the mid 1700's kings had proved themselves unable to manage the world, so the people tried governing themselves, ousting the kings in revolutions, and setting up a new form of government—of the people, by the people, and for the people. This enlightened (trust your mind/use logic) era evolved into the Neo-Classic (New-Greek) age, but it was short-lived. Revolutions ousted all the old ideas, and by the end of the 1700's another age was forming—the Romantic, (trust your heart/emotions, not your mind).
