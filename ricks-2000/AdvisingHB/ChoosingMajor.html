STEPS FOR CHOOSING A MAJOR

1. Self Assessment: Spend time in self-discovering. Take personality or assessment inventories or classes (offered in the Career Center). List your interests, values, strengths, dislikes, likes, abilities, and skills.

2. Experiences General Education: General Education is a great way to experience a variety of disciplines and to get to know professors from across the campus. List the courses you have enjoyed most. When has learning something new been a fun and challenging experience? How do you learn best?

3. Talk with Others: It is never too early to start building a network system. Get to know your professors, advisors, and other students. Talk with family, friends, professionals in various fields. Participate in clubs and activities. Join professional organizations. Connect with alumni who are doing things you'd like to explore!

4. Explore Options: Attend the Major's Fair and Career Day. Go to the Career Center and gather information on all the various careers that you'd like to explore. List at least three careers that you didn't know existed and that fit your interests and skills.

5. Read: Go to the library and read vocational biographies. Review the Wall Street Journal and business magazines (such as Inc.) If you were going to start your own business, what would you do?

6. Set Goals: Focus on one major goal--graduating from college. Don't let your indecision about a major detour you from moving toward this goal. Set daily goals, stay on track, complete general requirements, and take your courses seriously. Explore creating your own major or a general Liberal Studies Major. If you could create a major what would it look like and how could you make it marketable?

7. Gain Work Experience: Experience the world of work through part-time jobs, internship, co-op experiences, work-study programs, or volunteering. If you had to go to work full-time tomorrow, what job would you create?

8. Keep a Journal: Writing can be an effective tool for self-discovery. Describe your perfect day. Review and list your most satisfying accomplishments or achievements. Describe the kind of person you want to be.

9. Relax: Since the average person changes careers five times in a lifetime, you need to be flexible and willing to learn new skills. Focus on the big picture and on developing character, positive personal qualities and habits.
