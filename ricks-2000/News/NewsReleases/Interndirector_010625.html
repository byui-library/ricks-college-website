Ricks College News Release
June 25, 2001

Ricks hires new director 

for internship program

    
REXBURG, Idaho – Guy Hollingsworth has been named the internship director at Ricks College, a new position reflective of its growing emphasis on internships as it becomes Brigham Young University–Idaho. The internship program will be a vital piece of the identity of the four-year institution.

Most recently Hollingsworth worked as director of recruitment services at Idaho State University in Pocatello after working as assistant director of admissions and scholarships at Ricks College for seven years.

The internship program will give students valuable experience that will complement their studies and increase their marketability.

"We want the students graduating from BYU–Idaho to be a success," he said. "We want them to be a positive influence in all aspects of their lives, which includes the work place, and these internships will often make a huge difference."

Internships are becoming a necessity, he said. In 1985 five percent of college students had an internship before graduating. In 1999 that statistic rose to 80 percent, according to Jeffrey Ellman, co-founder of Internship USA.

"The internship program allows our students to be successful, and it allows the reputation of BYU–Idaho to go forth and make a positive mark on society and on the work force," he said.

The internships are also intended to bolster and enhance integrated bachelor’s degrees, which will give a broader educational experience. The integrated degrees will be customized to fit students’ interests and will become the primary academic offerings at BYU–Idaho.

"An internship enriches the integrated majors," Robert Wilkes, vice president of student life, said. "It prepares them for the world of work in a way that is quite helpful to them and to their employers."

Hollingsworth sees the internships mutually benefitting students and employers. For the students, it affords an opportunity to get real-life experience in an area of interest that the students may want to pursue as a career.

Internships allow employers to hire someone with relatively few strings attached and get viable work done by a student who is eager and excited to make a difference. One statistic shows that 60 percent of interns are offered a job within the same company.

"The internship becomes an extended job interview and it’s very effective," Wilkes said.

Internships will be located abroad, nationally, locally and within BYU–Idaho. Local businesses will be able to provide opportunities that have professional value for students. The internships will generally last for an equivalency of a semester.

The program will receive a boost from sharing resources with BYU in Provo, Utah, which has a nationally recognized internship program. Hollingsworth will have access to BYU’s database of hundreds of companies.

The transition creates a window for Hollingsworth to develop a program that will be tailored to the needs of BYU–Idaho, optimizing what works in similar programs elsewhere and eliminating what does not.

He is in a new position with little precedence and will rely on the faculty members’ specific areas of expertise to shape beneficial internship experiences in those respective areas.

"It’s got to have involvement from all areas of campus to develop a program that will work for everyone," he said. "I’m the facilitator – they’re the experts."

A faculty member from each department will act as internship coordinator who will remain in contact with the interns. Hollingsworth is developing parameters for internships to create a unified program, but he will still leave enough room for departments to find suitable opportunities that are specific to a given discipline. Academic criteria will be set so students may receive credit whenever possible.

Hollingsworth holds a bachelor’s degree in advertising from BYU and a master’s degree in curriculum and instruction from ISU.
