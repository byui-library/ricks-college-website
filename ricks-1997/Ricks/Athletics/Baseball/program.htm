2001 RICKS COLLEGE BASEBALL PREVIEW

    "We’ve got some experience this year," says Coach Jerry Schlegelmilch. "It’s a good mix of sophomores and freshman with a good mix of left handed and right handed hitters that will help us be more versatile."

    Let’s go around the horn, starting at pitcher where Aaron Ott has moved into the number one spot. Ott is a right hander from Missouri who has a "good fastball and splitter." Sophomore Scott Cannon is finally back in the Vikings lineup. After playing his freshman year with a nagging arm injury, Cannon came home from an LDS mission with an arm that was still sore. He looks healthy now and will pitch some, and play shortstop some due to the departure of Casey Hill. In the third spot in the rotation is Ryan Jessop out of Box Elder High in Brigham City. After that, "we’ve got question marks", says Schleg.

    Now to the infield. At first base is newcomer Beau Seil. Seil spent his freshman year at Washington State as a red shirt. "He’s big, strong, and solid in his skills. A good addition." David Perry could also see time at first base, but will certainly see a lot of time as designated hitter. "David has improved in many ways. He’s a solid hitter, strong, with good speed." Second base will be handled by Josh Kennedy, a freshman from Viewmont High in Bountiful, UT. He’s a left handed hitter who will lead off. "Josh is a great lead off man, quick and bunts well." While Scott Cannon is currently standing at shortstop, it will help the team if freshman Cam Walker of Rexburg can move into the starting spot, freeing Cannon up when his pitching day rolls around. "Walker is small and quick, and could be a big boost to us." At third base is Jake Stubblefield. Jake was in left field prior to his mission. He can handle either, but its at the plate that Stubblefield excels, hitting over 20 home runs as a true freshman. "Jake is a Division One prospect. He’s swinging the bat well again, adjusting to the wood, and he’s just solid all around." Behind the plate is another ‘Schleg’. It’s James Schlegelmilch, the coach’s son. He’s a true freshman, a power hitter that can switch hit. But behind the plate, young Schleg is wise beyond his years. "He’s a mature receiver. He’s been around the game all his life and handles pitchers well. His arm is adequate, but his release is quick and accurate."

    The outfield has experience and speed. Moving from center to left field is Jared Chappell. He can get to the ball and hit with power. Chappell’s move to left field came about with the return of David Dalling from his mission. "Dalling is a Division One prospect. He’s got power, speed, and a great arm." Dalling could even see some time on the mound. In right field, C.J. Milne of St. Louis, MO, has the edge. Pushing him is Jonathan Hemsley, a freshman from Texas who played for a Texas state championship high school team.

    Depth is a concern, especially in the middle of the infield and at pitcher. "If our pitching holds, we can score enough runs to compete in the league." The league will be balanced. "This league has nine good baseball schools. Dixie looks to be at the top again, Utah Valley, Salt Lake, CSI, and Treasure Valley always compete well. I think we have the talent to get back to the playoffs this year."
