Living and loving the ‘Mommy’ life

by Janielle Gurney
GUR01003@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Dawn Pulsipher, a homemaker in Rexburg, reads a story to her son, Michael at home. Dawn cares for four children daily, including two of her own. Scott Cameron / Scroll
Having to rise and shine to the sound of a baby crying is not always the most pleasant wake-up call. But for many homemakers and mothers, it is their divine responsibility.

Dawn Pulsipher, wife of BYU-Idaho professor David Pulsipher, has four children she cares for day and night: Elizabeth, 7 years old; Andrew, 5 years old; Jonathan, 3 years old; and Michael, 10 months old.

Dawn’s morning.

2 a.m.: Dawn awakens and nurses Michael back to sleep.

5 a.m.: She nurses Michael back to sleep … again.

Nursing is a task mothers perform repeatedly throughout the day. The time and demand for breast feeding can be a determining factor in a mother’s decision to use breast milk or formula for an infant.

Researchers have found that breast feeding is often more nurturing for an infant.

6:50 a.m.: Dawn awakens Elizabeth. Dawn makes a big breakfast, usually pancakes, waffles or something else for the family. She also makes Elizabeth’s lunch. Then Dawn brushes Elizabeth’s hair and empties the dishwasher.

7:30 a.m.: The family has prayer, and then she sends Elizabeth off to school. Dawn helps all the boys find outfits and gets them dressed.

8 a.m.: Dawn gets to shower, dress herself and find toys for Michael to play with. She reads with Andrew and sends David off to work with a kiss.

Dawn and many other mothers put their families’ care and needs before their own. Dawn makes sure all of her children are up and ready before she begins to think of getting ready herself.

Dawn’s day begins.

9 a.m.: She cleans up the breakfast mess in the kitchen. She folds some laundry and thinks about the meals for dinner. While washing dishes, Dawn calls her sister or friend, and they catch up on their day or make necessary appointments for the family. These may include doctor, swimming lessons, visiting teaching, etc.

10:15 a.m.: Dawn makes Andrew and Jonathan a before-lunch snack and gets Andrew ready for school. This includes making sure he brushes his teeth and hair and gets his coat, backpack and food before the school bus comes.

10:40 a.m.: She hugs and kisses Andrew and then waves him onto the school bus.

11 a.m.: Dawn nurses Michael to sleep.

11:05 a.m.: David comes home for lunch so Dawn can go walking with her friend Carol for 30 to 45 minutes.

Mothers often need a break from their demanding lives. Dawn uses the time her husband gives her to exercise and release some energy with a friend. But many mothers are not able to take a break because of different circumstances while caring for their families.

12:30 p.m.: She plays a game with Jonathan — usually Candyland or Trouble. Dawn then reads a book to him, eats lunch, cleans the kitchen and does some housework. The work consists of cleaning the bathroom, dusting and returning toys and objects to where they belong. If Dawn is lucky, she reads a book for 30 minutes while Michael is napping, and Jonathan plays Jump Start 2nd Grade on the computer.

2 p.m.: Andrew bursts in the door, unloads his backpack and tells his mother about his day.

2:25 p.m.: Elizabeth comes home to unload her backpack and tells her mother about her day.

2:30 p.m.: Dawn makes snacks for her children and cleans up the kitchen. She then encourages Elizabeth to practice the piano and do her homework.

Dawn nurses Michael.

3 p.m.: Dawn gets everyone ready to go to the library, run errands or arrange for friends to come play. She feeds Michael yogurt and changes him.

3:45 p.m.: Dawn starts dinner and a batch of cookies with Andrew and makes necessary phone calls for her position as the Visiting Teaching coordinator for her ward.

She also manages to play with Michael.

Dawn winding down.

5 p.m.: Dawn sends friends home and encourages Elizabeth to set the table for dinner.

5:20 p.m.: David comes home and the whole family washes their hands, then they eat. After dinner Andrew clears the table. Dawn does the dishes while David changes and holds Michael.

6 – 8 p.m.: The family could have Family Home Evening, David may be at a bishopric meeting, Dawn goes visiting teaching, or David goes home teaching. If none of these responsibilities need to be done, the family will play a game, do a puzzle, hold Michael, take baths, laugh a little or chase Andrew and Jonathan. After the day is almost over, Dawn wonders where the time has gone.

Dawn’s night.

8 p.m.: Dawn encourages the children to get into their pajamas, changes Michael and gives bedtime snacks and vitamins. The family reads scriptures, and then David reads Horse and His Boy for the kids.

8:45 p.m.: After family prayer, Dawn tucks the children in bed. She lies with Jonathan while nursing Michael to sleep.

9:30 p.m.: If Dawn is not too tired from an exhausting day, she reads or gives David a debriefing of the day. She then is ready for bed and falls asleep, for it all will begin again soon.
