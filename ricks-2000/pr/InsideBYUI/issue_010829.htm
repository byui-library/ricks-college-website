STUDENT ACTIVITIES PROGRAM WILL PROVIDE NEW OPPORTUNITIES

The new Student Activities Program at Brigham Young University-Idaho is in the formulative stages and is expected to open new opportunities in sports, the arts, and service to everyone. While intercollegiate athletics will be discontinued following the 2001-2002 school year, highly competitive athletics will continue within the institution and will actually involve a higher percentage of students. In the expanded Activities Programs students will be participants, not just spectators.

"At the present time we serve probably 280 intercollegiate athletes, and that’s a great enterprise," President David A. Bednar says. "But we’ll move toward a program where we can serve hundreds and even thousands with the same resources." He adds, "Instead of a place where you buy a ticket and sit in the stands and watch, at Brigham Young University-Idaho, if you choose to, you’ll be on the field doing it."

President Bednar says, "I believe people will find many of the events to be quite entertaining. Imagine the Hart Auditorium packed with students and community residents who have an interest in coming to watch a women’s final four or a men’s final four made up of teams that have been competing on campus. The community will be invited, and we hope they will be excited to come. This will be great fun both to participate in and to watch."

He also expects to see Viking Stadium used more in coming years. "It’s presently used a limited number of times each year. We’ll get much more use out of it. You can expect to see the lights on many nights."

This new concept broadens the access of competitive sports far beyond "intramurals." The program will include structured coaching, regular practices, full uniforms, and equipment¾a level formerly characteristic of only varsity athletics.

Students may also explore artistic and service opportunities within a similar structure, says Devin Shaum, director of the Student Activities Program. The program is structured in three areas: service and social, visual and performing arts, and physical activities, giving students numerous opportunities for active involvement at various levels of interest and commitment.

BYU-IDAHO AND ISU MUSIC FACULTY JOIN TALENTS FOR FUN NIGHT OF OPERA

Faculty from Idaho State University and Brigham Young University-Idaho are teaming to present "Antonia: A Tale of Hoffmann," the climactic act of Jacques Offenbach’s opera "The Tales of Hoffmann." The shows will be performed Aug. 30 in ISU’s Goranson Hall in Pocatello and Sept. 6 in BYU-Idaho’s Barrus Concert Hall. Both performances begin at 7:30 p.m. Admission to the Rexburg concert is free.

"It will be a beautiful evening of magic and fantasy," said Kristine Ciesinski, opera director and part-time music instructor at BYU-Idaho. "‘Antonia: A Tale of Hoffmann’ is a fantastic tale of romance and an operatic tragedy. This opera has been a joy to produce, and it will be a fun evening for the audience."

Ciesinski’s opera career has included performances in La Scala, Italy; Paris, France; and the Bolshoi in Russia. A native of Delaware, Ciesinski won both the Geneva and the Salzburg International Opera Competitions.

Diane Burt, an ISU adjunct music faculty member, performs a lead role as Antonia. Hoffmann is played by David Peck, a BYU-Idaho history professor. ISU associate professor of voice Kathleen Lane is featured in the opera as Antonia’s mother. The role of Dr. Miracle is played by Dan Rodgers. Pocatello singer Floyd Anderson, baritone, performs the role of Crespel, Antonia’s father. Marsha Jones of Pocatello will accompany on piano.

"The Tales of Hoffmann" was composed by Offenbach (1819-1880) and was first produced in 1881. This performance of the last act will run about 50 minutes. (More: http://www.isu.edu/pressrel/hoffmann.html)
