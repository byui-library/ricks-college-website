COURSE SYLLABUS

Department: Home Economics
Course#: ID 295
Title: Lighting Credit: 2
Text: Interior Lighting For Designers, Gary Gordon & Frank Nuckolls

Course Objective:
To expand the student’s basic knowledge of lighting principles.  To develop a working vocabulary of lighting technology.  To expose students to lighting catalogues for practical use.  To have the student report on one research article from trade journals, or professional magazines relating to lighting design, or technology, To expose the students to various lighting techniques for residential and contract installations.  To emphasize the artistic approach to lighting design.  To expose the student to “daylighting” as a means of energy conservation.  To require the student to compile a file of lighting applications and information.  To evaluate the student’s understanding and competency by written exam and practical application.

Description:
A course designed to introduce the student to the principles of lighting design, lighting systems, communication of lighting design, and practical application of learned principles.
 
Special Requirements: (Research Papers, Projects, etc.)
Each student will read and analyze one research article from journals or lighting magazines.  The report will be presented in class orally and in written form.  A topic that coincides with information being presented in class should be selected.  Each student will organize a Lighting Resource File with magazine photos, lighting, research articles, resource lists, and bibliographies, and class notes.  This should be used as a resource in the future.  Students will complete one conceptual design presentation of assigned project.  Each student will select one project from their portfolio and rework the lighting design.  A notebook with the lighting specs will be required.  Each student will be required to take four exams to test their understanding and comprehension of material presented.  Homework assignments at the discretion of the instructor will be required.
