BYU-Idaho Internship Philosophy

BYU-Idaho is rethinking education....We realized that a key to the success of our business program is to stay in close contact with the professional environment, and to require our students to prove themselves on the real business playing field of battle prior to graduation.

Professional work experience is not an option in our program, but a requirement for all graduating business management students. It is the ultimate application of classroom learning before graduation.

We invite the business world to join with us as partners in education. Through on the job applications and supervision, students will become refined and primed for graduation. With company evaluations and feedback, our business program can stay abreast of current business recruiting expectations and become more aware of how to improve our classroom preparation for graduating, qualified business managers. Businesses can screen future employees, take advantage of low cost government subsidized internship wages, and use educational facilities for research and problem solving. All parties can become business partners in a more beneficial way for students, industry, and education.

As we teamwork in refining the educational process, the university, the workplace, and most of all the students will all be winners in the process. Internship work experience is definitely a WIN-WIN-WIN partnership proposition.

Business Advantages:

  • Cost Savings: The State of Idaho will pay 50% of the wages for all resident Idaho Interns working for Idaho Businesses. What a valuable resource! For those businesses outside of Idaho or Idaho businesses who hire non-Idaho interns, cost savings are still in effect as you acquire low cost, quality, students who have the latest training in business concepts and computer applications.
    •  
  • Screening Device: Many businesses use the internship program as a cost effective, full-time recruiting method. By using the temporary internship employment training as a screening period, companies find compatible, competent employees who will "fit" into their business culture on a full-time basis after graduation.
  • Project Resource: Do you need a special marketing research project, or competitive marketing project completed? Or do you need some financial spreadsheet analysis, or database management production? Why not hire a business student intern fresh out of the training classroom to manage such a project? How about an E-Commerce study or set up? You not only have the student intern as a resource, but the network of the entire business management program in some cases can be used as a resource. Using qualified student interns on your team can be an economical, progressive, creative tool of management.
    •  
  • Be a part of the Educational Team: The Business Management Program will be constantly updated and evaluated in how they can bring into the classroom the current training demands of business. Through the internship evaluation process, you can give valuable suggestions on not only what we need to include in our curriculum, but how to articulate the best methods business skills can be taught. We need you! We also use the business professionals from our internship sponsoring companies as special guest speakers in specialized classes and at major events.
    •  
  • International Resource: Many of our students have had two or more years of missionary experience in a foreign land. If a business is engaged in, or planning to open up foreign commerce, our interns can help with cultural orientation, and foreign language expertise/interpretation. We have many students who can speak Spanish, Portuguese, Russian, Japanese, Chinese, and other languages.

  • Motivated Employees:  Student are working for grades and high evaluations along with the other compensation they receive.  That is why over 75% of U.S. businesses prefer temporary interns to other temporary employees.

  • World-Wide Business Community Service: Many of our interns have indicated that the work experience adds a great dimension to their educational experience and career preparation. Internship sponsors are helping our students to be winners as they complete their university training. In a national study, researchers have found that a graduating student who has completed an internship work experience will be offered on average a 8.9% higher salary than those who have not. The internship experience adds value not only to the student, but to the business he/she works for. This is a win-win educational partnership.

Profile of a Typical BYU-Idaho Business Management Intern:

What is the profile of a typical BYU-Idaho Advanced Internship Student?

Average GPA: High - 3.5

Classroom Experience: Balanced-we will not send a student out on an advanced internship until he/she has completed 2.5 years in our program and taken a variety of classes in business including:

-Basic Principles of Business Management
-Business Communications (oral, written, electronic)
-Computer Applications (D-base, Spreadsheets, Internet, Presentations, etc.)
-Economics (macro, micro)
-Business Law & Legal Environment
-E-Business
-Financial Business Management
-Marketing
-Business Operations
-Organizational Behavior
-Financial Accounting
-Managerial Accounting

LDS Mission Experience: Equals maturity--most of our male junior business students have served a two-year mission in a foreign culture developing a high level of human relation and communication skills. They are accustom to much travel and experiencing new situations and new relationships.

Foreign Language: Many of our students have spent two years in foreign countries learning and speaking the native tongue. Many are fluent in Spanish, Portuguese, Russian, and the Pacific Rim languages. They are eager to put this skill into practice.

Sales/Marketing: Two years of door-to-door missionary presentation experience coupled with a beginning internship in sales, makes many of our student very attractive in the sales, marketing area. Many have been top salesmen for door-to-door sales companies during their freshman and sophomore summer experiences. They know how to overcome objections and rejection.

Commitment to Clean Living: The well-known standards at BYU-I include quality dress and grooming, Christian living, and adherence to a strict code of honor and morality.

Leadership Experience: Most of our students have had years of leadership training and experience in youth groups, clubs, and extra curricular activities. From a young age they have been involved in making oral presentations, planning special events, setting and reporting on personal goals, and working in teams.

Survey of Top Qualities: In a recent survey to determine the qualities of our interns, a sample of 75 supervisors rated our students far above expectations in the following areas: Teamwork and leadership skills, problem solving skills, communication skills, and entrepreneurship spirit.

How to become a sponsoring partner:

Question: What are the responsibilities of the sponsoring internship partner?

Answer: BYU-I has a very basic non-complicated agreement contact that sponsoring companies must agree to by signature. This agreement explains the basic requirements of all parties involved, and provides the student with liability insurance which is a great asset to the sponsoring company. View the BYU-I Agreement Contract.

Question: Do I get to choose who I hire?

Answer: Yes, once your request a student intern, we will send you as many as you want to interview, and you decide if any will fulfill your request. You can look at the intern resumes on our website and decide who you want to interview. You will do the actual hiring when the time comes.

Question: What are the steps in becoming a sponsoring partner with BYU-I?

Answer: Call or e-mail our Internship Director and request your company be included on our Business Internship Database. Complete and mail or e-mail to us a "Sponsoring Internship Company Datasheet" or send in a business card with the appropriate information. You do not need to sign a BYU-I Agreement Contract until you actually hire one of our student interns.

Other Questions:

Question: Is the sponsoring company required to pay all interns?

Answer: No. It is strongly suggested that each intern be paid a competitive wage, but some students might be persuaded that the type of work experience is compensation enough.

Question: How many hours must a student work to complete an internship?

Answer: The minimum required hours are 270 over a minimum eight-week period.

Question: Will all business related jobs in my company fulfill the requirements of the internship?

Answer: No. Each work opportunity will be evaluated and approved by the Internship Director prior to internship enrollment. We are expecting that each intern will be given a level of "management training opportunity" that will help him/her apply the principles and skills learned in the classroom. Special projects, and rotation of responsibilities although not required, would be valuable tools to help the intern mature in his/her application and vision of business concepts.

Question: If I want to sponsor an intern, do I need to create a special "Internship Job" or will current jobs be sufficient?

Answer: Eighty percent of internship experiences in the U.S., are done in "normal" jobs, the special "internship" programs that some of the larger companies have are the exception rather than the rule. No, you do not need to create a special type of job, but you will need to do two internship evaluations of job performance for your student in whatever job he works.
