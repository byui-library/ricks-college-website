Blenheim Palace
{blen'-uhm}

Blenheim, in Woodstock Park near Oxford, is a very large English baroque palace designed by Nicholas Hawksmoor John Churchill, duke of Marlborough.  Named for the site of Marlborough's 1704 victory in Europe, Blenheim was a public gift to the duke for his great victories.  The vast North Court, flanked by two service courts, is dominated by the tall entrance to the central block and by the fantastic ornaments of the four corner towers.

Foundations were laid in 1705 but financial problems and disputes held up completion until 1727.  The original formal gardens were replaced in the 1760s with an extensive and picturesque "English" park.  In this century the palace has been restored in many areas.  The palace is the birthplace of Winston Churchill.
