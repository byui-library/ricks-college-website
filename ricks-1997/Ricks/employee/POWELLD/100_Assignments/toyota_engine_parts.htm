Toyota Engine Parts

1.    Timing Belt - Coordinates valve timing with engine operation.  Some engines use a chain.  If it has a cogged belt             like this one, it should be changed every 60,000 - 90,000 miles as recommended in owners manual.  If it breaks, it can bend the valves and cost a lot of money.

2.    Water Pump Drive Pulley - turns the water pump to circulate coolant throughout the engine.

3.    Front Crankshaft Pulley - drives accessories, such as air conditioning, alternator, power steering, etc.

4.    Valve Cover - covers top of cylinder head which houses the valve train and camshaft on many engines.

5.    Cylinder Head - forms a "lid" for the cylinders, and houses the valves, spark plugs, and camshaft.  Sealed to engine block with a head gasket to provide a gas and water tight seal.

6.    Temperature Gauge Sender - tells the gauge on the dash how warm your engine is, or turns on the temp light.

7.    Distributor/ Coil Pack - driven by camshaft so spark plugs fire at the proper time, at the beginning of the power stroke.

8.    Spark Plug Wires - conducts high-voltage electricity from the coil to the spark plugs to ignite the air-fuel mixture at the beginning of the power stroke.

9.    Alternator - recharges the battery to replace the electricity used to start the car, and to run the accessories and electrical system.

10.    Oil Pan or Oil Sump - bolts to the bottom of the engine block to provide a reservoir for the lubricating oil.  Usually holds four or five quarts of oil.

11.    Oil Drain Plug - Removed to allow dirty oil to be drained from the pan and replaced with clean oil.  Always use a box end wrench to remove and re-tighten.

12.    Oil Filter - filters dirt and contaminants out of the oil as it circulates through the engine.  Should be replaced when the engine oil is changed.

13.     Dip Stick - allows the driver to check the level of oil in the oil pan.  Should be done when you fill up with gas.

14.    EGR Valve - Stand for Exhaust Gas Recirculation.   Helps reduce emissions and engine detonation or pinging.

15.    Intake Plenum - Used to be called the intake manifold on carbureted engines, but on fuel injected engines the plenum only conducts air to the cylinders, where it is mixed with fuel to be used in the engine.

16.     Throttle Position Sensor - Attached to gas pedal linkage, tells the computer how fast the driver wants to go.

17.    Throttle Body - Regulates the amount of air entering the engine, which is then mixed with fuel to be burned in the cylinders.

18.    Fuel Injector - is controlled by the computer to regulate the amount of fuel mixed with the air to keep the mixture at the ideal ratio for the operating conditions of the engine.

19.    Fuel Rail - holds the fuel under pressure to feed the injectors and meet the fuel needs of the engine.

20.    Fuel Pressure Regulator - located on the end of the fuel rail.  Uses spring pressure and engine vacuum to keep the fuel pressure in the fuel rail at a constant pressure, and increases the pressure under high fuel demands, such as climbing hills, passing, pulling loads, etc.

21.    Coolant Temperature Sensor - tells the computer the temperature of the engine, which richens the mixture for a cold engine, and leans it out as the engine warms up.

22.    Engine Block - forms the "backbone" of the engine.  Contains cylinders, pistons, crankshaft, water and oil passages, and all necessary parts to mount the engine and accessories.  Usually made of cast iron, very rigid and strong.

23.    Core Plug - sometimes called "freeze plugs" because they usually pop out if the coolant freezes.  But they are really to allow the engine casting to be cleaned during the manufacturing process.

24.   Water Jacket Drain Plug - provides a place to drain the water jacket if all the coolant needs to be drained from the engine.

25.    Lower Radiator Inlet - lower radiator hose attaches to this to allow the water pump to draw cool water into the engine for cooling.  The hotter water exits the engine through the upper radiator hose.

26.    Flywheel - Attaches to the back of the crankshaft to allow mounting of the transmission torque-converter or clutch assembly.  Smooths out the power pulses of the engine for smooth engine operation.
