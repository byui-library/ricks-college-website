ELDER DAVID A. BEDNAR SAYS IT IS ALL A REFLECTION ON FAITH

"Faith is reflected when we get up and work diligently to accomplish that for which we have prayed," said Elder David A. Bednar in his opening address to the students at the devotional on August 29. "The combination of (1) future-facing hope and (2) evidence acquired through experience (3) produces action. Faith always is the beginning or origin of action. True faith always is manifest in faithfulness; this is why good works always flows from faith. As we read in James, 'Faith without works is dead ' (James 2:20)."

It has become tradition for the president of Ricks College to give counsel to the new student body at the beginning of each semester. Elder Bednar's direction challenged the students and employees to exercise faith in a season of change. He stated that the first principle of the gospel is faith in the Lord Jesus Christ. Referring to the teachings of the Prophet Joseph Smith, Elder Bednar quoted ". . . faith is . . . the principle of action in all intelligent beings" (Lecture 1, p. 31).

Elder Bednar outlined four fundamental truths about faith: (1) We receive no witness or evidence until after the trial of our faith. (2) Faith is a gift from God. (3) Obedience strengthens faith; sin and wickedness diminish faith. (4) Faith typically is evident over time in many small events and things rather than in a few big events and things. "Answers often come gradually and are more like dew distilling from heaven than a sudden flash flood," he said.

He concluded, "I know that faith in the Savior is a gift from God and a principle of action. I also know we can be blessed with this gift as we make and keep sacred covenants and are obedient." (See http://www.byui.edu/Ricks/Devotionals/)

STUDENTS START THE YEAR WITH SPIRIT OF SERVICE

Ricks students got into the spirit of service Monday afternoon with a giant humanitarian project on the intramural playing fields. "I'm so impressed by the youth who are here and their willingness to serve," said Kim Clinger, a sophomore from American Falls and part of the New Student Orientation team.

Ryan Olson, a freshman from McAllen, Texas, added, "It's pretty fun knowing it's going to a good cause." Helping to tie a quilt for the first time, he said, "I've seen my Mom do it - it's not too hard."

About 2,000 students, mainly incoming freshmen, turned out to the mammoth project that included assembling 8,800 personal hygiene kits for the LDS Church Humanitarian Service Center. Large assembly lines were set up to package the kits containing towels, toothpaste, toothbrushes, combs, bottles of shampoo, soap and a sewing kit. The kits were then boxed, taped, loaded onto pallets and trucked to Salt Lake City where they will await distribution throughout the world.

The students also tied 100 quilts and cut out fleece toy bunnies and fleece jackets. Nearly all the materials were donated by the LDS Humanitarian Service Center for the project.

Allan Jones, coordinator of New Student Orientation, explained, "Part of the Spirit of Ricks is to serve one another. We wanted to instill the value of service in our students early on."

The students were also told about various ways they could continue to serve throughout the school year. Such programs include Adopt-A-Grandparent, Big Brothers/Big Sisters, Family Crisis Center, Friendship/Homework Club, Merit Badge Powwow, Radio Reading Service, Rexburg Nursing Home and Special Olympics.
