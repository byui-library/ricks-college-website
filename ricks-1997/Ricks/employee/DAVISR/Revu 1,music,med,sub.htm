REVIEW  1     Subject, Source, Music, Medium  EXAM 1          DAVIS

 

Art is influenced by the age which produced it. The choice of subject usually reveals something about the artist/when he lived/styles of that time/his choices in life, etc.

Art is not nature (a painting/photo is never exactly the same as what the artist saw. He purposely changes some things to suit his taste.)

The “applied arts” are practical (functional), like making cars, architecture, glass-blowing, furniture, shoes.

Nothing will help you appreciate and understand art more than your own personal experience. The more experiences you have, the more things you will enjoy.

The four major arts are art, music, literature, and architecture. Of these, music is the most subjective. Literature would contain the most examples with subject; architecture, the least.

All art comes from five major areas: nature, history, religion, mythology, and other works of art.

Early art was to instruct, but most art is created simply to be enjoyed.

Medium is what the work is made of. It is essential—you can't make apple pies without apples, or oil paintings without oil.

Fresco uses water-base paints on wet plaster. Used for large walls or ceilings, never small. Measured in square feet. (old churches in Europe; no heat)

Mural is same, but on dry plaster. (our temples; modern heating systems, so walls don't expand.)

Mosaic is bits of colored tile (or glass or whatever) set in wet plaster (or concrete, whatever).

Encaustic uses wax to make the image. (The House of Wax)

Etchings are prints made from a metal plate which has had the image etched (eaten) into the surface by acid. (How we make tiny computer chips with 5 million transistors in them.)

Engravings are prints made from metal plates (how we print $20 bills). Dürer is one of the best.

Woodcuts are prints made from carved wood blocks. Are usually small (inches) and were used to illustrate books, bibles, etc. Poor man's art. Japan was best as a nation in making these, but the best artist was Dürer, from Germany, in 1500s.

Bronze is great for statues but difficult to work with, very expensive, but very strong. Hollow. We have several on campus.

Sculpture is carved rock, usually marble. The sculptor is the person who did the carving. Note the spelling: sculpture (item) vs. sculptor (person).

Illumination is decorating pages of a book with hand-painted pictures, as was done in the first bibles, copied by monks.

Oil is the world's most popular medium. Introduced to world by Van Eyck brothers in 1400s. Takes a long time to dry, so changes are easy to make even days later. Permits great detail (Arnolfini).

Music is as old as Man, but notation started about 800AD. The staff (clef) is five lines, while the Grand Staff includes both the treble and bass clefs. Each line and space is used to define a pitch, moving up alphabetically, from A through G, and then repeating. The treble clef is a G, the bass is an F.

Most music has no subject, and is called absolute music; when the composer wants his audience to picture a specific object or idea, even going so far as to include program notes, we have program music.

Form follows function (the shape of something is determined by its function, i.e., shoe, pen, sail).

Sharps (#) and flats (b) are called accidentals and raise or lower a pitch by one half step respectively.

The time signature (44) determines the basic beat of the music. The top number tells how many beats are in each measure, while the bottom number tells what kind of note (4 = quarter, 8 = eighth, etc.) gets one beat.

The overall pulse of music is called rhythm, and an irregular rhythm (jerky, not on the beat) is syncopated.

Note values are relative. Each of the following receives twice as much time value as the next: whole, half, quarter, eighth, etc. (So if a quarter note gets one beat, a half would get two, and a whole note would receive four beats, etc.)

The human heart is the basis for speed (tempo) in music. All music faster than the average resting heart (about 70 beats/minute) causes the heart to speed up (allegro, vivace, etc.) while slower speeds tend to make us slow down (adagio, largo, etc.)

Music is composed in keys or scales, which are units of 8 notes. A C# major scale would start on C#. Major and minor scales are created by the position of half steps in the scale. Major sounds happy (the heroine), and minor sounds sad or evil (the villain).

A simple melody (you humming) is monophonic (single, unaccompanied melody), but ordinary music (almost everything you hear on the radio) is homophonic (melody plus accompaniment). Polyphonic music is when you have two or more melodies going on at the same time, creating a harmony of their own (a round, any chorus in the Messiah).

A cappella literally means “for the chapel,” We interpret the phrase as “no accompaniment.” (The early church didnt allow organs in meetings!)

The longer or larger the vibrating body, the lower the pitch. The converse is true for higher notes.

timbre is a French word for “sound color” meaning “it sounds like a trumpet” or “that sounds like a piano.” Timbre is determined by the overtones of the instrument.

Dynamic marks (p, f, ppp, >, etc.) tell you to play soft, loud, very, very quiet, etc.

The orchestra has four basic sections: woodwinds (i.e., oboe), brass (trombone), strings (violin), and percussion (drums). A choir is also composed of four sections: soprano, alto, tenor, bass. For the vibrating body, some instruments use a single reed (clarinet), others use a double-reed (oboe). Still others use the players vibrating lips (trumpet).

In range the piccolo is the highest, and the contra-bassoon the lowest.

Organs are ranked in size according to how many ranks (complete sets  of pipes) they contain. An average organ has three. The one in Barrus Hall has 146, Salt Lake 192, while some in New York and Los Angeles have over 400.

     Like a choir or orchestra, the organ has four families or divisions of pipes: diapasons, strings, flutes, reeds.
