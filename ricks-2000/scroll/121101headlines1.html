BYU-Idaho presents a Christmas Tradition:
The Messiah

by Lindsie Taylor
tay01027@byui.edu
Arts Staff

Nathan West, a junior from Pocatello, Idaho, plays the violin during BYU-Idaho music department’s presentation of The Messiah.
Seen as the world’s most famous oratorio, Georg Friederich Händel’s The Messiah was performed Thursday, Friday and Saturday in the Ruth H. Barrus Concert Hall. And although the music was the same as in years past, the presentation style was different in both size and sound from previous years at Ricks College.

“When we do it in the Hart, it has everything – a full organ, brass and winds, but it is a little different this time,” Kevin Brower, choir department director, said. “Now it is more of a historical and authentic performance. We have tried to recreate it how Händel would have seen it.”

The combined efforts of Brower and Robert Tueller, Chamber Orchestra professor, have made The Messiah to resemble more of what Händel most likely intended his performance to be like.

After a lifetime of success, Händel’s music began to lose fame and his life took a negative turn. A friend, Charles Jennens, proposed a composition possibility for a charity event. Starting Aug 22, 1741, Händel composed a new oratorio, The Messiah, in 24 days.

Although he was trained in composition and was a master of music, The Messiah was no small feat for Händel. Divided into three sections, the work depicts Jesus Christ’s life, death and resurrection.

“I get so excited to sing The Messiah. It is the highest possible way to worship God through music,” Marie Giles, a sophomore from Westminster, Colo., said.

In Händel’s time, the choir and orchestra consisted of about 30 professionals. A small, baroque-style performance became obsolete when Mozart revised The Messiah for his classical tastes. With over 3,000 members, the musical ensemble performed to thousands of spectators during the 1850s.

It was after this style that Ricks College patterned many of its 67 performances of The Messiah. Only twice in the past century has The Messiah been performed in the Barrus Concert Hall in the more baroque style.
