RICKS GEARING UP FOR FALL SEMESTER

There's a bit of a lull at Ricks this week as summer classes have just ended and Fall Semester classes won't start until Aug. 29. Registrar Kelly Hymas reported there were a record 3,349 students enrolled during the final weeks of summer school. Early reports indicate we'll see an increase in students for Fall Semester with estimates between 8,900 and 9,000 students.

Twenty-three new full-time faculty members will be working at Ricks when fall semester classes start. Max Checketts, assistant academic vice president, says the new employees have been hired to fill vacancies mainly caused by retirements. The overall number of full-time faculty members remains steady at approximately 370.

UPDATE ON SPORI BUILDING AND NEW HOMEPAGE

August 11 was a historic day for the Spori Building as classes met for the final time in the building that is scheduled to be torn down later this fall in preparation for a new building that will be constructed on the same location. Departments that have been housed in the Spori have been temporarily relocated in other buildings across campus. The Celebrating the Spori Committee is working on plans to commemorate the building in the fall, with several events planned in conjunction with Homecoming.

For more information about the Spori, check out our new and very informative homepage entitled "Celebrating the Spori" at http://www.byui.edu/PR/spori. It includes information about the building's history, memories, photos and press releases. We will also post upcoming events that will commemorate the building.

PRESIDENT'S COUNCIL ANSWERS QUESTIONS

The President's Council held a question-and-answer session Aug. 9 for interested employees and students. Several questions were asked pertaining to our transition to a four-year university. We thought you might be interested in this update:

When will the first upper division classes begin?

President David A. Bednar: "I'm not willing to say, but it's conceivable some will start in the fall of 2001 or by January 2002. It would be foolish to let arbitrary timelines get in the way of what we hope to accomplish. We're going to do it right."

Have any specific baccalaureate degrees been approved yet?

President Bednar: "Nothing has been approved. We'll keep you informed about the approval process."

Are we going to change from three five-week summer terms to two eight-week terms?

Don Bird, Academic Vice President: This is being discussed but no final decision has been made. Even if this happens, some programs will be given the flexibility of being shorter, such as the present five-week Discovery programs.

A university usually implies graduate-level courses. Will we truly be a university?

Don Bird: Graduate classes are not required to be a university. BYU-Hawaii has a few graduate-level courses but no graduate programs. BYU-Idaho will be governed by the same Board of Trustees as BYU-Provo and BYU-Hawaii. Consequently, we can function like other university systems, such as the University of Wisconsin system that even has a junior college as part of its system.
