Text: Neuman, W. Lawrence.  2000.  Social Research Methods: Qualitative and Quantitative Approaches (Fourth Edition).  Allyn and Bacon.

 

Course Description:  This course is designed to introduce the student to the tools of social research, such as: surveys, experimental design, content analysis, scale construction, electronic data processing and analysis.

Course Objectives:  Upon successful completion of this course the student can be expected to:

 

1.       Explain the difference between scientific research and other methods of gaining knowledge.

2.       Explain the relationship between theory and research.

3.       Describe the basic scientific research process, including: planning, designing, and analyzing.

4.       Describe quantitative data collection and analysis.

5.       Describe qualitative data collection and analysis.

6.       Participate in research process by applying their knowledge of quantitative and qualitative research methodologies.

Attendance:  Attendance is necessary for the successful completion of this course.  There are 100 participation points that are directly related to attendance.  This amounts to five points per day we cover a text chapter (with one free day).  You are responsible for all materials and information that is given in class.  Tardiness or non-attendance does not excuse you from these responsibilities or from any assignments or activities.  This means that no matter the reason, if you do not do the work in class at the appropriate time you will have to make it up according to the make up policy.

 

Make Ups:  You can make up anything in this class or do any of the work late with certain conditions.  Any missed assignments are to be made up as soon as possible and will automatically be assessed a 10% penalty and an additional 10% penalty per week.

 

Assignments:  You will be expected to participate in two research projects: the first quantitative, the second qualitative.  For each you will provide me with a proposal justifying and outlining your research, and then you will collect data.  For the qualitative study you will analyze and report on your findings.

            Research is often a collaborative process carried out in a community of scholars; therefore, these research projects will be group projects.  You will be accountable to me and to your peers (graded by all involved).  The defense of these projects will basically be an oral exam of the chapters covered in that section and the project work.  We will discuss these projects in much more depth as the semester progresses.

 

Plagiarism policy:  Plagiarism is not tolerated in this class.  Any evidence of plagiarism will be grounds for failure of the assignment (zero points).  Plagiarism is copying someone else’s work.  This includes your peers, any written sources and the internet.  Any information that is not your own original thoughts should be properly documented.

For example, if you write about the relationship between religion and people, and include complete sentences or ideas from another author you need to acknowledge them.  It is ok to include something that Thomas Jefferson wrote, just make sure that it is clear that you are not presenting it as your own work.  It is appropriate to write:   Thomas Jefferson said, “yada yada yada”.  It is inappropriate to say that religion is the opium of the people without acknowledging that Marx said this, not you.
