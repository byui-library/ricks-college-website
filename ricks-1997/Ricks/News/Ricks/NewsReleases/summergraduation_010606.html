Ricks College News Release
June 6, 2001

 

Thomas E. Ricks speaks 
at last college graduation

A hundred years after his death, Thomas E. Ricks returned in the form of an actor to the final Ricks College commencement Wednesday morning in Rexburg.  As part of the last graduation before Ricks becomes known as Brigham Young University-Idaho, college President David A. Bednar welcomed the founder of Rexburg and namesake for Ricks College as the final commencement speaker.  Ricks, who died in 1901, was portrayed by Brent Kinghorn, who recently stepped down as community services vice president at the college. Kinghorn’s wife, Donna Jean, is a great-granddaughter of Thomas E. Ricks.

"I decided that this year, 2001, would be a good time to come back and visit my school and see what you’ve done with it," Ricks told graduates and members of the Ricks family who were special graduation guests. "It was 100 years ago when I left here for good – 1901, September, it was. I was 73 years old at the time."

The former president of the board of trustees of Bannock Stake Academy (later to be renamed Ricks Academy and then Ricks College) said he had heard the school’s name was changing to BYU-Idaho. "It doesn’t bother me," he said. "Gosh, I was embarrassed when they named it after me in the first place."

Ricks said he had known Brigham Young, for whom Ricks College will soon be named. He said the church president had called him on a couple of missions in the early years in Utah. "I feel okay about this name change," he said. "And by the way, I feel Brother Brigham would feel okay about it too. He might be embarrassed about it, but I think he would be pleased. Downright pleased."

Ricks told the graduates that there should be a value to one’s education. "Our people felt that way back when we founded this school. That’s why we started this it in the first place," he said. "We wanted a school where we could even teach the principles of the multiplication tables in the spirit of the gospel of Jesus Christ."

He added, "Now, what I hope for you, however, is that you’ve also learned some other things here. How to become people of integrity – honest people, fair-minded people, dependable, and faithful. That’s what this world needs is people like that. Oh, book-learning is good, but it’s secondary to becoming people of integrity and people of faith."

President Bednar said 649 degrees were conferred at summer graduation for a total of 3,556 for this academic year. Of the 623 graduates, 416 were females and 207 were males. He said 174 had served full-time missions and 21 were foreign students.

In speaking to graduates and their families, Bednar likened graduation to a space shuttle launch that he once witnessed in Florida. "In a very real sense, you are blasting off," he said. "May I suggest as you leave this wonderful campus that the lessons I learned about space flight in Florida are applicable to you today as you commence a new phase of your lives."

Just as it takes an enormous amount of fuel to generate the power necessary to lift the shuttle off of the launch pad, he said, "In a similar way, you will need a great deal of energy and resolve to launch out of your comfort zone here at Ricks College and move into new challenges and routines and, ultimately, achieve a higher spiritual and professional orbit. Please do not underestimate the demands of successfully moving to a higher level of spiritual and personal development, and of continued progression over time."

He added, "Initially, the shuttle flies nearly straight up in order to free itself from atmospheric drag. Likewise, you and I need to fly straight and in accordance with the sacred covenants we have made and with the commandments we have received. Flying straight enables us to avoid the drag of temptation and sin and the detours of despair and remorse. Flying straight reduces worldly drag, facilitates righteous acceleration, and enables us to obtain a higher spiritual plane."

Bednar said that once in orbit, the shuttle depends upon its own oxygen supplies to help power the engines. Unlike an airplane engine that depends upon atmospheric oxygen to burn fuel, the shuttle’s engines must have their own oxygen supply so they can continue to burn when outside of the earth’s atmosphere. He said, "In like manner, you and I must live and obey and act such that we can qualify for the companionship of the Holy Ghost—and a supply of power and light that is unaffected by the darkness so prevalent in the world."
