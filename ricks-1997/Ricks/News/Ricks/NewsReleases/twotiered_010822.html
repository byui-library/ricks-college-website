BYU–Idaho News Release
August 22, 2001

BYU-Idaho will be a two-tiered institution 

offering both bachelor's and associate degrees

    
REXBURG, Idaho – The transition to BYU-Idaho allows students to choose between associate or bachelor’s degrees.

Beginning Sept. 5, junior-level courses will begin at BYU-Idaho for the first time since the announcement that Ricks College would become a four-year university known as BYU-Idaho.

The announcement added bachelor’s degrees to the institution’s offerings while retaining and consolidating the existing associate programs, creating a two-tiered institution.

"The notion of a two-tiered institution is simply that we will retain some specialized associate degree programs," says BYU-Idaho President David A. Bednar. "BYU-Idaho will be one of the only places in [The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints] for those specialized associate degrees, and we think it’s important to retain them."

With a specialized associate degree, a student can be ready for employment in about two years. Students will learn skills in specialized areas such as culinary arts, criminal justice, welding, and agricultural management.

BYU-Idaho will offer these programs, which are traditionally found at community, vocational or junior colleges, in its unique academic environment due to its affiliation to the LDS Church. Students agree to live by certain behavior standards, including dress and grooming standards.

"If we were to forego those particular degrees, there would basically be students who would not have that opportunity in all of [LDS] Church education," says Assistant Academics Vice President Max Checketts.

During the 2000-2001 academic year, Ricks College offered 122 associate programs. Many of those programs will be shifted to baccalaureate degrees, others will remain as associate degrees and some programs will be phased out.

In such a transition, the university is obligated to take care of its current students, allowing them to complete their current program. It is anticipated that by 2005 current programs will be transitioned or discontinued.

Though some programs will be discontinued or shifted, "We will not have to let any of the faculty go," Checketts says.

Over the next five years approximately 50 integrated and specialized bachelor’s degrees will be developed and phased in as time and resources make it feasible. Nineteen associate degrees will be offered.

The two types of baccalaureate degrees give additional options to students. With a maximum of 45 credit hours in a given major, integrated degrees focus on breadth, integrating traditional curriculum with a student’s interests. With a maximum of 70 credit hours, specialized degrees focus on depth, concentrating more intensely on a given subject.

During the first year, integrated degrees will be offered in accounting, English, history, and recreation education, and specialized degrees will be offered in business management, elementary education, interior design and nursing.
