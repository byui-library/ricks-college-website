  This course has been designed to provide an opportunity for experienced students to increase their understanding of contemporary floral terminology and theory, with particular interest in European influences on design, both recent and historical.
        You must be a sophomore in the 217 major, and have completed the Hort 135 flower arranging course, or have the consent of the instructor.
 

We will focus on:
      Floral terminology
        Floral identification
            Hand-tied bouquets
                High Style designing
                    Current trends

General Information about the class:

 You will be responsible to do some assigned reading, collecting of pictures and ideas, and preparation of a reading/research report that will help you in understanding some specific european designs.  A final design project will be required to be completed and turned in by the 9th of March.  This project will be placed on display during the Spring Floral open house on the 16th during women’s week.  The project will be constructed of permanent design materials of your provision.
 

 There is no separate lab fee for the course, but the lab/travel fee you have paid as a floral design major is utilized in part for lab materials.  If you have not registered for Hort 095, you will need to do so if you are enrolled in this class.  We will use plant materials from time to time in the class to learn, demonstrate, and practice technique.  For some of the lab work in the class, you may be required to provide your own material.  Also, subscriptions to floral magazines and membership in student educational organizations will be explained and encouraged.
 
 

 Grades will be based on the following breakdown, standard percentages:
  Exams and assignments    300 points
  Lab attendance and participation 200 points
  Total possible    500 points
 

Tentative Course Topics:

Materials
 Getting reacquainted with flowers
 What floral materials are most useful for which designs?
 Learning to get a feel for the proper blending of the parts to make the whole.
 Using colour as a tool

Historical, and period design styles
 Review of Historical Perspective [with modern terminology]
 Egyptian--symmetry, repetition, hairpieces
 Classical--wreaths and garlands
 Renaissance--symbolism
 Flemish--"millifleur"
 English country--Georgian, Victorian
 French country--the "garden look"
 German influence:
  A visit with a german master designer

Contemporary styles
 Terminology--today's names for yesterday's ideas
 Clustering, Grouping--the "euro look"
 Parallel influence, and New Convention,
  Landscape and Vegetative
 Neo-Classical--viedermeyer
 Freestyle
 Hand-tied bouquet construction
 

The texts:   Holland Flower Book, from the Flower Council of Holland
 This book will be a useful tool as you converse with suppliers and designers trying to locate flowers.  (available from the department)
  The Art of Floral Design, by Norah Hunter
 This book should be quite familiar to you, and will prove a useful reference tool in the chapters that refer to contemporary design.
  The Book of Fresh Flowers, by Malcolm Hillier
 This european designer adds a refreshing new perspective to floral design techniques and materials.
