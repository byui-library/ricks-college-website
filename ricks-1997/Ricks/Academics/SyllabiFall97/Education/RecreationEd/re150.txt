I. COURSE DESCRIPTION:  Introduction to riding and handling 
	horses in a backcountry situation.

II. COURSE OBJECTIVE:  
	A. To provide the student with background knowledge 
	and skill in basic backcountry horsemanship through 
	lectures and field experience.
	B. To develop awareness of modern techniques and ethics 
	for handling horses in the wilderness.
	C. To develop a background in the care, selection, and 
	use of tack and equipment used in Trailriding.
	D.   To provide a basis for the development of a lifelong 
	recreational pursuit.

III. TEXT: Syllabus will be provided in class.  Books of interest:
	A. Techniques & equipment for Wilderness Horse Travel, 
	USDA Forest Service.
	B. Packin in on Mules and Horses, Smoke Elser.
	C. Horse Camping, Ron Waters.

IV. FEES:  Lab fee of $40.00 will cover transportation, group gear, 
	lab assistants, and program materials.

V.  DRESS:  Come to class dressed for riding.  Loose comfortable 
	clothing.  Boots and hat for protection from the sun.  
	A water bottle and small snack may be helpful on the long 
	afternoon rides. Sunglasses and gloves when weather calls 
	for them. Rain Gear if needed.

        Note:  because of limited travel space and horses, guests 
	and spouses are not permitted.

VI. In compliance with applicable disability law, qualified 
	students with a disability may be entitled to "reasonable 
	accommodation."  It is the student's responsibility to 
	disclose to the teacher any special need she/he may have 
	before the end of the first week of class.
