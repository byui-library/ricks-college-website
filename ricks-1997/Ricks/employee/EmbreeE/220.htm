Communication 220

Interpersonal

Textbook: Looking Out/Looking In

by Adler and Towne, Harcourt Brace Jovanovich, 1996.

This course is designed to help you improve your knowledge of the interpersonal communication process. We will be doing exercises where you can see the different processes working. This is a hands-on class. This is the reason for the attendance policy. The lessons learned in class can not be made up by a simple reading of the text. You will not become an expert communicator in one semester, but this class will give you the tools to continually work toward more effective communication.

The grade you receive in this class will be determined by the amount and quality of work you choose to do. There are six areas in which you may earn credit. They are described below. Your attitude toward the class may also make some difference in your final grade.

Attendance: As what occurs in class is unique and can never be relearned, you will be expected to attend class. Short quizzes on the reading will be given every day. These quizzes can not be made up without an excellent excuse (I.E. Some of the more advanced forms of death.) If you are not present when a quiz is given, you loose the points. A quick glance at the point breakdown for the course will give you a good idea how much this could effect your final grade...

Reading: As mentioned above, you will be quizzed on the reading. Most of our time in class will be spent on fun activities designed to give you a chance to experience first hand the concepts discussed in the reading. If you haven't read the chapter, you wont get what you should out of the activities. You should have the chapter read before the presentation on that chapter is given so that you can participate in the discussion, and then quickly review it the next day to prepare for that day's quiz and discussion.

Tests: There will be three exams throughout the semester. They will be worth 100 pts. each and will be short answer, multiple choice, short essay and long essay. There will be no final exam, instead you will have a final project(also worth 100 pts.) I'll tell you more about that later.....

Chapter Presentations: You will be divided into groups and each group will be assigned to take 45 minutes and present a chapter from the text to the class. You will be given complete discretion as to how you wish to do this. Feel free to use visual or audio aids, activities, or whatever you wish to get the concepts across. Use your imagination. This presentation will require a great deal of work and preparation, and will comprise a significant portion of your grade for the class - so don't scrimp.

The grading of your presentation will be based on the following:

Group grade by instructor and peers

  • Group involvement
  • Class involvement
  • Coverage of materials
  • Application of materials to everyday living
  • Overall effectiveness.

Application Papers: You will be asked to write and submit a short (approx. 2 pages) paper on each chapter - 10 in all. You will be asked to write about a concept or concepts from that chapter and how it applies to your life. These papers will be graded on a pass/fail basis and are worth ten points each. They are due one week after the chapter reading for that chapter is due. Please type!

Assignments: During the course of the semester, I may assign some small projects. These will as a rule not consume a great deal of time - but they will count toward your final grade.

Grade Summary:

  • Quizzes.....................100 pts.
  • Tests.........................300 pts.
  • Presentation..............100 pts.
  • Papers......................100 pts.
  • Misc. Assignments....50 pts.
  • Final Project.............100 pts.

As I mentioned earlier, your participation and attitude toward this class could make some difference in your final grade.

Extra Credit:

For a suitable bribe (I've found that baked goods work best), I can be persuaded to offer extra-credit assignments BUT you must take int account two things:
1. You must give me an excellent reason why you should be allowed to make up points.
2. You must expect to work twice as hard as normal for the same amount of points. In other words, you will have to do twice as much work to earn extra credit points as points earned through regular assignments.
