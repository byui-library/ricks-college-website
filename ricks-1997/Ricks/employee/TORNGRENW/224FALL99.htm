2. Reading assignments/journal entry (10 points per assignment = 270 points)
 

READING ASSIGNMENT/JOURNAL ENTRY You will note the reading assignment on the attached paper; you are to have done for class the reading indicated next to the date that class is held. From your reading you will keep a journal and answer the following five questions, (1) Who are the people (groups, individuals, civilizations, societies) being dealt with in this reading block, (2) What time frame is being talked about, the years B.C. or A.D. etc.?(3) What principles or doctrines are being taught or discussed in this reading block? (4) How does(has/is) a prayerful and sincere study of these points of doctrine or principles affect my testimony? (5) How is (can/has/does) this study of these doctrines or principles affecting your life? At the beginning of each class you will show your group leader your completed assignment . This will be recorded on your group record sheet. On the designated days listed on the reading assignment sheet you will discuss with your journal group "important items" you select from your journal. .One week prior to mid-semester, and one week prior to the final class period your group will review your journal to see that you have done the required work and to grade your journal accordingly.
 

a. Each journal entry will be worth 3 points, 2 point for quality and 1 point for quantity.
 

b. Entries to receive full credit must be done by the date they are due. You can submit late journal entries to your group for their approval and receive ½ credit, but to receive credit for mid-semester grades, this must be done prior to or at the mid-term journal checks. To receive credit to be added to your final grade all make up must be submitted prior to or at the final journal check.
 

c. To receive full credit each entry must be considered by the group to be well thought-out and to have required some effort; consistent "one liners" will not do.

3. Three Exams (100 points each = 300 points possible)
 

All exams will be administered in the testing center at the posted times on your syllabus. The only time an exam may be taken other than the posted time is if arrangements have been made with me prior to the opening of the exam.. Any exam taken after the posted closing date in the testing center will be a "late take" and will be charged the required "late take fee." There will also be a penalty of a minus 20 points assessed on any test taken after the closing date.
 

4. Daily quizzes-- (27 @ 5 pts = 135 pts)

There will be a 5 question quiz worth 1 point for each question, given at the beginning of class each day, the quizzes will be over the material that should have been read for class that day. The quizzes must be taken in class on the assigned day, there will be no make up quizzes for quizzes missed due to absences. For quiz scores to count the student must be in class the entire class period.
 

5. Personal Projects (100 points Possible)

The personal project may be one of two types:

a. A scholarly project in which you research some doctrine or topic related to our course of study that would significantly increase your personal knowledge; or
 

b. A personal improvement project where you really work on one of those areas of spiritual growth you feel in need of, such as making or breaking a certain habit, increasing effectiveness of prayer, becoming more charitable, developing more self-control, etc. This type project should result in your becoming a more Christ-like person.
 

By the THIRD class period you should turn in a one page, somewhat detailed explanation of what your are going to do, and how your are going to do it. (20 pts.) At mid-semester you should turn in a progress report of one paragraph telling how things are going.(20 pts) One week prior to the last class you should turn in a one page summary/evaluation of your project.(60 pts.)
 
 
 

6. Article review and Research Paper

A. Article Review (50 points)

Your article review will be worth (50 points). It should be at least one type written page. It will be graded on your understanding of the article and your clearly articulated appraisal of it.
 

B. Research Paper (100 points)

Research an event, topic, or doctrine, to be studied in this semesters course of study.

Documentation for all materials used

Submit at least 5 pages, double spaced, (12pt font, std margins) The five pages does not include the cover page or bibliography.

a. Well thought out thesis statement 10 pts

b. Outline 10 pts

c. Format, construction and organization 25 pts

d. Apparent understanding of support

materials as demonstrated in the paper 35 pts

e. Conclusion 10 pts

f. Bibliography/works sited sheet 10 pts

Total Possible 100 pts
