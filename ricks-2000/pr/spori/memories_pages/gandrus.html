Gloria Andrus (64)

I first enrolled as a full-time student at Ricks College for the Fall Semester 1963 when I was 27 years old. I drove to and from Rexburg each day with three other students who helped pay the gas for my little red Volkswagen.

This was the time of the great building boom after the proposal to move Ricks College to Idaho Falls had been squashed. That fall, dedicatory services were held for eight new buildings, and the “old” Administration Building was named in honor of Jacob Spori.

At that time Home Economics classes were taught on the third floor of the Spori. I had a nutrition class from Sister Cleda Auger and a clothing class from Sister Helen Lamprecht.

However, my most vivid memory of the Spori is tied to a beautiful warm fall day--November 22, 1963. It was in the middle of the afternoon. My classes were over, but I was waiting to meet my riders several hours later. Studying and dozing in an empty classroom on the first floor in the southeast corner of the Spori, life was good. Suddenly, some person came running through the building shouting, ‘The President has been shot! The President has been shot!’

People began pouring out of classrooms and offices; radios were turned on and tuned to receive the latest information. Finally, the announcer related that President John F. Kennedy had died in Dallas, Texas. Being a staunch Democrat, I was devastated.

Even after all these years, when November 22 rolls around and news reporters mention the Kennedy assassination, I am transported in memory back to the Spori.”

Gloria Andrus (64)
Rexburg, Idaho
