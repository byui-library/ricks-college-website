Journal Assignment Guidelines

REQUIREMENTS

Reading Lab students are required to keep a journal with an entry for each tutorial session. Each journal entry is worth 5 points. The total points possible will vary according to the number of scheduled tutorial sessions. For example if you are scheduled for 30 sessions (1.0 credits) your journal would be worth 150 points.  If you are scheduled for 15 sessions ( .5 credits) your journal would be worth 75 points. Tutors may choose to use the last five minutes of each session for journal writing, or you may be required to complete your journal as homework.

 

RATIONALE

Because many students need practice in formulating complete sentences, journal writing is an excellent exercise. Expressing thoughts in complete sentences will benefit students in these ways:

1.                  Attention is focused on major concepts and ideas learned in the tutorial.

2.                  Provides practice in expressing thoughts in complete sentences. (This is a good start on writing summaries for E-211 or writing essay test answers.)

3.                  Writing in complete sentences promotes and strengthens language development. It also provides the tutor and student an opportunity to concentrate on correct spelling and word usage.

4.                  Journal responses demonstrate reading comprehension.

 

GENERAL GUIDELINES

(2 points) The first paragraph should be a summary of what was accomplished in the tutorial. Students should review subjects and material covered.  They may list new concepts studied and should include explanations and definitions.

(3 points) The second paragraph should include students thoughts and feelings about the session and the material covered. They should react to what they have read, seen, and felt, relating new ideas to their own life. New knowledge and insight gained should be explored. Emotions and feelings generated during the session should be shared.

 

JOURNAL VARIATIONS

Personalize your journal. Here are some suggestions on how you can do a good job and earn all of the possible points.

- Tutor can give student an open ended question to answer about the session.

- Student can respond to a statement or phrase in the reading.

- Student can relate how they felt after learning a new reading strategy.

- Students can incorporate vocabulary words into their own creative story.

- Your first and last journal entries can be exercises that can be compared to one another        so your improvement can be observed. Ex. First entry-”How your grandmother would        introduce you to her friends”. Last entry-”How you would introduce yourself to your          FHE group”.
