Tonight

I sit tonight
back against concrete foundation
of our house housing but three of us
while you and the other three
are away on an outing
designed to serve the needs of your mother,
but which, I suspect, is serving
deeper needs of your own:
needs unmet, unmeetable by me.

Before tonight,
I would have ached
to read these words
so comfortably penned just now.
They would have seemed
a confession of inadequacy
impotence,
of perhaps, insensitivity.

But tonight they confess
nothing like these,
only the truth about
this man and you,
a woman needing perhaps
what no man can give.

What is this insecurity
muscling it's way between us-
between me and you,
between me and myself-
against presumption that all
you would need, all you could need,
could be given by me?

What kind of jealousy
held my heart chained
the more I tried to be
everything for you,
while forced somewhere nameless
to admit
I was becoming less-so
for all the effort?

Where did I take on
the responsibility,
the weight of trying to make you
happy so you could
in turn
make me happy
the way
I wanted
to be made
happy?

Too heavy to bear
this responsibility,
this priesthood purpose
fashioned with,
but outside,
the bounds
of holy covenant!

Too heavy
this bulky obligation
stacked upon my shoulders
yoking me intently
to promises made
more sure as mortal vision
clouded words of doctrine, verse,
council, and direction
in ways darkly pressing me into
dutiful service while
disguising silent irritability
and quiet turmoil
by gestures absent of abuse
in an effort to practice patience,
long suffering,
and love unfeigned.

Too heavy
this unwhole and fragmenting priesthood,
undivine yet fashioned in the image
of a holier order that must wield the power
to whisper
Amen
to 19 years of weighed hours
spent in pleasantries of rude determination
to lead, cajole,
persuade, enforce,
masquerading through outward acts
of kindness and gentleness
while a strange sentiment of sacrifice
built altars to a being who learned
too well
how disguise pouting
resentment, accusation, and bitterness.

Too heavy to bear
born for too long
not to feel,
and, feeling,
not to doubt,
and, doubting,
not to know.

Tonight
the evening breeze blows gently,
invisibly,
through the stirring of trees
teasing the tickle in my hair.
You are miles from me,
from this house,
from my thoughts,
from all I have been ignorant of,
but from which you mounded a past defense
behind which to keep safe enough.

From this distance
I see
more clearly,
I think,
how past priesthood intentions
must give way
to a holier order,
an order true to the divine
within both of us,
pursued by myself first
that I may be tutored by grace
in order to live
more gracefully with you.

David L. Ward
(Org. July 18, 1994; rewritten October 12, 1999)
