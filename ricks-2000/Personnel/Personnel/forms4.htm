Instructions

1. STATEMENT OF POSITION PURPOSE

The position purpose is a brief, undetailed, but specific statement of the major end result expected of the job. This statement explains why the position exists in the organization and should clearly distinguish the job from all others. In particular, it should differentiate the job from the supervisor’s job, from reporting jobs, and from different jobs at the same level.

The statement should answer the questions:

  • What is the incumbent paid for achieving?
  • What part of the company’s total objectives does the incumbent accomplish in the position?
  • What wouldn’t get done in the company if the position didn’t exist?

The statement of a position purpose should contain the following elements:

  • Action Verb(s) -- HOW the function is performed.
  • Function -- WHAT activity, function, area, department, etc., is affected by the position.
  • End Result -- WHY the function is performed.

Example: Vice President of Operations: Plans and directs overall operations of the company to ensure the timely and cost efficient manufacture and delivery of products and services.


2. DIMENSIONS

This section of the description is a listing of statistics which indicate measurable areas upon which the position has a direct or indirect impact. It should include all relevant statistics which give a clear sense of the job’s impact or contribution to the organization.

Three dimensions should be described in the position description:

  • Dollar Dimensions -- Any dollars for which the position has a direct or indirect impact, including revenues, costs, expense items, assets, etc.
  • Personnel Dimensions -- Any measure of the scope of the job in supervising the work of others. This includes the number of exempt and non-exempt workers that are either directly or indirectly supervised.
  • Other Pertinent Dimensions -- Any quantitative date which helps measure or describe the scope of job duties, such as equipment used, production volumes, etc.


3. NATURE AND SCOPE

As the major narrative portion to the job description, the nature and scope section represents the heart of the description. This section tells what the job is all about, how it fits into the organization, and how it relates to superior, peer, and subordinate positions. It describes the environment or "climate" within which the job operates, identifies the job’s major challenges, and the significant roles the job plays in the organization.

Before writing the narrative ask yourself:

  • Why does the job exist?
  • What is it really supposed to accomplish?
  • What obstacles have to be overcome?
  • What should the reader know about this job to understand its nature and complexity?
  • What makes this job different from others that might initially appear to be similar?


Topics to be included in the Nature and Scope

Location: This section includes the position to which the incumbent reports, other positions reporting to the same supervisor, and immediate subordinate positions.

Narrative explanation: This section describes the overall arena, climate, or culture within which the job operates and includes relevant internal and external influences. The facts, things, and issues included here directly influence, affect, or describe the performance of the job duties. The reader must be able to discern the relationship between the influences and the job’s end results. It includes the frequent contacts and reasons for those contacts and major committee assignments.

Problem Solving and Thinking Challenges: This segment describes the kinds of thinking challenges and problems the job exists to solve, including a description of their complexity and how they are in fact solved. The problems and challenges should be described at the highest level normally encountered by the job and should only include problems and challenges the incumbent actually encounters or is expected to solve.

The job’s thinking environment is guided by goals, policies, and practices. Thinking challenge, on the other hand, is the complexity of the thought process required of the job holder. It includes the authority and freedom the incumbent has to take action or make decisions and under what circumstances.

Knowledge, Skills, and Experience: This section describes the specific background, schooling, and/or years of experience in a specific discipline required to perform the job effectively. The knowledge, skills, and experience must show a clear job relationship.

Principal Accountabilities: Principal accountabilities are statements of the important end results which the job exists to achieve. Each end result, or accomplishment area should support the statement of Position Purpose and parallel the information contained in the Nature and Scope section of the position description. The statement should be action oriented descriptions of what the accountability is and why it is critical to the job’s success from the organizations viewpoint. It should identify an outcome or end result of such significance that effectiveness in achieving it, or contributing to its achievement would be considered an element in evaluating the incumbent’s performance. For most jobs, accountabilities should number about four to eight. Not all accountabilities have equal weight; therefore, accountabilities should be listed in priority order to help put the job into perspective and include the percent of time spent on each accountability.


Principal Accountabilities are:

  • A basic end result expected from the position under consideration.
  • Related to the job and not the incumbent.
  • Accurately reflect the position’s impact, role, and/or authority regarding the accomplishment of the end result.
  • Stated in such a way that allows performance to be measured objectively, in quantifiable terms whenever possible.


4. ORGANIZATION CHART

Complete a chart to show where the job fits within the organization.


5. REQUEST FOR REEVALUATION OF POSITION DESCRIPTION

For re-evaluation of an existing position, rewrite position and turn in to Personnel office.

Job Title: Cook I Position Code: 000
Incumbent: John Smith [ ] New [X] Revised
Supervisor: John Doe Origin Date: August 1, 1978
Department: Food Services Revision Date: September 15, 1999

 
Position Purpose:

This position has as its principle responsibility the preparation and cooking of food, and general cleaning.


Dimensions:

 

Direct Impact

Indirect Impact
Annual Operating Budget: none none
Annual Payroll: none none
Personnel Supervised: none 2 students
Other:    


Nature and Scope:

Reports to: John Doe

As does/do: Cook II, Cook III, Baker, Secretary

Reporting Directly to this position: none

Narrative explanation: This position has as its principle responsibility the preparation and cooking of food, and general cleaning. Specific duties include the following:

  • Help in the preparation of food items for cooking i.e. cooking, dicing, mincing, and cutting of various vegetables and meats.
  • Help prepare various dishes for other operations on the premises.
  • Assists in the putting away foods after each meal, and the closing of the kitchen after the evening meal.
  • Checks to make sure all equipment is off and empty of food items, after use.
  • Will maintain safety and sanitation requirements, including personal hygiene.
  • Responsible for making sure that all work areas are kept clean, and steam jackets are clean at all times.


Problem Solving and Thinking Challenges:

  • Be able to get along with others.
  • Use common sense.
  • Must be able to read and follow recipes.
  • Must be willing to learn proper functions and use of each piece of equipment and the utensils.


Decisions and Issues Referred to a Supervisor or Other Individual:

Decisions and issues regarding budgetary concerns and personnel problems should be referred to the supervisor.


Knowledge, Skills, and Experience:

No experience necessary, but is willing to learn and grasp new ideas.


Principal Accountabilities:

80% 1. Preparation and cooking of food.
15% 2. General cleaning.
5% 3. Other duties as assigned by supervisor.
