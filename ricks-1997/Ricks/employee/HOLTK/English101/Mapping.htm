MAPPING ASSIGNMENT

DATE DUE __________________

A map is a visual representation of written information. The primary purpose of mapping is to demonstrate the relationship of major and minor supporting details to the topic and main idea. A map can be used before reading has begun to introduce a topic; creating a map after a brief survey of the information is an excellent activity for accessing prior knowledge, previewing material, and preparing to read. A map created after active reading (which includes previewing, marking, and making flashcards) provides a visual image of the chapter that can help a student review for assignments, prepare for class discussions, and study for exams.

STEPS TO CREATING A MAP

1. SURVEY. Preview the reading. Is a framework for a map suggested?

2. PREDICT A FRAMEWORK. Draw a rough draft representation. Survey again to see how the text will fit. Would it be best to use a spider map, a fishbone map, a network tree, a cycle map, etc.? (Refer to the attached Graphic Forms handout.) Access your prior knowledge. What do you already know about the topic? What do you want to know?

3. READ with a purpose. As you read, mentally fill in the gaps in your map. Identify the topic, the main idea, and major supporting details. Will the information fit the framework you predicted? Adjust your rough draft.

4. MAP. Fill in the map you predicted in step #2. Center your supporting details around the main idea or topic. Label your map with class, text, and chapter. Include a key for symbols and colors.

5. SUMMARIZE. Review your map, then write a 1-2 paragraph summary of the information in your map. (Do not write a summary of your mapping experience, rather summarize the information from your chapter that is contained in your map.)
