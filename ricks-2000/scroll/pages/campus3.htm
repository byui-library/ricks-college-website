BUSINESS EXPO LETS STUDENTS WITNESS CHALLENGES OF BUSINESS

by LYNZI HALE
city editor

Twenty-seven students will get the opportunity to travel to New York, Boston and London as part of a hands-on learning business tour.

The Business Expedition Tour allows students to find out what it really takes to run a business. Students who attend the eight-week summer term will spend five weeks studying subjects, ranging from business law and college writing to Doctrine and Covenants and FA 100, and three weeks getting hands-on business experience. they can earn up to 11 credits.

The tour's three advisers, Kirk Gifford, Karen Holt and Joshua Holt, will teach the lessons to coordinate with what the other classes are learning. Because this is a group tour, the students will primarily have most of their classes together.

Following the five weeks in the classroom, the students will travel to New York, where they will spend five days observing the New York Stock Exchange and talking with top executives from companies such as Macy's and Morgan Stanley Dean Whittier.

Students not only get the chance to observe business in action, but also have the opportunity to tour Church historical sites, attend a Broadway show and see other cultural events to fill the FA 100 requirements.

After the week in New York, the students will travel to Boston, where they will speak with a representative from Harvard Business School and visit literary sites such as Walden Pond and Concord, Mass.

"Can't you just envision sitting out there on Concord Square and reading a story about where you're at? I think it is going to be an awesome experience for our students," Holt said.

Following the week in Boston, the group will head to London to learn about international business and trade. They will visit cultural locales, including the Shakespeare and more Church history sites.

When the students return from the trip they will have 2-3 days to e-mail a paper for a final grade to one of the advisers about what they learned.

The cost of the tour is $2,600, which includes travel costs, event fees, lodging costs and some meals. Other expenses include the cost of credits, a $100 deposit and the cost of some food and personal items.

Interested students can pick up a brochure at the Business Management Department office in Smith Room 231. A few tour spots are still available.
