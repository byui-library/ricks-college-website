English 111C: Curriculum Schedule

What follows is a day-by-day breakdown of concepts taught throughout the semester, and assignments given.  I have made no attempt to rewrite them for any particular audience.  Instead this is a working copy of my planning of and preparation for this course as I learn what my students know, and discern what they need to be taught, to experience the mind and heart changing effect of insight born of a genuine education.

A Taste of Reading to Make Connections

Read the first two paragraphs of "First Thoughts" with students. Emphasize the need for students to read and reread to come to an appreciation of the concepts and spirit of Goldberg's ideas.

Demonstrate the power of reading when one is reminded of something another person said that augments and expands upon a particular statement read. Use Joseph Smith's statement of "The Spirit of Revelation" in connection with Goldberg's thought of "the way the mind first flashes on something."

Take students through the procedure of inserting a footnote to make an editorial comment about what Goldberg has said, and to show how one can integrate one's paraphrase of what another author said (Joseph Smith, in this case,) with a direct quotation from the source.

Finish reading Goldberg's second paragraph.

Drop off first draft of "Error" Assignment in Jupiter G drive

Assignment:

Read and reread the article "First Thoughts." Work through this article until Goldberg's language and images about writing are vivid in your mind-and until you can feel their significance to you. Then use Goldberg's suggestions during the writing and the rewriting you are doing while responding to "The Role of Error in This Course." In doing so, reread what you've written to discover statements that represent writing what you're supposed to write and think. Goldberg calls these "second thoughts," "1 + 1 = 2 logic." Rewrite these into what you need to write-the "truth," as Goldberg calls it.


January 14:

Sharing Written Introductions:

Download Palmer’s "Space and Hospitality." G:\shared\wardd\note\space and hospitality. Send to A:\

Dropbox: Second draft of response to "Role of Error."

Read "Space and Hospitality" together

Invite students to choose someone in class with whom to exchange their personal introductions. Have both get together and read each others' introductions to each other-making sure they understand what was written, and how to read it in an interesting way. Have students listen carefully to what they would change if they were to rewrite it. Make change to glaring mistakes, otherwise leave into as written. Then invite each student to stand at the head of the classroom, indicate whose introduction they are reading, and read it. At the end of the personal introduction, invite the readers to ask the class if they have any questions of the person who wrote the introduction. Then proceed to share the quotation and the reason for citing it.

Note: Sharing these personal introductions will most likely take two class periods. In the meantime, assign students to begin working on the next written response having to do with "The Role of Error in This Course."

Assignment:

Read and reread "Space and Hospitality" for Monday. Choose a text-unit you feel impressed by, and write a third of a page, single-spaced, response to it. Remember to draft in the way Goldberg directs. Be real in your effort to understand what you need to understand about yourself in order to contribute to "opening the learning space" in our class.

January 24

First Principle of Audience: Rewriting to Discover and Own Personal Thought.

Dropbox: Best draft of Response to "Creating a Learning Space."

Open "Writing and the Hero Path." Walk students through the format of the response, before reading it together.

Read with students "Writing and Hero Path." Invite students to discuss what Palmer may call impediments to opening up a learning space within the white paper or screen facing a student.

Share the Principle of Audience. Help students to relate to the need for their writing and rewriting to discover insight into a problem that matters to them. Otherwise they are simply generating information that may be somewhat interesting, but is insignificant and trivial.

In connection with this, look over "Slaughtering Impulses" and "Self-Reliance."

Assignment:

Read and reread "Writing and the Hero Path." Choose some excerpt from it as a text-unit, and write your own discovery about breaking free (or the attempt to break free) of writing in the way you’ve been taught, or assumed writing to be. In doing so, integrate references from "Slaughtering Impulses," and Emerson’s "Self-Reliance"–even the Principle of Audience, if you want.
