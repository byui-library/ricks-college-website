Course Syllabus
Automatic Transmissions - Auto 265

Instructor: Staff this fall
Text: Brejcha & Tuuri, Automatic Transmissions & Transaxles, 4th edition, Delmar, 1997

Automatic Transmission Repair is one of the most challenging and rewarding areas of work in the automotive field. There is a high demand for good transmission technicians, and pay is above average for those that can repair them.

It is the purpose of this course to give you an understanding of the theory and operation of automatic transmissions used in today’s automobiles. They are becoming increasingly sophisticated and complex, and it is important that you understand the basic principles of operation and diagnosis required to service and repair them. There is a tremendous amount of material to cover in a very short time (though it seems like forever at this point), and you must stay current on your assignments or you will rapidly become lost.

As you review your Daily Schedule you will know what assignments are due at what times. The questions at the end of each chapter are to be turned in at the beginning of class on the day that chapter is to be discussed in class. Any late assignments will be given a maximum of ½ credit, so get them in on time! Tests to be given are listed on the daily schedule. If you miss a test, you can not make it up! If you have to be gone, arrange to take it early.

Before any work is started, the job MUST BE APPROVED BY THE INSTRUCTOR! If you want to do a transmission for someone, talk to me before you commit yourself. Some transmissions are much more difficult than others, and I don’t want you getting in over your head and not getting it finished up before the end of the semester. We don’t give incompletes, and any job not completed by the end of the class does not warrant a passing grade!

Grades for the semester will be assigned as follows:
                Written Assignments/Tests    40%
                Lab Work                            40%
                Attendance                          10%
                Attitude/Desire                     10%

The good news: you get to do a class presentation. If you look toward the end of the Daily Schedule you will see where students will be making presentations on various types of automatic transmissions. You need to choose which you would like to do and let me know as soon as possible. Each transmission will only be covered by one person, so if you are slow, someone else may get the one you would like. If you don’t let me know by mid-term I will assign you one.

You need to still do professional articles. Hand them in each Monday, beginning Sept. 10th. Again, they must be either xeroxed or hand written. No originals!

Our classes are really full this year, so clean up on time, get out on time, and don’t ask to work outside of the regularly scheduled lab period. Because the transmission lab is also used for other classes, benches must be kept neat and tidy. Keep the floors, walls, and equipment clean and free of spilled oil or parts. Make sure any non-transmission parts (drive shafts, tires, axles, etc) are kept in the car or some other secure place. You don’t want to buy lost parts for the customer! ;

It is the student’s responsibility to disclose to the teacher any special and/or learning disabilities he/she may have before the first week of class is over.

If you have concerns or questions about this class, feel free to discuss them with me. My office is Austin 126, and I would be happy to sit down with you at our mutual convenience to help you any way I can. Have a great semester!
