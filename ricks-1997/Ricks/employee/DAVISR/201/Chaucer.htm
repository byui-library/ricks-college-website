CHAUCER 1340? - 1400

(Dante writes INFERNO in 1310)

- born into a well-to-do bourgeois family

- trained as a page, so two choices:

       - go to university (church)

       - work in the household of a rich person (who would continue your education (Chaucer chooses this)

- marries Philippa, daughter of a knight and lady in waiting to Queen of Edward III

- forms connections with John of Gaunt, who becomes his patron

- at 19 is sent to France, where he is taken prisoner

       - ransomed

- appointed by king to several offices

       - given grants of land, money, house, wine, etc.

- buried in Westminster abbey

His Work

- probably inspired to write poetry while in France

       - poetry to a lover, never to your wife

       - love is "the most beautiful of all absolute disasters"

- you are to never betray your lover (but you can your wife)

- he starts a trend that will last over 200 years: hold office so you will have time and money to write

- his reading public was small

       - press not invented for another 60 years

- illiterate public (even in 1533 - 150 yrs later - only 4 in 10 can read at all.

The Tales . . .

30 pilgrims

17,000 lines - unfinished

original plan was for 120 stories/only did 22

 

 

St. Thomas a Becket 1118-1170

- came from poor family

- sent to be raised by the bishop of Canterbury

- finally he is appointed bishop by king (friend since birth)

       - doesn't want it

       - knows they will fight

       - king insists

- Becket tries to get back church lands taken over by king/fight

- insists deacons be tried in church, not civil courts

- long duel, finally forced to flee to France

       returns with papal support

- feud starts again

- group of lords murder him in church

- public outcry

       king forced to do penance in public

- canonized 3 yrs later 1173

- site of annual pilgrimage until 1538 when Henry VIII destroys all remnants of gothic/medieval era
