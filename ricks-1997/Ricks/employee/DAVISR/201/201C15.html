Chapter 15

Music and its beginnings

600 Gregorian Chant

- most typical church music

    • single, unadorned melody is "official" (liturgical)
    • a cappella
    • no rhythm, no division into accents

staff invented in 900’s (had only 4 lines)

Mass

    • church service began to expand
    • tropes added (unauthorized text added between standard words)
      Our Father
       who protects us from all evil (trope)
      Who Art in Heaven
       which is the greatest of all Thy creations (trope)

Sequence

    • that which follows
    • additional music after the mass
    • is the start of true music
      (will be abolished by Council of Trent/Reformation)

Goliards - hippies (rebels, artists, disenchanted students, etc.)

Jongleurs - same, but of even lower class

Troubadours and Trouveres - educated aristocrats who composed love poetry and songs

Composition advances:

Chant – single melody

Organum – same as above with added parallel line

Cantus Firmus – single bass line (or tenor) to "hold" it together

tenor is "to hold" or restrain (tenir)

motet – first true hymn.
sacred music with non-scriptural text
