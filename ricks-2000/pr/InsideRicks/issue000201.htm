RICKS COLLEGE RECEIVES BEAUTIFICATION AWARD

Ricks College was presented a Beautification Award from the Rexburg Chamber of Commerce for its recent work on the Taylor Quad and adjacent buildings. The award was given at the annual Farmer-Merchant Banquet held in the Manwaring Center on January 27.

The college has neared completion of the largest landscaping project ever undertaken on campus involving a new pedestrian quad and two new buildings. The formal-looking Taylor Quad has become a new center of activity on campus. It is surrounded by the Spencer W. Kimball Student and Administrative Services Building, the John Taylor Building, and the Hyrum Manwaring Student Center. "We have endeavored to create a new heart of campus - the social hub centered in the student center, the spiritual hub in the Taylor, and the administrative hub in the Kimball," said President David A. Bednar.

Viking Drive once cut through the area, but was closed because of safety concerns. (It was estimated there would be about 20,000 pedestrian crossings of the street on a daily basis.) Today, a person can walk between the major academic buildings on campus without crossing a street. "We've taken a problem area and created a place of beauty and safety," said Chuck Frost, director of Physical Plant.

The large quad is constructed of brick and cement walkways and grassy areas, and features extensive landscaping with more than 200 large trees (3 inches or larger in trunk diameter), 84,000 square feet of sod, and several hundred tons of landscaping rock. The night lighting for the quad was designed to reflect off of the trees from ground-mounted lights. The quad also offers a beautiful view of the Upper Snake River Valley to the west. It was designed by the Salt Lake City architectural firm of FFKR - the company that also designed the Taylor and Kimball buildings - and was built by Pacific West of Idaho Falls. Landscaping work was done by Ricks College employees and students. The college has reserved a central place in the quad for some type of sculptured piece in the future.

WINNERS AT BUILDERS CONVENTION

The Ricks College Architecture and Construction Department participated in the International Builders' Show in Dallas in January. Brian Blaylock won the "Outstanding Junior Faculty Award." His students add a personal insight to Blaylock's impact as a teacher. "He cares about his students, where they're going, what they're doing. He just wants us to succeed," said sophomore Austin Cope from Salem, Utah.

Ricks students also earned honors at the convention. There were numerous hands-on opportunities for the estimated 75,000 participants who attended the convention. One of the events entered by the 31 Ricks students was a team competition on a situational construction problem. A Ricks team of five students won fifth place for their efforts of modifying a house plan for an addition, drawing the design, and calculating the exact materials and cost. The students were Austin Cope; Darren Wood, a sophomore from Sarnia, Ontario, Canada; Kathryn Zachreson, a sophomore from Libby, Mont.; Matt Gregston, an alumnus of Ricks from Idaho Falls; and Brett Munden, a sophomore from Lindon, Utah.

MULTICULTURAL WEEK

The Associated Students of Ricks College are sponsoring Multicultural Week. Numerous activities provide students with opportunities to get a better understanding for different cultures. Feelings of unity are sought so there will be "no more 'ites' among us," said Kevin Miyasaki, assistant dean of students, referring to scriptural reference of 4 Nephi 1:17. Displays in the lower lobby of the Manwaring Center represent various cultures. Diversity Training Workshops will be presented by Miyasaki and Moses Diaz and on Thursday afternoon in MC room 318. These workshops are designed to make students more appreciative of cultural diversity and to also recognize personal barriers which may be restricting their enjoyment of other cultures.

The finale of the week is the Multicultural Festival on Friday with an opportunity to sample food from various countries in the MC East Ballroom at 6 p.m. A program in the Kirkham Auditorium will begin at 8 p.m. with song and dance performances will be by the Utah band, Colors; the Contemporary Dance Team; the American Folk Dancers; the Ricks multi-cultural choir; and Showtime Company. Tickets to both the food festival and program are available through the Ticket Office (208) 356-1130.

"Even though we recognize and appreciate diversity just by being members of the Church, we are united; and that's what brings us together," said Emily Eyring, ASRC academic vice president.
