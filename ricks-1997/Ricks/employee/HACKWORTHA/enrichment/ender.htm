Ender's Game

Orson Scott Card

 

 I just finished Card's Speaker for the Dead.  Earlier this semester I read Ender's Game and Seventh Son.  I also bought Saints when Card was here, but I not sure if I will start that book right now.

I would like to read Xenocide, but that book is available only in hardback at $20.00.  I have not decided yet if I want to spend the money or wait and buy the book when it comes out in paperback.

Ender and Speaker were worth my time.  It was a strange experience to live in these two worlds which were structures and mirrors (rooms and forests) of the mind of Scott Card.

We cannot connect electronically with the nerve / brain cells of another human, but we can partially connect to articulate individuals who have the skills to transfer their thoughts to text. Experiencing Scott Card's mind through these books has been rewarding, expanding, and entertaining.

I like the idea of connection, of communi- cation, of information management and sharing.  I like the idea that through community we gain strength and growth. Through collective efforts we gain intelligence and knowledge.

Our intelligence is limited in isolation.  Isolation is a condition where information is not shared;  it is not just a condition where one lives alone like a hermit.   Books, dialogue with others, the transfer of thoughts (information) removes one from isolation.  A society which actively and appropriately shares information moves individually and collectively towards intelligence.

It is intriguing to consider what is valuable about humans.  And when does one become human?  What is the worth of a human or what is it that we should value?  Is it intelligence?  Does intelligence provide value?  As intelligence grows within an organism, within some body, does that creature become more precious and worthy to be preserved?  Is the type, quality, dimensions of a body less significant than pure intelligence?

We value a human even though that human may be a stump (Miro type).  Yet we value or at least we are more impressed with stumps that are intelligent.  Additionally, the most valued stump would be an intelligent person who also is kind, loving, sensitive to others, and altruistic.

Would Christ's life have been less significant if he had had a peg leg, a withered arm, a fat stomach, and weak eyes?  No, although some may have been put off by his appearance.  Would Christ's life have been less significant if he would have been a moron?  Yes.  His powers would have been diminished.
