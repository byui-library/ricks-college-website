Background information
about future of Spori Building
June 1, 1999

The Jacob Spori Building had been a major concern to college leaders for several decades. Determining the appropriate course of action was particularly challenging because of the historic value of the building, and much time and effort was expended in trying to find ways to preserve and/or restore the facility. After extensive research and deliberation, it was determined that restoring the building was not a feasible option.

The building is constructed of rhyolite, a soft stone that was taken from a nearby quarry. Though it was built to the standard of the early 1900s, it does not meet today's building criteria. Any renovations done to the Spori Building would need to meet current building codes. Consequently, the entire interior of the building would have to have been removed during a renovation, including the roof, floors, interior walls, and electrical, mechanical and plumbing systems. Only the outer wall of rhyolite stone, which is eight inches thick, would potentially have remained, and preserving it would have presented its own unacceptable risks and challenges.

Ricks officials selected the architectural firm of FFKR of Salt Lake City to design the new building because they believe it has the vision and capability to design a building that will reflect the heritage and legacy of the aging building. Ricks officials were impressed with FFKR's extensive expertise in historical work.

Currently, FFKR is designing a replacement of the LDS Church's historic Nauvoo Temple in Illinois. It also did the designs for the conversion of the Vernal (Utah) Tabernacle into an LDS temple, the conversion of the Hotel Utah into the Joseph Smith Memorial Building in Salt Lake City and the restoration of the Noyes Administration Building at Snow College in Ephraim, Utah.

FFKR also designed both of Ricks College's most recent buildings; the Spencer W. Kimball Student and Administrative Services Building and the John Taylor Religion Building.

The design process will take the full calendar year of 2000 with construction beginning as soon as the weather breaks in 2001. It is estimated that the new building will take from 20 to 24 months to complete. The Spori Building will be vacated for the fall semester of 2000 and classes will be redistributed to other locations.

Construction of the current Spori Building started in the spring of 1900 and was completed in the fall of 1903. It was the first permanent building on the Ricks College campus and is named after Jacob Spori, the first principal of the Bannock Stake Academy (the forerunner of Ricks College). The building currently houses the Communication and Art Departments; along with KRIC and KWBH radio stations, Graphic Services; and the campus newspaper, the Scroll.
