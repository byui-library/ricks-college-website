British Literature - Neoclassic and Romantic
BRITLIT/NEO&RM
CLASS CODE: ENG 332 CREDITS:  3
DIVISION: LANGUAGE & LETTERS
DEPARTMENT: ENGLISH
GENERAL EDUCATION: This course fulfills a General Education - Letters requirement.
CATALOG DESCRIPTION: Examines the influence of historical events, philosophical ideas, and literary trends on works and authors of the British Neoclassic and Romantic periods from Dryden to Keats.
DESCRIPTION: Examines the influence of historical events, philosophical ideas, and literary trends on works and authors of the British Neoclassic and Romantic periods periods from Dryden to Keats.
TOPICS: Development of the Neo-classic and Romantic periods, including its major movements, genres, and authors; examination of how these two historical periods and types of literature interrelate; use of the fundamental critical and theoretical interpretation in the discussion, analysis, and evaluation of literature of these periods.
OBJECTIVES: (1) Knowing and using the fundamental tools and vocabulary of literary study (i.e. symbolic language, characterization, conflict, theme, point of view, form, and style) in the discussion, analysis, and evaluation of literature of these periods.
(2)Identifying (both orally and in writing) the interrelationships of works and ideas within and across these two periods. (3) Using various critical approaches (as introduced in English 251 and English 314) to appreciate and analyze works from different genres and periods. (4) Effectively researching and discussing works of this period using reliable sources and MLA style.

Comprehension--accurate summary, paraphrase, or translation with accurate interpretation.

Application--use of information in a situation different than originally introduced.
Students shall analyze, interpret and appreciate literary devices such as tone, setting, characterization, figurative language, plot, etc.
Students shall support interpretations of literature with specifics.
REQUIREMENTS: Extensive reading. Discussion and oral presentations. Interpretive, analytical, and/or critical evaluation papers. Journals.
