Standards Committee
Ricks College is committed to the preservation of student rights and to fair treatment of all students as a result of any grievance or complaint brought against them.  If the Dean of Students office determines that a student has violated the Honor Code and the appropriate sanction appears to be dismissal, the student will have a right to request a review before the Standards Committee to determine if he/she should be dismissed.
The committee is comprised of a chairperson, three faculty, staff or administrator members, three students, a secretary (nonvoting, and the Dean of Students or his/her representative as an ex-officio member of the committee.  The ex-officio member will serve as a nonvoting advisor to the committee.
The students are selected by the ASRC president and approved by the Student Life Vice President.  Decisions of the committee are by a simple majority, and the committee may impose any sanction which it deems appropriate.
A student who has received a major sanction may within two school days of receiving oral or written notification of that sanction petition the Dean of Students to review the decision of the Standards Committee to determine if there should be mitigation of the sanction imposed.  The Dean of Students will then review the case with the Student Life Vice President.   Consideration for mitigation by the Dean of Students or Vice President will be based on the following:  (a) the student establishes to the satisfaction of the Dean and Vice President that he or she was denied a fair committee meeting; or (b) the student establishes to the satisfaction of the Dean and Vice President that since the committee meeting, new and material evidence has been discovered which, had it been introduced at the committee meeting, would probably have changed the decision.

Student Employment and Career Placement Center
The Student Employment and Career Placement Center are located in the Kimball Building, Room 244, and is open from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., Monday through Friday.
Student Employment:  Students who are seeking employment are assisted in locating both on-campus and off-campus positions (normally part-time).  Candidates are referred for on-campus jobs on the following basis:  1) first-come, first-served, 2) job skills and qualifications, 3) availability to work, 4) existing job openings.  A variety of job openings will occur during the calendar year.  In the fall and winter seasons, on-campus and off-campus jobs are extremely limited.  If you are available during a 2-4 hour block of time between 8 a.m. and 2 p.m. daily, and are willing to accept any type of job, and work the hours required by the job, it will enhance your chances of employment both on or off-campus.  The best opportunity for student employment on-campus, however, is to begin working during one of the summer terms.  Students must be taking at least one credit hour to be eligible for work during the summer terms.
Employment for International Students:  International students are not allowed to work off campus without a work permit.  On campus employment is allowed without such a work permit as long as the campus employer can certify that employing the international student does not displace a U.S. citizen or permanent resident alien student on the job.
See International Students.
Career Placement:  Students or alumni who are looking for full-time careers after graduation or are looking for internships, cooperative work, or summer work related to their careers, will be assisted in our office.  Students are assisted in the following services by the placement center personnel:  effective resume and letter writing, proper interviewing techniques, job searching skills (including networking, Internet, job fairs, employment centers, and personal searches), career researching, and effective follow-through.  Critical analysis of resumes and letters, and career counseling are also provided by our trained personnel.  Call 356-2170 to make an appointment.  It is highly recommended that students begin their job search in the fall or as early as possible.

Student Review Procedure
Students should assume responsibility for identifying and dealing with the appropriate college office in all matters pertaining to their educational experience.  When problems are not satisfactorily resolved at those operational levels, or when the application of regular policies causes an undue hardship because of extraordinary personal circumstances, students may request a review for relief in the following manner:
1.  Academic Regulations.  (Withdrawals, add-drops, credits, probation, suspension, etc.).  Check first with the Registrar's Office and if the problem remains, the student may file a formal petition to the Exception to the Rules Committee, stating their personal situation and reasons for an exception.  Petition forms may be obtained from the Registrar's Office.
2.  Grading Disputes.  The student should first confer with the instructor.  If the explanation is not acceptable and the dispute remains, the student may request a review by the department chairman.  Cases still unresolved should next be referred to the division chairman and finally to the assistant academic vice president.
3.  Behavioral Matters.  (Disciplinary sanctions, probations, dismissal, etc.).  Follow the review procedure outlined in the Honor Code procedures listed in the Standards section of this handbook or contact the Dean of Students Office (Kimball 230) for assistance.
4.  Policies Concerning Housing Units.  Housing (contracts, deposits, refunds, fines, etc.).  Work directly with the apartment managers and Housing Office (Kimball 196).  If not satisfactorily settled, appeal to the assistant vice president of Student Life (Manwaring Center 207).
5.  Financial Services (fees, refunds, loans, dishonored checks, etc.).  Contact the director of Financial Services (Kimball 290) for assistance.  If, because of extraordinary circumstances, the problem persists, the student may appeal to the Dean of Students (Kimball 230).
6.  Other:  Miscellaneous problems occurring outside the categories listed above may be taken to the Dean of Students for direction and assistance on how to proceed.

Telephone Service
Telephone service for students residing in on-campus housing is provided by Ricks College's privately owned telephone system.  Each dorm unit is provided a telephone with local dial tone access.  A Ricks College CALLING CARD gives long distance access from dorm rooms.  This Card may be obtained by signing up at the Cashiers Office, and paying a $5 non-refundable application fee. The Calling Card can be picked up in the Accounting Office the next working day.  A Ricks College Calling Card is an extension of credit by the college to the card holder.  The calling card is to be used ONLY by the person to whom it is issued, all charges made against a card are the legal responsibility of that person.  All Ricks College Calling Cards are the property of Ricks College and will be revoked for fraudulent or abusive use.  Ricks Calling Cards are not available to students living off campus. Students residing in off-campus housing will need to arrange for telephone service through their respective housing unit and through a private long distance carrier.
Other calling cards (i.e., AT&T, Sprint, MCI) can be used at a pay phone.  Overseas international calling is not available on the Ricks College system; however, calls can be placed to Canada, Alaska, or Hawaii.
Any questions you have concerning the Ricks College Telephone system can be directed to: Ricks Telephone Office, RM 129 PPLT, telephone number (208)-356-2011.

Test Week
This is a period of time set apart at the end of each semester for taking final exams.  The day and time of the exam are dependent on the day and the time the class met.  This schedule can be found on the back page of your class schedule.
All finals given in the testing center are arranged by the instructor.  Students should check with their instructors for exact place, dates and closing time of  tests.

Testing Center
Located in the basement of the Library, this center provides a centralized location where examinations are given.  They open at 8 a.m. Monday-Friday with the last test given out at 9 p.m.  The center closes at 10 p.m. each week day; it opens on Saturday at 10 a.m., last test goes out at 3 p.m. and closes at 4 p.m.  Closed for devotionals on Tuesdays and holidays.  The Testing Center closes when classes end.  For example, if classes end at 12:00 noon on a particular day then the last test will go out at 11:00 a.m. and the Center will close at 12:00 noon.  Closing hours for the last day of test week is 5 p.m. (last test given at 4 p.m.).
Closing dates and times are computer controlled and the computer is unforgiving when a student is late.  Testing Services student assistants cannot override dates or times scheduled by the faculty and set in the computer and they cannot be held responsible when students come late and the test cannot be given to them.  Students must work those problems out with their teacher, not Testing Services.
Your student I.D. is required in order to take a test.  Ricks College Dress standards and Honor Code are enforced.  There is an auxiliary test room in the Taylor Religion building, room 130, for all religion and humanities tests, that opens at 3 p.m. and is open until 9:45 p.m. with the last test given at 8:45 p.m. Monday through Friday.

Teton Mountain Student Leadership Institute  (TMSLI)
TMSLI provides three program services for students at Ricks College:  1) Training Retreats, 2) Volunteer Center, and 3) Marriage Enrichment.
Training Retreats
TMSLI sponsors evening workshops, overnight retreats, and
Saturday conferences to help students strengthen their testimonies, develop leadership skills, enhance self-esteem, and plan service projects.  These retreats are held at a scenic lodge overlooking the teton mountains 60 miles Southwest of campus.  All students can participate.  To become involved, visit Manwaring Center 380 to sign-up for upcoming events.  Transportation is provided and programs are at a minimal cost.  Clubs and other campus organizations can also use the lodge facility for training purposes.
Volunteer Center
TMSLI identifies meaningful service opportunities in the community for Ricks College students to participate in.  Service opportunities can be either “one-time” service projects for individual or groups, or “on-going” service opportunities for individual students, such as Adopt-a-grandparent, Special Olympics, Big Brother/Big Sister, middle grade tutoring, etc.
Marriage Enrichment
The Teton Lodge also provide training retreats for newly married couples at Ricks College.  These overnight retreats are taught by  experienced instructors who help couples enhance their marriage.
