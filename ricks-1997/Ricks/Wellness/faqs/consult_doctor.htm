Should I consult a doctor before I start exercising?

Most people do not need to see a doctor before they start since a gradual, sensible exercise program will have minimal health risks. However, there are those who should the advice of a medical professional.

  • if you have been told by a doctor that you have a heart condition, heart murmur, or have had a heart attack
  • if you have frequent pains or pressure- in the left or midchest area, left neck, shoulder or arm- during or right after exercise
  • if you have feelings of faintness, or spells of extreme dizziness
  • if you experience extreme breathlessness after mild exertion
  • if your blood pressure is to high and is not under control
  • if you have bone joint problems such as arthritis
  • if you are over and 60 and not used to physical activity
  • if you have a family history of coronary artery disease
  • if you have a special need medical condition, such as insulin dependant diabetes.

    • List was developed by the British Columbia Ministry of Health, Department of National Health and Welfare, Canada.
