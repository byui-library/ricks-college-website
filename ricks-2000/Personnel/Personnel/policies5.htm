Professional Development Leave - Faculty (5-1)

I. PURPOSE

To encourage better future performance of faculty members by providing leave with pay under specified conditions.

II. OBJECTIVES OF PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT LEAVES

Professional development leave is normally granted after service has been rendered. Professional development leave is not intended as a reward for past service nor is it primarily an item of added compensation. Rather, it is granted for the purpose of encouraging better future performance of University personnel. This may be accomplished through additional academic training; through professional improvement as a result of research, consulting, work outside the University environment in assignments related to the academic disciplines, appropriate travel, and study abroad; or through attendance at professional conferences, workshops, or internships.

Professional development leave is not an earned right but is a privilege granted to encourage self-improvement which will benefit both the University and the individual. The granting of such a leave is at the discretion of the Board of Trustees.

III. TYPES OF PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT LEAVES

A. One or two semesters (or equivalent) at one-half salary.

B. One semester at full salary.

C. To accelerate progress toward completion of a higher degree, a faculty member in a special case (after three full years of service at the University) may be granted a full-year professional development leave at half pay. However, this arrangement must be based upon an agreement that the faculty member will return to the campus following such leave for a period not less than the number of years remaining of the six normally required to accumulate such leave, plus one year. Moreover, this special arrangement in advance of the regular date for the first professional development leave is not intended to advance the date of a second professional development leave, which would be earned only after six full years of academic employment, including the year of professional development leave.

IV. ELIGIBILITY

A. Eligibility Requirements for Professional Development Leave

1. Regular full-time faculty members (including professional personnel in the Counseling Center, library professionals, and department chair and college dean of academic areas)

2. Six years service subsequent to beginning employment and six years service after return from a previous professional development leave, except as explained under III D

3. Recommendation of the President of the University

B. Accumulation of Time Toward Professional Development Leave

1. Credit for unused time accumulated toward professional development leave may be granted for those transferring from Brigham Young University, BYU-Hawaii, or from seminaries and institutes. Such time should be certified to the office of the Academic Vice President by the institution involved.

2. Faculty members joining BYU-Idaho in midyear will not accumulate time toward a professional development leave for the fraction of a year. The accumulation will begin in the fall of the subsequent year, which will be the first full year of service.

3. Accumulation of time by full-time faculty members on part-time assignments in academic work will be proportional to the fraction of time spent in academic work. For this purpose, academic work is defined as actual teaching, administration in academic colleges or departments, or University-funded research. This principle is illustrated by examples as follows:

a. If a faculty member on a full-time faculty contract is engaged in academic work 3/4 of his time and on an administrative or staff assignment for 1/4 of his time, he will accumulate 3/4 of a year toward the six year requirement for professional development leave.

b. If a faculty member on a full-time contract is permitted to accept a half-time assignment outside the University, he will receive credit for one-half year toward his professional development leave for the half-time assignment which is in academic work.

c. If a full-time faculty member is released from academic duties to spend half-time on research funded from sources outside the University (even though administered through the University), time spent on such research activities will not count toward professional development leave time accumulation except in those instances where the outside source provides funds for University fringe benefits, including professional development leave. If such funds are provided by an outside source, time spent on research will be treated as part of the academic work of the faculty member.

4. Unapproved leaves of absence result in loss of accumulated time for professional development leave.

C. Questions Concerning Eligibility

Unresolved questions which may arise pertaining to eligibility for professional development leave shall be referred to the office of the Academic Vice President for interpretation.

V. SPECIAL PROVISIONS OR LIMITATIONS WITH RESPECT TO PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT LEAVE

A. Generally Acceptable Leave Projects and Activities

1. Advanced study in approved colleges and universities

2. Research activities

3. Experience in highly responsible business or industrial firms or governmental offices provided the practical experience will be professionally beneficial at BYU-Idaho.

B. Teaching Limitation

Because the Board of Trustees is desirous that all faculty members obtain full advantage of professional development opportunities, a person on professional development leave shall not engage in teaching in another American college or university to a greater extent than approximately half-time as measured by teaching loads at BYU-Idaho. Teaching more than half-time will not allow adequate time for study, research, and other creative activities. Furthermore, teaching at another American college or university will be permitted only on professional development leave for a full year. Any special deviation from this rule must be approved by the Academic Vice President and the President.

C. Other Income During Leave

All persons granted professional development leave shall be entitled to the designated salary payments whether they receive other income during the professional development period or not. However, professional development leave will be granted only for purposes which will mean new educational experience for the applicant which will be of benefit to the University.

D. Promotions and Salary

A faculty member will receive the same salary increase for the period of his professional development leave as though he were not on leave.

E. Tuition, Books, and Travel

Tuition participation is determined on an individual basis and is dependent on funds available. Participation may be up to, but not exceed, half of the total tuition. Participation for books and travel is not covered.

F. Persons Called to be Mission Presidents

Faculty members called to serve as mission presidents will be granted professional development leave for this purpose. Application and approval for such leave should be made in the same way as any other leave. The period of absence to fulfill a mission call will not be counted toward the six-year accumulation for subsequent professional development leaves.

G. Foreign Assignments

Faculty members who serve under contract in foreign assignments will be permitted to count one year of such foreign service for accumulation toward an earned professional development leave. However, this one year will be counted only when a faculty member has been serving as a full-time teacher or administrator in the Church Schools (which includes BYU) immediately prior to his departure for foreign assignment.

VI. APPLICATION FOR PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT LEAVE

Application for leave of absence (professional development or noncompensatory) shall be made through the department chair, college dean, and Academic Vice President not later than November 1 of the year preceding the leave in order to facilitate the arrangements necessary to make such leave possible. If circumstances in an individual case change after November 1, a late request will be considered, but each application must be considered on its merits. After a leave has been granted, deferment will be considered only if no replacement has been engaged.

College deans and department chairs shall distribute requests for leave in the departments as evenly as possible among faculty members so as to relieve the need for temporary replacements for a large number who request leaves in the same year.

Professional development leaves are granted to faculty members on the basis of information furnished by them in their applications as to what they intend to do during leave. Any change in plans on the part of any teacher who has been granted leave must be approved by the President and the Executive Committee of the Board of Trustees. Otherwise, the leave is cancelled.

VII. SUBSEQUENT SERVICE REQUIREMENTS

A. Professional Development Leave Agreements

Every faculty member taking professional development leave (either regular or special) will be required to sign a "Professional Development Leave Agreement" which covers service requirements subsequent to the leave. Professional leave request forms can be obtained from the Associate Academic Vice President’s Office.

B. Continued Service

When an applicant accepts a regular professional development leave, he is required to continue in the service of the University for at least one full year following the leave. When a special professional development leave is granted, the service requirement is one year plus the number of years of service required to complete the first six years of employment.

C. Return of Funds

Persons failing to meet the requirement to return to the University following professional development leave shall be required to render to the University the entire gross pay, plus the University’s matching amounts for Social Security tax and group insurance premiums credited during the period of professional development leave. The repayment will be in accordance with an agreement to be made with BYU-Idaho, but in no case later than one year following termination of said leave. The unpaid balance will bear interest at a rate to be determined at the commencement of the leave.

D. Death During Professional Development Leave

In case of the death of a person on professional development leave, no return of money paid by the university will be required of the estate.

VIII. REPORTING WHILE ON PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT LEAVE

While on leave, the faculty member must keep the Academic Vice President informed as to his whereabouts and how he can be reached by mail and telephone. Any changes shall be reported immediately.

IX. NONCOMPENSATORY LEAVES - FACULTY

Applications for leave without pay shall be made on the same time schedule as those for professional development leave. Noncompensatory leaves, when approved, are normally granted for one year only. When such a leave is grated for one year, a position will be held for the faculty member on leave. As with professional development leaves, noncompensatory leaves are subject to approval of the Board of Trustees.

X. EXTENSION OF LEAVE

If a faculty member is on leave, either professional development or noncompensatory, and desires an extension of the leave, he should request the extension on a timely basis. If the extension of a noncompensatory leave is made beyond one year, a position is assured only if one is available at the time the faculty member desires to return.

Faculty Grievances (5-2)

I. PURPOSE

To provide an effective and fair process for receiving and resolving employee grievances. This policy and procedure is intended to cover all faculty grievances such as charges of unfairness, discrimination, poor working conditions, relationships, etc.

II. POLICY

A. It is the policy of BYU-Idaho to provide all faculty an opportunity to air their complaints.

B. All complaints should be resolved at the lowest administrative level possible.

C. Though faculty will be expected to follow their designated line of supervision to process a compliant, they may, when necessary, meet with the associate academic vice president for instruction to discuss their complaints.

D. Every effort will be made to resolve complaints as expeditiously as practicable.

III. PROCEDURE

A. Any faculty who feels aggrieved shall take his or her complaint to his or her immediate supervisor.

B. If the supervisor cannot resolve the complaint to the faculty’s satisfaction within a reasonable period, the employee shall put the grievance in writing and file the written grievance with the next higher administrator in the employee’s reporting structure. The administrator shall meet with the faculty as soon as is reasonably convenient and shall attempt to resolve the problem.

C. If the employee is not satisfied with the actions of this administrator, he or she shall file the written grievance with the appropriate academic vice president, who shall meet promptly with the aggrieved faculty and attempt to resolve the problem. The associate academic vice president for instruction should be involved in an advisory role as requested by the faculty, the supervisor, or the academic vice president.

D. If the academic vice president is unable to resolve the problem to the faculty’s satisfaction, the vice president will, on written request, afford the employee an opportunity to appear before a panel consisting of the Personnel Advisory Committee and such other persons as may be appointed by the President. The decision of this panel shall be final.

E. If the faculty’s grievance involves dissatisfaction with the actions of a person with whom the faculty would ordinarily file a grievance under this procedure, the faculty may skip that person and take the grievance to the next person in the supervisory line.

F. Grievances should, where possible, be filed promptly and in any case within two months of occurrence. Supervisors are encouraged to meet with faculty to try to resolve their complaints as expeditiously as practicable.

G. In cases involving claims of discrimination, the director of Personnel Services shall assist the faculty in resolving his or her grievance at each stage of the procedure.

IV. NONRETALIATION

At no time should a faculty suffer retaliation or harassment for having submitted a grievance. Any retaliatory action taken by any employee against another employee as the result of that person’s seeking redress under these procedures is prohibited and maybe regarded as a separate cause for complaint under these procedures.

V. DOCUMENTATION

A complete record should be maintained of grievances that become formalized that cannot be solved with the immediate supervisor. Copies of all documentation should be sent to the associatet academic vice president for instruction.

BYU-Idaho RESERVES THE ABSOLUTE RIGHT TO DEVIATE FROM THIS POLICY WHEN CIRCUMSTANCES WARRANT.

Faculty Probationary Status (5-3)

I. POLICY

A. All new faculty members at BYU-Idaho are hired on one-year contracts renewable at the sole discretion of the university and are on probationary status pending the award of Continuing Faculty Status ("CFS"). There is no obligation on the part of the university or right of the faculty member to renewal of a contract.

B. The probationary status normally continues until CFS is granted or denied, which decision is generally made at the conclusion of three years. Continuing Faculty Status is granted only upon demonstration by the faculty member to the satisfaction of the university that the faculty member has performed his or her job duties with a high degree of professionalism, has conducted oneself in an exemplary manner consistent with the Honor Code and the standards and values of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, and through the probationary period has acquired appropriate degree credentials (minimum expectation: master’s degree).

II. PROCEDURE

A. A three-person CFS Committee will be appointed by the college dean within two months of the beginning of the first school year after the faculty member is hired and will serve until CFS is either granted or denied. This committee will act in a review and appraisal capacity only and will not have the responsibility to be an advocate for the faculty member. For the purpose of mentoring and aiding the new faculty member in the contract and CFS review process, a senior faculty member in his/her department will be assigned as soon as possible after the faculty member is hired.

The faculty member’s department chair generally serves as ex-officio chair of the CFS Committee (i.e., the chair will have no voting authority). Where possible, one voting committee member should be from the faculty member’s department and the other two voting members should be from another discipline.

B. The new faculty member may request in writing a change in the membership of the CFS Committee to the college dean who has the sole discretion to approve or deny the request.

C. The faculty member bears the burden of persuasion that his/her performance is sufficient to justify the granting of CFS. The faculty member may choose to use the CFS Committee’s appraisal to help carry that burden or may present any other material deemed appropriate.

A written appraisal of the faculty member’s performance will be submitted yearly by the CFS Committee through the college dean to the academic vice president. Recommendations concerning contract renewal and the awarding of CFS will be made by the committee, by the college dean, and by the academic vice president. The faculty member will be notified of recommendations, allowed to inspect the record upon which the recommendations are based, and will be permitted to respond. Furthermore, any claims by the faculty member that the appraisal of the committee or the recommendations for nonrenewal of a contract or denial of CFS are pretextual or based upon an improper or illegal basis must be raised by the faculty member in response to the appraisal or recommendations so that the matter can be fully documented before the appraisal, recommendations, and response are presented to the president.

The president will make the final determination on whether a contract will be renewed and whether CFS will be awarded. The president will review, but need not follow, the committee appraisal and the recommendations and may take into consideration any relevant information which comes to his attention from any source, regardless of whether or not reviewed by the committee, in making the contract renewal and CFS decision provided that the faculty member is given a fair opportunity to comment on this information before a final decision is reached. Should the president determine that a final decision cannot be made based on the appraisal and recommendations that are received, the president can take such other actions deemed necessary to gather sufficient information to make a final decision.

Reasonable efforts will be made to promptly inform the new faculty member of any decision concerning contract renewal and CFS. However, such notice may be given at any time during the probationary period.

Hiring for Approved Positions (Faculty) (5-4)

I. PURPOSE

To establish the policy and procedure to be used in hiring persons to fill approved faculty positions.

II. DEFINITIONS

A. An approved position is a position that has been authorized by the Board of Trustees and the President’s Council and for which budget is available.

B. An applicant is a person who has submitted a completed, signed application, a resume, official transcripts, letters of recommendation, and has kept that application current through contact with the office of the Associate Academic Vice President for Instruction.

III. POLICY

A. BYU-Idaho will hire the applicant most suitably qualified for a position regardless of race, sex, color, national origin, age, or, if otherwise qualified, persons with disabilities.

B. Only approved positions will be opened for hiring. No person may be considered for employment without a position being open. No job offers may be made for an unapproved position.

C. Only the President and appropriate vice president have authority to make commitments regarding faculty hiring which are binding upon BYU-Idaho.

D. The university’s interest in hiring the best qualified available candidate dictates the wisdom of a broad employment search. Ordinarily, available positions will be advertised through an open search process; however, the Academic Vice President is authorized to dispense with a search when warranted.

E. The Associate Academic Vice President for Instruction will compile and submit to the appropriate academic college dean or administrative director a list of all applications submitted for each approved open position.

F. For each candidate who was interviewed but not hired, the appropriate college dean will submit reasons for not hiring to the Associate Academic Vice President for Instruction.

G. Departments will complete employment reference checks on the principal candidates (those who will be presented for Board approval) and forward that information to the office of the Associate Academic Vice President for Instruction.

H. The principal candidate must have an interview with a General Authority before the Board meets and a final offer of employment can be made.

IV. PROCEDURES

A. Once a position has been approved, the college dean will submit a job description to the office of the Associate Academic Vice President for Instruction.

B. The office of the Associate Academic Vice President for Instruction will advertise appropriately through media available to that office for a minimum of five days after posting and announcing the position. College deans and department chairs may make contact with other schools and professional associations to obtain a broad dissemination of the announcement.

C. After an appropriate period for advertising and evaluating received applications, the college dean will submit a prioritized listing of the candidates to the Associate Academic Vice President for Instruction.

D. The Associate Academic Vice President for Instruction will obtain ecclesiastical and Executive Board of Trustees clearances on the top candidates prior to any interviews. A General Authority interview for the principal candidate will be scheduled.

E. The Associate Academic Vice President for Instruction will set up an interview schedule for the candidates.

1. Departments wishing to have candidates teach a class as part of their interview will make arrangements for all candidates to teach.

2. All candidates should be treated substantially the same way in regard to teaching opportunities, interviews, etc.

3. College deans and department chairs will be responsible to make appropriate reference checks before the committee meets to make a decision.

F. The President, after consultation with appropriate faculty and administrators, will decide who to recommend to the Board of Trustees for hire. Following the General Authority interview, final approval will be obtained from the Board of Trustees. The appropriate college dean or administrative director will then inform those candidates who are to be hired.

G. The appropriate college dean or administrative director will inform those candidates who were not hired of their nonselection.

Faculty Disciplinary Procedures (5-6)

I. PURPOSE

The decision to invoke a disciplinary action shall be made by the academic vice president after consultation with the department chair and college dean. Disciplinary action may consists of verbal warning, written warning, change in assignment, probation, dismissal, or other appropriate sanction determined by the academic vice president.

However, before disciplinary action involving change in assignment, probation, or dismissal, the faculty member will be given reasonable notice of the charges and a fair opportunity to respond to them.

If a faculty member is aggrieved by a disciplinary action involving change in assignment, probation, or dismissal, the faculty member may request a hearing before a peer review committee to be appointed by the president of the university. The committee will informally investigate the circumstances of the disciplinary action and offer recommendations to the president. The president will review, but need not follow, the recommendation of the committee and will make the final decision with respect to an appropriate disciplinary action, if any. The president may take into consideration any relevant information which comes to his attention and not reviewed by the committee in making the decision provided that the faculty member is given a fair opportunity to comment on this information before a final decision is reached.

Continuing Faculty Status (CFS) is an expectation of continued employment. The university may terminate the appointment of a faculty member with CFS for adequate cause only. Examples of adequate cause for termination of a faculty appointment at BYU-Idaho include, but are not limited to:

A. A serious violation of the Honor Code or other university policy.
B. Moral turpitude.
C. The faculty member’s breach of the employment contract with the university.
D. Incompetent performance.
E. Behavior or expression that seriously and adversely affects the university or the Church.
F. Violation of the university policy concerning sexual harassment and inappropriate gender-based behavior.
G. Gross abuse of trust in a faculty-student relationship.
H. For LDS faculty, excommunication or disfellowshipment from the Church, or failure for a reasonable period of time to meet the standards of conduct consistent with qualifying for temple privileges.
I. Discontinuance of an academic position for financial reasons, enrollment drops, or cancellation of a program or department of instruction.

1. Although the university reserves the right to discontinue an academic position, a reasonable effort will be made to retain faculty members affected. If this is not possible, the faculty member will be given at least one calendar year of notice or will receive full salary for one academic year from the date or notification whether or not his or her duties are continued during that time.

2. If a faculty member’s employment is terminated because an academic position has been discontinued, the position will not be filled by a replacement for a period of two years unless the position is first offered to the released faculty member and he or she is given adequate time to decide whether or not to accept.

Faculty having CFS can be terminated only by the president of the university. A faculty member may request an appeal to the commissioner of Church Education who may, in his sole discretion, determine whether or not to hear the appeal. If an appeal is accepted, the commissioner of Church Education will establish fair procedures to be observed but will not reverse or modify the president’s decision unless it is clearly erroneous.

Faculty Conflict of Interest (5-7)

GENERAL STATEMENT

Faculty members are expected to be available during each workday to prepare, teach, consult with students, meet with advisees, and provide support to the university by attending meetings, forums, devotionals, college/department and campus-wide faculty meetings, fulfilling committee assignments, etc. While no set working hours exist, full-time faculty will be working a 40-hour week or more. The enthusiasm for the work and the example of industry reflect the professionalism of the faculty member and the standard of BYU-Idaho. The university expects a full measure of the faculty member’s best creative and professional effort. In fairness to students, faculty colleagues, faithful tithe payers, and other qualified applicants who were not selected to teach at BYU-Idaho, faculty members are expected to avoid outside activities which detract from their best efforts.

To allow for maximum flexibility and latitude in the work environment, BYU-Idaho intentionally reduces the number of rules and regulations that govern faculty members. For example, there are no strict policy limitations governing time taken from work due to illness, funeral attendance, weddings, missionary departures, professional meetings and conferences, etc. The overriding guideline is simply that faculty inform and, where necessary, obtain approval from the department chair and then take personal responsibility to ensure that classes and other faculty assignments are fully covered. The hallmark of BYU-Idaho faculty has always been to support each other in meeting university responsibilities. With the freedom of flexibility in the work environment comes the concomitant responsibility to meet every faculty expectation effectively.

I. CONFLICT OF INTEREST POLICY AND GUIDELINES

The intent of the Faculty Conflict of Interest Policy is to assist both the university and its full-time and part-time faculty to be more effective in carrying out assigned responsibilities and to ensure that BYU-Idaho receives the amount and quality of effort, judgement, and loyalty necessary for the university to meet its mission objectives.

Faculty members in a position to influence a decision on policy or purchases where they might directly or indirectly receive benefit or give improper advantage to associates, or those faculty members desiring to engage in outside activities that could substantially conflict in time, energy, or performance with their primary university commitments must first consult with the college deans and department chairs and then receive written approval in those instances where a conflict is apparent or possible. Conflicts of interest may require contractual adjustments. Should questions or concerns arise, seek advice, clarification, or appeal through regular academic channels.

Potential conflicts of interest with substantial time and involvement must be approved by the academic vice president. All approvals are subject to annual review and are considered using the following criteria:

A. What impact, if any, does the proposed involvement have on the mission of BYU-Idaho?
B. Will it place a burden on faculty colleagues?
C. What will be the extent and duration of the involvement?
D. Will there be negative effects or burdens placed on students?
E. Will it adversely affect or conflict with the faculty member’s performance or his or her job duties?
F. Will involvement result in double payment for the same duty?
G. Will the involvement be consistent with the highest standards of good judgement, professional commitment, and ethics to protect the best interests of BYU-Idaho?

II. DEFINITIONS

A. Conflict of Interest. A conflict of interest occurs when faculty members are in a position to influence a decision on policy or purchases where they might directly or indirectly receive financial benefit or give improper advantage to associates or when the faculty member’s involvement in outside activities substantially interfere with his or her primary commitments to the university and its faculty and students.

B. Consulting. Any outside activity that brings financial gain to the faculty member and at the same time results in professional benefit to both the university and the faculty member.

C. Moonlighting. Any outside activity that provides financial gain to the faculty member without bringing benefit to the university.

D. University Business. Any official university-related activity conducted on or off the campus.

III. SPECIFIC APPLICATIONS OF THE CONFLICT OF INTEREST POLICY

A. Business Dealings with the University

1. Private Business Interests. If a faculty member owns or is professionally involved with a commercial enterprise that could do business with the university:

(a) The faculty member may not represent nor lobby the university in its dealings with the organization.

(b) The faculty member’s business affiliation must be reported in writing both to the college dean and the organizational unit of the university that is conducting the business.

(c) The faculty member must take appropriate action under the circumstances to ensure that no terms or agreements are influenced by a faculty member’s relationship with a private business interest.

2. Surplus Property. Any faculty member may bid on all properties that have been declared surplus to the university and placed on open bid in accordance with established policy and procedure. However, no faculty member may influence the sale of such for either personal or professional monetary gain.

3. Other University Employment. Faculty members may not generate additional compensation beyond the contracted full-time salary with BYU-Idaho while working during regular daytime hours (8 a.m. to 5 p.m., Monday through Friday) for a second campus entity, or for another university, without prior written approval from the college dean.

B. Personal Use of University Property, Personnel, Equipment

1. Equipment. University equipment is not normally lent to university employees. Any exceptions require prior written approval from the college dean.

2. Supplies. Except for incidental use of such minor items as pencils, paper, etc., faculty members will compensate the university for the personal use of expendable supplies.

3. Personnel. The use of university personnel, such as secretaries or service maintenance employees during their assigned workday for personal projects must have prior written approval subject to the conditions stipulated in I.A-G.

C. Outside Interests

1. Personal Monetary Benefit. Whenever a faculty member’s position at the university presents and opportunity for personal monetary gain beyond that paid by the university, the circumstances of the benefit must be disclosed to the department chair.

2. Consulting. The criteria for approval is I.A.-G. Any consulting must have prior written approval of the college dean and will be subject to yearly re-evaluation.

3. Moonlighting. Although moonlighting is not prohibited, it is discouraged and, at the election of the university, is subject to the conditions specified in I.A-G.

4. Nonpaid Activities. Permission to engage in nonpaid activities during normal working hours will be at the discretion of the department chair, subject to the conditions specified in I.A-G.

D. Other Conflicts of Interest

Other circumstances that may result in a conflict of interest and illustrative examples of where disclosure is required include:

1. Research designed or conducted at the university to serve the needs of a private or public organization beyond BYU-Idaho.

2. Engaging in similar projects or research for both the university and a private organization without the knowledge and prior approval of both organizations.

3. Using privileged or confidential university information for personal gain or for the gain of others, such as forthcoming programs or the selection of contractors in advance of public announcements.

4. Accepting gratuities or favors from any firm or organization under circumstances that might influence or reasonably be interpreted as an attempt to influence the recipient.

5. Using university position to obtain personal privilege or privileges for others from persons or firms outside the university or from the university itself.

6. Using university affiliation, except as to establishing credentials, to give credibility or other material support to a private undertaking.

7. Using students in class projects, assignments, or related outside activities that could be reasonable interpreted as exploitive, or that might result in personal monetary gain above the university salary.

8. Using university time and resources for substantial Church service. In general, church sponsorship of BYU-Idaho does not justify use of time and university resources during regular working hours for Church callings. The university encourages service in the Church and recognizes the need for flexibility. Unforeseen duties such as funerals, weddings, and urgent counseling may require attention during school time; however, to hold scheduled meetings, regular interviews, tithing settlement, etc., during the workday would constitute a conflict of interest.
