Cry for Freedom

Cry as we might for freedom,
the space to be our own,
unfettered by demands of others
upon that which we take to be ours,
we can never out-distance
the truth that shapes us
unmistakably and without exception,
engraving its signature
in countenance and deed alike-
eventually to reveal
who we have loved
and what we have loved for.

There never was a time
when love hasn't worked
its work
on every man
every woman.
The question
pressed by embrace
never has been
whether one loves
or has loved;
but who one loves
and to what purpose.

So, then,
I love you

is always more than words
of kindness
bestowed upon one
fortunate to be the
object of another's affection.
These three syllables
seal in promise
a recommitment
each time they are exchanged
verbally or silently:
tongue or eye
tying together each to each
in mutual cause of blessing or curse
through encouragement or seduction
that must occur as
change moves on to change
until . . .
change is no more.

David L. Ward
(October 12, 1999)
