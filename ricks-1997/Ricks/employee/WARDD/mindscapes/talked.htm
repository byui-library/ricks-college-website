We Talked

We used to talk
to get to know each other.

Times are that
event has passed upon event,
children have followed children,
birthday has slipped past birthday
until school, work, house, church
have given us cause to learn
about each other, to live along side
each other, and yet . . . .

In it, and through it all,
we lost touch,
lost feeling,
lost the need to know each other,
thought we knew all there was
except for the growing tendency
to complain that things were
getting hard; that knowing each other
as the other has been
was stale--and little to live for.

Yet against this stalemate
we took a walk through
forest berthing lycon and mushroom,
stepped upon ground made
soft by inches of pine needles
pieced together by wind and rain and moist earth:
a welcome mat for lovers to be,
welcoming without end
the mutual steps of two struggling
to become one.

We talked with our feet cushioned
about then and now.  Then our talk was
mutual, without guarded words
intending not to hurt, but hurting nonetheless
by their contrivance.  This afternoon, our words
were warm, made soft and comforting
by a renewed desire to know each other
for the first time . . . again.

David L. Ward
September 7, 1993
