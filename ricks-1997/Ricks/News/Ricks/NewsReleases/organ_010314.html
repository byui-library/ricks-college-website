Original Thomas E. Ricks organ back at college one more time

REXBURG, Idaho —  A trip to a local antique shop ended in the retrieval of a piece of Ricks College history.

Two Ricks art teachers, Gerald Griffin and Matthew Geddes of Rexburg, who often go antiquing, found the organ that belonged to Rexburg founder Thomas E. Ricks in the Main Street Antique Mall in Rexburg.

The two teachers knew it was part of local history when they found documentation that it had been on campus for a number of years, Geddes says.

"It is meaningful to the area and to the college," Geddes says. "We felt like it should be part of Ricks College."

According to Geddes, they thought it was something Ricks College should have on campus. As a result, the antique was bought through money from the college’s Performing and Fine Arts Division budget.

Currently, the organ is on display on the second floor of the Eliza R. Snow Center for the Performing Arts.

The Crown pump organ was brought on raft across the North Fork of Snake River into Rexburg in about 1883 or ’84. Upon arrival, the organ was kept at the local meetinghouse of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, which was also home of Bannock Stake Academy that in later years would become Ricks College.

The organ then was inherited by Thomas Ricks’ brother, Nathan Ricks, and was kept in his home for a number of years. The organ remained there until Arzetta Parkinson purchased the home, after which she donated the organ to be used for devotionals on campus.

Eventually, the organ was put in the attic of the Spori Building where it collected dust for many years. While rewiring the Spori Building, the organ was found. With the consent of college President John Clarke, it was given to 13-year-old Lowell Parkinson as a surprise gift in May 1962 by a school friend, Glenn Legget.

Lowell, who is Arzetta’s grandson, says his father saw the old organ and said it looked familiar. When hearing the story, his father said, "I cannot believe it. We donated that to the college and it’s back in the family again."

Lowell kept the organ for most of his life. He sold the organ because "I am starting to downsize my antique collection."
