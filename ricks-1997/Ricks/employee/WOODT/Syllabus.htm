CLASS OUTLINE
English 215 Advanced Writing in the Social Sciences

Class Objectives
   Our class aims to help you become more confident, skilled writers. We will read essays by professional writers, discuss what makes them effective, and write many assignments. We will discuss writing principles and implement them in our writing. This class requires a good deal of work, but I hope to make the time you spend here enjoyable as well as informative.

Note
   This class fills the advanced writing requirement for BYU as well as for other colleges. IMPORTANT: Contrary to former policy, ANY GRADE (D or better) will transfer and fill the required credits, so PLEASE relax about your grades. Since this is an upper university level writing class, grading is strict; therefore, if you are one who likes to discuss grades with his/her teacher, I'm NOT the teacher to take. However, if you want to ask me specific questions pertaining to difficulties you are having with your writing, PLEASE MAKE AN APPOINTMENT and let's talk it over.

Class Requirements
    There are basically only two course requirements:
        1. Attend Class
        2. Be Prepared

Class preparation includes reading assignments in texts (expect quizzes) and bringing essays and drafts of essays to class when they are due (no late work is acceptable). Please expect to LOSE POINTS right off the top if you turn work in late. College is a place to demonstrate maturity, and part of maturity is being responsible in your attendance and assignments.

Reasonable Accommodation
   In compliance with applicable disability law, qualified students with a disability may be entitled to 'reasonable accommodation.' It is the student's responsibility to disclose to the teacher any special need she/he may have before the end of the first week of class.

   Ricks College offers a writing lab on the second floor of the administration building. Open 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. M through F (except during devotionals or on Thursdays at 2:00), the lab is a great resource should you need assistance with a specific part of a writing assignment.

Standards
   This class will firmly support Ricks College standards. It is a privilege to attend Ricks--honor that privilege.

Plagiarism
   Plagiarism means copying any part of someone else's work or using his/her ideas whether intentionally or not. This is a serious error and will not be tolerated in this class. Make certain you give proper credit to sources you use.

Read and Respond Journals

The read and respond journal tends to be more introspective than most of our formal assignments. It will focus on responses to the essays we read in Thinking about Thinking. Sometimes I will furnish a topic based on a reading--you will respond with at least one page (typed, double spaced) of your best, most thoughtful writing. These journal entries must do two things: 1.) Demonstrate by a thoughtful response to my first question that you HAVE read the assigned essay; 2.) Give a personal response to the second question that is original and well written--focused by a thesis, well organized, concrete, and interesting. I will gather these pieces before midterm and grade SELECTED pieces then repeat this process at semester's end. Enjoy these journal entries--infuse them with YOURSELF (which is sometimes difficult to do with more formal writing assignments).

NOTE: At times I may have you do some in-class writing which I will ask you to develop into a full-length journal entry. These may be more creative and are worth 15 points each as are the other journal entries.

Vocabulary List: At midterm I will ask you to turn in a list of 20 unfamiliar words you have picked up from your reading for this class. Include their definitions. From the lists I will choose about twenty to include on the final exam.
