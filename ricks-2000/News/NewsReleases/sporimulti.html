November 8, 2001

Work at BYU-Idaho on schedule

for Spori and Mutli-use buildings


REXBURG, Idaho- Construction on the new Spori and multi-use buildings at Brigham Young University-Idaho are right on schedule, according to Physical Plant Director Chuck Frost.

Construction began on the same site as the original Spori Building during the summer. "At this time, they are on schedule," Frost says. "They are pouring the basement walls and trying to bring the foundation up to first floor level. The intent is to put on the first floor deck and work inside the basement all winter."

The basement will hold some classrooms, but the majority of the winter will be spent working on the mechanical room where all the piping and heat exchanges are controlled.

Frost adds that 50 stone blocks are being quarried on the Idaho-Montana border near Medicine Lodge by Idaho Travertine of Idaho Falls.

"The blocks weigh 25,000 pounds apiece and will be used for the exterior of building," Frost says. "This particular stone is called walnut covered travertine, a pattern somewhat similar to what was on the original Spori. They’ll be quarrying the stone until snow shuts down the roads."

The completion date for the Spori is set for March 2003.

Another project currently under way at BYU-Idaho is the Multi-Use Building located on the southeast side of campus. Work by Jacobson Construction began on the building in April and is also going according to plan.

"Right now they are placing plywood sheets on the roof to close it in. During the winter, all the entrances will be sealed with plastic and they will work inside," Frost says. "I’m sure everyone has noticed that we are running steam lines to the building. The steam will be used to heat the building during the winter."

The asphalt for the parking lot by the Multi-Use Building was laid last week, but will not be open for use until the building is completed. The completion date for the Multi-Use Building is July 2002.

"As far as the Kimball parking lot is concerned, part of it will be closed throughout most of December," Frost says. "The contractor is very organized and is doing a great job."
