State of Affairs as we Prepare to Receive the Savior His Second and Final Time

And behold, the watchman upon the tower would have seen the enemy while he was yet afar off; and then ye could have made ready and kept the enemy from breaking down the hedge thereof, and saved my vineyard from the hands of the destroyer. (D&C 101:54)

Latter-day prophets have spoken clearly on the issue of the foreordained roles of men and men in these the last days. Through the united voice of the First Presidency and Quorum of the Twelve they have lifted a proclamation of warning that if goes unheeded will bring calamities foretold by prophets of old. Satan is enjoying more than a measure of success in his efforts to redefine the family and confuse the roles of men and women. Many in the church seem to be a variance with one another on these issues.

More recently, President Gordon B. Hinckley said: “The family is falling apart all over the world. There are too many broken homes among our own [people]. The love that led to marriage somehow evaporates, and hatred fills its place. Hearts are broken, children weep. As I look to the future, I see little to feel enthusiastic about concerning the family in America and across the world. Drugs and alcohol are taking a terrible toll, which is not likely to decrease.

Harsh language [and] indifference to the needs of one another all seem to be increasing. There is so much of child abuse, spouse abuse, [and] abuse of the elderly. All of this will happen and get worse unless there is an acknowledgment and conviction that the family is an instrument of the Almighty. It is His creation. I lift a warning voice to our people. We have moved too far toward the mainstream of society in this matter. . .there are too many [families] in trouble.” (Ensign, Nov. 1997, p. 69)

President James E. Faust stated:“Members of the Church have the most effective cure for our decaying family life. That cure is for men, women, and children to honor and respect the divine roles of both fathers and mothers in the home.”

“God has revealed through His prophets that men are to receive the priesthood, to become fathers, and with gentleness and pure, unfeigned love to lead and nurture their families in righteousness, taking for their pattern the way the Savior leads the Church. Men have also been given the primary responsibility for providing for the temporal and physical needs of the family.

Women have the ability to bring children into the world and have been given the primary role and opportunity to lead, nurture, and teach their little ones in a loving, safe, and spiritual environment. In this divinely sanctioned partnership, husbands and wives work together.” –Elder M. Russell Ballard, Counseling with Councils, pg., 55-57

Priesthood and Motherhood

Priesthood and motherhood go hand in hand. Neither is complete without the other. Both are eternal. An eternal family requires the proper fulfillment of both. This life is to help us fulfill these two responsibilities, that we may exercise them through eternity. If motherhood, like priesthood, is a foreordained role, then it is reasonable to believe that God will, if let him, help us develop and refine those qualities so vital to our success in these roles.

I believe that as a woman fulfills her divine responsibilities as a wife and mother, that she will be helping her husband fulfill his role as priesthood husband and father. Husbands and wives should work as a team, united in every aspect in equal partnership to fulfill those divine roles-“Neither is the man without the woman, neither the woman without the man, in the Lord.” (1 Cor. 11:11)

Elder Neal A. Maxwell said: “We know so little. .. about the reasons for the division of duties between womanhood and manhood [and]. . .motherhood and priesthood. These were divinely determined in another time and another place. . .[by divine design]. . .” (“Women of God,” Ensign, May 1978, pp. 10-11)

President Benson said: “Before the world was created, in heavenly councils the pattern and role of women were prescribed. You were elected by God to be wives and mothers in Zion. Exaltation in the celestial kingdom is predicated on faithfulness to that calling.” (Teachings of Ezra Taft Benson, p. 546)

Motherhood From the Proclamation on Family we learn a woman is to be a : Mother–provide bodies for children, nurture, and teach children. Companion–be an equal. Homemaker–assist husband in home, not earn a living

Roles of Motherhood

President Benson said: “Since the beginning, a woman's first and most important role has been ushering into mortality spirit sons and daughters of Father in Heaven. . .[and] to teach [them] eternal gospel principles. She is to provide her children a haven of security and love regardless of how modest circumstances might be.” (“The Honored Place of Woman,” Ensign, Nov. 1981, p. 105)

President Benson further warns, “Mothers, you are your children’s best teacher. Don’t shift this precious responsibility to day-care centers or baby-sitters. A mother’s love and prayerful concern for her children are her most important ingredients in teaching her own…Your children will remember your teachings forever, and when they are old, they will not depart from them. They will call you blessed—their truly angel mother. Mothers, this kind of heavenly, motherly teaching takes time—lots of time. It cannot be done effectively part-time. It must be done all the time in order to save and exalt your children . This is your divine calling.” (“To the Mothers in Zion”)

Companion/Wife

President Howard W. Hunter said: “The Lord intended that the wife be a helpmeet for man (meet means equal) -- that is, a companion equal and necessary in full partnership.” (“Being a Righteous Husband and Father”)

President Spencer W. Kimball said: “We do not want our LDS women to be silent partners or limited partners in that eternal assignment. Please be a contributing and full partner.” (Ensign, Nov. 1978, p. 106)

Homemaker

President Spencer W. Kimball said: “Women are to take care of the family -- the Lord has so stated -- to be an assistant to the husband, to work with him, but not to earn the living, except in unusual circumstances. . .Too many mothers work away from home to furnish sweaters and music lessons and trips and fun for their children. Too many women spend their time in socializing, in politicking, in public services when they should be home to teach and train and receive and love their children into security.” (TSWK, pp. 318-319)

President Benson said “Our beloved Prophet Spencer W. Kimball had much to say about the role of mothers in the home and their callings and responsibilities. I am impressed tonight to share with you some of his inspired pronouncements. I fear that much of his counsel has gone unheeded, and families have suffered because of it. But, I stand this evening as a second witness to the truthfulness of what President Spencer W. Kimball said. He spoke as a true prophet of God.” “I beg of you, you who could and should be bearing and rearing a family: wives, come home from the typewriter, the laundry, the nursing, come from from the factory, the cafe. No career approaches in importance that of wife, homemaker, mother. . .” (“To the Mothers in Zion”)

President Gordon B. Hinckley said: “Some years ago President Benson delivered a message to the women of the Church. He encouraged them to leave their employment and give their individual time to their children. I sustain the position which he took. Nevertheless, I recognize, as he recognized, that there are some women (it has become very many in fact) who have to work to provide for the needs of their families. To you I say, do the very best you can. I hope that if you are employed full-time you are doing it to ensure that basic needs are met and not simply to indulge a taste for an elaborate home, fancy cars, and other luxuries.” (“Women of the Church,” Ensign, Nov. 1996, p. 69)

An Elect Lady in the Last Days

“As our Lord and Savior needed the women of his time for a comforting hand, a listening ear, a believing heart, a kind look, an encouraging word, loyalty—even in his hour of humiliation, agony, and death—so we, his servants all across the Church need you, the women of the Church, to stand with us and for us in stemming the tide of evil that threatens to engulf us…it seems to me that there is a great need to rally the women of the Church to stand with and for the Brethren in stemming the tide of evil that surrounds us and in moving forward the work of our Savior.” –Pres. Howard W. Hunter

“I say unto you, all those who will receive my gospel are sons and daughters in my kingdom.” (D&C 25:1) “This is my voice unto all” (D&C 25:16)

An Elect lady Is faithful and Virtuous: “if thou art faithful and walk in the paths of virtue before me, I will preserve thy life, and thou shalt receive an inheritance in Zion”. (verse 2)

Pres. Hinckley, in commenting on this scripture says: “Faithful to what? Faithful to ourselves, to the very best that is within us. No woman can afford to demean herself, to belittle herself, to downgrade her abilities or her capacities. Let each be faithful to the great, divine attributes that are within her.” Speaking of virtue he says, “there is nothing as beautiful as virtue… no quality so becoming, no attire so attractive.”

An Elect lady murmurs not: Emma is told to “murmur not”. Who, in the history of the Church, had more cause to murmur than Emma? Women whose husbands have demanding church callings (and those whose husbands don’t) could complain about a lot of things, but instead, we are told

An Elect lady comforts,” and speaks “with consoling words in the spirit of meekness: Be a “comfort” unto our husbands and to speak “with consoling words in the spirit of meekness.” “This comes first. A woman’s first and highest calling is to be the guiding spirit of the home, under her husband, who is the head of the household. The home is a divine institution. The Church depends on it. If the home is not a sanctuary where God is present, the church will be cold and cheerless. But the home depends, largely, on the wife and mother.” (Hyrum M. Smith and Jane M. Sjodahl, Doctrine and Covenants Commentary)

An Elect lady is given to writing and to learning much: Pres. Hinckley says this, “To you women of today, who are old or young, may I suggest that you write, that you keep journals, that you express your thoughts on paper. Writing is a great discipline…it will assist you in various ways, and you will bless the lives of many—your families and others—now and in the years to come, as you put on paper some of your experiences and some of your musings.” Emma was to devote her time to learning and also “to expound scriptures, and to exhort the church.” She was to be a teacher of righteousness and truth, and was to study the gospel and things of the world in which she lived.

An Elect lady will lay aside the things of this world and seek for the things of better (verse 10), “lifts up her heart and rejoices and cleaves unto the covenants which she made.” (vs. 13) “keep my commandments continually and a crown of righteousness thou shalt receive.” (Verse 15)

President Spencer W. Kimball prophesied that “much of the major growth that is coming to the Church in the last days will come because many of the good women of the world, in whom there is often such an inner sense of spirituality, will be drawn to the Church in large numbers. This will happen to the degree that the women of the Church reflect righteousness and articulateness in their lives and to the degree that the women of the Church are seen as distinct and different--in happy ways--from the women of the world.” (Ensign, Nov. 1979, pp. 103-104)

An Elect lady delights in her husband: Pres. Benson tells that on occasion when attending stake conferences, and members of the stake presidency and other local brethren were speaking, that he would look over the audience and try to pick out the wives of the men who were speaking. He said, “I like to look into their faces as their husbands are up bearing their testimonies and preaching the gospel. Sometimes you will find a sister who puts her head down as if she felt sorry for the poor man. But more often you will find the type of support which brings her to look squarely at him, and you can just feel and see in her face that she is praying for him, that she has faith in him, that she wants him to succeed...It is a glorious thing to have that kind of support.”

Sister Hinckley is an ideal example of that kind of love and support. Ruth Faust, wife of President James E. Faust, remembers an experience she had with her during the time President Kimball and President Romney were ill and President Hinckley was carrying such a difficult burden in the First Presidency. Sister Hinckley asked if she could have a ride to a particular function with Sister Faust. She said, “I’m not going to drive anymore.” When Sister Faust asked her why, she (Sister Hinckley) said that she had returned home a little late recently and her husband had told her how relieved he was and how much he worried when she was out in the car.

“Well,” she said, “I don’t want to add to your problems. You can cross that worry right off your list. I’ll quit driving.” Sister Faust said, “I’ve thought many times about that unselfish gift, so quickly and easily given, and wondered if I could give up my independence that generously. Marjorie Hinckley is truly an elect lady.”

Howard W. Hunter said, “To reach success in the family, parents must have love and respect for each other. Husbands, the bearers of the priesthood, should hold their wives in the highest esteem before their children, and wives should love and support their husbands. In return, the children will have love for their parents and for each other. The home will then become a hallowed place where the principles of the gospel can be best lived and where the Spirit of the Lord can dwell. To be a successful father or a successful mother is far greater than to rise to leadership or high places in business, government, or worldly affairs. Home may seem commonplace at times with its routine duties, yet its success should be the greatest of all our pursuits in life.”

We all know that “No success can compensate for failure in the home,” and that Satan is working with all his might to destroy the family. May we listen to the watchmen on the tower and be blessed by the counsel they give is my prayer, in the Name of Jesus Christ, Amen.
