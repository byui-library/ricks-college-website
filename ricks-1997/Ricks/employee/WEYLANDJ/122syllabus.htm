During office hours, you are invited to come in and ask for help in working assigned problems or to ask about concepts discussed in class.

TEXTBOOK: Physics for Scientists and Engineers, Serway, Fourth Edition

GRADING: There will be four "hour" exams. Your lowest hour exam will be dropped. Note that homework, quizzes count one sixth of your grade. Because homework will be collected and graded, and because each class period will have a quiz or other material handed in for grading, at the end of the semester, the quiz points, etc, may add up to several hundred points. These will then be normalized to 100 points. No matter how well you do on exams, it will not be possible to get an A on the course unless you earn at least 70% on homework and quizzes.

Homework will not be accepted late. It will not be possible to make up missed quizzes. If extenuating circumstances, such as sickness, family emergencies, etc, occur, contact Brother Weyland as soon as you can for a possible exception to this policy. (Leave a message.)

There will be a three-hour final exam which everyone must take. The final exam will be comprehensive over material covered during the semester. You may bring to each of the "hour" exams one 3"x 5" card upon which you have written notes or equations. For the final exam you may bring the four 3"x 5" cards you have used in the hour exams (or the equivalent area)

All "hour" exams will consist of two parts:

(1) Four problems similar to homework

(2) Five qualitative questions testing your understanding of the concepts. The qualitative portion is extra credit.

The qualitative or "concept" exams will be given in class on the days specified in the syllabus. The "problem" exams will be given in the testing center. The opening and closing dates for each exam are given below:

Two of the five class periods will be spent with you working on problems in small groups. Learn to work together. Those who understand should explain it to those who don't. In this way everyone's understanding will increase. The role of the instructor in group learning is that of a facilitator. Some of the quizzes given in class will be group quizzes. Your group should get together twice a week to work on problems and discuss the concepts you are studying.

You should spend about 2 hours between each class period studying the textbook, discussing physics concepts, and working problems. You should have spent time on problems before coming to group problem solving sessions. Work hard but, also, work smart. Don't spend hours without a clue on one problem. Get help from someone in your group or from Brother Weyland.

Attendance Requirements

Although role is not taken, you will turn in an assignment or take a quiz every class period. Ordinarily quizzes cannot be made up, so come to class every day.

Course Policies

In turning in homework, the following format should be followed:

Use engineer's computation paper.

Write on only one side of the paper. Problems should be found on the paper in the order given in the assignment.

In the upper right hand side of the paper, write;

Your First and Last Name

Physics 122

The date you turn in the assignment.

In your homework show clearly how you obtain your answer. Correct answers without supporting work will not receive full credit.

You are expected to support by your actions the dress and grooming standards and the Code of Honor. Of course you can work together on homework and group quizzes, but those found cheating on exams or giving information to other students about what's on the exam will be reported to the Dean of Students office.
