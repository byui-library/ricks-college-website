1. How to Write a Resume

Many different ideas exist on the proper procedure and style for completing a resume. Some people insist on one thing or another. Although, when it comes right down to it, the resume is yours. Add what you want -- those things that sell you the best. Be sure that the final product is conservative and looks professional. Following are some great things that probably should be included on a resume:

Basic Information
Name should be in a larger font.
Provide address (permanent & school address) including phone #.

Objective Statement
Optional, but recommended.
Keep it concise, understandable, and specific.

Education
List in reverse chronological order.
If you are/were a college student, high school information is optional.
Include school, city, state, degree or major, and graduation date.
List pertinent classes.
GPA is optional. Include only if over 3.0.

Experience
List in reverse chronological order.
Include job title (emphasized), company, city, state, and dates.
Description of duties (emphasize notable achievements/skills).

Additional Information
Awards, honors, and achievements.
Memberships in clubs and organizations.
Community service.
High school and college activities.
Special skills and abilities.
Characteristics, interests, or hobbies.

References (optional)
 State "Available on request."
Or, list them on your resume.

3. Resume Writing Tips

A resume is an advertisement for you! Market yourself well.
It's your resume, so put your personality into it. Bottom line, make it professional.
The purpose of a resume is to solicit an interview.
Try to hand your resume directly to the person who will be doing the hiring.
No "corny" resumes. Most employers dislike gimmicks.
Proof-read your resume! There should be NO mistakes!
Absolutely no misspelled words or incorrect grammar.
Laser print your resume on quality bond paper.
Paper should be physically clean. No smudges, marks, or creases.
Make your resume easy to read. Don't make employers struggle to read it.
Using "bullet points" is helpful if you want your resume to be easy to read.
One page for every 10 years of post college experience.
Keep sentences short and concise. Use action verbs.
Leave out unnecessary words such as: I, me, my, and "Resume of."
Use the bold, underline, or italic function to emphasize particular items.
Possible sections on a resume include:

Education

Experience
Other Experience

Awards and Achievements

Left and right margins should be no smaller than one inch.
Make sure that the document looks balanced and attractive.
Use a conservative, pleasant, and readable font.
Your resume is not a confession sheet. Only list items that sell you to the employer.
Leave these things off of your resume unless the employer specifically requests them: salary, jargon, age, race, religion, sex, physical health description, height & weight, social security number, marital status, photographs, weaknesses, and exaggerations.
