More than one and a half centuries ago, the first Mormon choir sang in a crude bowery on Temple Square in what was to become Salt Lake City. Less than a month had passed since the arrival of the first wagon train, on 24 July 1847, an event that marked the beginning of the settlement of the Great Basin area of the American West.

Today's 340-voice Mormon Tabernacle Choir, supported by one of the world's great pipe organs, is heard by millions worldwide through radio, television, recordings, concert tours, and satellite transmissions.

For its first 46 years, the choir sang mostly in Salt Lake City and other cities established by The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. Its first major concert tour took place in 1893. A special train carried the singers to the Colombian Exposition in Chicago, where they won the second prize of $1,000. The choir has since made numerous national and international tours, singing in such arts capitals as New York, Boston, Philadelphia, Chicago, Los Angeles, San Francisco, Toronto, London, Stockholm, Helsinki, Oslo, Copenhagen, Marseille, Paris, Rome, Berlin, Vienna, Zurich, Geneva, Brussels, Prague, Budapest, Warsaw, Barcelona, Madrid, Lisbon, Moscow, Tokyo, Jerusalem, Tel Aviv, Sydney, Melbourne, Mexico City, and São Paulo.

The choir's first international tour was a 7-week, 12-concert tour of Europe in 1955. The longest tour in terms of distance was a 3-week, 18-concert tour to Hawaii, New Zealand, and Australia in 1988. The choir's most recent concert tour was to southern Europe in 1998. Earlier international tours included Japan in 1979 and 1985; benefit performances for abandoned children in São Paulo, Brazil, in 1981; and a Scandinavian and northern European tour in 1982. This is the choir's 27th major concert tour, 17 of which have been outside the United States. During a 1991 tour to Eastern Europe and the former Soviet Union, the choir sang in 10 languages.

Music critics around the world have attested to the choir's superb sound. A Christchurch, New Zealand, critic wrote, “It was impossible not to be affected by the emotional electricity that crackled in the Town Hall auditorium." In Auckland a reviewer reported, “Whatever the type of music, the sound was unfailingly beautiful, . . . exquisite pianissimo, . . . choral virtuosity would not be an exaggerated description.

Describing a performance in Israel, a critic wrote, “They sound like an opening flower. In 'piano' it opens into a spectacular flower. Their sopranos have come down from heaven, and the tenors have voices out of this world, pouring out so easily. The altos and basses are deep and velvety. Together, their music is like crystal dipped in a flow of warmth." Another Israeli critic added, “With voices like theirs, it's not hard to believe in God.

Special requests for performances have resulted in trips to Washington, D.C., where the choir has performed at the inaugurations of Presidents Lyndon Johnson (1965), Richard Nixon (1969), Ronald Reagan (1981), George Bush (1989), and George W. Bush (2001).

The choir has released more than 150 albums. For several recordings the choir collaborated with Eugene Ormandy and the Philadelphia Orchestra, one of which won a Grammy from the Recording Industry Arts Association. The choir has also recorded under Leonard Bernstein, Maurice Abravanel, and Michael Tilson Thomas, with such soloists as Kiri Te Kanawa, Robert Merrill, Frederica von Stade, Shirley Verrett, Stanford Olson, Sherrill Milnes, and Marilyn Horne. Five of the choir's recordings have attained gold status (sales of 500,000 or more) and two have reached platinum (sales of one million or more). The choir's recordings are currently available on Telarc, Sony, London Decca, and other labels.

Touring and recording have enhanced the choir's reputation, but its weekly nationwide network radio broadcast, now in its 72nd year, has brought the largest audience and the most fame. Millions of listeners tune in each week for Music and the Spoken Word, a half-hour program of music and inspirational thought. The program originates from the Salt Lake Tabernacle at the “Crossroads of the West" and is carried by radio, television, and cable systems around the world. The choir has also been featured in several American network television productions and in 1962 participated in the first intercontinental satellite broadcast from Mt. Rushmore, South Dakota.

Wherever the choir travels, its concert performances are often broadcast live on radio and television and rebroadcast throughout the host countries, sometimes for years after the choir's visit. Such has been the case in recent years in New Zealand, Australia, Canada, Germany, Switzerland, Austria, Hungary, the former Czechoslovakia, Poland, Russia, and Israel.

Each year some five million people from around the world visit Temple Square. Hundreds of thousands of these visitors attend the choir's free weekly public rehearsals and broadcasts as well as many other special concerts. This remarkable volunteer choir, whose members include businessmen, doctors, homemakers, attorneys, educators, and government employees, is one of the best known, best loved musical organizations anywhere. It has been referred to as "America's Choir" by Ronald Reagan, “a national treasure" by George Bush, and "the world's greatest choir" by Eugene Ormandy.

The choir has recently undergone some changes to help it keep pace with the ever-expanding global exposure it receives through audio and video satellite and Internet broadcasts, as well as for its scheduled appearances in connection with the Salt Lake 2002 Winter Games. The Temple Square Chorale and Training School were founded to sharpen the skills of new singers as well as long-time choir members. In addition, the Orchestra at Temple Square was established as a full symphony orchestra consisting entirely of unpaid volunteers. Both the Temple Square Chorale and the Orchestra at Temple Square join with the Tabernacle Choir for concerts, broadcasts, recordings, and performances.
