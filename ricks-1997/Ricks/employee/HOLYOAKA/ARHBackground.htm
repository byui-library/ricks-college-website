Alan R. Holyoak - Professional Background    

I've come full circle in my academic career.   I started my academic experience as a student at Ricks College, and now I'm part of the faculty at BYU-Idaho -- the ultimate in recycling?  I come to BYU-Idaho after 10 years as a member of the biology department at Manchester College in NE Indiana.  My assignment at BYU-I includes teaching courses for non-majors, developing and teaching a course in biostatistics, and other courses needed by the dept. 

While at Manchester College I taught courses for non-majors as well as invertebrate zoology and aquatic biology, marine biology and marine ecology, orientation to research, environmental studies, and more.  I was also director of the environmental studies program and served a three-year, half-time stint as Associate Dean of Academic Affairs.

I earned my PhD at the University of California at Santa Cruz (UCSC).  My dissertation research focused on the development and ecology of colony-forming invertebrate chordates.  While there I received the UCSC Award for Outstanding Teaching Assistant.  I did my lab work at the UCSC Long Marine Lab, and my field sites were at the Hopkins Marine Station of Stanford University, Pacific Grove, CA.

Prior to being at UCSC I spent a year doing doctoral research at the University of Hawaii at Manoa, Honolulu.  My work there looked at aspects of the co-evolution of coral colony form and butterflyfish feeding behavior.  I transferred to UCSC to concentrate more fully on invertebrates.

I earned a masters degree in zoology at BYU-South (er...Provo).  My masters research addressed the behavioral ecology of subtidal marine snails and their various invertebrate predators.  That work was conducted at the Friday Harbor (Marine) Laboratories of the Univ. of Washington, San Juan Island, WA.  

My undergraduate career included a degree in biology from Ricks College, a semester at BYU-Hawaii studying marine biology, and a bachelor's degree from BYU-Provo in zoology  with an aquatic biology emphasis. 

Research interests

  • The biology of modularity
  • The reproduction and development of invertebrate animals
  • The ecology of aquatic organisms and aquatic ecosystems
