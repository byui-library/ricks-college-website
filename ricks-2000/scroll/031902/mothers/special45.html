Rexburg prepares for Mothers’ Weekend

by Laura Bohon
BOH00002@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

As the BYU-Idaho campus prepares for approximately 3,000 mothers to join its ranks during Mothers’ Weekend, the town of Rexburg also prepares.

Many area businesses look forward to the heightened business of Mothers’ Weekend year round.

“There is a major impact when the mothers come,” Donna Benfield, executive director of the Rexburg Chamber of Commerce, said. “They stock up the cupboards ... it’s a major boost to our economy.”

Businesses affected the most include the area’s motels, restaurants and gas stations, Benfield said.

“All the mothers go to the [gas] stations and fill up right before they go home,” she said.

“We have all of our motels filled already and they have been for some time now,” Benfield said.

The town of Rexburg has approximately 220 motel rooms, all of which are reserved for Mothers’ Weekend. The demand for a vacant motel room is so high that surrounding areas have been affected; motels in St. Anthony, Rigby and Driggs are full as well.

Alicia Riley, an employee of the Best Western Cottontree Inn in Rexburg, affirmed Benfield’s statements.

“We are usually completely booked around August or September; moms come and make reservations when they drop their kids off for fall,” Riley said.

Although the weekend is a large source of revenue for the motel, a few precautions must be taken by businesses to prepare.

“One of our biggest problems is making sure we have enough employees on hand that weekend. Some of our employees are students, and they can’t do their studies because we’re so busy,” Riley said.

Other business employees echo her sentiments.

“It’s always very, very busy,” Irean Christensen of A Plus Rent-a-Car of Rexburg said. “We rent almost everything that we have. Mothers call weeks in advance; it’s had a big impact on our business.”

“It’s our busiest time of the year. It gets kind of hectic,” Mandy Sutton, an employee at Big Jud’s Restaurant, said. “We’re already starting to prepare; to make sure we have all the food we need. We just have to make sure to get here early.”

Even businesses outside of Rexburg have seen an influx of business from Mothers’ Weekend. Ben Martin, an employee of Avis Rental Car in Idaho Falls, noted a flood of rentals for every Mothers’ Weekend. For Martin, however, competition has begun to cut into his business’ Mothers’ Weekend sales.

“A lot of mothers are realizing that the shuttles will take them up to the campus,” Martin said. “Two years ago, I had almost 80 reservations for Women’s Week, and only 30 showed.”

Martin is not the only employee to see a drop in sales.

“It’s slower this year than normal,” Christensen said. “We aren’t completely booked.”

However, Mothers’ Weekend will continue to bring a “marked increase in local shopping,” Benfield said.

As local businesses prepare for more customers, enthusiasm is building.

“We look forward to it,” Riley said.
