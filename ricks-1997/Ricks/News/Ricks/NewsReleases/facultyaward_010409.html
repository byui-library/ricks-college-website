Ricks College News Release
April 9, 2001 
Longtime art teacher honored
by his peers at Ricks College

By Jennifer Yates
Ricks College News Service

REXBURG, Idaho — Helping students develop a love for art is one of the main goals of Richard Bird, who was honored April 6 as the recipient of the Exemplary Faculty of the Year Award by the Ricks College Faculty Association.

As part of the prestigious honor that is awarded to one Ricks College faculty member each year, the Rexburg man addressed his peers at Friday night’s Faculty Association banquet.

He said, "I love teaching. It is exciting for me to help someone understand a concept, a process or even in explaining individual steps toward solving a problem."

Bird, who attended Sugar-Salem High School and Ricks College, went on to obtain an MFA degree from Brigham Young University majoring in painting and watercolor. He has taught at Ricks for 30 years and served as director of Graphic Services at Ricks for 23 years.

Besides teaching, he has stayed active as a designer and artist, believing that in order to be a better teacher, he must be a practicing artist.

The award is given to a teacher who exhibits a positive influence in the lives of both students and colleagues, who exemplifies the highest standards and is liked by students and fellow workers, while "staying alive" in his or her teaching area.

Division Honorary Faculty Awards were presented to faculty members receiving the most votes behind the exemplary faculty nomination. The division honorary award recipients were Tony Turley in the Agriculture and Biological Science, Kent Marlor in Behavioral and Social Sciences, Phil Packer in Business, Gordon Black in Engineering and Technology, Kim Van Wagoner in Health and Education, Larry Thompson in Language and Letters, Ed Williams in Physical and Math Sciences and Kathleen Gee in Religious and Family Living.

The Honorary Non-Classroom/Extended Teaching Faculty Award was presented to learning assistant Karl Edwards and the Honorary Adjunct-Part-Time Faculty Award went to Dawn Anderson of the English department.

The association also recognized three faculty members for their innovative contributions in the classroom. This year’s Innovation In Teaching Awards were given to Roy Huff in religion and Stephen McNeil and Jack Weyland, both in physics.

Robyn Bergstrom was presented as the Academic Advisor of the Year Award. Bergstrom, who chosen by the student body, currently is a faculty member of the communications department and participates in forensics.

Retiring faculty members include Moana Boyle, Don Dansie, Alden Partridge, Golden Snell, Tony Turley, Peter Valora and Ed Williams.
