Student compaigns for legistlative seat

Running for political office was just a dream for Sterling Hatch, but being in the right place at the right time with the right ideas made the dream come true: he is currently campaigning for a seat in the Idaho State Legislature.

"This is real," the 21-year-old Ricks College sophomore said. He has had business cards printed, silver ink on navy paper, and a Republican pin glisteng on his conservative suit, a silver elephant on navy pinstripe. He has press releases; he has signs and donors and a campaign slogan - "A Sterling Candidate."

A triple major in agriculture business, animal science and beef production, Hatch doesn't plan to make a career of politics but has always been interested in civic involvement.

Faculty recognized at banquet

Scott Samuelson, an member of the English department, was awarded the 1998 Exemplary Faculty member and many other Ricks College faculty members were honored by the Faculty Association.

Samuelson served as the chairman of the English department, led students on the British Literature Tour, helped initiate the Connections program and instituted the Cohorts program for faculty enrichment.

Exemplary Employee awards were given to Ruby Whittaker, a secretary in the Hyrum Manwaring Student Center who has been at Ricks for seven years and loves the atmosphere; Maurice Wilkey, the video services supervisor who graduated from Brigham Young University and has worked at Ricks for 10 years; and Brent Ashcraft, the Ricks Bookstore controller system administrator who graduated from Brigham Young University and has been at Ricks for 13 years.

Ricks plans to keep up with technology

Technology has the potential of changing virtually every aspect of education, from the books we read to the way we are taught to read them, according to a 1995 article in Issues in Science and Technology.

Max Checketts, assistant academic vice-president for instruction, said one thing to remember when talking about technology and how it will change the future is that it is a new thing for everyone. No one knows what to expect or exactly what it will bring.

A 1995 article from The Record said that virtual classrooms were the wave of the future. Ricks College is involved in a virtual classroom in conjunction with Eastern Idaho Technical College and Madison High School. However, Checketts said that schools are beginning to "re-think" the expense of being involved in such a program.
