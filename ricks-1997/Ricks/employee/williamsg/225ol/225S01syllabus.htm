Welcome

Welcome to the Semester On-line version of Doctrine and Covenants 225. I am excited to participate with you in this experience. Dr. H. Dean Garrett, Professor at BYU, wrote the course and I have been asked to monitor the class on this campus.

This course is unique and will prove to be very rewarding. This course is based on a unique text. The Doctrine and Covenants differs from other scripture in that it was received by prophets in our day. It contains the word and the will of the Lord for us in this dispensation. It contains truths that are only hinted at in other scripture.

One of the unique points of the Doctrine and Covenants is that the story line of the scriptures is not contained in the revelations. However, in order to have a correct understanding of the revelations, they must be studied in their historical setting. For this reason, the beginning of each section will begin with a discussion of the events that brought about the revelation.

This course begins with section 71 of the Doctrine and Covenants. A brief overview of the events that were transpiring in the Church at that time will help one better understand the second half of this inspired work. The Prophet Joseph Smith saw the Father and the Son in the grove of trees on a spring day in 1820. After some time, the Lord sent the angel Moroni to the Prophet to give him instructions. On September 23, 1828, Joseph Smith was given the gold plates to translate. This he did with the aid of several scribes, including Martin Harris and Oliver Cowdery. Many of the first nineteen sections of the Doctrine and Covenants were received during events surrounding the translation of the Book of Mormon. On April 6, 1830, under the direction of the Lord, the Prophet Joseph Smith organized The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter day Saints. Missionary work commenced and the Church grew rapidly. Sections 20 to 35 were received during that time. In December 1830, the Lord gave the command for the members of the Church to move from New York to the Kirtland area in Ohio (section 37). Missionaries had traveled from New York through the Kirtland area on their way to teach the Lamanites (Indians) in the territory west of Jackson County, Missouri. These missionaries converted a large number of souls in the Kirtland area. The Lord gave many revelations to the Saints in the Kirtland area including the Law of the Church (section 42).

After the Saints moved to Ohio, some were commanded to move to the Jackson County area to help establish Zion. The Prophet made a trip to Missouri to visit the Saints and there received revelations in their behalf (sections 57-62). Upon his return to Kirtland, he conducted a series of conferences. During one of these the decision was made to publish some of the revelations which had been received up to that point (section 67). Joseph Smith worked hard to prepare these revelations for the publication that was to be known as Book of Commandments, which contained sixty-five revelations. Sections 68-70 were received during this challenging time. The attempt to publish the Book of Commandments failed because of mob action in Missouri in 1833; however, these revelations, along with others added to them, were finally published in August 1835 in Kirtland, Ohio. This publication was titled The Doctrine and Covenants and contained 102 sections. Since that time, other editions have been issued which include more revelations. Today we have 138 sections of revelation along with two official declarations of the Church.

As pointed out in D&C 88:118, "Seek learning, even by study and also by faith." You will find that you will get the most out of this course when you use both methods of learning. In addition, the Lord has commanded us to study items out in our minds and then ask if they are right. He will confirm or invalidate the answer we have arrived at (D&C 9:7-9). By this method we can learn "the truth of all things" (Moro. 10:5).

Course Objectives

After completing this course and pondering and praying about these great revelations, you should be able to do the following:

1. Better understand the Atonement of Jesus Christ and the personal nature of that Atonement. The revelations that are studied in this course contain a wealth of knowledge concerning the Atonement and how it applies to us personally. You will gain a greater appreciation of the role of repentance and the hope that can come from its application in your life.

2. Better understand and appreciate the Prophet Joseph Smith and the restoration of the gospel of Jesus Christ upon the earth in this dispensation of the fullness of times. You will obtain a much clearer understanding of the role of Joseph Smith as the head of this dispensation and why we emphasize his role in the Church today. Joseph Smith's experiences with God and his messengers, and the revelations given through him, form the foundation of the doctrines of the Church. What we know about our Father in Heaven, our Savior Jesus Christ, and the Holy Ghost is based in a large degree on the experiences and revelations of
Joseph Smith.

3. Better understand the plan of salvation. We learn much about the pre-earth life, the purpose of life, and what the post-earth life will be like, because of the revelations in the Doctrine and Covenants. We find great insights into our relationship with our Father in Heaven and our eternal nature from our study of the Doctrine and Covenants.

4. Realize the importance of continued revelation in the kingdom of God, that the heavens are still open, and that Prophets are on the earth today. A study of the Doctrine and Covenants will help you gain a better understanding of the relationship of the members of the Church to the Prophet, and the Prophet to the Lord. This is the "only true and living" church on the earth (D&C 1:30). One reason for that is that we have continuous revelation in the Church, to its members and through the Prophet. The Holy Ghost plays an active part in the guidance of the Church.

5. Apply the scriptures to your personal life. The great value of studying the Doctrine and Covenants is gaining the knowledge of what our Father in Heaven wants you to do in order to become like him. What has been revealed in these sections is for your benefit (D&C 82:9) and will result in greater spiritual growth if you apply them to your life.

Lessons

Generally, each lesson in the course has four distinct parts: an introduction, lesson objectives (to guide your study), a "How to Proceed" section (steps to follow), and discussion material (background and commentary). You will also be required to read sections from the CES Student Manual. The discussion material in this course manual is subdivided by thought questions and then followed by an assignment to be submitted. These may be printed for future reference and study for assignments and the final exam.

The discussion material in each lesson is further divided into three parts: background, commentary, and thought questions. The background section will help you understand why the revelation came. The commentary, primarily from the prophets, seers, and revelators in this dispensation, will help you understand the material better and will clarify some of the scriptures you have read. The thought questions, which appear periodically throughout the discussion material, are designed to help you review the material and make it part of your understanding. I expect you to answer these questions carefully. They will help you understand what you have read and studied. Thought questions are for your own consideration. You do not need to write out the answers and submit them. These will help you prepare for the assignments. In additon to thought questions, there will be 'Discuss' questions. These questions will be posted on the discussion board located under the COMMUNICATION icon. Take the opportunity to integrate the facts you have learned with some applications for our times.

Of course, the greatest teacher of all is the Holy Ghost. As you search and ponder what you have read, you will gain a clearer understanding of how it applies to your life. Revelation is very personal and is available to all.
