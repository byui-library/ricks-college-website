Honors classes are of two types: 1. Classes with the Honors prefix. These classes are unique to the Honors Program. 2. Classes also available to the general student body, but which are taught in a section exclusively for honors students. These classes have a departmental prefix and are followed by the "H" suffix.

Honors Prefix Classes

These types of classes are included under the following topics. Consult the regular college class schedule to see when specific classes are being offered.

Hon 200 Readings in Western Culture: Ancient World** 3

An examination of selected primary works of the Hebrew, Greek, Roman, and Medieval periods with historical setting and thematic structure provided by three lectures per week. Areas considered include literature, history, and philosophy.

Hon 201 Readings in Western Culture: Modern World* 3

A continuation of Honors 200. Renaissance to the present.

Hon 202 Literature** 1-3

Topics vary: C.S. Lewis, Mark Twain, biography, etc.

Hon 203 The Arts **** 1-3

Topics vary in the fine arts.

Hon 204 Philosophy** 1-3

Topics vary: Ethics.

Hon 206 History* 1-3

Topics vary : Thucydides, Martin Luther, Brigham Young, American West, etc.

Hon 207 Behavior and Society* 1-3

Topics vary in communications, psychology, and sociology: Communication Theory, Social Foundations of Human Behavior, Family and Culture.

Hon 208 State and Economy * 1-3

Topics vary in economics, geography and political sciences.

Hon 212 Physical Science *** 1-3

Topics vary: Geology and Man.

Hon 213 Biology and Man*** 1-3

Topics vary: Biology and Man.

Departmental Prefix Classes

These types of classes are listed with a departmental prefix and are taught in sections exclusively for honors students. Except for the classes listed just below, a course description will be found under the appropriate department elsewhere in this catalog. Courses typically included are: English 111, 250 and 282, Humanities 201 and 202, Ltrs. 201 and 202, and various religion classes. Consult the regular college class schedule to see when specific classes are being offered.

Art 211H Art and Philosophy I**** 3

Three hours per week

The development of art styles from ancient times to the Renaissance, as those styles relate to the philosophy of each period. Includes criteria for evaluating works of art.

Art 212H Art and Philosophy II**** 3

Three hours per week

The development of art styles from the Renaissance into the twentieth century, as those styles relate to the philosophy of each period. Includes criteria for evaluating works of art

Engr 102H Man in Technological Society 3

Prerequisite: two years of high school mathematics.

Three hours per week

Understanding the engineering/ technological world via a study of the engineering design process and the man-made world. Consideration of the social, legal, ethical aspects of the process. Investigation of the engineering profession. A typical engineering student design project will be a major component of the course. Emphasis upon process rather than analytical or detailed component design. Intended for engineering majors in the Honors Program and other interested non-engineering students who are in the Honors Program.

LETTERS AND PHILOSOPHY

These classes, coordinated through the Honors Program, are available to the general student body and honors students, depending on the listing in the class schedule each semester.

Ltrs 201** Our Intellectual Heritage I 3

Three hours per week

An analysis of basic topics (God, freedom, organizations, knowledge, arts) that are meaningful in our lives as seen through literature, philosophy, science, religion, art, and history. Ancient through Medieval periods.

Ltrs 202** Our Intellectual Heritage II 3

Three hours per week

An analysis of basic topics (God, freedom, organizations, knowledge, arts) that are meaningful in our lives as seen through literature, philosophy, science, religion, art, and history. Renaissance through modern times.

Phil 110** Introduction to Philosophy 3

Three hours per week

Articulating, assessing, and defending fundamental positions on topics such as reason, knowledge, science, education, ethics, politics, and religion.

Phil 201** History of Philosophy: Ancient and Medieval 3

Three hours per week

History of western philosophy from Greek antiquity through the Middle Ages. An examination of its historical development and leading texts.

Phil 202** History of Philosophy: Modern 3

Three hours per week

History of western philosophy from the Renaissance to the present. An examination of its historical development and leading texts.

Phil 203* History of Religious Thought 3

Three hours per week

Is not considered as a religion class

The development of religious thought, particularly Western religious thought, and its influence in the world, from antiquity to the Christian Era.

Phil 204* History of Religious Thought 3

Three hours per week

Is not considered as a religion class

The development of religious thought, particularly Western religious thought, and its influence in the world, from the Christian Era to modern times.

* Gives G E credit in Social Science

** Gives G E credit in the Letters area of the Arts & Letters requirement.

*** Gives G E credit in Natural Science

**** Gives G E credit in the Arts area of the Arts & Letters requirement
