Students participate in Make a Difference Week

by Lacey Smith
SMI01025@byui.edu
Scroll Staff

For students looking to make a difference in the world, the Students in Free Enterprise (SIFE) club may be the answer.

According to a SIFE “Changing the World” brochure, more than 1,000 college and university campuses in 25 countries host SIFE teams dedicated to “improving the quality of life and standard of living for millions of people around the world.”

Last week found students participating in various activities as “Make a Difference Week” took place.

“Eveyrone during this week partners up with businesses and higher education places to apply free enterprise to the nation,” Erika Miller, a sophomore from Ogden, Utah, said. “We go to competitions and are judged on projects we do during this week.”

SIFE is divided into five different committees that are each geared towards a specific area.

On Monday and Friday the financial and business areas teamed up to go into two different Idaho Falls high schools where they taught students principles of economics. They played an economic marketing game that the students really seemed to enjoy; one student even stayed for two class periods and told teacher that he stayed because he found something he could succeed in, Miller said.

Tuesday found the international committee traveling to the LDS Montessori School to Jason Richardson’s class where they taught 4th and 6th grade students bout how the September 11 tragedy affected the domestic and world economy. Walmart donated 1,000 cards for these elementary-age students to send to people in New York.

“Another school in a different state did the same thing,” Miller said. “This two-state project will [help us] in competition.”

The technology team hosted a welcoming class for all interested students and community members on Thursday night in the John Taylor Building. Various topics were covered including how to buy a web domain and how to design a personal web site to make it appealing.

“It gave a lot of good tips for those who are interested in making their own successful website,” Miller said.

On Friday the education committee went to Madison High School and taught students interviewing skills and how to network (find connections) in the job field. Students were challenged to have people interview them in the next couple of weeks, Miller said. Then the students will meet with the committee, and the person with the most interviews will receive a prize.

Saturday topped off the week as “Make a Difference Day” across the nation. This day, created in 1992 by US Magazine, exists as a day when service projects are carried out by different organizations.

The new venture committee comprises the final committee. Steve Dalby, a sophomore from Minneapolis, Minn. and Brad Harker, a ______ from ____, are two of the masterminds behind the creation of a new food card. Some of the nicest restaurants in Idaho Falls, including the Texas Roadhouse, Jakers, Applebees, Sizzlers, Garcias, and Wingers, will offer various discounts for food card holders as part of this new venture.

“Anyone planning to go out two or three times a year will save money,” Dalby said. “The whole idea is for students and married couples to be able to go out and take advantage of the card.”

Miller summed things up when she said, “Companies like to hire SIFE students because they are the ones who are out making a difference. Once you’ve done 50 service hours you can put your resume on internet, and it’s there for life. If you put effort into it, you can have a job by the time you graduate.”
