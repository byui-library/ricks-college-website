Pillow Problem

In 1895, Charles Dodgson (a.k.a. Lewis Carroll--author of Alice in Wonderland and other children books)  was a noted Mathematician  who taught at Christ's Church in Oxford, England.  Before nodding off to sleep, Dodgson would often solve certain math problems in his head.  Dodgson compiled these problems in a book he titled   "Pillow Problems."  While the following problem is not one of Dodgsons,  It is cute and simple enough to do in your head before entering your dreamworld of "Mad Hatters" and "Cheshire Cats."

            It is possible to take an irrational number (a), raise this number to an irrational power (b) and come up with an answer (c) that is a rational number. In other words,  ab = c where a and b are irrational and c is rational.  Find specific values of a, b, and c that make this equation true.

If you come up with a solution  type it up and turn it in to me.   I could probably find about 10 points of extra credit to reward you with.   Sort of the "Tooth Fairy" idea.    Remember this problem should be done in your head, without help from others, preferably before going to sleep.  If your  head starts to ache over this problem and you can't sleep, and since you are the chosen, whoops I mean calculator generation,  I guess you could use your black box.  Only as a last resort though.  In your solution you turn in tell me if you were able to do it in your head or did you use a little "extra calculator" help. Also, if you should receive startling revelation on this problem during daytime, that's O.K. too.  When you do come up with a solution, I ask that you don't share it with others until after the deadline has passed.
