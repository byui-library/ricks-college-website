September 14, 2001

BYU-Idaho enrollment shows 
changes in upperclassmen, married students

     
REXBURG, Idaho – The official fall enrollment count is in at BYU-Idaho and there are some definite changes from past years. The most marked differences are found in the number of upperclassmen, married students, returned missionaries and transfer students enrolled.

A record 9,200 students are enrolled at the Rexburg university for fall semester. The number meets the target that administrators were hoping for as the school begins offering bachelor’s degrees along with traditional associate degrees.

The number includes 1,654 students who are classified as juniors or seniors (having 60 or more credits).

The total is slightly higher than last fall’s enrollment of 8,949, but the significance lies in the composition of these numbers.

Attending BYU-Idaho this fall are 1,058 married students, nearly doubling last year’s 546. Another increase is noted in the number of returned missionaries attending the university this semester: a total of 2,975, up 22 percent from last year’s 2,448. The third noticeable difference is found in the number of students transferring to BYU-Idaho from other schools. The fall semester brought in 2,625 transfer students, up from last fall semester’s 2,129.

Kevin Miyasaki, the university registrar, says the numbers were expected to rise, but not quite so much. "We are very excited about the number of students, especially the married students and the returned missionaries. We are very pleased with the students who have enrolled, although the numbers were a little surprising."

The rest of the registration numbers are nearly identical to last year. The university has 6,348 continuing students, with 771 returning after an absence of one or more semesters, including those who are re-entering after serving church missions.

There are 5,309 females (58 percent) compared to 3,890 male students.

Full-time number of students total 8,654, with 546 going part time.

Students from all 50 states are represented in the university. Idaho has 3,228; Utah, 1,309; California, 845; and Washington, 732. There are 245 international students representing 43 countries. Of these, 134 are from Canada.

Statistics are based on enrollment after the first 10 days of class.
