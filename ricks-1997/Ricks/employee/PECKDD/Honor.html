THE RICKS COLLEGE CODE OF HONOR

  • Be Honest
  • Live a chaste and virtuous life
  • Obey the law including college regulations
  • Use clean language and exemplify modesty and cleanliness
  • Respect the rights of others
  • Abstain from alcoholic beverages, tobacco, tea, coffee and drugs
  • Help others fulfill their responsibilities under the Code of Honor
  • Participate regularly in church services
  • Observe college dress and grooming standards
 

THE CODE OF HONOR AND YOU

In this class you are expected to observe the Code of Honor: above all, be honest! Refrain from plagiarizing or any form of cheating. This means that you will be scrupulous in grading quizzes, and in making sure that your assignments are your own work. I will rely upon your integrity in grading quizzes and other materials. By allowing you to grade your own quizzes you have the opportunity to quickly analyze and correct your errors. Please do not abuse this privilege.

What's more: Obey all the college rules and regulations. Remember that the Ricks Standards are generally the product of decades, even generations, of experience with young people. They may not be perfect, but they represent the wisest and most insightful guidelines for your health and spiritual welfare that the faculty and administration can formulate.

The Dress Code

Our dress is a significant part of our environment. I take this opportunity to remind you of the commitment you made to live the Code of Honor when you signed your application form to Ricks. The Code of Honor is your personal pledge to live an upright and moral life. Since college is a place where we continue to civilize ourselves, where we come to appreciate the value of noble conversation, modest and courteous behavior, it is natural that the General Authoritiesof the church, and the administration and faculty at Ricks should want our students to civilize themselves in respect to their dress. Learning to dress appropriately is just another dimension of our commitment to "lay hold upon every good thing."

Our clean and modest dress may also serve to bind us together as a community of Christians committed to a style of our own, untroubled by the world around us. It may symbolize our stability, our simplicity and courage in the face of the obsessive and eccentric gestures of a self-promoting world obsessed with somehow being noticed or praised. Modest dress sets the "ego" in a nice balance between being an individual and being a member of the Body of Christ. The intention of the Ricks College Administration in promoting clean modest dress is part of a deep concern to create an uplifting, wholesome environment at Ricks.

The dress and grooming standards are fully defined in the "R" book and in the Ricks College catalogue. There are some specific details that you should understand; however, the essentials are expressed in two words: modesty and cleanliness. Of course, the saintly life cannot be realized entirely in terms of outward appearances. But the committed saint will want to express his inward values openly. Thus, often times our dress reflects our inward commitments. The Book of Mormon discusses the modest dress standards of saints:

 

And they [the saints] did impart of their substance, every man according to that which he had, to the poor, and to the needy, and the sick, and the afflicted, and they did not wear costly [i.e. strange, outlandish, vain, vulgar, eccentric] apparel, yet they were neat and comely.
Alma 1:27

Let's have our dress express both our rejection of worldliness and our commitment to possess everything that is "lovely, praiseworthy or of good report."
