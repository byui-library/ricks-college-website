Guidelines for Application Essays
"Simply put, choosing to do the will of God at all hazards brings a righteous and necessary self-awareness and self-confidence, a perfect faith in God and in our ability to do his will.  We then know something about ourselves that God has known all along.  President Hugh B. Brown, in answer to the question of why Abraham was asked to 'offer his only hope [Isaac] for the promised posterity," said:  'Abraham needed to learn something about Abraham.'" (Larry E. Dahl, The Abrahamic Test, in A Witness of Jesus Christ: The 1989 Sperry Symposium on the Old Testament, p. 64)

Purpose

    The above quote seeks to establish the purpose of this kind of an application opportunity.  When this process is understood and accomplished under the influence of the spirit, you should come away having learned something about you that will either give you the confidence necessary to exercise more faith in Christ and better seek to build His kingdom, or change whatever is necessary to develop this confidence.  If you will listen to the promptings of the spirit, He will direct you into paths of self-awareness that are healthy and beneficial.  If you seek to simply fulfill an "assignment", the experience could become unhealthy, demeaning, and of no value.  Therefore, I cannot overstate the extreme need to have the spirit involved in this process.  If there be anything in your life regarding serious sin that would keep you from having the spirit, STOP!  This opportunity is not for you!  Seek to do what is neccessary (repent?) to receive revelation from the spirit before you attempt such a process.
    However, if you feel you are ready to be taught from above, please consider the words of C.S. Lewis:

"We begin to notice, besides our particular sinful acts, our sinfulness;  begin to be alarmed not only about what we do, but about what we are.  . . . [S]urely what a man does when he is taken off his guard is the best evidence for what sort of a man he is.  Surely what pops out before the man has time to put on a disguise is the truth.  If there are rats in the cellar you are most likely to see them if you go in very suddenly.  But the suddenness does not create the rats:  it only prevents them from hiding.  In the same way the suddenness of the provocation does not make me an ill-tempered man: it only shows me what an ill-tempered man I am.  The rats are always there in the cellar, but if you go in shouting and noisily they will have taken cover before you switch on the light." (Mere Christianity, pp. 164-65)
    Seeking to apply gospel principles in our lives is a necessity.  However, I have noticed in my own life that often my application is superficial where I discover what I do, but never why I do it.  It is easy to see my sinful acts, but I often fail to see what it is about my character that allows the sinful acts to occur in the first place.  We need to take application to a deeper, more meaningful level where we are recognizing elements of our character which stand in the way of becoming more like Christ.  It is the rats in the cellar that you are seeking to discover in this process.  So in an attempt to take application to a more meaningful level, here are the guidelines for a process that is intended to provide many opportunities to be "provoked" and see the rats.

Guidelines

    You must recieve personal revelation for this process to be of any value to you.  You have the responsibility to go to the Lord and seek understanding.  The degree to which you are able to do so will be reflected in the depth of the essay you write.  This "assignment" will be impossible to accomplish properly without you receiving personal revelation.  Also remember that issues concerning the nature of serious sin should not be delt with here.

Writing, and then reading what you wrote will be key to your experience.  Therefore, an essay format will be used in this process.  The essay should contain four major sections.  INCLUDE THE SECTION HEADINGS IN YOUR ESSAY!  Don't forget this as it will help you distinquish thoughts and ideas in your mind!  If you plan on not writing, then quit now and forget it!

Section #1   General  Begin by selecting a doctrine or principle from a list of choices found under "Class Articles" for your specific class.  They will be principles or doctrines we will be covering in class.  Begin by studying and researching the topic you selected from the list.  Go beyond the simple "Bible Dictionary" approach to your understanding.  What have the Brethren taught?  What do the scriptures themselves say?  After you feel you have sufficiently gained a greater understanding, begin typing your essay by writing a "General" summary of what you learned.  There is no need to regurgitate everything.  Just the  highlights or the things that impressed you most will do.

Section #2   Process   Having gained a greater understanding of your doctrine, it is now time to make plans as to how you will go about applying the doctrine in your life.  Here is where revelation will be key.  Once you feel you have a pretty good idea of your plan, type the second portion of the paper by spelling out what your plan is.  Please know that during the application process itself, the process will change.  You can come back to this part of the paper and modify it as you go along.



It is now time to begin the actual application process.  I suggest that you seek to apply this principle or doctrine in your life for at least two weeks.  The longer the better.  As you begin applying this gospel principle or doctrine in your life, the attempt will provoke thoughts, feelings, and actions.  Pay attention to these in addition to what you may continue to learn about the doctrine/principle you are applying.  Write these thoughts feelings and actions down in a journal of sorts.  Look over them again and again during the two weeks.  Begin asking yourself why you felt the way you did.  Why did you think that? Why did you act or react the way you did?  Really try to understand what it is about you that motivated these thoughts, feelings, and actions.  Are they in harmony with the thoughts, feelings, and actions of the Savior?  If not, why not?  What is standing in the way?  What needs to happen so they can be brought into subjection to the will of the Lord?  Continue to ponder these ideas for the two weeks and be ready to respond to them in the Self-awareness section of the essay.  Please don't forget that personal revelation will be the key to all of this!


Now that the two week application period is over, complete the following sections of the essay:

Section #3   Application   In this section, portray for yourself  what you learned about application itself.  How well did you execute your process?  When was it hard?  When was it easy?  What kind of people were you around when it was hard or easy?  What will you do differently next time you apply something?  Anything else?  Make this precise.  Don't ramble.  Be specific and to the point.

Section #4   Self-Awareness   Return to your journal of thoughts, feelings, and actions. Look over them again and again.  Begin asking yourself why you felt the way you did.  Why did you think that? Why did you act or react the way you did?  Really try to understand what it is about you that motivated these thoughts, feelings, and actions.  Are they in harmony with the thoughts, feelings, and actions of the Savior?  If not, why not?  What is standing in the way?  What needs to happen so they can be brought into subjection to the will of the Lord?  What do the answers to your "why" questions suggest about your character?  Continue to ponder these ideas for the two weeks and be ready to respond to them in the Self-awareness section of the essay.  Please don't forget that personal revelation will be the key to all of this!   Be honest with yourself as you write.  You will be amazed with what you learn about yourself as you read over what you have written.

Final Notes

If you would like me to read over this and perhaps respond with some "provoking" thoughts, please remember to include the section headings in your paper, and make sure that these essays include your Name, Class and Hour, and are Typed, Double Spaced in a 12 font, and Stapled.   This will allow me to better respond to your experience.

I will repeat that you have the responsibility to go to the Lord and receive revelation for this assignment.  The degree to which you are able to do so will be reflected in the depth of the essay you write.  I also reiterate that issues concerning the nature of serious sin should not be shared with me and I am confident that if you write this under the influence of the Holy Ghost, that will not happen.  May you come to learn something new about you that God has known all along!  Good luck!
