Subject Verb
 

 

 

 As you read these web pages and work through the exercises,  realize that in learning terms, you often need to understand one term in order to understand another.  Ideally, it would be good if we could learn the meanings of several words simultaneously.  Yet, we can only learn one term at a time.
 
Consequently, if you are weak in your understanding of grammar concepts, study all the materials completely.  Remember that everything will make more sense the second time through, and the third, and the fourth.  Re-study the pages until all the concepts are completely clear.

Subjects  Predicates

A sentence can be divided into two parts, a subject and a predicate.  The predicate includes the verb and the words which come after the verb.  For example, Mark Singleton drove the race car.  "Mark Singleton" is the subject; "drove the race car" is the predicate.

Verbs 

When you analyze a sentence, first identify the verb.  The verb names and asserts the action of the sentence. Consider, "Eating fourteen toads gave Harold a stomachache."  The verb is "gave"; the predicate is "gave Harold a stomachache"; the subject is "eating fourteen toads."  Verbs identify our activities.  For example, we eat, sleep, run, jump, study, think, digest, shout, walk, etc.

Be Verbs

Verbs also show a state of being.  Such verbs, called BE VERBS or LINKING VERBS, include words such as: am, is, are, was, were, be, been, being, became, seem, appear, and sometimes verbs of the senses like tastes, feels, looks, hears, and smells.  Consider this sentence:  Crabs and lobsters are my favorite sea foods.  The verb "are" is a linking (be) verb.

Finding Subjects

Once you determine the verb, ask, "who or what" of the verb.  This will locate the subject(s).  For example: Mark Singleton drove the race car.  Who "drove the race car"?  Mark Singleton  the subject.  Eating fourteen toads gave Harold a stomachache.  What "gave Harold a stomachache"?  Eating fourteen toads  the subject.  Crab and lobster are my favorite sea foods.  What "are my favorite sea foods"?  Crab and lobster  the subjects.  The subject(s) of a sentence will answer the questions, "who or what."

Understood Subjects

A complete sentence must contain a subject and a verb.  But sometimes the subject is ony "understood."  If I say, "Come to my house for dinner," the subject of "come" is "you."  (You) come to my house for dinner.  In this instance, (you) is understood.
