VICE PRESIDENT LEAVING COLLEGE FOR MISSION ASSIGNMENT

Employees, students and community friends will have a chance to say goodbye to Community Services Vice President Brent Kinghorn and his wife, Donna Jean, on his last day of work, May 31, from 2-4 p.m. in the executive offices reception area located in the Kimball Building.

The Kinghorns will soon be leaving for Albania where he has been called to serve as president of the Albania Tirana Mission. He will take a three-year leave of absence from the college with the prospect of a job upon his return if he so chooses. The couple will enter the Mission Training Center in Provo, Utah, June 24. After a week of intensive training, they are expected to reach the capital city of Tirana where they will live July 3.

Kinghorn is in his 34th year of working at Ricks. During his years at Ricks he served with seven presidents, beginning with President John Clarke and including Henry B. Eyring, Bruce C. Hafen, Joe J. Christensen, Steven D. Bennion and David A Bednar. He also worked with Harry Maxwell who served as acting president for a year.

ALL GIRLS CLOG TEAM WIN GRAND CHAMPIONS AWARDS

The summer school all girls clog team won two grand champion awards in the Western National Clogging Championships at Lagoon in Farmington, Utah last weekend. The team is taught by Charles West, chairman of the Dance Department and former president of the National Folk Association. "They were awesome," says West. "They did us proud!" The winning numbers will be included in the Dancer's Showcase on June 5 at 9:10 a.m. in the Kirkham Auditorium. Other summer school dance classes will also be participating including social dance, additional clog teams, and jazz dance. The program will demonstrate excellence in creativity, choreography, costumes, and teamwork.

CONSTRUCTION UPDATE - GROUND BREAKING PLANNED FOR NEW SPORI BUILDING

Taking advantage of a time when fewer students are on campus, Ricks College looks like it's under full construction this summer. Preliminary ground work has started on two major new buildings -- the new Spori Building and a yet unnamed multi-use building. Major renovation of the McKay Library is just about finished, and work continues on the Smith Building and the Manwaring Center. A women's residential hall is being converted into family housing, and two floors of a men's dorm are being converted into temporary faculty offices.

Spori Building: Elder Henry B. Eyring will oversee the official ground-breaking ceremony for the Jacob Spori Building on Tuesday, May 29, at 2:30 p.m., in the Kirkham Auditorium. Students and employees of Ricks College and the general public are invited to attend. Following the services participants may join at the building site at about 3:30 p.m. for the formal ground- breaking ceremony.

Excavation and blasting on the foundation are under way even before all aspects of the building's final design have been completed. Edstrom Construction Inc. of Rexburg is excavating the site that already had a partial basement from the prior building. College officials hope to finalize the design of the new building soon with a bid opening in June. Construction is expected to start in early July and take about 20 months. The building will house the communications and art departments with completion in 2003.

Multi-Use Building: Blasting and excavation are proceeding on the new 54,000 square foot building between South Second East Street and the demonstration gardens. The building will serve both religious and academic needs as the college prepares for future growth. Because a standard plan already is designed, the building can be constructed quickly. Completion will take about 14 months, and it will be ready for use for Fall Semester 2002. The building is patterned after the institute buildings at Idaho State University, University of Utah, Salt Lake Community College, and Dixie State College. The contractor is Jacobsen Construction of Salt Lake City, and the architect is Jensen Haslem of Logan.

Faculty offices in Rigby Hall: The first and second floors of the south wing of Rigby Hall will be converted into temporary offices for about 40 faculty members later this summer. The men's dorm rooms will be used as offices for several years while new buildings are under construction to accommodate the expanding faculty.

McKay Library: Work is essentially complete on a major addition and renovation of the David O. McKay Library. A total of 144,000 square feet of library was renovated during the last year and a half. The transformation of the former administration building into the east wing of the library was finished last year. This project involved nearly gutting the building, constructing new rooms and installing a new heating, ventilation and air conditioning system, new electrical system, new lighting and new carpeting.

Smith Building: Work will continue for a second summer on the Joseph Fielding Smith Building. R.E. Beck of Idaho Falls is continuing work it started last summer on the project to solve heating and cooling problems that evolved with expanded use of computers in the building. This summer's work is focused on the fourth floor, which is now closed to faculty and students. Work involves a new ventilation system and cabling for computer labs on the floor. Work will be completed in early August, but additional work will be necessary in future summers on the second and third floors.

Lamprecht Hall: Work has started on converting a single women's hall into family housing. Lamprecht Hall, also known as Dorm 5, will be switched from housing for more than 200 single women to 37 units of family housing. The job has been contracted to Double E Construction of Ririe, and the units are expected to be ready for occupancy for Winter Semester of 2002. Remodeling will involve converting the current three-bedroom units into two-bedroom units with a kitchen and living area.

Manwaring Center: The Manwaring Center will be kept open as work continues on an extensive renovation to the buildings's ventilation system. New ductwork, ceilings and lights are being added in the kitchen and East Ballroom areas.

East Viking Drive: East Viking Drive near South Second East will be rebuilt with new subsurface soil drains. Work will be done by Beco Construction of Idaho Falls and is expected to be finished by the end of June.
