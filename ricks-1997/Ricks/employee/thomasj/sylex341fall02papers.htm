Religion 341 (Thomas)

Expanded instructions on Lesson Plans and Case Files

 

Instructions that go beyond the syllabus are in italics.  I hope this will be helpful to you.

 

4. Prepare two original, typewritten lesson plans to teach:

i.                     One of the chapters in the CES Manual (1-23).

ii.                   A gospel principle illustrated with multiple examples from Church history.

Your lesson plan should outline the following things: intended audience; purpose; teacher preparation resources; suggestions for lesson development—introduction, discussion and application, conclusion. You can find models of such lesson plans in the curriculum section of the Church web page. All sources should be documented.

                *Think of the format for your typewritten lesson plan as follows:

First, a series of brief statements reporting (A) Your intended audience; (B) The purpose or purposes of the lesson; and (C) All resources for the teacher to study in preparation for the lesson—this would include all materials that are used at any point in the lesson, plus any background resources that might help—for the first plan, start with the relevant chapter in the textbook, and add articles, scriptures, talks, videos, etc that might help a teacher prepare. Basic expectation: you will identify at least one clear purpose for the lesson, and you will identify at least two resources beyond the textbook for a teacher to use in preparation.

Second, a series of descriptions, quotations, questions, etc that tell what and how to teach in each part of the lesson: (A) Introduction; (B) Discussion and application; (C) Conclusion. Be specific about what is being taught and how it will be taught—identify the main ideas being taught, and type the questions, supporting statements, and other learning activities that will help each idea get across to your audience. Section B of this second part should be the longest section of the lesson plan. Basic expectation: You will have at least two questions and one other learning activity in part A; and you will have at least two main ideas to teach in part B, with at least two questions, two supporting statements, and one other learning activity for each main idea.

                Just below the conclusion of your lesson plan, add a third section. Here you describe what you learned from teaching the lesson to a “class.” If you do not want to teach the lesson, you need to ask two “teachers” to read and critique the plan, focusing on what you plan to teach and how you plan to teach it. In this section, you should write the main suggestions others gave you, and you should identify specific changes you would make based upon the teaching or review experience. For our purposes, a “teacher” is anyone who has a call or an assignment to teach and a “class” is two or more people other than you. Basic expectation: Describe the teaching or review experience, and identify at least four specific suggestions or changes you would make as a result of the experience.

Due dates: The first plan (on a chapter in the book) is due no later than class on Thursday, Oct.17 (that is a change from the syllabus). The second plan (on a gospel principle) is due no later than Thursday, Nov. 21.

 

5. Find and collect two “case files” to learn and teach about:

i.                     The “times of need” and “real-life situations” that led to prophetic inquiry and divine revelation in the early church (see Explanatory Introduction of the Doctrine and Covenants).

ii.                   The consequences of obeying or rejecting revealed counsel in the lives of early Saints.

Start your search in the textbook (see pages 650-52, 661-66) and History of the Church (7 vols.). Extend it by looking at Church and historical periodicals, and consider also unpublished sources. The focus of your case files should be contemporary materials—records and reflections of those participated in the early history of the Church.

The format and basic expectations for the case files is as follows: First, identify the revelation with a full and accurate reference--for Case File 1, it must be a section in the Doctrine and Covenants; for Case File 2, it must either be a reference in the Doctrine and Covenants or counsel given by Joseph Smith or the entire First Presidency by 1844.  Second, type at least two statements made by a person who lived at the time of the revelation. These statements must not be found in the textbook. For Case File 1, the statements should illuminate the “times of need” or “real-life situation” associated with the section. For Case File 2, you should have at least one statement about obeying the revealed counsel, and at least one statement about disobeying the same revealed counsel. The reference for every statement must be fully and accurately cited.

 

                Due dates: Case File 1 is due no later than class on Thursday, Oct. 31. Case File 2 is due no later than class on Thursday, Dec. 5.
