THIRD ANNUAL EDUCATION WEEK DRAWS SEVERAL THOUSAND TO RICKS

From growing teenagers to retired grandfathers, several thousand converged on the Ricks College campus June 1-3 for a unique educational experience during the college's third annual Education Week. Gospel-centered learning on topics as varied as getting teenagers to work to the power of music attracted 2,700 people from throughout the western United States and Canada to the Idaho campus. Squeezed in between the first and second summer terms, the three-day event offered 348 classes taught by 71 experts in their various fields of study.

Elder L. Aldin Porter, Senior President of the Presidency of the Seventy, spoke of the importance of the Holy Ghost in one's live during a morning devotional June 2. He said the Holy Ghost "is the most powerful tool the Lord has ever given to us by which we can learn. . . It is a tool largely unused." While the Holy Ghost is active in the Church today, he said much of the world lives in a day of spiritual darkness that will continue to increase in intensity. He said, "We can be too busy to pay attention to the Spirit. . . . The best men and women on earth can be immune from the Spirit if they are not listening - if there is not some quiet time."

He counseled people to listen carefully for the promptings of the Holy Ghost. "We have to learn how to learn the language of the Holy Spirit so we can come to know it when the Spirit speaks to us." Elder Porter added, "The Holy Ghost will give us utterance. The mother who teaches in the home will be given utterance in those very special teaching moments . . . for the Lord loves those children under her care."

Other morning devotional speakers included Ricks President David A. Bednar and his wife, Susan, and Anne Wirthlin, former First Counselor in the Primary General Presidency. Campus Education Week will be held next year from June 7-9, 2001. It is sponsored by the Ricks College Division of Continuing Education.

RECORD NUMBER OF DEGREES AWARDED BY RICKS COLLEGE

The annual summer graduation at Ricks College was held May 31 completing a record year in number of diplomas awarded by the largest private junior college in the United States. According to Ricks Registrar Kelly Hymas, 593 students applied to graduate following completion of course work during the summer. A total of 624 degrees were awarded, bringing the total to a record 3,812 degrees for the school year. The traditional summer commencement followed the first five-week summer term that ended May 30 and included students who will be completing graduation requirements during one of the three summer terms.

Dean R. Sorensen, a retiring faculty member and former academic vice president Ricks, was the commencement speaker. "Life is very large; time is fleeting. We must choose carefully what to let go of and what to hold on to," he said. "We can safely let go of the things we cannot carry into eternal life, among them pretense, worry, discontent, and self-seeking. We can confidently hold fast to those things we can carry into eternal life--learning, common duties, friendships, the happiness of today, and faith."

Sorensen said perhaps the most distinctive thing about learning at Ricks is that a student can plunge confidently into the world of academia without losing their spiritual moorings. "Your testimony when properly exercised, becomes your most important scholarly asset, for it gives you a true frame of reference by which you can anchor all your learning," he said.

He added that one of the pitfalls of higher education is its tendency to reduce the religious commitment in many capable students. "This sometimes happens when the values of students are challenged by scholars who, lacking the Gospel, teach that all knowledge is contextual; that truth and error, good and evil are all relative to the situation," he said. "Faith can also be undermined when intellectual attainment prompts feelings of pride and self-sufficiency, the same way riches often do. You have avoided these pitfalls at Ricks. For while you have been exposed to the full range of scholarly disciplines, your faith and conviction in the Gospel of Jesus Christ have grown, and thus, so has your ability to measure and gauge the philosophies and fashions of the day through a standard that is enduring, unchanging and true."

In speaking to the graduates, Ricks President David A. Bednar counseled them to appreciate the present. Referencing Philippians 4:11, he urged them to follow Paul's example to be content with " . . . where we are and what we have at any particular time in our lives."

He explained, "Now, I am not suggesting we passively resign ourselves to any and all circumstances that may come our way in life. Each of us is an agent with the power of independent action, and we have the ability to influence the circumstances of our lives in significant ways. Nor am I suggesting us to live only in and for the present moment." He further cautioned that "it is possible to waste away our lives wishing the past would return and hoping the future will arrive more quickly. It is also possible to be so busy dealing with the details of daily living that we fail to recognize the small miracles occurring regularly in our lives. You and I must learn to appreciate the present."
