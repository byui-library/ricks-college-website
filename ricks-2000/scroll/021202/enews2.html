Love is in the air at the Winter Olympics

SALT LAKE CITY (AP) — Happy Valentine’s Day. Now pass me my rifle.

From biathletes to figure skaters, lovestruck competitors at the Winter Olympic Games likely will be cuddling each other on Valentine's Day Thursday with 11 married couples here at the Games plus who knows how many partners.

In many cases, they’re also partners together in competition such as in figure skating where the husband throws his wife into the air hoping that she will land safely instead of in an embarrassing heap on the ice.

Not surprisingly, seven of the married couples here are figure skaters, some with a chance of gaining medals.

Elsewhere, it could be coach and athlete such as Romanian figure skater Gheorghe Chiper who is married to his Swiss coach and choreographer, Sandra Schaer. Chiper began competition on Tuesday and wasn’t considered a strong medal prospect.

By contrast, French biathlete Liv Grete Poiree won a silver in the 15K race on Monday while husband Raphael placed 10th in the men's 20K.

“We are together almost all the time. If we are not, we normally will go training,” Liv Grete said. “When we're finished with training we think about other things, like normal people.”

“I haven't been home since the first of December and, if I have a husband who works at home, it would be very hard for me to stay away. Now, we train together, and he's a big motivation.”

Bronze medalist finisher behind Poiree in the women’s race was Magdalene Forsberg whose husband, Henrik, was in the men’s 20K later in the day on the same course at Soldier Hollow.

Magdalena, considered the best woman biathlete ever with 12 medals in World Cup events, collected her first Olympic medal while her husband so far hasn’t got anything to match it. He placed 47th.

Polish pairs skaters Dorota Zagorska is likely to get a Valentine's card off husband Mariusz Siudek on Thursday. After all, he did propose to her at the closing banquet of the 2000 World Championships on the romantic French Riviera and they got married the following spring.

Of all the married couples at the figure skating arena, Russia's Irina Lobacheva and Ilia Averbukh stand a good chance of a medal.

Married for nearly seven years, they placed fifth at the last Olympics at Nagano, Japan, in 1998 and were bronze medalists at the world championship at Vancouver last year.

Switzerland’s Maya Pedersen is likely to go home with a medal from the women's skeleton but husband Snorre, with a best of 13th at world championships, is likely to lose out.

Germany’s Gunda Niemann-Stirnemann, a multiple speedskating gold medalist, would have been going for more Olympic records and medal had she not become pregnant ahead of the Olympics. She will deliver in June. This time around she is reduced to commentating for the German public broadcaster ARD-ZDF.

Speedskating coach Peter Mueller won’t have much time for sweet-nothings on Valentine's Day. Three days later his wife Marianne Timmer will be going for gold in defense of her Olympic 1,000 meters title.
