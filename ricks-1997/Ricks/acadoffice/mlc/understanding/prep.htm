Understanding

Personal Preparation & Worthiness

Posted by Anne Killian (Nursing) on January 17, 2000 --

The other day as I was finally looking at the information that had been gathered from the Ricks College Family on the subject of understanding, I noticed that there were no comments under this heading, Personal Preparation and Worthiness. (I thought that is was, no doubt because I was so late in becoming a part of the dialogue and just maybe everything written before 1/1/2000 was erased by Y2K because I can't believe it is because there is no one that will venture into the realm of worthiness.) So, I have made my humble attempt to address this topic because I feel that it is vital to all that we do. My prayer is that what I write might be understandable.

In Alma Chapter 17:2-3, it talks a little bit about how the Son's of Mosiah prepared themselves to be men of understanding, "...for they had searched the scriptures diligently, that they might know the word of God." When we think of Alma and the Son's of Mosiah in relationship to what they needed to understand at the time the angel appeared, it seems that these men had a dramatic need to apply the atonement to their lives. The Lord has identified the essence of his gospel as "...repent and believe in me." (3 Ne. 11:32) We become men and women of sound understanding as we relate the "Word" as "God" (John 1) to our own lives. As we look at our own lives with the Savior's life as the standard, oft times it leaves us feeling a need to change, to repent, something that maybe the Son's of Mosiah did during those times when "...they had given themselves to much prayer and fasting" (Al 17:3).

As it is through the process of repentance that we are cleansed, we subsequently become more worthy and better prepared to hear and understand not only the promptings of the Holy Ghost, but the strugglings in the spirit of those around us. That people have problems in applying "The Word" to their lives is evidenced by the states of hopelessness, never-ending guilt and spiritual pain some Christians seem to exist in. We can only understand them by The Gift of the Holy Ghost. Nephi explained that the Holy Ghost can bring all things to our rememberance that are expedient for us to do. (2 Ne. 32:3-6) With some of the expected behaviors of being a Christian being 1-a comfort to those who stand in need of comfort and 2-standing as witnesses of God, (Mosiah 18:8-10) then surely part of our role would be to be able to understand the pains and the afflictions of each other so that we can mete out that which should be meted unto every man in the very hour that they need it (D&C 100:5-6). The ability to recognize needs is one of the blessed gifts granted to us by the Holy Ghost (D&C 46). Whether it be in terms of their relationship to God or in their relationships to others, (which James and Mosiah clearly identify as pure religion) or even in their future course, we can help by being in tune with the spirit and opening our mouths that they may be filled with the words of life.

Having experimented on The Word of God and applying it to our lives gives all of us experience and as the Lord says, "shall be for [our] good" (D&C122:7). To me that means going through the good and bad times, suffering the consequences for our actions and repenting.  Somehow in God's definition of understanding, ignorance is not the best experience of life. There has to be as Lehi put it, a 'compounding in one' (2 Ne. 2:11) of at least the 'understanding' of righteousness and wickedness so that we can choose the former and despise the later. We have to recognize the existance of both and choose. 

In the next week I will be starting a Spanish class for the nursing students that will be going down to Mexico next fall. What I am going to tell them is that there is only one group of beings that understand exactly what is going to be required of them down there and even in their lives, who can help them to prepare for that work, and that is God. Through the Holy Ghost we can be prompted in our studies to focus our attention and seek understanding of that which will help us develop our God given talents and thus prepare us to be useful in helping our fellow beings pass through the experiences of life in unique ways. That applies no matter whether it be in our own language or in a second language and we are admonished by Nephi that we must not perform any thing unto the Lord save in the first place "ye shall pray unto the Father in the name of Christ, that he will consecrate thy performance unto thee, that thy performance may be for the welfare of thy soul."  After all the Savior said, "in as much as ye do it unto one of the least of them my brethern, ye do it unto me" (Matt. 25:40). Are we not all providing some form of service?

It was the Prophet Joseph Smith, I believe, that told Brigham Young to tell the Saints to get the spriit and keep it. As we become worthy to recevie the promptings of the Holy Ghost through the process of repenting which is our personal preparation, we become worthy to receive even more promptings from the Holy Ghost. By acting upon the promptings of the Holy Ghost that we receive, true understanding comes, along with more promptings to act as men and women of understanding who are enabled to choose the way of life. Helping others relate these verities of God to all that is going on in the world and their lives is key to increasing the understanding of all men here on earth. As confusion reigns among men in the world, it behooves us to search more diligently to understand and know "The Word" so that we can practice this gift through responding ever more quickly and fully to the promptings of the spirit in opening our mouths and letting them be filled with the knowledge, wisdom and power that can come only from Him.

I recall the words of the Prophet, Ezra Taft Benson, when he was speaking to the church about Pride. He said that we must know how the Lord uses the word in order to be able to apply correctly the Lord's standard to our own lives. I hope and pray that we can all become men and women of sound understanding having the fullness of such brought to pass in our lives through repenting and searching out "The Word" so that we might be able to truly help our fellow brothers and sisters grasp the reality of the Savior's role in saving us all, for isn't this true   understanding?
