Newspapers

Current newspapers are located near the periodicals. 
Newspapers tend to be published more often than periodicals. 
The distinction between newspapers and periodicals blurs. 
Indexing has been one distinction. 
Indexes to periodicals tend to not index newspapers as completely, if at all. 

Microforms

Microform is a term used to describe materials that are microscopically reproduced. Microforms save space. 
A month of newspapers can easily be stored on film that will fit in your hand. 
Microfilm is like uncut photographic film. Special machines are used to read or to make a photocopy of the information on the microfilm. Lenses may allow different degrees of enlargement. Near the current newspapers are cabinets with

The New York Times

  • started publication September 18, 1851.
  • was indexed from first volume and issue (Index AI21.N44)
    • 1851 to 1858 gave only issue number
    • 1913 and 1947 was divided into quarters
    • early entries were hand written
    • no "see" entries appeared until print issues
    • index was originally intended for the newspaper staff use
    • dates, pages, and column numbers are abbreviated
    • Current paper indexes are issued twice a month.
    • Quarterly and annual cumulative indexes are issued.
    • Look at the current issues for help translating abbreviations.
  • indexed by ProQuest & Academic Abstracts
  • covers major events in United States and world
    • use dates to find local coverage
    • theatrical productions New York Theatre Critics' Reviews. (Ref PN2000.N76)
  • has New York Times Magazine in Sunday edition
  • has New York Times Book Reviews in Sunday edition
  • New York Times Obituaries Index 1858-1968 (Ref CT213.N47)

Personal Name Index to "The New York Times Index" (Index Z5301.F28)

  • is NOT published by the New York Times
  • covers personal names
  • covers all the indexes from 1851 to 1974;
  • uses supplements to cover the time since the original set
  • gives only which year and page of the index
YOUNG, BRIGHAM has 53 entries from 1858 to 1970 in volume 22 of the oldest set.
    The first entry for 1858 gives page 296.
    1877 has two entries with a 'd' by them.
        Brigham Young died in 1877.
        The entries with the 'd' cover his death.
    Twenty entries follow the 1877 entry for his death.
    The supplement has his birth and death dates in parentheses, (1801-1877).

Steps needed to use the information.

  1. Consult the index for 1858.
  2. Look on page 296.
  3. On page 296 "YOUNG, Brigham" has the cross reference to "see" UTAH.
  4. On page 296 "UTAH" has this entry
    • "Young financial investments noted, N 15,8:1."
  5. Translate entry.
    • Month and day follow the summary of the article. November 15, 1858
    • No section is given. Only one section was printed for that date.
    • Page is separated from the column by a colon. Page 8: Column 1.
  6. Go to the microfilm and find the roll that covers, November 15, 1858.
  7. Turn to the date, section, page, and column.
"Capone, Alphonse (Scarface) - 1925, IV, 98;"
  1. Consult the index for 1925.
  2. Find the fourth quarter of the 1925 index.
    • During that time the index is broken into quarters.
    • October to December is the fourth quarter.
  3. On page 98 find... etc.

Digests and compilations

Facts on File is a digest of news events.

A digest condenses articles.
Facts on File condenses news stories from wire services, press releases, and other news sources.
Looseleaf binders and a CD ROM disk contain the full text of condensed news articles and maps.

An index in the back of the looseleaf has an explanation of how to use it.
This weekly publication is a great place to find the latest governmental nominations and appointments.
Copy the whole reference for those that seem useful from the looseleaf binder.

U.S. GOVERNMENT -- Cabinet & White House Staff -- Appointments & Resignations.
"Members listed 1-10, 144F2; 7-3 756G2"

Translation: January 10, [1995] page 144 margin letter F column 2;
July 3, [1995] page 756 margin letter G column 2.
The compact disc version of Facts on File uses a format similar to the ones used by Academic Abstracts.
Use the introductory screens or help key for details.

NewsBank is a compilation of news articles.

  • paper indexes (Index HT 108.N49) 1993-1995
    • Copy the complete entry
    • Use the key to abbreviations at the bottom of the printed index
    • Example:
    HOMELESS PERSONS
    See also Runaway and Homeless Youth
        children = subtopic Homeless Persons -- children
        studies and reports (chart) = subtopic Homeless Persons -- studies & reports [has a chart]
            Iowa - 1995 WEL 21:D5-6  = subtopic Homeless Persons -- studies & reports -- Iowa [1995 = date; WEL = abbreviation of subject fiche; 21= fiche #; D 5-6 = grid coordinates]
     
    • Go to the microfiche cabinet
    • Look in the drawer for the year: 1995
    • Look for the abbreviation: WEL
    • Find the fiche number in the upper corner: 21
    • Use a microfiche reader to find the row and column: D5-6
    • Copy down the information:
      1. Author of the article, if a byline was given.
      2. "Title of the article"
      3. Name of the newspaper.
      4. (State) in which the newspaper is located
        • (parentheses) for Chicago style
        • [square brackets] for MLA style
      5. Date of the newspaper
      6. Located in NewsBank [microform], category, year, fiche number, and grids.,
    • Format for footnote or bibliography
      • Phoebe Wall Howard, "Iowa homeless: half are children," Des Moines (Iowa) Register, August 24, 1995 (Located in NewsBank [Microform], Welfare, 1995, 21:D5-6, fiche).

    search format or syntax

    1. Phrases may need quotation marks.
    2. Capitalization may limit results. LOWER CASE letters should be the first choice.
    3. Truncation may be allowed by using *. Some searches truncate right and left.
    4. Boolean operators may be abbreviated to + (plus sign) or - (minus sign).
    5. Comma (,) is used to separate names and titles in some Internet searches.
    6. Phrases such as "link:" and "title:" and "url:" and "domain:" are used to restrict a search to a specific part of the Internet homepages.
    7. "Near" and "ADJ" are used to search for words that appear next to each other but not just as a phrase.
    8. Most searches have tips or examples to help you be familiar with those available.
