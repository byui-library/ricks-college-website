Brigham Young University–Idaho offers students a superb educational opportunity through its China travel study program. The tour allows students to experience, in context and on site, many of the most significant sights, sounds and places in Chinese civilization. Participants travel for four weeks with knowledgeable LDS educators, following one week of intensive coursework on campus (April 29–May 3, 2002.

Students must enroll in at least 6 credit hours, selecting from courses offered in Chinese culture, English, Business Management and Communication. All travel study participants are also required to complete a one-credit seminar prior to departure

 

 

 

 

Ease Into China via Hong Kong  

 Following our intensive week-long classroom experience on campus, we’ll fly from Salt Lake City to the Pearl of the Orient: Hong Kong. Here we will stay at the Ann Black Guest House, just a short walk from the hustle and bustle of Nathan Road, the main artery of Kowloon. You’ll have lots of free time in Hong Kong for exploring, (and shopping, of course) but we’ll also take some group excursions. We’ll ascend Victoria Peak via a very steep tram, take a trip to Stanley on double-decker busses, and take a cruise through Hong Kong harbor, with a stop on Lama Island for some excellent seafood.

To the PRC by Train

Just when you think you’re doing well at finding your way around Hong Kong on the MTR (mass-transit railway), we’ll pack our bags and hop a train for Guangzhou, capital of Guangdong Province, just across the border from Hong Kong. Here we’ll explore one of the most famous food markets in all of China and visit Shamian Island. After just one night in Guangzhou we fly to Guilin, land of the famous karst mountains—some of the most beautiful countryside in the world. We’ll spend a day in Guilin and a couple more just to the south in a quaint little village called Yangshuo. You’ll be free to explore the countryside on bicycle, take boat trips down the Li River, or even go spelunking (if you’re not afraid to get muddy).

After our stint in bucolic Yangshuo, we’ll head westward to Chongqing, where we’ll board a cruise ship to take us down the longest river in China, the Yangtze.

We’ll spend three days on the river, sailing through the breathtaking Three Gorges, just in time before they are drowned by  the largest hydroelectric dam in  the world, scheduled to be finished in just a few years. We’ll debark in Wuhan, where we’ll look at daily life of both urban and rural workers of contemporary China. From Wuhan we’ll take an overnight train to Xi’an, the ancient capital of Qin, Han and Tang dynasty China. The famous Terra Cotta Warriors will be just one of the magnificent sights here. After Xi’an we’ll visit Chengde, and finally Beijing. Of course we’ll have time to explore the Great Wall, Tiananmen Square, the Forbidden City, etc.

Your Tour Director, Scott Galer (BYU-I Foreign Language faculty), has lived and traveled extensively in China. He speaks fluent Mandarin and knows where the cool stuff is. Please don’t miss this opportunity to acquaint yourself with the country that a fifth of the world’s population calls home.  

How Much Is It?

The anticipated travel portion of the tour is $3,199. This includes round-trip airfare from Salt Lake City, all travel expenses in China, all hotels, meals, museum entrance fees, boat rides, and tickets to entertainment events throughout the trip. In essence, the cost includes all expenses except passport ($60) and personal spending money (a little goes a long way in China). Note: Tuition fees and the cost of room and board during the one-week on-campus instruction are NOT included in the tour cost.

Admission to the Program

Acceptance to the BYU-Idaho China travel study program does not constitute regular admission to BYU–Idaho. Non-BYU-Idaho-students may apply and be admitted to the program without being accepted as a matriculated BYU–Idaho student.

Program participants will receive BYU-Idaho credit on a standard transcript.

Applicants should complete:

•  an application form (stamped by Cashiers Office showing receipt of $300 deposit)

• Assumption of Risk and Release Agreement

 [These forms are available in  the Academic Office (290 Kimball), Language and Letters Office (227 Smith) or from Scott Galer (467 Smith)]. Application does not guarantee admission to the program—only approximately 40 participants can be accommodated in the program. Admission will largely be based on a first-come, first-served basis.  

Passports: Passports must be given to the tour director by April 1st (so they can be processed at the Chinese Embassy for a group visa).

Passports cost $60 and take  about 6 weeks to receive.  For $95 they can be processed in 2 weeks.  There are a variety of materials you will have to submit to receive a passport (birth certificate, etc.) so apply early to avoid disappointment.
