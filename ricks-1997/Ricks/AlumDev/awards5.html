Outstanding Teacher Award
Patricia Boyle Christensen 

Over twenty years of teaching eighth-grade English has not diminished Patricia's love of trying to shape the minds of young people. Now retired, she'll head for Mexico City for two years this August with her husband, Mark, to volunteer-teach English at an LDS Church-owned high school called Benemerito. Patricia traces her love of what teachers ought to be to two Ricks College instructors in the late 1950s, Ruth Barrus and Betty Peterson. "I knew that they loved me," she says of her former teachers. A native of Ririe, Patricia graduated from Ricks College in 1959, pursuing a degree in business. Two years later, with a hint of what was to come, she graduated from Brigham Young University with a Bachelor of Science degree in business education. For two years she then taught at Lehi High School in Utah but turned to secretarial work a year after marriage. In 1966 she returned with her family to the Upper Snake River Valley and taught at Bonneville High School and in the Madison School District for one year each. After moving to California and raising her children, Patricia and her family again returned to Idaho. She became a substitute teacher in 1977 and a year later taught social studies at Bonneville Junior High School. She then certified as an English teacher and spent the remainder of her public school career as an eighth-grade instructor, retiring in 1999. In all, she has taught for about 25 years.

Patricia and her husband are the parents of five children and have 14 grandchildren. Over the years they have also had several American-Indian foster children in their home. Patricia and her husband are stake missionaries in the Ucon Third Ward, Ucon Idaho Stake. She has been president of her ward's Relief Society and was a counselor in three Relief Society presidencies. She is also the ward organist, "for too many years to count," she says.
