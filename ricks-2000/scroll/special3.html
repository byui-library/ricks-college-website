Prepare early, winterize car

by Bridget Dalin
dal00001@byui.edu
Scroll Staff

Tina Kent, a sophomore from The Dalles, Ore., checks her antifreeze in preparation for the cold Idaho winter.
With winter come big changes in Rexburg. Because the weather changes, students must prepare for the cold, snowy days to come.

Many students come from warmer states and they have never had to drive in winter conditions before.

BYU-Idaho students who have a car here need to take proper steps to winterize their car so they will be prepared for winter driving conditions.

Jake Niederer, a junior from Hamer, Idaho, knows exactly what has to be done to winterize his car.

He changes the oil and coolant if needed and insists that students who own a car with a diesel engine find an electrical outlet to plug their engine into.

“I put a piece of cardboard in front of the radiator to keep the car warm,” Niederer said. This helps the car run hotter and gets the temperature up to normal quicker.

The two most important things students need to do to get their cars ready for winter are get the battery tested and check the antifreeze, Damion Barney, a mechanic at Barney Auto Repair, said.

“Cold weather is hard on batteries,” Barney said. “If your battery is weak, it won’t start in cold weather.”

Barney also said they fix a lot of motors because the antifreeze freezes up. Those kinds of repairs can get expensive.

Another wise step students can take towards properly winterizing their cars is making sure their cars have good tires.

“If you have bad tires, you won’t go anywhere,” Barney said.

Students should be able to go to any local mechanic shop or dealership to get these simple steps done, and it is very cost efficient.

Several places in Rexburg include Barney Auto Repair, Call’s Sinclair Service Station, Taylor Chevrolet Olds Buick GEO Company, Stone’s Town and Country Motors Inc. and D&T Auto Repair.

Driving safely is crucial in places with hard winters. Properly preparing a car for winter is the first step in attaining that safety.
