Bats, balls, bases, brothers — it’s a family thing
Lindstroms’ brotherly love helps strengthen baseball team

by Benjamin Martin
MAR00002@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Matt Lindstrom hands a baseball to his brother Rob during practice in the Hart Building. These brothers support each other while playing on the team. Emily Warr / Scroll
Baseball coach Don Scheiss wants his team to be like a family. With Matt and Rob Lindstrom on the team, he doesn’t have to try very hard.

Matt and Rob are brothers from Rexburg, and both are starters on the Ricks College baseball team; Matt is the No.1 pitcher and Rob is the third baseman. Although this is Rob’s first year playing for Ricks College and Matt has been away from the game serving an LDS mission, Scheiss said he feels they will be a good addition to the team.

“While they both lack experience, they both have a lot of natural ability,” Scheiss said.

Having them on the team together is helping to speed up the growth process, he said.

“They are 100 percent behind each other,” Scheiss said. “It’s good for them to play with that camaraderie.”

The brothers have both been playing baseball since they were 9 years old, starting in Little League and progressing from there. They have not been on the same team until now, however, and even that was not planned.

Rob had not planned on playing for Ricks College. In fact, he has already graduated from BYU-Idaho, but sees this as a way to spend time with his brother, who he has not seen much in the last four years.

“Matt was in the [MTC] when I got home from my mission, so we only saw each other once in four years,” Rob said.

Most of the time they were not even in the same country, with Rob serving in the Germany Munich Mission and Matt in the Sweden Stockholm Mission.

While Matt played for the team before leaving on his mission, Rob’s main motivation for trying out for the team was the time with his brother. His natural ability helped him make the team, as he beat out two scholarship players for the starting spot at third, Matt said.

Regardless of their motivation, Scheiss said he is glad to have them on the team, he said.

“As a team, we try to get as family-like as we can,” Scheiss said. “While they both need to make their own way, they have a warm competitive relationship that helps them grow.”

Rob is enjoying playing for the team, he said.

“It’s like my dad’s dream fulfilled,” he said.

The only time they seem to feel any competition with each other is during batting practice when Matt will throw a few pitches to Rob.

“I gear up more for him than anyone else,” Rob said.

During the games, however, it is a different story.

“There’s no competition between each other. It’s not like we get jealous,” Matt said.

Watching each other play can even give the brothers a boost while on the field.

“Since I only play one game in four, I enjoy watching him play and excel when I’m not in the game,” Matt said.

Rob feels the same when his younger brother is on the mound, he said.

“When I’m on third and I catch a ball, I enjoy bringing it to my brother. I hope it gives him some support,” Rob said.

Matt said it definitely does.

Both of the brothers have interest in eventually playing for the Major League, with Matt hoping his fastball can get him in to the draft this year and Rob considering a few more years of Division One college ball before moving on.

“Our futures really depend on this season,” Matt said.

“We’re going to play to win every game, but realistically, we are going to lose some,” Rob said.

Scheiss is optimistic with regards to the playing ability of both brothers, he said.

“Rob has made some major league plays at third, and Matt has all kinds of potential on the mound,” Scheiss said.

Rob can see the growth in himself as well.

“Every game I learn something new. Coach is not really teaching me anything physically, but it’s the mental part of the game that’s important,” he said.

Both Lindstroms gave thanks to their mother for supporting their baseball games as children in Little League.

“Even though she was soft- spoken at games, we knew she was there, and we appreciated the support,” Rob said.
