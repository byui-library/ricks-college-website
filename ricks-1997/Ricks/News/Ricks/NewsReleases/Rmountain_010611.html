Ricks College News Release
June 11, 2001

Ricks College will not reapply

for permit to paint giant 'R'

    
REXBURG, Idaho –The east side of North Menan Butte that is presently home of the giant block "R" may eventually return to its natural state, say Ricks College officials, who have decided not to reapply this year for a permit with the federal Bureau of Land Management to re-paint the letter.

With the college changing its name to become Brigham Young University-Idaho, officials say this may be the most appropriate time to abandon the "R" that stands for Ricks.

Student Life Vice President Robert Wilkes says safety of students was the main reason for the college’s decision. The college has serious concerns about potential liability that could result from accidents involving the steep slope, rolling rocks, and lime, which is used in a mixture of white cement and water that burns the skin and eyes.

The college also acknowledges the desire by some people to erase the "R" from the volcanic mountainside by allowing it to return to a natural state.

Because the "R" is located on BLM ground, the college has been required to reapply for a permit every three years. "There has also been some uncertainty about long-term access to the site," Wilkes says. "The BLM has been very good to work with us."

College officials will work with the BLM to begin the process of restoring the site.

The "R" was designed and built in 1962 by Mathletes, a college math club and has been painted biennially as part of homecoming festivities.
