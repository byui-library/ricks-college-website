ACADEMIC POLICIES

REGISTRAR'S OFFICE
The Registrar's Office processes all student records after the student has been accepted by the Admissions Office. Registration and graduation are both functions of the Registrar's Office, as well as handling and dispersing of forms which change student records. Transcripts are kept up to date in this office and mailed out upon written request from the student.

POLICIES
Transcripts Requests
Transcripts of your academic record cannot be ordered over the telephone. You must come to the Registrar's Office and present your photo ID to request a transcript in person. If you are requesting a transcript by mail or fax, the following information must be included: Full name and all names used while attending Ricks, your U.S. Social Security Number, or the Ricks ID number, date of birth, your dates of attendance, your current mailing address, and the address where the transcript is to be mailed. Your request must include your signature. The Registrar's Office fax number is (208) 356-1035.

FULL TIME STUDENT STATUS
Students must be registered for 12 credit hours to have full time student status. This is a requirement for International students, for full Pell Grant awards, Stafford Student Loans, SLS, PLUS, private scholarships, on-campus employment, health insurance coverage, varsity athletic competition, Social Security benefits, full Veterans' benefits, running for student government offices, and fulfilling major appointive offices in student government. The full-time equivalency for Block registration is 6 credit hours and for a Summer Term, 4 credit hours.

RELIGION REQUIREMENT
The study of religion is the core of academics at Ricks College. Religious study provides the basis for our very existence as a Church school.

Eight credits in religion are required for graduation. Students must enroll and receive a passing grade in a religion class anytime until they have fulfilled the eight credits required for graduation.

Only one religion class per semester may count toward the religion graduation requirement. A student may, with the permission of the Religion Department Chairman, take two religion classes during a semester; but the second class will be counted as an elective and not toward fulfillment of the religion requirement.

If a student takes a religion class during one of the summer terms or during the Christmas break, that class will count toward the eight hours in religion required for graduation. Any time following the taking of a summer or Christmas class, a student, with permission from the Religion Department Chairman, may be excused from enrolling in a religion class one semester.

Part-time or block students must take one religion course for each 16 credit hours earned at Ricks.

Students not registered for a religion course during Fall or Winter Semesters without an approved waiver, or students registered for a religion class who do not earn a passing grade, will not be permitted to continue enrollment at Ricks until they have completed at least one semester at another educational institution. Exceptions must be approved by the Religion Department Chairman, Smith 271.

ATTENDANCE AND ABSENCE FROM CLASS
Students are responsible for their attendance in the courses for which they are enrolled. No absences, whether approved by the college for participation in college sponsored activities or necessitated by sickness or other personal emergency, are "excused" in the sense of relieving the student of responsibility for work assigned or carried on by a class during the student's absence. It is the student's responsibility to arrange with instructors to make up missed work. A student who has been absent from class has lost some of the content of the courses, regardless of the cause of absence. In most classes, repeated absences will cause the student to receive a lower grade or even fail the class.

ACADEMIC HONESTY
As part of the Ricks College Code of Honor, each student is expected to be honest in all academic work and to help others fulfill the same responsibility. One serious form of academic dishonesty is plagiarism, presenting another person's writing or ideas as if they were one's own. Most writing required at Ricks College should consist of sentences and paragraphs of the student's own composition. Care should be taken to identify information that is not original with the student.

Minor offenses may result in an "F" on the project, paper, exam, or assignment, or in the necessity to repeat the work for a reduced grade, and/or in a lower final grade for the course.

Major offenses which involved premeditated cheating may result in an "F" for the course and action by the Dean of Students Office such as probation or dismissal from school. Some offenses may also have legal implications. The student has the right to know the specific charge, hear the evidence and have an appropriate hearing, if needed.

ACADEMIC STANDARDS
Probation, Suspension, Re-Admission, and Dismissal
Your academic standing at Ricks is determined by two measures of your work. One `is your grade point average (GPA), and the other is the satisfactory progress you make in completing courses for which you have registered. Grades are considered to be either "progress" grades or "non-progress" grades. "Progress" grades are in the D- to A range; "non-progress" grades are F, W, and repeats (if you repeat a course in which you received a passing grade (D- or above), the original grade is then also counted as a "non-progress" grade).

SATISFACTORY ACADEMIC STANDING
You are making satisfactory academic progress when your cumulative GPA is 2.0 or above and when more than 75 percent of your grades are "progress" grades. The 75% policy is tracked only for full time (12 or more credits) students; it is not traced for Second Block students or Summer School Term students.

ACADEMIC PROBATION
You will be placed on academic probation when your cumulative GPA (including transfer courses) is between 1.00 and 1.99 or when your progress is below 75 percent.

If, while on probation, you complete the next semester with 12 earned credits (6 block or 4 summer term) with a 2.0 GPA of better, you will be continued on probation rather than being placed on suspension. Any "non-progress" grades received would still be calculated into your 75 percent progress.
When you are placed on probation, you should contact your faculty advisor immediately. It is also recommended that you take advantage of the resources in the Study Skills Lab and the Tutoring Center. You should also plan to repeat those courses in which you received a D or F grade.
If you are receiving college or federal financial aid, check with the Financial Aid Office to see if you are still eligible. If you have an on-campus job, check with the Student Employment Office to see if you are still eligible.

ACADEMIC SUSPENSION
One or more of three situations will cause you to be placed on academic suspension:
(1)When your first semester/term or any subsequent term GPA is below 1.0, you will be suspended immediately;
(2)When on academic probation, you fail to bring your cumulative GPA to 2.0 or above, or you fail to receive atleast a 2.0 GPA for the semester / term; or
(3)When you receive additional "non-progress" grades and fail to raise your progress above 75 percent during your current semester/term. Suspended students are denied continued enrollment at Ricks. Therefore, if you are suspended and desire to return to Ricks, you must meet the provisions for re-admission as explained below.

If you have pre-registered and/or paid any tuition for a subsequent semester / term, you must withdraw from school and arrange for a tuition refund.

RE-ADMISSION
After being placed on academic suspension, two conditions for readmission must be met:
(1) Students on academic suspension will not be allowed to return to Ricks for twelve months (two major semesters and all three summer terms). For example: If you are placed on academic suspension in December 1997, you may not apply for re-admission until Winter Semester 1999 (missing Winter 1998, Summer 1998, and Fall 1998); or if you are placed on academic suspension in May 1998, you may not apply for re-admission until Fall Semester 1999 (missing Fall 1998, Winter 1999, and Summer 1999).
(2) Students will be allowed to return only if they have completed 12 semester (18 quarter) credits with a 2.0 GPA at another college or university, or if they have completed a full-time mission for the Church.

ACADEMIC DISMISSAL
Students who have been academically suspended from Ricks for a second time are usually placed on academic dismissal. Your academic history will be carefully reviewed by the Academic Standards Committee, and school officials may determine that you have exhausted your opportunities to study and progress academically at Ricks. If you wish to continue your formal education, you will need to seek other options.

College Level Examination Program (CLEP)
Students may receive college credit for CLEP exam as specified on the approved list in the Testing Center. To receive credit, a student must score a minimum of 610 on the English general exam, 500 on the other general exam (with the exception of Math) and 50 on subject exam. Earned credit hours will be posted as CLEP credit with a "P" grade. Such credit may not be converted to a letter grade or correlated with an existing Ricks College course.

NOTE: BYU will not accept CLEP scores below 610 or subject exam below 50. Students intending to transfer credit by examination should check with potential institutions for specific credit-by-exam transfer policies.

Vertical Credit
Subject to Department Chair approval, students who have earned a grade of "C" or better in a class for which there are prerequisite course(s) they have not taken, may be given credit and a "Pass" grade for the prerequisite course(s). To apply, students must consult with the appropriate Department Chair. If he or she approves, an application may be picked up at the Registrar's Office, where a credit fee will be assessed. The fee is $15 for 0.5 to 3.0 credits, with an additional $5 per credit, but not to exceed $25. Although vertical credit counts toward total credit requirements for graduation, no grade is given for GPA computation.

GRADING SYSTEM
Grades are determined by each Instructor who bases his/her decision on an evaluation of reports, written exercises, and examinations. Classroom/laboratory contribution, mastery of pertinent skills, and promise of continuing success in sequential courses in the fields relating to the subject, are all valuable criteria used to evaluate progress.
"A" is an honor grade indicating superior achievement.
"B" is an honor grade indicating commendable mastery.
"C" indicates satisfactory ability.
"D" indicates substandard progress and insufficient evidence of ability to succeed in sequential courses.
"W" If a student officially drops a class during the first ten class days of a semester, the first five class days of second block, or the first four class days of a summer term, the permanent academic record will not show a registration for that class. If the student drops a class after the time periods stated above, the record will be marked "W".
"F" failing, indicates inadequate mastery of pertinent skills, or repeated absence from class.
"I" is a conditional grade. It indicates that the student has not completed course work due to circumstances beyond his or her control but has made a passing grade in all work completed in the course. It is not a substitute for a failing grade and is given only when the student can complete the prescribed course work without further attendance in the classroom. Fees and special instructions in obtaining an "I" grade and how to make up work is available at the Registrar's Office.

AUDIT CLASSES
Tuition is assessed for audit classes on the same basis as for credit classes (See Registration Fees). A student may not petition for credit or apply for credit by special examination for classes taken without credit (as an auditor) after the time has passed for adding classes.

A student who registers for a class as an auditor is not responsible for attendance, daily preparations or examinations. No grade is reported or recorded on the permanent record. An incomplete grade may not be made up by repeating the class as an auditor.

PASS/FAIL CLASSES
A limited number of classes are available on a "Pass/Fail" basis. Students who enroll in these classes will not receive the usual letter grade for the classes, but will either pass or fail depending on whether or not the criteria of the class has been met. When applicable, pass/fail classes will fill General Education requirements. All pass credits will count toward graduation, but they will not be included in the calculations of the GPA.

AMOUNT OF CREDIT PER TERM
Sixteen semester credit hours is the amount recommended for full-time students. Twenty-one credit hours is the recommended maximum. Additional credits may be taken after obtaining the necessary approval from the student's faculty advisor. The student will be charged a non-refundable fee of $30 for each credit over 21 credits. A one-half credit will be charged the same as one full credit.

CLASS PERIODS
The regular class period has a duration of fifty minutes. Laboratory periods are of two and three hours' duration. Three hours of laboratory work or two hours laboratory and one hour preparation outside of the laboratory per week for the full semester are required for one credit hour.

REPEATING CLASSES
Some courses at Ricks are repeatable for credit(ie., 100-level classes and internship); however, these cannot be repeated without losing the credit earned the first time the course was taken. For example, if a student takes REL. 121 one semester and receives a passing grade, and takes the same course again in later semester, the student will earn only two credits, not four.

Students may repeat at Ricks, classes taken at Ricks in the following ways:
a. Repeat at Ricks classes taken at Ricks (except those which are repeatable for credit).
b. Repeat at Ricks, classes taken at another college. (A statement of equivalency must be supplied by the appropriate Ricks Department Chair).
c. Repeat at another college classes taken there and have the credit transferred to Ricks.
d. Repeat at another college, classes taken at Ricks, and have the credit transferred to Ricks.
NOTE: When a class has been repeated, the grade used to calculate the GPA is the last grade earned and not the better of the two grades.
NOTE: When anticipating repeating at Ricks, classes taken at another college, students should check with the school to which they plan to transfer after leaving Ricks. Different policies apply at different schools in relation to a course being repeated at an institution other than the one at which it was originally taken.

ACADEMIC RENEWAL
In conformity with the principles of repentance and forgiveness, Ricks College will allow a student to petition, under certain circumstances, for academic renewal. This means that previous poor academic work, either at Ricks or another college or university, would be eliminated from the computation of credits and grade points in the student's academic record. Eligibility for academic renewal shall be subject to the following conditions: At the time the petition is filed, a minimum of four (4) years shall have elapsed since the most recent course work to be disregarded was completed.
The student must complete at least one semester as a full-time student (12 credits or more) with a grade point average of 2.5 before petitioning for academic renewal. If the petition qualifies under this policy, the student's permanent academic record shall be suitably annotated to indicate that no work taken during the disregarded semester(s), even if satisfactory, may apply toward graduation requirements. However, all work will remain on the records, ensuring a true and accurate academic history. This policy will not be used for individual courses.
Students should also be aware that this policy may not be accepted at transfer institutions. Academic renewal may be used only once during a student's academic career at Ricks College.

CONFIDENTIALITY OF STUDENT RECORDS
With the passage of the federal Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act of 1974, certain requirements are placed upon colleges and universities concerning student records. The Act applies to educational institutions which receive federal funds under programs administered by the U.S. Commissioner of Education.
Upon presentation of appropriate identification and under circumstances which preclude alteration or mutilation of records, a student will be able to inspect all records which are not considered by the college to be held under a pledge of confidentiality or considered to be private records of college personnel. A student is entitled to an explanation of any recorded data and may initiate action leading to a hearing, if necessary, to correct or expunge information he considers inaccurate or misleading.

Parents are entitled to receive grade reports and other pertinent information about a student enrolled at Ricks College if such student is, at the time the request is made, a dependent of the parents. Such dependency is assumed until satisfactory evidence to the contrary is presented in writing to be included in the student's official file in the Registrar's Office.
Faculty and administrative officers who have legitimate need to use student records will be allowed access to such records as needed without prior permission of the student. Data will be furnished to college accrediting bodies and government officials without permission of the student. Academic information about students will not be given to any third party (with the above exceptions) without written consent of the student and then only from records accessible to the student.

Public information will be released to the news media and to others upon request. Such information is defined as follows:

1.Name of student.
2.Local address and telephone number of student.
3.Hometown city and state of student and telephone number.
4.Year in school (freshman, etc.)
5.Verification of current enrollment.
6.Dates of enrollment.
7.Department or division in which enrolled.
8.Name of parent or spouse.
