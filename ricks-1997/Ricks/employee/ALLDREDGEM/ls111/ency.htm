Dictionaries emphasize words; encyclopedias place subjects in the context of all recorded knowledge.
"Encyclopedia" has its roots in words which could be interpreted as "instruction in the circle of arts and sciences."
The Greeks wrote the first encyclopedias because of their concern for a complete or liberal education.
Some encyclopedias are be published as brief dictionary style publications.
The most famous encyclopedias tend to include essay length articles with additional features.

Encyclopaedia Britannica

Encyclopaedia Britannica was first published in Edinburgh in 1771, three volumes in length.
The number of volumes for Britannica quickly grew.
The set has become preferred by scholars for its comprehensive bibliographies.
Recent sets of Britannica are divided into parts.
Macropaedia is "Knowledge in Depth" with long articles on broad topics.
Micropaedia is the "Ready Reference" with short unsigned articles.
Propaedia is an outline of knowledge with a key to contributors' initials.
Index volumes are necessary for successful searches.

An Internet version is much easier to use.  http://www.eb.com:180/
13,000 Internet sites have been chosen by the editors to supplement this encyclopedia.
Britannica Online was released for subscription over the Internet in 1994.
Free access to some features was made recently.
The author and credentials are a click away.
For example, Robert L. Collison is abbreviated as (R.L.C. ).
His credentials are:
Professor of Library Science and Information Studies,
University of California, Los Angeles, 1968-73. Author of
Encyclopaedias: Their History Throughout the Ages and others.

Encyclopedia Americana

Encyclopedia Americana was first published in the United States in 1829.
It maintains an emphasis on American topics.
An abbreviated credential appears with the author's name at the end of articles.
A partial list of the credentials of the contributors can be found in the first volume.
Recommended readings are not extensive.

An Internet subscription is also available for this encyclopedia. http://ea.grolier.com/ea-online/static/search.htm
At the end of the article appear the author and his credentials.
Herbert S. Hirshberg is the  Late Dean, School of Library Science Case Western Reserve University Revised by   James G. Tobin The New York Public Library

Academic American Encyclopedia

Academic American Encyclopedia was first published in 1980 and was produced completely by computer.
It was the first encyclopedia available as a database.
The articles are short with recent recommended readings.
The credentials of the contributor are found in the first volume.
The bibliographies include many recent publications.

Grolier is the company which publishes Academic American Encyclopedia and several other encyclopedias for different subjects and audiences.
The CD product based on Academic American Encyclopedia is called the New Grolier Multimedia Encyclopedia. Recordings include audio and video.
The menu and help screens assist the user of this product.

Encyclopedias are written for various levels of education.
General encyclopedias cover all fields of knowledge.
Academic American has very short articles on popular topics to appeal to high schools and junior colleges.
World Book Encyclopedia is aimed at an audience from elementary through high school with study aids.
The encyclopedia articles written for scholarly adult audiences are more likely to have a signed article with a list of recommended readings.

Specialized encyclopedias have been written to place information in certain fields of study in context with those fields.
For example, science, education, social science and art have specialized encyclopedias.

McGraw-Hill has published encyclopedias for general science, geology, ecology, and other scientific fields.
The group publishing the encyclopedia may provide the credentials.
For example, the American Medical Association is associated with the production of one encyclopedia.
When articles are signed, the credentials of the contributor or article author are usually given in the first volume or preface or the last volume or appendix.
 

Choosing an encyclopedia

With so many encyclopedias available, choose an encyclopedia that best fits your needs when using or purchasing one. Professional advice is available in several publications.

Kenneth Kister has written an encyclopedia guide which answers many questions.
Kister's Best Encyclopedias : A Comparative Guide to General and Specialized Encyclopedias (Ref AE 1.K57-1994)

The American Library Association publishes periodicals which review encyclopedias and other reference materials.

  • Booklist
  • School Library Journal
Encyclopedias, Atlases & Dictionaries (Ref AE 1.E48-1995) is another publication that reviews encyclopedias.

The best encyclopedia depends on the needs of the user.
A multiple volume set is no longer necessary.
CD ROM has made encyclopedias available in compact form.
Internet puts access to a current encyclopedia in homes and libraries.

Since different authors are chosen by different publishers, using several may give you different opinions and information.
Authors are called contributors.
The author's affiliation with colleges, government agencies, or other organization is called his credentials.

Using an encyclopedia

Best results will occur when the index is consulted first.
Some encyclopedias will have long articles on a broad subject with paragraphs on the area of interest.
Alphabetical order may be used only for long articles.
Different parts of the set may use alphabetical order for
    short articles (Britannica's Micropaedia) and
    long articles (Britannica's Macropaedia)
The index or the article may indicate cross-references to related articles.

The first volume often contains explanations of special abbreviations and a description of coverage.
Authorship often is detailed to include the names of contributors and the articles which they wrote.
The table of contents may give the page number which has the list of contributors.

Electronic encyclopedias have the advantage of allowing more types of searches.
Keyword searches are an advantage of electronic products.
Knowledge trees provide an outline of the field of study.
Time lines, maps, and searches limited to illustrations are all advantages of electronic encyclopedias.
 

Kinds of information

Related topics are found easily in the index volume.
Cross-references within the encyclopedia article are indicated by capital letters, italics or "see also" at the end of the article. World Book Encyclopedia has a study guide which will recommend other related articles.

Definitions may give other ideas for research.
For example, an article on "Abortion" will subdivide the topic into spontaneous abortion or "miscarriage" and clinical abortion. Statistics will give quantities or how many dimensions to the definition.
Definitions for technical terms are essential to finding more scholarly information.
Illustrations provide visual definitions.

The history of the topic tells what time period would have more information.
Dates provide a way to limit or narrow research.
Surrogate mothers have only been in the news at certain times, such as when famous court cases are decided.

Issues will give clues to where material can be found or ways to narrow the topic.
For example, research on abortion could be limited to medical, legal or ethical issues.
Literature that is written by or for physicians covers medical issues.
Political issues are discussed in election campaigns and public opinion polls.
Legal publications cover the ways judges and attorneys deal with legal controversies.
Moral issues are discussed by religious groups.
Newspapers and periodicals cover the issues that are of interest in the community which subscribes to the publication.

An outline of the topic is incorporated into the article.
The outline can be used in narrowing the topic or in organizing your paper or a speech.
World Book Encyclopedia has a separate outline at the end of the article in addition to the headings for sections.

Authorities on the subject are given.
The "Father" of a field of study is often identified.
Recommended readings include prominent authors on the subject.
The authors of the encyclopedia articles are chosen because they are authorities on the topic.
Credentials are the educational or other qualifications.
The credentials of encyclopedia article contributors can be found in the first or last volume of the encyclopedia set.

Britannica has its contributors abbreviated with the key to abbreviations located in the Propaedia.
For example, "G.An." wrote an article on "Censorship."
The author's name is George Anastaplo.
He is a professor of law at Loyola University.

Bibliographies are ways to identify authorities.
Recommended readings and bibliographies include authorities who have written information on the subject.
Authorities may be groups of professionals not just individuals.
The authors are often more important than individual titles.
If the author is alive, a newer more complete work may be just off the press.
Government agencies such as, the Center for Disease Control publish annual summaries of statistics.

An overview provides the background or setting of a topic.
An article on literature may have a time line with major authors listed.
References to the various national literary histories would lead one to the major authors of each nation.
The various types of literature would be discussed in the articles.
Likewise, science and other fields will have overviews.

Biographies of the famous people of the past will help you understand them.
Did Freud treat a different type of patient than Adler or Jung who were his contemporaries who differed in their views.
Current world leaders influence events.
McGraw Hill Encyclopedia of World Biography is a specialized encyclopedia which through supplements tries to maintain biographies of past and present famous people from various of fields of endeavor.

Geography provides additional insight into groups of people.
Did immigrants who passed by the Statue of Liberty have a different idea than those who sailed into our waters illegally?
If you are planning a trip, an encyclopedia will give historical and cultural insight.
Some encyclopedias will include maps of famous cities with famous landmarks indicated.

Diversity of articles is an characteristic of an encyclopedia which tries to include all information that is needed for a liberal education.
The text of famous documents would be included to allow you to know what the "Gettysburg Address" says.
Ethnic history tells about groups of people.
Some groups of people are not limited to geographic areas.
For example, all Jewish people do not live in Israel.
Scientific articles will explain why the sky is blue and why rainbows have so many colors.
General encyclopedias should be included as an early step in research.
They usually should not be quoted in research papers.

Specialized or subject encyclopedias

Specialized or subject encyclopedias provide information in relation to a field of study. 
Signed articles by authorities are more often found in subject or specialized encyclopedias. 
The general encyclopedias will give the overview and definitions that are needed to understand articles that are written in specialized reference tools. 
To find a subject encyclopedia:
1. Browse the reference collection for the call number assigned to the subject
  • BX Christian denominations
    • Encyclopedia of Mormonism (Ref BX8605.5.E62)
  • CT Biography
    • McGraw Hill Encyclopedia of World Biography (Ref CT 103.M27)
  • H Social Science
    • International Encyclopedia of Social Science (Ref H40.A2I5)
  • KF Law
    • Guide to American Law (Ref KF 154.G85)
  • Q Pure science
    • McGraw Hill Encyclopedia of Science and Technology (Ref Q 121.M3)
  • R Medicine
    • International Encyclopedia of Psychiatry, Psychology, ... (Ref RC 334.I57)
2. Perform a subject keyword search of the library catalog with the field of study and the word ENCYCLOPEDIAS
3. Consult a bibliography or index.
  • First Stop : The Master Index to Subject Encyclopedias (Reference Desk or Ref Z 5848.R93-1989)
  • ALA. Guide to Reference Books. (Ref Z1035.1.)
  • Subject Encyclopedias (Ref desk or AE1.M57-1999)
  • Background outlines [Internet Resources}
