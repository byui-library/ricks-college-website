CIS 235 - Internet Techniques

Fall Semester 2000 Syllabus

Credits: 3 Semester

Course Description:

Study of concepts, strategies, tools and ethical issues involved in the use of the Internet. Focuses on design and construction of Home Pages including HTML. Includes Web Server management concepts and development of Internet and Intranet web sites and servers.

Materials:

  • Sklar, J. (2000). Principles of Web Design. Cambridge, MA.: Course Technologies.
  • 3.5" HD Floppies for backup storage (A zip disk is recommended).

Assignments:

Each student in the course will:

Become knowledgeable about key design issues pertinent to web-based documents and demonstrate that knowledge by building a website.
Conduct a written design and operations review of at least three websites. A list of potential sites will be provided that the student can choose from but the review need not be limited to suggested sites.
Build a personal website demonstrating the ability to implement design elements effectively.
With a team, develop a site for a hypothetical business: I've Been Mugged.
(A specialty store dealing in custom mugs for individuals and businesses.)

The site must simulate a live business, by:

  1. meeting the needs of clients (offering what they want),
  2. be cost-efficient (make items easy to find), and
  3. having the potential of making a profit (balance advertising and other costs with item pricing and margins).
Develop a web policy that demonstrates an understanding of and application of  copyright law and privacy issues. This policy must be a part of the web site.
Demonstrate the ability to use E-mail correctly, particularly using attachments.
Use a web browser to manage Internet browsing, including:
   Secure Sites
   Accessing multimedia resources
   Expanding browser capabilities
Develop and implement basic code in web pages, including:
   HTML
   Javascript
Demonstrate a knowledge of basic issues of web server administration.

Engage in career-oriented readings to become more knowledgeable in the IT field:

  •    There's No Place Like Home
  •    Global Web Sites Prove Challenging
  •    The Evolving IT Worker
  •    Wanted: IT Workers of All Ages

Computer Ethics

The use of the Ricks College network, systems and data impose responsibilities and obligations upon the students to use these computer components in an ethical manner and in accordance with the guidelines found in the Ricks Honor Code. Students are to access only data and files that are owned by them, or the instructor for their use has given explicit authorization. Copyrighted material must be used in accordance with copyright law and applicable license agreements followed.

Attempting to circumvent or subvert system security measures or engaging in any activity that causes harm to systems or to any information stored thereon, such as creating or propagating viruses, disrupting services, or damaging files are considered inappropriate use. Violators are subject to disciplinary actions as prescribed by Ricks Policy and may also be prosecuted under applicable local, state, or federal civil or criminal law.

NOTE: A more detailed listing of Appropriate Use Guidelines is posted in each of the computer labs (Smith 440, 442 and 422). You should review these guideline posters.
