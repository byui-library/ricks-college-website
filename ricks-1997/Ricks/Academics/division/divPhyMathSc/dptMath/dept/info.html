Department History
Mathematics and Computer Science Department
Ricks College
Rexburg, Idaho
1999

Mission of the Department


The department is committed to teaching in a positive, exciting way; encouraging student expertise and curiosity; providing models for study and learning that can be used in any discipline. We provide students the opportunity to explore and use the elegant and powerful language of mathematics. In computer science we give the students the opportunity to solve complex and challenging problems that will shape how they think about their world and how it functions.


Mathematics and Computer Science Faculty

Since 1989-90 the department has increased from eleven full time members to the present size of twenty. Three of the twenty are female. Three of our department were hired prior to 1969, three were hired from 1970 to 1979, four were hired from 1980 to 1989, and ten were hired since 1990.

Three of our computer science instructors have experience in industry and government. Two of the mathematics instructors have experience in industry.

Nineteen of the twenty members of the department have Masters Degrees and one has a Bachelors' Degree. Fifty percent have a degree in mathematics, fifteen percent have backgrounds in statistics equivalent to a minor, twenty percent majored in CS or have strong backgrounds in that field, twenty-five percent have M Ed degrees in math or CS, ten percent do not have majors in mathematics or CS but in all cases they have taught mathematics for a number of years prior to coming to Ricks College.

Thirty percent of the faculty have given presentations at professional meetings. Several have served or are now serving in professional organizations. Thirty percent have published papers with one of the group having being the editor and publisher of a bimonthly national newsletter in mathematics and technology.


Significant Changes in the Last Ten Years with Special Emphasis on the Last Five Years Concerning Programs, Instruction, Research, and Service Courses.

The last decade has seen a complete overhaul of the computer science program, the addition of an Introduction to Proofs course to the mathematics major requirements, and the integration of graphing calculator technology into mathematics instruction. Computer science is just completing the transition to a new set of courses-only two of the previous courses were retained. The new courses more closely follow the guidelines established by the ACM (Association of Computing Machinery) and more explicitly demonstrate the connections between different areas of computer science. Feedback from mathematics majors that have transferred to universities indicated a need for greater exposure to rigorous mathematical proofs. Accordingly Math 280, "Topics in Pure Mathematics" was created and added to the requirements for mathematics majors. For several years the department has required students in most classes numbered 103 or higher to have a graphing calculator. The abilities of the graphing calculator have been incorporated into the curriculum requiring some changes in topics and emphasis.


Significant Changes in the Last Ten Years with Special Emphasis on the Last Five Years Concerning Faculty, Number of Faculty, Qualification and Coverage of the Field.

In the past two years, two qualified computer science faculty members have been hired to strengthen the quality of our offerings. In addition, two female mathematician were hired. There is a stronger balance today in the math and computer science faculty. The faculty is qualified to teach the first two years of college math and computer science curricula.

Significant Changes in the Last Ten Years with Special Emphasis on the Last Five Years Concerning Physical Facilities and Equipment.

In the fall of 1987 the mathematics department moved to the new addition to the Romney Building. This consolidated the entire faculty in the same geographical area. We have since added ten full time teachers. The new faculty offices needed were created from storage and work areas, a test center, and classrooms. We are presently adequately housed in these facilities. If any additional office space will be needed we will have to be even more creative than we have been in the past. Each faculty member has at least one computer in his or her office. We are on a five-year new computer rotation schedule but a faculty member can upgrade to a better used computer as another faculty receives a new one. The computer science part of the department has changed from math instructors with a strong interest in computer science to two full time teachers who occasionally teach a math class. Our computer science lab is expanding to serve other departments in our division. We currently have 50 computer terminals in this lab. Our library holdings have increased over the years as the budget for the purchase of reference books and videos has increased. We are comfortable with the number and quality of these holdings.

Significant Changes That Seem Likely to Occur in Our Department in the Next 3 to 5 Years.

During the next three to five years it is very likely that two significant changes will occur in the Department of Mathematics and Computer Science.

First requiring intermediate algebra as a prerequisite to both College Algebra (Math 110) and our new Math 108 (Quantitative Literacy) will strengthen the general education graduation requirement. With the new Math 108, it will be possible to meet the needs of the liberal arts graduate by having more "practical" content that at the same time is mathematically "on par" with college algebra. Academic Council approved Math 108 in September of 1998. Secondly, if enrollments increase in the area of computer science then growing specialization and other logistical concerns would seem to dictate a split in the two departments.


Quality of Teaching and Advising

Ricks College is fortunate to possess a very strong mathematics and computer science teaching faculty. Nearly every teacher receives student evaluation comments that read almost exactly the same: "This is the best math (or CS) teacher I have ever had." It is also common for this faculty to receive notes of appreciation from current and former students offering praise for the way in which they were taught and advised. One source of evidence comes from the student evaluations that show the overall averages for our instructors to be 6.05 (which is in the excellent category according or our standards with 7.0 being perfect) compared to a division average of 5.66 and a Rick College average of 5.77. Our course evaluations fare well with our overall average being 5.71 (again in the excellent category) compared to a division average of 5.21 and a Ricks College average of 5.48.


Major Strengths of the Department

One of the most apparent strengths of the department is the professional cohesiveness, collegiality, and cooperation that exists between our professors. The informal sharing of ideas in teaching and advising is highly encouraged and developed. There is excellent and helpful accessibility for our students on all levels. During a typical day it is not uncommon to see teachers going the extra mile to make sure their students understand the material through personal tutoring in between classes. Although there is a high degree of cohesion in our department this does not suggest that there is a lack of diversity in teaching styles and philosophies. On the contrary, our students have an opportunity to experience a wide spectrum of instructional approaches in their courses. Generally speaking, the department is well balanced in its mixture of professional backgrounds and skills, which promote the study of applied and pure mathematics and computer science.

Our department has a very organized and equitable way of selecting class loads each semester. A simple rotation system similar to a professional sports league draft has been in place for many years and affords everyone in the department an opportunity to select a fair load of class sections based on his or her particular interests and needs in a given semester. Professional development is highly encouraged by the department and administration and our teachers are continually looking for ways to improve the quality of mathematics and computer science education. There is a very focused and organized strategy that we follow in choosing and interviewing of candidates for a teaching position in the department.


Significant Planned Improvements

In recent years much progress has been made in terms of both physical and curricular expansion of our computer science area. Our lab has been remodeled and expanded to meet the needs of an increasing demand for CS courses and the continual changes in technology. We continue to upgrade programming languages and courses to stay in alignment with both industry and transferability to other institutions of higher learning. Both CS instructors have degrees in computer science and considerable experience in industry.

In order to better meet the needs of students regarding the Ricks College graduation math requirement we have modified the physical layout of the Math Lab to facilitate small group lecturing. This will help us supplement the self-paced/teacher-directed nature of Math 101, Intermediate Algebra. Two years ago the administration hired a full time developmental specialist to build a program meeting the needs of those students who need one-on one teaching in the Math 100 series prior to moving on to the Math 101 graduation requirement. Our liberal arts college math course has been significantly strengthened in the last few years providing students a viable alternative to Math 110, College Algebra. The content will now reflect a true quantitative literacy emphasis, which will not only fulfill the Idaho Core Math Requirement but should also transfer to other major universities outside of Idaho. Although pedagogical issues concerning mathematics technology will never be completely resolved, we continue to stay abreast of calculator and computer software evolution. Our department has found an equitable way of dealing with different calculator platforms in both the teaching and learning of mathematics. Policies are now in place to make sure students are carefully informed in the catalog and class schedule so they can choose the appropriate class, teacher and calculator if required. Each time significant changes occur in mathematics technology our department members share research and ideas with each other before making proposals for platform changes.

Future work is planned for solidifying our core curriculum as well as developing department placement tests and/or final exams in courses such as college algebra and trigonometry. With the expansion of faculty in the last few years, class overload hours are now fairly rare and we envision a little more time to work on professional development. Administrative support of sabbaticals has increased as well. We hope to reach a point where we will have one or two of our teachers on leave during any given semester helping us to stay educationally refreshed as a department.
