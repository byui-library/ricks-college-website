The Courage of Intimacy

        Social courage . . . is the courage to relate to other human beings, the capacity to risk oneself in the hope of achieving meaningful intimacy. It is the courage to invest one's self over a period of time in a relationship that will demand an increasing openness.

        Intimacy requires courage because risk is inescapable. We cannot know at the outset how the relationship will affect us. Will we grow . . . or will the relationship destroy us? The one thing we can be sure of is that if we let ourselves fully into the relationship for good or for evil, we will not come out unaffected. [And in one way or another, we all face the difficulty of holding back from a potentially healthy and satisfying relationship, because we gave too much in a relationship that was unhealthy and destructive to some degree.]

        A common practice in our day is to avoid working up the courage required for authentic intimacy by shifting the issue to the body . . . It is easier in our society to be naked physically than to be naked psychologically or spiritually--easier to share our body than to share our fantacies, hopes, fears, and aspirations, which are felt to be more personal and the sharing of which is experienced as making us more vulnerable [than the sharing of our bodies]. Hence people short-circuit the more "dangerous" building of a relationship by leaping immediately into bed.

(Rollo May, The Courage to Create, 9-10, adapted by David L. Ward)
