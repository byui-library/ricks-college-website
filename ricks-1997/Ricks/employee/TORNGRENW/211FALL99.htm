I. Texts: The texts required for this class are the scriptures of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.(Bible, Book of Mormon, Doctrine and Covenants, and Pearl of Great Price)

II. Grading:

A. Daily Quizzes: There will be a 5 question daily quiz given over the materials to have been read for that day. There will be no make up quizzes. You must be in attendance on the day the quiz is given to take it.
 

B. Scripture block Summary Outlines: Each Monday submit a summary outline of the assigned block of scripture. Twelve blocks are assigned during the semester. The purpose of these outline summaries is for you to identify and report the teachings of the scriptural passages in the assigned blocks in your own words.

These outlines must be computer printed or type written. Hand written outlines will not be accepted. Use 8 or 9 or 10 point font so that you can provide as much information as possible. Late outlines are not accepted. Late means submitted after the beginning of class on the day they are due. Rarely will I make exceptions to these rules, and any exception may result in a loss of points.

1. Format of Outline Summaries: The summary outlines should not be more than one (1) page in length. Single spacing is mandatory, because you should provide as much relevant information in one page as you possibly can. Fill up the page. Do not staple pages together or pin additional information to the page; only single pages are acceptable.

Use the chapter and designated paragraphs in the text to organize your outline summary. Identify and create a title for each paragraph or block of verses in each division and show the verses. Use an outline format or paragraphs not lists, as you provide your descriptive summary.

Include any questions, insights or personal applications you might have about the reading at the end of the outline. These are not mandatory.

The following 4 lines of information should be at the top-left of your page, in the proper order:

a. Name

b. The last four digits of your social security number;

c. The date the assignment is due;

d. The name of the assignment.

(an example would be like this:)

John Doe

8888

Sept 16, 1997

Matthew 3:1-7:29

2. Grading the Outline Summaries: Each assignment will be graded "on the curve" in the sense that the papers with the best and most information will get the highest grade. However, it is possible for everyone in class to earn the top mark; so in that sense it is not grading on the curve.

If you have trouble earning the mark you want, then examine the paper of students who earn the highest mark. This should be the best way for you to determine what you need to do to improve your work. If that doesn't help see your instructor.

Each assignment is graded with one of the following marks (or a 0 for no points)

"@" for 30 points "+" for 25 points "#" for 15 points
 

Your papers will not be handed back in class. Pick up your papers at least bi-weekly. They will be in folders at the front of the class. Papers will be discarded after a week or so. Save these for proof of scores.

IV. An Example Outline:

The following is an example of an outline for the scripture reading assignment. Note that each chapter is broken down into its paragraphs (noted by the symbol that looks like a backwards P). Then each paragraph is briefly summarized.
 

John Doe

8888

Sept 4, 1997

Luke 15-16
 

Luke 15

1-2 Scribes chide Jesus. As tax-collectors and sinners approach Jesus to be taught, Pharisees and scribes chide the Savior for associating with the lower class of society.
 

3-7 Parable of the Lost Sheep. The savior hurls three parables of rebuke at the Pharisees and Scribes denouncing their self-righteousness. The first parable concerns a man who lost one sheep of his ninety and nine. In the parable the Savior used sarcasm to denounce the Pharisees when he said God rejoices over the soul who repents than "over ninety and nine just persons, which need no repentance.
 

8-11 Parable of the Lost Coin. The next parable deals with a woman who lost a coin. Just as the woman searched the house to find her lost coin, so should the Pharisees be as diligent helping the lost souls.
 

11-32 Parable of the Prodigal Son. The final parable is the parable of the lost son (prodigal son). The parable equated the publicans and sinners with the lost son while at the same time the pharisees and Scribes were represented by the son who disfavored the return of his brother.
 

Luke 16

1-12 Parable of the Unjust Steward to the Publicans. Having denounced the Pharisees and Scribes the Savior now began to teach the publicans an sinners. He gave the parable of the unjust steward hoping to encourage the new converts to continue in the same creativity, resourcefulness, and forethought in the church as they did in the unjust activities they participated in before their conversion. He then encouraged them to use wealth to help build up the kingdom of God rather than build up themselves.
 

13-18 Two Masters. The Savior taught that we cannot serve two masters. The Pharisees, "who were covetous," began to deride the Savior's teachings. Jesus then reproved the Pharisees because of their misguided lifestyle.
 

19-31 Parable of the Rich Man. The Savior then taught the parable of the rich man and Lazarus condemning the covetous nature of the Pharisees.
