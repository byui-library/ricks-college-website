RICKS ANNOUNCES NEW S MULTIPLE-USE FACILITY

A new multiple-use building to serve both religious and academic needs will be built at Ricks College as it prepares for future growth. The approximately 54,000 square foot building will be located in an open site between South Second East Street and the floral demonstration gardens. It will be southeast of the college's newest major building, the Kimball Student and Administrative Services Building.

The building will be patterned after the LDS Church's Institute of Religion building at Idaho State University in Pocatello, says Ricks Administrative Vice President James Smyth. Similar buildings are also being constructed near the campuses of the University of Utah and Salt Lake Community College, both in Salt Lake City; and Dixie State College in St. George, Utah. Ground will be broken early this spring, Smyth says. Because a standard plan already is designed, the building can be built quickly and be ready for use by the fall of 2002.

The two-story building with a partial basement will stand on a hillside and be approximately 50 feet at its highest point. The contractor is Jacobsen Construction of Salt Lake City, and the architect is Jensen Haslem of Logan. Unlike other buildings on campus that were principally designed for academic needs, the building is principally designed for religious needs. The building will handle eight wards and be able to accommodate large congregations such as stake conferences and overflow for devotionals. Presently there are 57 wards and six stakes on campus. During the week, the building will be used by the college for classes. The building will have space for 20 to 25 faculty members, who will share offices with ward and stake leaders.

RELIGION DEPARTMENT HOSTS SYMPOSIUM ON THE SCRIPTURES

Drawing more than 500 participants from across eastern Idaho and western Wyoming in the past, the 15th annual Symposium on the Scriptures will entice students, young and old, to learn more about the LDS Church's Doctrine and Covenants on Saturday, Jan. 27. Ricks College will hold the free symposium in the John Taylor Building in Rexburg from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. The symposium invites students to explore and understand the Doctrine and Covenants better. Registration is not required.

The symposium is centered around this year's course of study in the gospel doctrine class of the Sunday School program. Keynote speaker, Susan Easton Black, a professor of church history and doctrine at Brigham Young University in Provo will introduce the symposium at 9 a.m. with her address entitled "The Importance of the Individual in the Doctrine and Covenants."

Members of the Ricks religion faculty and BYU-Hawaii religion faculty as well as local Church Educational System faculty members will present various one-hour classes on the Doctrine and Covenants. The classes will be taught at 10:30 a.m., 11:30 a.m., 2 p.m. and 3 p.m. There will be twenty-five presenters with various topics from which to choose. (See www.byui.edu/religion/symposium)

ENROLLMENT UP SLIGHTLY

The official winter enrollment count is in at Ricks College, and it is just slightly higher than last semester's record. There are 8,955 students registered for winter semester. The total is slightly higher than fall's enrollment of 8,949 and last winter's enrollment of 8,840.

Attending Ricks this winter are 626 new students and 7,801 continuing students. The remaining 528 are returning after an absence of one or more semesters, including those who are re-entering after serving missions. All in all, 2,884 are returned missionaries.

There are 5,069 females (56 percent) compared to 3,886 male students. Married students total 655. Full-time students total 8,441, with 514 going part time. Students from all 50 states are represented in the United States. The number breakdown show that more than 200 students come from each of seven states: Idaho reported 2,948; Utah had 1,336; California with 865; 726 from Washington; Oregon had 388; 276 from Texas; and Colorado had 243. There are 275 international students representing 48 countries. Of these, 152 come from Canada. Statistics are based on enrollment after the first ten days of class.
