PRESIDENT MONSON SPEAKS FROM RICKS TO YOUNG ADULTS WORLDWIDE

Modern technology was eminent in the Hart as President Thomas S. Monson spoke to a full-capacity audience, thousands in overflow locations on campus, and to young adult Church members throughout the world in the first Church Educational System fireside broadcast of 2001. The broadcast was carried live to congregations via satellite and the Internet. (http://www.lds.org/broadcast/ces/0,6751,395,00.html)

President Monson's message outlined three gates of success on a journey that leads to eternal life: preparation, performance and service.

"It is necessary to prepare, to plan, so that we don't fritter away our lives," he said. "Without a goal, there can be no real success. Success is the progressive realization of a worthy ideal."

In addressing the gate of performance, President Monson said, "Remember that the mantle of leadership is not the cloak of comfort, but the robe of responsibility. Accountability is not for the intention but for the deed."

He added, "It is a good idea to be ambitious, to have goals, to want to be good at what you do - but it is a terrible mistake to let drive and ambition get in the way of treating people with kindness and decency." He said, "Youth need fewer critics and more models to follow. Your own personal performance in all aspects of your life, including reading the scriptures regularly and following their teachings, will help you become such models."

Concerning service, President Monson said, "All who participate in (service) have discovered a new and personal appreciation of the Master's teaching, 'It is more blessed to give than to receive.' As we go through our daily lives, we discover countless opportunities to follow the example of the Savior. When our hearts are in tune with His teachings, we discover the unmistakable nearness of His divine help . . . . Seek heavenly help also to know how to serve others. There is no feeling so gratifying nor knowledge so comforting as to know that our Father has answered the prayer of another through you."

GOLD MEDALIST RULON GARDNER RETURNS TO RICKS COLLEGE

Gold-medal Olympic wrestler Rulon Gardner will return to Ricks to speak to students and community residents and to participate in the opening ceremonies of the Area 7 Special Olympics winter games.

The Afton, Wyo., native wrestled for Ricks from 1989 to 1991 and won the national junior college title his sophomore year. During the recent Summer Olympics in Sydney, Australia, he dethroned the three-time Olympic gold medalist Alexander Karelin of Russia.

Gardner will attend a wrestling match between Ricks and Yakima Community College in Washington Wednesday, Jan. 24, in the Hart Gymnasium starting at 7:30 p.m. Following the competition, he will give remarks to the wrestlers and the crowd. Tickets are $1 per person with proceeds going to Special Olympics.

On Thursday, he will attend a Ricks home basketball game against Treasure Valley Community College from Ontario, Ore. He will be introduced at half time of the men's game which starts at 7:30 p.m.

Gardner will take part in opening ceremonies of the Area 7 Special Olympics of Idaho Friday night from 6-7 p.m. in the West Ballroom of the Manwaring Center. Athletes, families, coaches, volunteers and the general public are invited to the free event and a dance will follow from 7-8 p.m. The Special Olympics athletes will compete in winter sports Saturday at Grand Targhee Ski Area near Alta, Wyo.

THEATRE DEPARTMENT TO PRESENT SHAKESPEARE'S "THE WINTER'S TALE"

The Ricks College Theatre Department will present William Shakespeare's "The Winter's Tale" Jan. 18-20, 24-27, 31 and Feb. 1-3 in the Kirkham Arena Theatre. Tickets are $6 for the general public through the Ticket Office at (208) 356-2230 or on the Internet at www.byui.edu/tickets.

Director Omar Hansen says the story tells of Leontes, king of Sicilla, who because of jealousy and rage, causes the death of his wife and son and then banishes his most trusted friends to exile. Later Leontes realizes that his actions have been unjust and attempts over the next few years to repent and mend his mistakes, hoping to restore much of what was lost. The play will be performed on a Shakespearean style set, complete with costumes from the Tudor Period.
