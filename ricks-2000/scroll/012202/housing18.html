House vs. dorms vs. apartments

by Carl Neil Ekman III
EKM98001@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Houses: “A house has a much quieter environment. Those who are serious about learning live in houses. It’s not so focussed on the social aspect. There’s a different attitude towards their studies in a house,” Verna Gneiting, Grandpa’s House manager, said.

Houses can be farther away from campus, but there can be benefits from the distance. The noise level is generally a lot lower and can be much more private for students. More people tend to live in an house than a dorm or apartment.

Houses start at $880 for men and $1,150 for women per semester.

Dorms: “It’s not like a dorm because you have a connection with everyone, but you still have your own separate apartment,” Lindsey Brown, a freshman from Mission Viejo, Calif., said

There is Internet access in every dorm, and there will be Internet access in the living room. Men’s dorms are almost done with living room access, while they are working hard to get women’s halls complete.

Dorms are also close to campus, allowing students easy access to their apartments.

Rent for dorms starts at $1,640 for men and $1,574 for women per semester; meal plan included.

Apartments: “The best thing about apartments is the social life,” Rosalie Evans, manager of Royal Crest, said.

Apartments tend to be more of a social atmosphere for students.

Apartments also provide more amenities for students to enjoy. These amenities may include:dishwashers, microwaves, TV, VCR, cable, recreational rooms, lounge and others depending on the apartment that is chosen. Some apartment complexes even offer swimming pools and weight rooms for tenants.

Apartments start at $1,100 for men and $1,275 for women per semester.
