August 13, 2001

Name may have changed, but BYU-Idaho 

is committed to preserving the 'Spirit of Ricks'

    
REXBURG, Idaho - Finding ways to preserve and enhance the same feelings and friendliness that people have always loved about Ricks College weighs heavily on the minds of administrators, faculty and student leaders as they "rethink education" at BYU-Idaho.

Often called the "Spirit of Ricks," BYU-Idaho leaders are determined to retain the phrase that describes a feeling that has been experienced by many people down through the years.

As the university expands and changes, those things that have bonded students, faculty and administration for more than 100 years at Ricks College will be retained, says BYU-Idaho President David A. Bednar. These experiences will continue to come from a tradition of service, hard work, friendliness and compassion.

"There is an overarching spiritual dimension to the 'Spirit of Ricks'," he says. "The 'Spirit of Ricks' comes from an environment where students are living in harmony with God's laws."

The president adds, "The 'Spirit of Ricks' is not found in a building. It is not found in a place. It is found in the people, the students, the teachers. The 'Spirit of Ricks' will not be diminished-in fact, if we do this transition properly, it will be greatly enhanced. We will continue to foster a nurturing, spiritual environment, which will continue to be referred to as the 'Spirit of Ricks.' "

Part of the "Spirit of Ricks" comes from the friendliness experienced on campus among students, faculty members, employees and others. "We want to continue to foster an environment where people are concerned and caring toward each other," President Bednar says.

Retaining a student-faculty ratio of 25 students to each teacher also will perpetuate the closeness that is felt on campus. Class sizes will remain relatively small and more personal at BYU-Idaho.

Although the university expects to grow from about 9,000 to 11,500 students per semester during the next several years, it will not grow so much as to lose the closeness and friendliness. This will be accomplished by maintaining freshman/sophomore enrollment at approximately 8,600 students. Increases will become evident as upper-level courses are added and the enrollment naturally increases to about 3,000 upperclassmen.

To handle more students, the university expects to hire approximately 100 new faculty members during the next five years. The first 25 already are beginning work this fall.

Spencer Haacke, this year's student-body president who is also from Sugar City, Idaho, says students also intend to continue traditions that foster the "Spirit of Ricks."

"Everything that's been going on that produced the 'Spirit of Ricks' we are going to try to enhance," Haacke says. "We're also going to start new traditions that we can add to the 'Spirit of Ricks.' "

Haacke says the "Spirit of Ricks" is two fold. "I think it has to do with unifying secular learning and spiritual learning in the classroom. It also has to do with the relationship you can have with other students here. The whole atmosphere is prone to personal relationships with other people."

He was recently on a university campus in the Midwest where "nobody would say hi to you - if you made eye contact, they'd look the other way. It's not that way here."

Student Life Vice President Robert Wilkes says, "There will be some pressures for us to become more sophisticated in who we are and how we act. We need to be committed to the idea that this is a different place. In some very important ways, we must stay who we are and what we've been."

Wilkes adds that BYU-Idaho will preserve policies and programs that have contributed to an uplifting environment on campus. "We will preserve the dress and grooming standards and the Honor Code that was part of Ricks College," he says.

Ric Page, assistant vice president of Student Life, believes that deliberately preserving the phrase "Spirit of Ricks" says a lot about the university's commitment. "This is a way we will honor the legacy that Ricks College has left for BYU-Idaho."

He says the university is developing some themes that are based around the "Spirit of Ricks." For example, the Athletics Department and Associated Students of BYU-Idaho are making T-shirts incorporating this phrase.

Page adds, "We're also planning to vigorously maintain the spirit of love, fellowship, cooperation and caring that exists among students, faculty and staff."
