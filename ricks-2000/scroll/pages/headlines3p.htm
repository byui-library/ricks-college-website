POP ART ON MASS DISPLAY ADDS VARIETY

by Nathan Hathaway
scroll staff


A new Ricks College summer art show is on display in the foyer of the Oscar A. Kirkham building, daily from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.

This year's show features pop art, a distinctive style of art from the 1960s and 1970s that "drew its inspiration from the objects of a culture deeply rooted in mass production and consumerism," Ricks art instructor Gerald Griffin said.

The art show, which typically showcases older, more traditional art, features the work of famous pop artists Andy Warhol, Roy Lichtenstein, Peter Max and Robert Indiana.

This is actually a kind of a visual historical document," Griffin said. "It documents one of the leading styles of the 20th Century."

The art department is displaying the more modern art this summer in order to bring variety to the Ricks College collection, according to Griffin.

"(We're displaying this work) just to start to bring a little balance to the collection, which has been heavily weighted to traditional imagery," Griffin said.

When pop art originally came about, it broke many of the "rules" about what was considered "fit subject matter" for art, but has had a major influence on modern society.

"It's had an impact on the way we see the world, bright colors, for example," Griffin said. "You go up to the ski slopes today, and it looks a lot like an Andy Warhol picture."
