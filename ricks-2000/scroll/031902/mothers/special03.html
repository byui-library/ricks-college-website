Fiction author conveys her life in works

by Jeff Blake
BLA97011@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

From the story of a Baptist preacher who takes his family on a mission to the Belgian Congo to a story of how women’s lives were transformed by an 18-month strike against a mining corporation, the novels of Barbara Kingsolver cover a wide range of topics all with the similar themes of family, community, the common good and the natural world.

Kingsolver was born April 8, 1955 on a farm in eastern Kentucky, a fact that has profoundly influenced her writing, most of which occur in rural America. This rural influence can be found in such Kingsolver novels as The Bean Trees.

It tells the story of Taylor Greer, a young woman who while escaping from her home in Kentucky to Arizona finds a three year old girl on the side of the road named Turtle. Though many of her stories take place in an environment she is familiar with, Kingsolver insists her stories are not taken directly from her own life.

“I figure it’s my job to invent lives that are more entertaining, instructive, and perfect than my own. That’s the main advantage of fiction over life. You get to control the endings,” Kingsolver said.
