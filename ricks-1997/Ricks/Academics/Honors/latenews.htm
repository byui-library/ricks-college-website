HONORS PROGRAM RETREAT: EVOLUTION AND THE GOSPEL
How compelling is the evidence for the evolution of species and, more particularly of the human species? Can one consistently accept the theory of evolution and the creationists' account of the origin of life?
Brother Ed Williams from the Biology Department will be addressing these and other questions on the topic of evolution and the gospel at an honors retreat on 18 October at the Bennion Lodge. All honors students are invited to attend; however, seats are limited to 25. Sign up in the main office of the Department of Humanities and Philosophy. Transportation and dinner are provided by the Honors Program. There is no charge. We shall be leaving from the Taylor west parking lot at 4:30 and returning by 10 p.m.

FEBRUARY RETREATS

Two retreats are planned this semester: February 9 and March 23. 

The first Honors Program retreat of the Millennium is this Wednesday, February 9, at the beautiful Bennion Lodge in Victor. Jerry Hansen of the Religion Department will be presenting and leading a discussion entitled, "Are you sure you want to be educated?"

Our second retreat will challenge your thinking as we bring together the teachings of the Romney and Taylor buildings: Evolution and the Mormon Church.  Ed Williams, a long-time member of the Geology Department, will bring his expertise and some searching questions as we discuss this seeming collision of two worlds. Don't miss this one---and bring your scriptures.

Vans will leave from the west doors of the Taylor Building at 5 pm. Dinner is provided. Because space is limited to 30, interested Honors Program students must signup in the Humanities Department office, Taylor 240. Honors Program faculty may also attend on a space-available basis.

NEW:  ARABIC CLASS OFFERED THIS FALL

Honors offers a new language course this Fall, Arabic I, taught M,W,F at 11:00-11:50 by Rachel Peck, a member of the Languages faculty. This is the first installment in a two-semester sequence, offering an entire year of Arabic to interested students, staff and faculty. This class is for those who are thinking of centering on Middle East (near Eastern) studies as a major or minor area. Anyone interested in studying this region should be aware that other colleges offer language courses to their Freshman and Sophomore students. Our graduates/transferees will be less competitive without language skills and background.

BYU Jerusalem offers an intensive (six-month) Arabic course (in Jerusalem) to those students that have three semesters of Arabic already. This means that our students can a year of Arabic here, and one additional semester elsewhere to qualify for this extraordinary opportunity.

 

October 99 retreat on Eastern philosophy

Honors retreat on the Yin/Yang

By Andrew McLean, Honors student correspondent

The Honors program retreat of October 28 to the Teton Lodge engaged honor students in a discussion of far-eastern culture. Brother David Peck of the History Department, using some impressive computer graphics, instructed students in the basics of Taoism, the I Ching, the Yin/Yang, and the compound nature of everything. "We basked in learning like sunflowers soaking in fresh sunlight," one student reported, getting in the spirit of the East. They also ate some pretty good lasagna, fresh salad, garlic bread, and dessert. A good meal and an fascinating discussion with interesting people in a beautiful location--it doesn't get any better than that!

College Bowl up and running

The College Bowl Club will hold its first meeting on October 7th, at 7 PM in Smith 223. The purpose of the meeting is to organize teams for competition. Students should come and either have a team of four to five members (from a range of disciplines) already organized or come prepared to join a team. National Science Foundation offering fellowships

Although Ricks students may not be ready for graduate fellowships yet, students in science, math, and engineering may be interested to know that the National Science Foundation (NSF) awarded fellowships to nearly 20% of applicants last year. The NSF is particularly interested in honor students. Students in the targeted fields may want to start planning for their future. For more information, contact Murray Hunt or NSF Fellowship Operations directly at nsfgrfp@orau.gov or at (423) 241-4300.
