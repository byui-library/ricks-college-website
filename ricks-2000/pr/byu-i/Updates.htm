The Transition: Updates
This page is a chronological listing of updates with the most current located at the top.  Additional information will be added as it becomes available.
The President’s Council held a question and answer session on Aug. 9 for interested employees and students. Several questions were asked pertaining to the transition to a four-year university. We thought you might be interested in this update:

When will the first upper division classes begin? President David A. Bednar: “I’m not willing to say, but it’s conceivable some will start in the fall of 2001 or by January 2002. It would be foolish to let arbitrary timelines get in the way of what we hope to accomplish. We’re going to do it right.”

Have any specific baccalaureate degrees been approved yet? President Bednar: “Nothing has been approved. We’ll keep you informed about the approval process.”

Are we going to change from three five-week summer terms to two eight-week terms? Don Bird, Academic Vice President: This is being discussed but no final decision has been made. Even if this happens, some programs will be given the flexibility of being shorter, such as the present five-week Discovery programs.

A university usually implies graduate-level courses. Will we truly be a university? Don Bird: Graduate classes are not required to be a university. BYU-Hawaii has a few graduate-level courses but no graduate programs. BYU-Idaho will be governed by the same Board of Trustees as BYU-Provo and BYU-Hawaii. Consequently, we can function like other state systems, such as the University of Wisconsin system that even has a junior college as part of its system.

The name change to BYU-Idaho is not immediate. (June 29, 2000)
Perhaps the most frequently asked question since the announcement by President Hinckley last week has been, "When do we begin using the name Brigham Young University-Idaho?" For the time being, we will continue as Ricks College. We will not begin using the name Brigham Young University-Idaho in the immediate future. Earlier today [June 29, 2000] a letter was mailed to the Northwest Accreditation Commission informing them of our intent to change to a four-year institution. We also are required to file a prospectus outlining our strategic plan and programs. Obviously, the prospectus will take time to develop. After the prospectus is filed, the Northwest Commission will respond to our request and either accept or reject us as a candidate for accreditation at the four-year level. Only at the point of being accepted as a candidate will it be appropriate for us to begin using the name of Brigham Young University-Idaho.

Therefore, the name of Ricks College will continue to be used for letterheads, business cards, correspondence, etc. Any materials that need to be printed at this time will continue to use the authorized Ricks College logo.
