Ricks College News Release
March 2, 2001

Devotional: Elder Andrew Skinner

By TRENT TOONE

Rick College News Bureau

REXBURG, Idaho — Andrew Skinner, a dean of religious education at Brigham Young University in Provo, Utah, and former member of the Ricks College faculty, spoke about the role of the atonement and Jesus Christ’s sacrifice Tuesday in the weekly Ricks College devotional.

“The atonement of Christ is the most important event in time or all eternity, and you and I are at the very center of it,” Skinner said. “I would like to invite you to review with me, in greater detail, what did happen in Gethsemane so long ago, with the end in mind, that you and I can be better sons and daughters.”

In order to teach about the atonement more effectively, Skinner divided his talk into four separate acts.

He told of Jesus and his disciples partaking in the feast of the Passover and Judas leaving to take of personal business as Act I of the story. The story is found in St. Matthew of the Bible. Jesus and the apostles went to the Garden of Gethsemane as Act II. Act III took place as Jesus went off on his own and began his act of sacrifice through the eyes of St. Mark in the Bible.

“Jesus began to feel very heavy, and it wasn’t because he had just eaten a big meal,” Skinner said. “He began to feel heavy because of what began to crush him in the garden. Not just the sin, it’s for suffering, hardship, all of the ugliness in life, it’s for all the unfairness, all the injustice in life that you and I experience in life, that is the awful arithmetic of the atonement.”

Act IV portrayed the physical effects of the atonement, through the eyes of St. Luke, who by occupation was a doctor. As part of this act, the Savior also received a visitation from the angel Michael, who helped Christ in the garden by giving him comfort.

Skinner said the final question in all of this was “Why did the Savior endure all he went through in Gethsemane for us? The Savior did this because of his great affection for you and me. He knows what it’s all like, like no one else can know. Let him take the burdens.”

Next Tuesday’s speaker will be Elder Alexander B. Morrison, an emeritus member of the LDS Church’s First Quorum of the Seventy. He will speak at 2 p.m. in the Hart Auditorium. Devotionals are broadcast live and repeated on KRIC-FM.
