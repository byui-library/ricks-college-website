Consider day care options carefully

by Sarah Lunt
LUN99013@BYUI.EDU
Scroll Staff

Day care worker Shelley Erfourth reads "Big Hungry Bear" to her preschool class at KinderCare in Spring Hill, Tenn., May 23, 2001. AP Photo Archive
Many families today put their children into a day care facility when there is not a parent at home to care for them.

Though there are numerous day care options for a concerned parent, not every day care is desirable.

Every state has strict codes that day care facilities must follow.

To open a business, a day care must pass a sanitation and fire inspection. The applicant is also expected to pay for and correct any deficiencies before continuing in the certification process.

Parents may be comforted to know that Idaho Code requires a criminal history check of the owners.

The license day care facilities obtain lasts for two years. Occasionally, a representative of the Department of Health and Welfare will visit the premises to ensure that all the requirements are still being met.

Family day care homes care for six or fewer children and do not need to have a basic day care license or certification.

Actual day care centers care for 13 or more children. One of these operating in Rexburg is The Children’s Place.

Carrie Kostial, center assistant, explained some of the requirements and qualifications for a day care center.

Nancy Davis, director of The Children’s Place has her early childhood education bachelor’s degree. She also has a master’s degree in education. Kostial has a bachelor’s degree in Elementary Education, and many of the teachers have training in elementary education, Kostial said.

When asked about the advantages of a day care center, Kostial wished to remind parents that children will not be baby-sat but rather taught. The day care has a lot of resources, including learning centers, she said.

“[There is] a lot more available at the day care,” she said.

Some may wish to place their children in small day cares that are based in a home. One of these is Diane’s Day Care, based in Idaho Falls.

These do not have to be licensed unless there are over seven children. The maximum children these day cares can have is 13 children, assuming there is only one adult to care for them, Diane Kofoed, owner, said.

Though she is not licensed, Kofoed is certified in CPR and first aid, so she may care for the children. She also chose to get some extra education in child care to make her day care better, she said.

Kofoed said she feels home day cares are a better choice because they are in a homey setting. She also believes the children get more one-on-one attention and have better care.

Besides that, Kofoed really loves her job, she said.

“It’s a fun thing to do. I really enjoy it,” she said.

For day care centers, prices in the Southeastern Idaho area range from $457 for an infant from zero to 12 months to $380 per month for children from six to 12 years old.

Parents who place their children in a group or family day care based in a home will pay around $375 per month for their infants and $346 for children six to 12 years old, according to Susan Allred of SEICAA Children Services.

Parents who are more aware of what is required of a day care and the differences between them may be much more able to make those decisions in the best interest of their child.
