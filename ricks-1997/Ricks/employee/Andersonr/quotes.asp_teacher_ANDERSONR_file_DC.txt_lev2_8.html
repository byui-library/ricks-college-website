It's O.K. to do Things you don't want to do- President Spencer W. Kimball
FROM A MISSIONARY MEETING, MONTEVIDEO URUGUAY--March 6, 1975--Spencer W. Kimball, President
I was interviewing a missionary once, who said: "But I don't like to work, I don't like to tract." When he said that, I said, "Oh is that so--I thought every missionary just loved to tract." He looked at me a little funny and I said, "Did you know that the worthwhile things of life are not the things that you just want to do. They're the things you should do. When you study, do you just love to study in the university? Do you just love to do everything that you do do, or isn't there anything in the world that you do because you ought to do it. You want to do it because you ought to do it." Well, I smiled at him and could see he was thinking a little bit. I said, "What do you like to do?" Well, he didn't like to tract and I said "neither do I." Neither did I enjoy tracting to the degreee that I like to play basketball. But I went forward with my tracting because it was a thing I needed to do, to develop me and to brings souls to Christ. I said, "My goodness my boy, you're headed for a total loss if you only do the things you want to do." I said, "I wanted to go on a mission. I was scared to death. A little fellow coming from a country district. I knew I couldn't do it, or I was quite sure I'd have difficulty, and yet I began to analyze and say, 'Well, John Doe did and Bill Duke did and so and so did and so and so did. I can play basketball better than they can and I can make more baskets, and can do more. So if they can all do it, maybe I can do it.' At any rate, I decided I wanted to go on a mission as I'd been taught all my life. So, when I got near the end of my high school, I went to Globe, Arizona and got a job, so that I could begin to save money, because I wasn't like some missionaries where all they have to do is say, 'Dad, I need some money.' My father was having a little problem with a large family to support, so I went and earned my money. I earned it for my school, for my last 2 or 3 years of my school and then for my mission.
I had a beautiful little black colt my father gave me when it was born. I watched it grow up until it was a handsome, little stallion. I tell you it was a beauty, and just about the time I needed to go on my mission, it was about that time that a man came along and offered me $175 for that colt. Well, that was unheard of, but every penny of the $175 went into my mission, as well as all the money I earned at the dairy.
I had grown up on the farm so I could milk cows. I could do a lot of things. My father bought me a little pitchfork when I was just a little boy and I went out into the field and helped put up the hay and haul it. Well, anyway, down at this dairy I found it wasn't easy either. I didn't like it. I'd milk the cows, and then I'd go in and do the washing, the washing of the cans and of the bottles. We'd use scalding water. Our fingers would get somewhat used to it. Then when I'd go to milk the cows the next time, my fingers would crack and bleed. When I went down to church every Sunday night at Globe Ward, about 2 miles down the railroad we'd walk, I'd hold my hands like this as we walked, (raises hands up into the air) so the weight of that blood wouldn't crucify me. It hurt terribly, so I walked this way. I guess people thought I was giving up. But I wasn't, I wasn't surrendering, I was just on my way. So my fingers bled every day, but every night I went back and milked the cows, (somewhere between 20 and 28 cows), every night and morning, by hand, not with machines. In between we'd go out after we'd turned the cows out into the field and with a square-pointed shovel, we'd clean up after the cows. You know, I never did like to clean manure. It was about the filthiest job I ever had. But I had to do it to keep my job. That was part of my job." So I said to this boy, "so you don't like to tract. Well, for heavens sake what do you like to do that will pay any dividends? Well, think about that too. If tracting is hard, and other visits are hard, and teaching the gospel is hard, and study is hard, will it pay big dividends?"
Missionary Standards Remain for Life- Henry D. Moyle
You cannot develop respect for someone who does not utilize his talents to the best of his ability. This is one of the great problems in the mission field. No matter how good you are, in every mission there are young men who take their callings as missionaries altogether too lightly. For every act of indifference-and this is pretty severe doctrine, but none the less true--for every degree of laziness, for every failure to live up to the highest capacity which is ours in the performance of our missionary duties--there is a penalty...That penalty is that when you leave the mission field, you take with you the standard that is going to be yours all of your life. If any of you Elders feel that you can rise above the standard you fix for yourselves while you are laboring in the mission field after you get home, I am sure you are in for a very, very sad disappointment. I have had the privilege of watching returned missionaries come home from the field for over fifty years, and I have yet to see the first case within my experience where a missionary ever did anything better than he did his missionary work. It is very popular throughout the world to seek to get something for nothing. As far as the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints is concerned, or the Gospel of Jesus Christ, there is no room for such philosophy therein. (Pres. Henry D. Moyle, Northern Calif. Mission)
Joseph Smith Visited Brigham Young- President Brigham Young
Tell the people to be humble and faithful, and be sure to keep the Spirit of the Lord and it will lead them right. Be careful and not turn away the small still voice; it will teach them what to do and where to go; it will yield the fruits of the kingdom. Tell the brethren to keep their hearts open to conviction, so that when the Holy Ghost comes to them, their hearts will be ready to receive it. They can tell the Spirit of the Lord from all other spirits; it will whisper peace and joy to their souls; it will take malice, hatred, strife and all evil from their hearts; and their whole desire will be to do good, bring forth righteousness and build up the kingdom of God. Tell the brethren if they will follow the Spirit of the Lord, they will go right. Be sure to tell the people to keep the Spirit of the Lord; and if they will, they will find themselves just as they were organized by our Father in Heaven before they came into the world. Our Father in Heaven organized the human family, but they are all disorganized and in great confusion. (Journal History, 23 February 1847.)
Joseph Smith Visited Wilford Woodruff Often- Wilford Woodruff
Joseph Smith visited me a great deal after his death, and taught me many important principles...Among other things, he told me to get the Spirit of God; that all of us needed it...He said, "I want you to teach the people to get the Spirit of God. You cannot build up the Kingdom of God without that..." (Wilford Woodruff, Deseret News [Weekly], 7 Nov.1896.)
Teach the People to Get the Spirit -Wilford Woodruff
After the death of Joseph Smith I saw and conversed with him many times in my dreams in the night season...I have had many interviews with Brother Joseph until the last 15 or 20 years of my life; I have not seen him for that length of time. But during my travels in the southern country last winter I had many interviews with President Young, and with Heber C. Kimball, and George A. Smith, and Jedediah M. Grant, and many others who are dead. They attended our conference, they attended our meetings. And on one occasion, I saw Brother Brigham and Brother Heber ride in carriage ahead of the carriage in which I rode when I was on my way to attend conference; and they were dressed in the most priestly robes. When we arrived at our destination I asked Pres. Young if he would preach to us He said 'No, I have finished my testimony in the flesh, I shall not talk to this people any more But (said he) I have come to see you; I have come to watch over you, and to see what the people are doing Then (said he) I want you to teach the people-and I want you to follow this counsel yourself--that they must labor and so live as to obtain the Holy Spirit, for with out this you cannot build up the kingdom; without the Spirit of God you are in danger of walking in the dark, and in danger of failing to accomplish your calling as apostles and as elders in the Church and kingdom of God And, said he, Brother Joseph taught me this principle." (Wilford Woodruff, JD 21 317 318)
