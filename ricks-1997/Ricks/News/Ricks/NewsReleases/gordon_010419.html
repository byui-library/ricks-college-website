Ricks College News Release
April 18, 2001 
Devotional: James Gordon III

REXBURG, Idaho —  James D. Gordon III, a professor at the Reuben J. Clark Law School at Brigham Young University, spoke on having a commitment to the gospel of Jesus Christ at Tuesday’s Ricks College devotional.

"We don’t live the law of consecration right now," Gordon said. "However, we are expected to consecrate our time, abilities and material possessions to build up the kingdom of God. We are expected to be fully committed to the gospel of Jesus Christ."

Gordon outlined four principles that, if followed, he said would help people to be more committed to the gospel.

The first principle is faith. "We need to exercise our faith," he said. "Just as the Lord was there for (the Apostle) Peter, he is there for us too. If we trust him, we can, figuratively speaking, walk on water. We cannot do it by ourselves. We must have faith in him to overcome our fears and feelings of inadequacy. But with his help, we can work miracles."

Courage is the second principle. "Faith and courage overcome fear," Gordon said.

He illustrated courage by telling the courageous story of Drusilla and James Hendricks, early converts to The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. Soon after joining the church, James was shot and paralyzed from the neck down and Drusilla, the mother of five children, overcame many challenges as she took care of the family and successfully got the family to the Salt Lake Valley.

Gordon said the third principle is putting the gospel first.

"We want the blessings that come from consecrating everything we have to the Lord, but we also want to hold things back," he said. "I learned for myself that wealth does not satisfy; it does not bring joy. Living the gospel brings joy. Feeling the spirit brings joy. Loving and serving others brings joy."

His last principle is to have an eternal perspective. Gordon said the most important blessing is eternal life, the kind of life that God lives.

"We give everything we have to our Heavenly Father, and in return he has to us," he said. "By doing as the Lord asks, we receive eternal life. In addition, we become better, stronger and happier people. We feel more joy in our lives, we are not here on earth to satisfy our pride or our greed. We are here to serve God and to prepare ourselves and those around us for eternal life."

The next devotional will be held May 8 when Keller F. Ellsworth, manager of Support Facilities at Ricks, will speak at 9:10 a.m. in the Taylor Chapel.
